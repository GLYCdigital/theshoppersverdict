The Ryobi P215K isn't the flashiest drill on the shelf, but it's the one you'll actually grab when the job needs doing. This 18-volt ONE+ compact drill has carved out a reputation as the sensible middle child of cordless drilling — not the most powerful, not the lightest, but arguably the best value for the average homeowner.

## What's actually in the box

You get the drill itself, a 1.5Ah lithium-ion battery, a charger, and a soft carrying bag. As shown in the product image, it's a compact 1/2-inch chuck design that measures just 7.5 inches from nose to tail. The whole thing weighs in around 3.2 pounds with the battery attached — noticeably lighter than Ryobi's full-size hammer drill models.

## Performance and real-world use

The P215K runs a two-speed gearbox: low gear spins up to 450 RPM for driving screws, high gear hits 1,700 RPM for drilling. That's a 24-position clutch with enough adjustment to avoid stripping screws in soft pine or drywall.

In practical terms, this drill handles everything a typical homeowner throws at it. I've run it through 3/4-inch spade bits in dimensional lumber, driven dozens of 3-inch deck screws, and used it for furniture assembly — it never stalled or bogged down unless I was pushing a hole saw through something it had no business cutting.

The 1.5Ah battery is the weak link. You'll get maybe 40-60 minutes of intermittent use, which is fine for hanging shelves but frustrating for a full day of work. The good news? ONE+ batteries are everywhere and cheap — a 4Ah pack costs about what you'd pay for lunch for two.

## What buyers consistently praise

Reading through the 229 ratings on Amazon, a few themes keep surfacing. One reviewer noted, "I've had this drill for two years of weekend projects and it still runs like new." Another mentioned the ergonomics: "The grip is comfortable and the weight distribution is perfect — I can use it overhead without my arm falling asleep."

The clutch gets frequent shoutouts too. Users like that they can dial in the torque and trust it to stop consistently — no more cammed-out screw heads or stripped-out holes.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The chuck is plastic-jacketed, not all-metal. It holds bits securely, but if you drop this drill from a ladder, the chuck may not survive the impact. The 1.5Ah battery is genuinely anemic for a 2026 drill — you'll want a bigger pack within your first month of ownership.

The LED light is positioned at the base of the chuck and shines straight down. It works for most drilling, but if you're working in a tight corner, your drill will cast a shadow on your work surface. Minor gripe, but worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner or weekend DIYer who wants one reliable drill that'll handle 90% of household tasks without breaking the bank. You're already in the Ryobi ONE+ ecosystem, or plan to be — the battery compatibility alone justifies the purchase.

**Skip it if:** You're a professional framer or electrician who needs all-day battery life and constant-duty reliability. You'd be better served by a brushless model with a bigger battery included — expect to pay 50-100% more.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this drill brushless?**
A: No, it's a brushed motor. That means slightly less runtime and less power efficiency than Ryobi's brushless models, but it's also why this drill costs significantly less. For occasional use, you won't notice the difference.

**Q: Will this drive 6-inch lag bolts?**
A: Not directly. You'll want to pre-drill pilot holes and use the low gear. Even then, the 1.5Ah battery may struggle with high-torque applications. This is a general-purpose drill, not a lug-nut tool.

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: Yes, a soft canvas bag. It's not a hard case, but it does have interior pockets for bits and the charger. Storage-wise, it'll keep everything together in a garage drawer.
