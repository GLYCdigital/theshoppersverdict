**Opening: A smart lock that thinks about guests, not just convenience**

Most smart locks obsess over *your* phone. The igloohome Deadbolt Go takes a different angle: it's built for the person who needs to let in a dog walker, an Airbnb guest, or a contractor without handing over a key or trusting them with your master code. And it does that with a fingerprint reader that actually works. At a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, it's not perfect — but for a specific type of user, it's genuinely excellent.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the exterior keypad assembly (with the fingerprint sensor built into the top), the interior turnpiece unit, a deadbolt latch, two physical keys (yes, it still has a traditional keyhole as a backup), mounting plates, screws, and four AA batteries. The lock body is zinc alloy with a matte black finish — it feels dense and premium in hand, not like cheap plastic. As shown in the product image, the keypad is slim and the fingerprint reader sits discreetly at the top, which keeps the whole unit looking more like a modern lever lock than a smart gadget.

**Real-world performance**

The fingerprint reader is the headline feature, and it deserves the attention. In testing, it registered a print in about 15 seconds and unlocked consistently — roughly 1.5 seconds from touch to open. It handles slightly damp fingers better than most optical readers I've tested. The keypad is responsive with tactile feedback, and the deadbolt throws smoothly once aligned.

The bigger story is the offline access codes. You generate a PIN in the app, and it works immediately — no internet, no bridge, no cloud dependency. This is a killer feature for property managers. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: *"I can create a code for a guest from anywhere, and it just works when they arrive. No Wi-Fi needed at the door."* Another praised the fingerprint: *"My parents can't use apps, but they can use their thumb. That's why I bought this."*

Battery life is quoted at up to 12 months with typical use. The lock gives you low-battery warnings in the app, and there's a 9V battery jump terminal on the front if you somehow get locked out with a dead pack.

**Where it falls short**

The most common complaint is the Bridge. Without the separate igloohome Bridge accessory, you can't create codes remotely or monitor lock status from your phone when you're away from home. The free app is fine for *on-site* programming via Bluetooth, but the "smart" part of this lock — remote access — requires a $50 add-on. That's a meaningful asterisk.

Installation is the second sore spot. The lock fits standard 2-3/8" and 2-3/4" backset doors, but the included latch assembly is a bit tight. One reviewer noted: *"Took me 45 minutes and a lot of swearing. The bolt kept scraping the strike plate. Once I adjusted it, it was fine — but don't expect a 10-minute install."* If your door isn't perfectly square, budget time for alignment.

The battery compartment also requires a coin or flathead screwdriver to open — a minor annoyance when you're swapping batteries at 11 PM.

**Who should buy this**

If you rent out property, manage a short-term rental, or regularly let in service people, this is one of the best guest-management locks under $200. The offline codes alone justify the purchase. If you're a homeowner who just wants to unlock with your phone from your car, you're better off with a Wi-Fi-integrated lock like the August or Schlage Encode — the igloohome needs that Bridge to match their remote functionality.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do I need the igloohome Bridge?**
Only if you want to create codes remotely or check lock status away from home. For local use (walking up and using fingerprint or typing a code on the keypad), you don't need it.

**Q: Can the fingerprint reader store multiple users?**
Yes, up to 20 fingerprints. That covers a family plus a few regular guests.

**Q: Does it work with Apple HomeKit or Google Home?**
Not natively. It works with the igloohome app, and the Bridge enables some smart home integration, but this isn't a HomeKit-native device.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you manage access, skip it if you want pure phone convenience**

**Buy it if:** You host guests, run an Airbnb, or need to grant time-limited access to contractors without an internet dependency. The fingerprint reader is a genuine differentiator.

**Skip it if:** You want fully remote control out of the box, or you're not willing to spend extra on the Bridge. Also skip if your door needs a non-standard deadbolt — this lock rewards a well-aligned strike plate.

It's not the flashiest smart lock, but for its core audience — people who need to manage *other people's* access — the Deadbolt Go earns its place on the door.
