## This Alarm Does Exactly What It Promises — Nothing More, Nothing Less

If you're replacing a dated smoke detector or wiring a new build, the First Alert Interconnect Hardwire Dual Sensor Smoke Alarm is the no-nonsense workhorse you're probably looking for. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's not flashy, but it's consistently reliable. Here's the real deal after digging through buyer feedback and spec sheets.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the detector unit, a mounting bracket, the hardwire connector (with the standard 3-wire pigtail — black, white, and the red interconnect line), plus a 9V battery for backup. There's also a dust cover for construction phases. No screws included, which is a minor annoyance if your old unit's screws are stripped — you'll be making a hardware store run.

## Dual Sensor Technology: Why It Matters

This is the headline feature. The unit combines **ionization sensing** (catches fast-flaming fires — think grease fires, paper) with **photoelectric sensing** (catches slow-smoldering fires — think upholstery, wiring behind walls). Most cheap detectors only have one type. If you can only install one alarm per floor, dual sensor is the smarter investment because it covers both fire profiles.

One buyer noted: *"My old detector was photoelectric only. I tested this one with a controlled smolder and a quick flame — it caught both. My old one missed the smolder until it was really going."*

## Hardwired Interconnect: The Real Value

The interconnect feature is what separates this from a $15 standalone unit. When wired correctly (the red wire daisy-chained between alarms), if the basement unit triggers, every connected alarm on the circuit sounds simultaneously. That's a genuine lifesaver in a two-story house where you might not hear a distant alarm from the basement.

**Installation warning:** This requires a **3-wire connection** (hot, neutral, interconnect). If your home was built in the 70s or 80s with 2-wire smoke alarm circuits, this won't interconnect without running new wire. Check your ceiling box before you buy.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The housing is solid polycarbonate — not flimsy. The test/silence button is responsive. The LED blinks red every 30 seconds in normal operation, which is subtle enough not to be annoying. The alarm itself is loud — 85 decibels at 10 feet, which is standard but sufficient.

The **battery backup** is a double-edged sword. It works — the alarm keeps functioning during outages. But it uses a standard 9V, not a sealed 10-year lithium battery. Expect to swap it annually. A few reviewers mentioned the low-battery chirp started around 11 months. Plan for it.

## Where It Falls Short

- **No voice alerts.** It's just a beep. If you're trying to distinguish "smoke in kitchen" from "smoke in basement," you'll need a smarter (read: pricier) system.
- **Interconnect setup is finicky.** The connector is a push-in type, not screw terminals. One reviewer reported: *"The wire push-in connector felt cheap. I had to re-seat it twice before the interconnect actually worked."* Test your interconnect by holding the test button on one unit and confirming all others sound.
- **No tamper lock.** If you have kids, they can pull it off the bracket. Minor, but worth noting.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners replacing a single failed alarm** — straightforward swap if you have 3-wire wiring.
- **Builders or landlords** installing multiple units in a new build — the interconnect is cost-effective versus smart systems.
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want dual-sensor coverage without paying Nest or Google prices.

**Skip it if** you need smart home integration, voice alerts, or a sealed 10-year battery. Look elsewhere.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Can I interconnect this with my existing First Alert alarms?**
A: Yes, if your existing units are hardwired with the same 3-wire interconnect standard. But mixing brands or very old models can cause compatibility issues — test the interconnect function after installation.

**Q: Does this alarm detect carbon monoxide?**
A: No. It's smoke-only. You need a separate CO detector or a combo unit.

**Q: How do I silence a false alarm from cooking?**
A: Press the test/silence button. It will temporarily reduce sensitivity for about 8 minutes, then return to normal. It's not a permanent mute.
