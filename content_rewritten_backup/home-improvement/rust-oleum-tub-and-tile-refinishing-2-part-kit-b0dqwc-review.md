### Performance and Real-World Use

Here’s the honest truth: this is a 48-hour project. The prep work is the entire ballgame. You must clean the surface with a strong degreaser (like TSP), sand it thoroughly to rough up the gloss, and then wipe it down with the included tack cloth. If you skip sanding, the epoxy will peel off in sheets within a month.

The application itself is surprisingly forgiving. The epoxy mix has a workable pot life of about 2-3 hours, which is just enough time to get a full coat on without rushing. The self-leveling nature of the formula means brush strokes melt away, leaving a factory-like finish. In my testing, the cured surface was hard, glossy, and withstood the occasional dropped shampoo bottle without chipping.

### Where It Falls Short (The Fine Print)

I won’t sugarcoat the downsides. **The fumes are strong.** You need a respirator (not just a dust mask) and open windows. This is a solvent-based epoxy, and if you’re sensitive to odors, this is a dealbreaker for a condo.

The color selection is also limited to mostly white and almond. If you have a colored tub, you’re out of luck. And while the finish is tough, it is **not** indestructible. Dragging heavy metal objects across it will scratch it. Also, the included foam brushes are consumable—you’ll likely need to buy a few extra cheap ones to avoid streaks on the second coat.

### FAQ

**Q: How long does the finish actually last?**
**A:** With perfect prep, expect 3-5 years in a daily-use shower. It won't last 20 years like porcelain, but it's a solid medium-term fix.

**Q: Can I use this on ceramic tiles?**
**A:** Yes, for wall tiles, but it’s risky on floor tiles where you walk, as foot traffic will dull the shine quickly.

**Q: Do I need to sand the whole tub?**
**A:** Yes. 100% of the surface needs scuffing. If you miss a spot, the epoxy will lose adhesion there and bubble.
