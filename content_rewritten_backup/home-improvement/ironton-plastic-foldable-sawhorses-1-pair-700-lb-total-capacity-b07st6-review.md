If you've ever wrestled a wobbly metal sawhorse that folds up mid-cut, you know the frustration. The Ironton Plastic Foldable Sawhorses aim to fix that with a rigid plastic frame, a 700-pound combined rating, and a price that undercuts most wooden alternatives. After digging through 215 ratings and testing the geometry against real workshop scenarios, here's the honest breakdown.

**What you actually get**

The box contains two identical sawhorses, each rated at 350 pounds individually. They arrive fully assembled — no bolts, no Allen wrenches, no 20-minute assembly dance. Each unit measures roughly 31 inches tall with a 30-inch top surface. The legs fold inward via a center hinge mechanism, collapsing the pair to about four inches thick for storage. As shown in the product image, the design uses cross-bracing molded into the plastic rather than metal pins, which keeps weight down to about 12 pounds for the pair.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The plastic is a thick, glass-filled polypropylene that feels closer to a contractor-grade toolbox than a cheap lawn chair. The top surface has a non-slip texture and slight lip on both edges — useful for keeping 2x4s from rolling off mid-measure. The legs lock open with a positive click, and there's no lateral wobble when you lean into a cut.

Where these shine is sheet goods. A full 4x8 sheet of 3/4-inch plywood spans the pair with maybe a quarter-inch of bow in the middle — that's acceptable deflection at this price point. One reviewer noted, "I've had these for two years and they've held up to daily use framing out a basement. The plastic doesn't crack in cold weather like I worried." Another echoed that durability: "Used them for a full kitchen remodel. They still lock tight and show zero stress cracks."

The folding mechanism deserves credit. It's a simple scissor-style collapse that doesn't require pinching fingers or fighting springs. You can fold one with a single hand while holding a work piece in the other — that's rare in this category.

**Where it falls short**

The feet are the weak point. On smooth garage concrete, they'll slide when you're ripping long boards with aggressive feed pressure. Several buyers mention this: "Works fine on my driveway, but on polished garage floor they skate." A strip of rubber drawer liner or adhesive cabinet pads fixes it for under $5, but it's an annoyance out of the box.

The fixed height is another limitation. At 31 inches, these are comfortable for average-height users standing upright, but if you're over 6-foot-2 or prefer working at bench height, you'll be bending. There's no height adjustment — what you see is what you get.

Finally, the center seam on the top surface is a raised ridge. It's subtle, but if you're cross-cutting thin trim or veneer, the seam can catch the workpiece. Run a quick pass with sandpaper if it bothers you.

**Who should buy these**

- **DIY homeowners** doing weekend projects: drywall, paint, trim, temporary work surfaces. These replace rickety metal sawhorses with something that won't collapse.
- **Contractors needing portable support**: the flat-fold design and 12-pound weight make them ideal for van or truck storage. One reviewer said, "I keep a pair in the work truck and they've replaced my wooden sawhorses entirely."
- **Apartment or small-shop users** short on space: fold them flat, hang them on a wall hook, done.

**Skip them if** you need adjustable heights, work primarily on uneven ground (the plastic feet don't self-level), or plan to exceed the 700-pound rating — that's a hard no.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use these with a circular saw on top?**  
A: Yes, but the surface is only about 4 inches wide. Clamp a straightedge or sacrificial board to the top for stable cuts.

**Q: Will they hold a 350-pound person?**  
A: Technically rated for it, but I wouldn't recommend standing on them. The top surface is narrow and the legs splay slightly under load. Use a proper ladder or step stool.

**Q: Do they fold flat enough to fit between studs?**  
A: At roughly 4 inches thick folded, they'll slide into most wall cavities or under a workbench. They won't fit in a standard 3.5-inch stud bay, so plan accordingly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want lightweight, genuinely foldable sawhorses that handle real work without the wobble of cheap metal alternatives. The 700-pound capacity is honest, the build quality exceeds the price point, and the flat-fold design is a genuine space-saver.

**Skip it if** you need height adjustability, work on polished concrete without adding grip, or expect to exceed the weight rating — those are the dealbreakers.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — solid value with two fixable flaws.
