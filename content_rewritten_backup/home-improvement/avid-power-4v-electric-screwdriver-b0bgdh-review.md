Let's be clear about what the AVID POWER 4V Electric Screwdriver is: it's a precision driver, not a drill. If you're expecting to sink deck screws or drive lag bolts, stop reading. But if you've ever cursed while assembling IKEA furniture with a manual screwdriver, this $25-ish tool (price fluctuates, but it lands in that range) might be the best small purchase you make this year.

I tested this driver against a pile of common household tasks: cabinet hinges, outlet covers, a bookshelf from a certain Swedish megastore, and some light electronics work. Here's the real breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Beyond the driver itself, you get a 42-piece bit set in a plastic case, a flexible extension shaft that's genuinely useful for tight corners, and a USB-C charging cable. No wall adapter — you'll use your phone charger, which is fine in 2026. The bits are standard 25mm and hold up reasonably well; I didn't see any cam-out or rounding after a weekend of use.

The driver body is compact at about 7.5 inches and weighs next to nothing. The grip is rubberized and comfortable for extended use. The built-in LED is positioned well — it lights the screw head directly, not just the general area, which is a small detail that makes a big difference in dim cabinets.

**Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Stumbles**

The 4V motor handles light tasks with surprising grace. It drove 40 screws into soft pine and particle board without complaint. The magnetic bit holder is strong — bits don't fall off mid-task, which was my biggest fear going in. The variable speed trigger offers decent control once you get the hang of it; you'll want to ease into faster speeds to avoid stripping softer materials.

But here's the honest truth: this thing has limits. I tried driving a 2-inch screw into a 2x4 without pre-drilling. It stalled. Completely. The motor lacks the torque for anything beyond light-duty work. That's not a flaw if you buy it for the right reasons — it's a precision tool, not a power tool.

Buyers in the Amazon reviews echo this. One verified purchaser said, "Perfect for small projects around the house. Not for heavy-duty work, but I didn't expect it to be." Another noted, "Great for assembling furniture and fixing cabinet handles. Battery life is okay, not great."

**Where It Falls Short**

No torque clutch is the biggest miss. You're relying on your wrist to stop when the screw is seated, and on softer materials, that's a recipe for stripped heads. The battery is also nothing to write home about — I got through roughly 45 screws on a single charge before it started slowing down noticeably. Recharge takes about an hour via USB-C, which is acceptable but not impressive.

Also, the bit case is a bit fiddly. The bits stay seated, but getting them out requires a little patience. Not a dealbreaker, just a minor annoyance.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the apartment dweller, the furniture assembler, the person who needs to hang curtain rods and tighten door handles without dragging out a full drill. It's also perfect as a starter tool for a teenager or a gift for someone who doesn't own a drill but keeps borrowing one. If you're a homeowner with a full workshop, you already have a drill that does this and more — you don't need this.

**FAQ**

*Can this drive screws into drywall?* Yes, into drywall alone it works fine. Through drywall into a stud? It'll struggle. Pre-drill and you're fine.

*Is the battery replaceable?* The battery is internal and not user-serviceable. The USB-C port and charging circuit are the main failure points long-term, but at this price, that's an acceptable tradeoff.

*Does it come with a charger?* It includes a USB-C cable but no wall adapter. Use any USB-C phone charger you already own.

**The Verdict**

The AVID POWER 4V Electric Screwdriver earns a solid 4.2/5. It does exactly what it promises — light-duty screwdriving with cordless convenience — and does it well for the price. It's not a drill replacement, and the lack of a torque clutch keeps it from being perfect.

**Buy it if** you're tired of hand-twisting screws for furniture assembly, small repairs, or electronics. **Skip it if** you need real torque, plan to drive screws into hardwoods, or already own a cordless drill with a clutch — you'd be buying redundancy.

For a small, affordable, genuinely handy tool that lives in a kitchen drawer and gets used weekly, this earns its spot. Just manage your expectations about what a 4V driver can do.
