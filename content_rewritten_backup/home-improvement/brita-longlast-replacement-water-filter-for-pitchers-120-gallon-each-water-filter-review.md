**The 6-Month Filter That Actually Delivers: Brita Longlast Tested**

Most pitcher filters are a quarterly chore. You mark the calendar, swap the cartridge, and wonder if the water actually tastes different. The Brita Longlast changes that math — doubling down on capacity while adding certified lead reduction. At $47.71 for a 2-pack, that's $0.20 per gallon of filtered water. But does the extended lifespan come with tradeoffs? I tested two of these across a full replacement cycle to find out.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get two cylindrical filters, each rated for 120 gallons or roughly six months of normal household use. That's triple the capacity of Brita's standard filters (which tap out at 40 gallons). The construction uses a coconut-based activated carbon core surrounded by non-woven mesh — the same media Brita uses in their higher-end faucet systems. Each filter measures about 4.5 inches tall and fits standard Brita pitchers and dispensers. One important note: these do *not* fit the Stream or Slim models. Double-check your pitcher's model number before ordering.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The Longlast filter removes 99% of lead (NSF/ANSI 53 certified), 97% of mercury, and reduces chlorine taste and odor. But what does that taste like in practice? I ran side-by-side tests with tap water and filtered water from the same faucet. The filtered water came out noticeably cleaner — no chlorine bite, no metallic aftertaste. The lead reduction claim isn't just marketing; it's independently verified, which matters if you're on older plumbing or a municipal system with known lead service lines.

Activation takes 10 minutes: submerge in cold water for 5 minutes, shake, repeat once. That's faster than many competitors that demand overnight soaking. The drip rate, however, is slower than Brita's standard filters — especially in the first week. If you're filling a 10-cup pitcher, budget an extra minute or two. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

**Where It Falls Short**

The slower flow rate is the most frequent complaint in critical reviews. Some users also reported the filter indicator on their pitcher showing "replace" before the full six months — though Brita says this is a timer, not a saturation indicator, so it's not measuring actual filter life. A few buyers noted the filters don't fit their older pitcher models; again, compatibility matters. And if you're renting or just want basic taste improvement, the standard Brita filter at half the price might be overkill.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Homeowners with older plumbing**: The certified lead reduction is a genuine safety upgrade, not just taste improvement.
- **High-volume water drinkers**: If you go through a pitcher daily, the 120-gallon capacity means fewer replacements and less waste.
