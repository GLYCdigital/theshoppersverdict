Let's talk about this gold bathroom faucet honestly. With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but it's also not what a lot of shoppers expect when they click "gold faucet."

**What you actually get**

The box contains the two handle assemblies, the spout, three flexible supply lines with pre-attached nuts, and mounting hardware. That's it. No drain, no pop-up stopper. Factor that into your budget — a matching antique brass drain will run you another $20–40.

The finish is the headline here. It's an antique brass treatment, which means it's deliberately darkened and brushed to look aged. As one buyer put it, "The finish is much more muted than the bright gold in the listing photos — it's closer to a champagne bronze." That's accurate. If you want that blinding, polished-gold look you see in luxury hotel bathrooms, this isn't it. If you want something that won't show water spots and fingerprints ten minutes after you wipe it down, this is exactly right.

**Build and performance**

The body is solid brass, not that zinc-alloy stuff you find at the low end. The spout has a nice heft to it, and the ceramic cartridge inside each handle gives smooth, precise temperature control. No drips, no squeaks. The 8-inch widespread configuration fits standard three-hole sinks, and the supply lines are flexible enough that you won't need to contort yourself under the vanity.

One thing multiple reviewers flagged: the crystal handles are smaller than the photos suggest. They're elegant, but if you have larger hands or arthritis, they can be awkward to grip. That's a real ergonomic consideration, not a nitpick.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the finish surprise and the missing drain, there are two honest complaints in the reviews. First, installation is doable for a competent DIYer but not trivial — you need to be comfortable working under a sink and getting the handles aligned just right. Several reviewers mentioned the handles needed adjustment after a few weeks as they settled. Second, the antique finish, while durable against water, will scratch if you're aggressive with abrasive cleaners. Stick to mild soap and a soft cloth.

**Who should buy this**

- **The renovation purist** doing a vintage-style bathroom where a bright chrome or polished nickel faucet would clash. The antique brass here pairs beautifully with oil-rubbed bronze fixtures and warm wood tones.
- **The low-maintenance homeowner** who doesn't want to polish faucets every week. The brushed finish hides water spots remarkably well.
- **The mid-range budget shopper** who wants brass construction without paying $400+ for a name-brand equivalent.

Skip it if you need a matching drain, want bright mirror-gold, or have a single-hole sink without a deck plate.

**Q: Will this fit my existing sink?**
A: It requires a three-hole sink with 8-inch center-to-center spacing. Some two-hole and single-hole sinks with a deck plate can work, but measure carefully — returns are a hassle.

**Q: Is the finish actually gold or bronze?**
A: It's an antique brass finish — think aged, brushed gold with darker undertones. It's not bright or shiny. If that's what you want, this is the wrong product.

**The Verdict**
