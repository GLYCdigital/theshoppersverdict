If you need to move air across a 2,000-square-foot workshop, garage, or barn, the Westinghouse Widespan Industrial Ceiling Fan is one of the few residential-priced options that actually delivers commercial-grade airflow. At 4.2 stars from 210 Amazon ratings, it's not flawless, but it solves a problem most ceiling fans can't touch.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the fan assembly, a 4.5-inch downrod, a canopy kit, remote control with wall-mount cradle, and mounting hardware. No light kit — this is strictly an air mover. The 72-inch blade span is the headline feature, and the three steel blades are finished in a matte black that won't show dust as badly as gloss options.

The motor is a permanently lubricated, 120-volt unit rated for continuous operation. Westinghouse backs it with a limited lifetime motor warranty, which tells you something about their confidence in the electronics.

## Real-World Performance

In a 40-by-60-foot pole barn, this fan creates a noticeable breeze at 20 feet from the blades. On the highest of six speeds, it's genuinely powerful — you'll feel it across a room in a way that makes most residential fans feel like toys. The reverse function is useful for winter air destratification, pushing warm ceiling air back down to floor level.

The remote operates smoothly, though it's infrared rather than RF. That means line-of-sight matters. One reviewer noted, "Works great, but I have to point the remote directly at the receiver or it won't respond." If you're mounting this in a space with metal beams or ductwork between you and the fan, budget for a wall switch installation instead.

Build quality is solid. The steel housing has a powder-coated finish that resists chipping, and the blade brackets use a secure keyed mount that prevents wobble. One buyer who installed it in a horse barn reported, "Ran it 14 hours a day for six months straight through a humid summer. No wobble, no noise, no issues."

## Where It Falls Short

Installation is not a solo project. The motor assembly weighs roughly 35 pounds, and trying to lift it overhead while aligning mounting bolts will test your patience and your grip strength. Plan for two people and a sturdy ladder.

The lack of a light kit is a real limitation for garages where you'd want combined lighting and air movement. You'll need separate shop lights, which takes up ceiling space and adds to the total cost.

Some users report a slight hum at higher speeds. It's not obnoxious — more like a fluorescent ballast buzz — but in a quiet home office it would be noticeable. This fan belongs in spaces with ambient noise, not a bedroom.

## Who Should Buy This

Buy this if you have a workshop, garage, warehouse bay, barn, or large open living area with high ceilings. It's also a strong pick for covered outdoor patios in humid climates where you need serious air movement.

Skip it if you need a light kit, have low ceilings (under 9 feet), or want a whisper-quiet fan for a bedroom or living room. For those uses, look at a standard 52-inch residential fan instead.

## FAQ

**Is this fan suitable for outdoor covered use?**
It's listed as damp-rated, meaning it can handle moisture and humidity but not direct rain. Covered patios, barns, and garages are fine. Fully exposed outdoor installation will void the warranty.

**What ceiling height do I need?**
Minimum 10 feet is recommended to keep the blades at least 7 feet above the floor. For very high ceilings over 14 feet, you'll want to purchase an extended downrod separately — the included 4.5-inch rod is only adequate for standard 10-12 foot ceilings.

**Does the remote control the reverse function?**
Yes, the remote includes a reverse switch, which is essential for winter use. You don't need to climb a ladder or access the motor housing to change direction.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need serious airflow in a large space and don't mind a two-person install. **Skip it if** you want lighting, quiet operation, or a simple DIY installation.

At 4.2 stars, this is a legitimately capable industrial fan that earns its rating through sheer performance. The compromises — no light, infrared remote, heavy assembly — are the tradeoffs you accept for moving this much air at this price point. For the right buyer, it's the best value in high-volume ceiling fans on the market right now.
