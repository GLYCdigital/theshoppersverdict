If you've ever stood in a shower with a single spray pattern and thought, "This is fine, I guess," the Delta R11000 diverter valve is designed to make you question that acceptance. This is the valve that lets you run a rain head, a handheld wand, and body sprays — all from one control. It's a serious upgrade, but it's not a simple one.

## What's Actually in the Box

The R11000 is the valve body — the part that lives behind your wall. You're getting the brass valve core, mounting brackets, and the necessary connection fittings. What you're not getting is the trim (the handle and cover plate you see from inside the shower). That's sold separately, and it's important to know: this valve only works with Delta's R10000 series trims. That's a closed ecosystem, so your design options are limited to what Delta offers for that line.

The valve itself is compact — roughly 6 inches tall — and built to fit between standard 16-inch on-center studs without cramping. The body is solid brass with a ceramic disc cartridge, which is the heart of why this thing lasts.

## Performance That Justifies the Price

The core selling point here is the cartridge. Delta's Diamond Seal Technology uses ceramic discs that are harder than stainless steel. There are no rubber washers to degrade, no seats to replace. In practice, that means the handle stays smooth year after year, and you won't get that annoying drip from the showerhead after you shut it off.

The real-world function depends on which version you buy. The 3-setting model controls one overhead showerhead and one handheld — you toggle between them or run both simultaneously. The 6-setting version adds more functions, including a pause mode and combinations with body sprays. Switching between functions is a smooth, positive click. There's no guessing whether you're in the right mode; the detents are firm and audible.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't an accident. A recurring theme in the feedback is the build quality. One reviewer noted, "The brass body is heavy and substantial — you can tell this isn't a plastic valve from a big-box builder pack." Another mentioned the installation specifically: "Took my plumber about an hour to rough in. The connections are clearly marked, and the bracket makes it easy to get square."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the proprietary trim requirement. If you fall in love with a different handle style from another brand, you're out of luck. Second, the 6-setting version is significantly more expensive than the 3-setting, and for most people, three functions (showerhead, handheld, both) are all they'll actually use. Body sprays are a luxury, not a necessity.

Third — and this is the big one — this is a rough-in valve. If you're replacing an existing shower, you're cutting into the wall, redoing the plumbing, and likely hiring a pro unless you're comfortable with soldering copper or PEX. This isn't a weekend DIY project for a novice. Budget for a plumber if you're not already handy with pipe work.

Also worth noting: the valve doesn't include the pressure-balancing mechanism you'll find in some competitors. If you want anti-scald protection, you'll need to pair this with Delta's pressure-balance cartridge or choose a different model. For families with kids, that's a consideration.

## Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Will this work with my existing showerhead?**
Yes, the valve uses standard 1/2-inch connections, so any showerhead with a standard thread will work. The valve doesn't care about your head — it just controls the flow.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
If you've done plumbing rough-in work before, yes. If not, hire a professional. Getting the valve square and at the right depth behind the wall is critical — get it wrong and your trim won't fit flush.

**Q: Does the 6-setting version require special trim?**
No, both versions use the same R10000 series trim. The difference is in the internal cartridge, not the external hardware.
