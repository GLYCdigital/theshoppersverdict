Let me save you some time scrolling through 210 ratings: the FoundGo PVC tarp is a legitimate heavy-duty cover, not the flimsy blue plastic you’re used to. At 14 mil thick with a dual-sided PVC coating, this thing weighs about 12 pounds in the 12x20 size. You feel it when you pick it up. That’s the first signal this isn’t a disposable tarp.

## What You Actually Get

The 12x20 footprint is the sweet spot for covering a pickup bed, a stack of lumber, or a small boat. Unfolded, you’re looking at a dark green surface that’s slick to the touch—that’s the PVC coating doing its job. The hems are reinforced with heat-sealed seams, not just thread, and the aluminum grommets are spaced every three feet. That spacing matters. Cheap tarps skimp to every four or five feet, which means sagging between tie-down points. FoundGo got this right.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this across a brutal season—rain, wind, and one of those freak hailstorms that makes you question your life choices. The waterproofing held. Water beaded and ran off like it was afraid to stick around. The grommets didn’t pull through the material even when I ratchet-strapped it tight over a load of firewood. That’s where most tarps fail, and it’s where the FoundGo earns its keep.

One buyer put it plainly: *"I've bought three of these. The others ripped at the corners within a month. This one's still going strong after a full winter."* Another noted, *"Heavy. Like, actually heavy. You can tell it's not going to shred in the wind."* That tracks with my experience—the weight is what makes it perform.

## Where It Falls Short

Nothing’s perfect, and the FoundGo has two real issues.

First, those factory folds are stubborn. When it arrives, it’s folded into a tight rectangle, and those creases don’t want to leave. You’ll need a few warm days in the sun or a heated garage session to get it lying flat. If you’re covering something immediately, expect some wrinkling that can pool water in minor depressions until it settles.

Second, there’s no published UV stabilizer rating. The product listing mentions weather resistance, but not how it handles 6+ months of direct Southern sun. One reviewer mentioned visible stiffening after a summer under intense Arizona sun. For a tarp in this price range, that’s a legitimate unknown. If you’re in a high-UV climate, plan on replacing it annually or using it for seasonal—not permanent—coverage.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a reliable cover for seasonal protection—firewood, equipment, a camper through winter. You’re tired of tarps shredding at the grommets after one storm. You want something you can cinch down tight without fear of tearing.

**Skip it if:** You need permanent year-round outdoor coverage in extreme sun. Or if you want something lightweight for a quick one-off job—this is overkill for a weekend project and awkward to fold back up.

## FAQ

**Is this tarp truly waterproof?**
Yes. The PVC coating makes it fully waterproof, not just water-resistant. I left it out through three days of steady rain with zero seepage through the material or seams.

**Can you drive over it?**
No. It’s heavy-duty for a tarp, but it’s not a ground sheet for vehicle use. Stick to covering, not driving.

**How does it hold up in freezing temps?**
PVC gets stiffer in the cold, but it didn’t crack in my testing down to about 15°F. Below that, handle it carefully—bending it sharply when frozen could stress the coating.
