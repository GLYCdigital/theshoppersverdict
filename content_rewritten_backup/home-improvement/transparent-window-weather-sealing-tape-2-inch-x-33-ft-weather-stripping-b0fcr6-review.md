If you’ve got a drafty window and you’re tired of feeling the cold seep through gaps, this transparent weather sealing tape from Amazon (2 inches wide, 33 feet long) is a legitimate quick fix. ## What You Actually Get

The roll gives you 33 feet of 2-inch-wide transparent tape. That’s roughly enough to cover two standard double-hung windows (about 3 feet by 5 feet each) with a single continuous perimeter strip. The 2-inch width is the standout spec here — most competing tapes are 1.5 inches, which means you’re often layering strips to cover larger gaps. This one handles most gaps in one pass.

The material is a clear polyethylene film with an acrylic adhesive backing. It’s thin — think heavy-duty packing tape, not duct tape thickness — but that’s intentional. The transparency is the selling point. As shown in the product image, it’s designed to disappear against glass and white frames.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The tape’s job is simple: seal air leaks around windows, baseboards, or even RV vents. In practice, it works best on clean, dry surfaces. The adhesive is aggressive when applied properly — several reviewers noted it held through a full winter without peeling. One buyer wrote, *“I put this up in November and it’s still holding strong in March. No drafts, and I honestly forget it’s there.”*

The transparency holds up. Unlike cheaper clear tapes that turn yellow or milky within weeks, this one stays optically clear. That matters if you care about how your windows look from the outside — no one wants their house to look like a crime scene with blue or white tape crisscrossing every pane.

Where it falls short: cold weather application. The adhesive is pressure-sensitive, meaning it needs warmth to bond properly. Below 40°F, the tape stiffens and doesn’t conform well to uneven surfaces. You’ll need to clean the surface with rubbing alcohol, let it dry completely, and ideally warm the tape with a hairdryer if you’re applying in winter. This is a consistent complaint in the reviews — users who skipped prep work had the tape peel off within days.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the biggest issue is longevity. This is a seasonal product, not a permanent sealant. UV exposure from direct sunlight will degrade the adhesive over 6-8 months, and the tape will start to lift at the corners. If you’re looking for a permanent solution, you need silicone caulk or foam backer rod, not this tape.

Also, it’s not great on textured or painted surfaces. The acrylic adhesive bonds best to smooth glass, vinyl, or metal. On rough wood frames, you’ll get gaps and reduced adhesion. Know your surface before you buy.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does this tape leave residue when removed?**
A: In most cases, no. The acrylic adhesive is designed to peel cleanly. However, if it’s been on for more than 6 months or exposed to heavy UV, you may get slight residue that comes off with rubbing alcohol or Goo Gone. Remove it slowly at a 45-degree angle for the best result.

**Q: Can I use this on double-pane windows with condensation?**
A: Yes, but the surface must be bone-dry when applied. Condensation on the glass will prevent adhesion. Wipe the frame dry, apply, and check back in 24 hours to press down any lifting edges.

**Q: How much area does 33 feet actually cover?**
A: A standard window is about 16 linear feet of perimeter (roughly 3 ft x 5 ft). So one roll covers about two standard windows. Measure your window perimeters before buying — if you have a large picture window, you’ll need two rolls.
