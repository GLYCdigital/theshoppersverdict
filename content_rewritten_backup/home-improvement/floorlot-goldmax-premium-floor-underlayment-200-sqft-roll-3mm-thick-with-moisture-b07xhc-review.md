# FLOORLOT GoldMax Premium Floor Underlayment: The 3-in-1 Layer That Actually Delivers

Most underlayment rolls promise the world and deliver a flimsy sheet of bubble wrap. The FLOORLOT GoldMax is different. This 3mm closed-cell foam with an attached 6-mil poly film combines padding, moisture protection, and sound dampening in one 200-square-foot roll. And at 4.7 stars from 235 ratings, it's earning its reputation. But let's dig into whether it's right for your specific project.

## What You Actually Get

The roll measures 54 inches wide and covers a true 200 square feet — that's roughly a 10x20 room with minimal waste. The 3mm thickness is the sweet spot for most floating floors: thick enough to smooth minor subfloor imperfections, thin enough to keep your flooring's locking mechanisms stable. The attached moisture barrier is the standout feature here. You're getting a combined product that would normally require buying two separate rolls.

## Performance in the Real World

**Installation:** The GoldMax cuts easily with a utility knife and lays flat without curling at the edges. The 54-inch width is a genuine advantage — you'll have fewer seams to tape. One installer noted, "I covered my entire living room in about 20 minutes. The film doesn't tear when you staple it, which was my biggest fear."

**Moisture protection:** The 6-mil poly film is the real deal. It's thick enough to hold up against concrete slab moisture without ripping. If you're installing over a basement or ground-level concrete, this single-layer solution covers the vapor barrier requirement most flooring warranties demand.

**Sound reduction:** Here's where you need realistic expectations. At 3mm, you're getting an IIC rating around 65-68 dB — decent for footfall noise but not concert-level soundproofing. One reviewer put it plainly: "It dulls the 'hollow' sound of laminate, but I can still hear my kids running upstairs. Buy this for moisture protection, not soundproofing."

**Compression resistance:** The closed-cell crosslinked foam holds up. Unlike cheaper open-cell options, it won't compress to nothing under heavy sofas or appliances. After six months, one reviewer reported "zero footprints or divots where my furniture sits."

## Where It Falls Short

The attached film is both the product's greatest strength and its most annoying quirk. If you unroll too fast, the poly film separates from the foam and wrinkles. You'll need to unroll slowly and smooth as you go. It's manageable, but it requires patience.

Second, there's no built-in adhesive strip between rolls. You'll need to buy foil tape separately, which adds a few dollars to your total cost. It's minor, but worth budgeting for.

## Who Should Buy This

**Yes, buy it if:**
- You're installing floating floors (laminate, LVP, or engineered hardwood) over concrete
- You need one product that handles moisture and padding without layering multiple materials
- You're covering a large area and want fewer seams

**Skip it if:**
- Soundproofing between floors is your primary goal — look for 6mm+ cork or rubber underlayment instead
- You're installing over wood subfloor in a dry climate where moisture isn't a concern — you'll pay for protection you don't need

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with radiant floor heating?**
A: Yes, but keep the floor temperature below 85°F. The closed-cell foam won't degrade, but the poly film can soften at sustained higher temperatures. Check your heating system's specs first.

**Q: Does it work under vinyl plank flooring?**
A: Only if your vinyl plank manufacturer allows underlayment. Many rigid-core LVP products are designed to go directly on subfloor. Check your warranty before adding any underlayment — some brands void coverage if you use it.

**Q: How do I overlap the seams?**
A: Butt the edges together tightly with no overlap, then tape with a quality foil tape. Overlapping creates a bump that telegraphs through thin flooring.
