### The 1/4" Tempered Glass Question, Answered

Sliding shower doors are a gamble. Cheap ones rattle, leak, or arrive with cracked glass. The FELYL Framed Single Sliding Shower Glass Door sits in the middle of the market — not the cheapest, not the premium custom stuff — and with 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right door for *your* bathroom? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

### What's in the Box

The package includes the framed glass door panel (1/4" tempered safety glass), the sliding panel, top and bottom tracks, rollers, handles, and all mounting hardware. You also get a threshold piece and rubber sweeps for sealing. Notably, the frame is aluminum with a brushed finish — not plastic-coated, which is a common failure point on cheaper units. The glass arrives pre-drilled for handles and rollers, so there's no on-site drilling of tempered glass (which would shatter it instantly).

### Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The 1/4" tempered glass is the star here. That's the thickness you want for a shower door — anything thinner (like 3/16") flexes and feels flimsy. The tempered rating means it's safety glass; if it breaks, it crumbles into pebbles, not shards. The aluminum frame adds rigidity to the sliding panel, which reduces the "wobble" you get with frameless or semi-frameless units in this price range.

The sliding mechanism uses a top-hung roller system with ball bearings. This matters: bottom-roller systems wear out faster and collect soap scum. The top-hung design keeps the track cleaner and the door glides more smoothly. Buyers consistently mention this in reviews — one noted, *"the roller system is surprisingly smooth for the price; my last door (a cheaper brand) started dragging within six months."*

The door adjusts to fit openings roughly 56-60 inches wide, with a height around 72 inches. That's standard for most tub/shower combos, but measure carefully — this isn't a "one size fits all" product. The frame is adjustable by about 1-2 inches, but if your opening is significantly out of spec, you'll need a different product.

### What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Glass quality**: Multiple reviewers describe the 1/4" glass as "solid" and "heavy-duty." One wrote, *"You can tell just by lifting it that this isn't cheap glass. It feels substantial."*
- **Smooth operation**: The top-hung rollers get repeated mentions. *"Slides like it's on ball bearings — my kids can open it easily,"* one parent noted.
- **Modern look**: The brushed aluminum frame with clear glass fits both contemporary and transitional bathrooms. As one buyer put it, *"Looks way more expensive than it was."*

### Where It Falls Short

**The clear glass is a fingerprint magnet.** If you have hard water, expect water spots within days. This isn't unique to FELYL — it's a clear-glass reality — but the framed design means more metal surfaces to wipe down. Several reviewers recommend a water-repellent coating (like Rain-X) applied immediately after install.

**Installation is not trivial.** This is a two-person, half-day job if you're handy; a full day if you're not. The instructions are adequate but not detailed — no video link, no step-by-step photos with torque specs. One reviewer said, *"The door is great, but the instructions assume you've installed a shower door before. Watch a YouTube video first."*

**The bottom track can collect debris.** Despite the top-hung rollers, the bottom guide track still catches hair and soap scum. It's easier to clean than a full bottom-roller system, but it's not zero-maintenance.

**Sealing is good, not perfect.** The rubber sweeps do their job, but if your tub edge isn't perfectly level, you'll get minor drips. Shimming the track helps, but it's an extra step most people don't plan for.

### Who Should Buy This

- **DIYers with moderate experience** — you've installed a faucet or replaced a toilet; you can handle this.
- **Homeowners replacing an old sliding door** — the dimensions are standard, and the quality upgrade is noticeable.
- **Anyone with a 56-60 inch opening** who wants the 1/4" glass thickness without paying custom prices.

**Skip it if** you have a curved tub, an opening outside the standard range, or you expect zero water leakage without shimming. Also skip if you want a completely frameless look — the aluminum frame is visible and gives a more traditional aesthetic.

### FAQ

**Q: Does this door fit a standard 60-inch tub?**
A: The door adjusts to fit openings from about 56 to 60 inches. Measure the *actual* opening between the walls, not the tub length. If your opening is wider than 60 inches, this won't work.

**Q: Is the glass really tempered, or is it just "safety" glass?**
A: It's genuinely tempered — you'll see the small temper stamp in the corner of the glass. It's also marked with the ANSI Z97.1 certification, which is the US safety standard. This is not a gimmick.

**Q: Can I install this alone?**
A: You *can*, but you'll regret it. The glass panel weighs around 70-80 pounds. Two people are strongly recommended for lifting and aligning the panel into the track. One misstep and you're buying a replacement panel.
