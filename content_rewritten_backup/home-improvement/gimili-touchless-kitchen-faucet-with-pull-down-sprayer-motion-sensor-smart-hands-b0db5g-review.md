The GIMILI Touchless Kitchen Faucet isn't trying to reinvent the sink — it's trying to make the most annoying part of cooking (touching a handle with raw chicken fingers) disappear. At 4.4 stars across 220 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But "touchless" comes with quirks, and you should know them before you cut into your countertop.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body (a Moti design — that's the curved-neck model with a high-arc spout), the pull-down spray head, a weighted hose, mounting hardware, an AC adapter, and the sensor control module. You're looking at a single-handle, high-arc faucet standing about 18 inches tall with a 9-inch spout reach. The spray head docks magnetically, and the hose retracts via a standard counterweight system — nothing exotic, but it works.

The finish options are brushed nickel or stainless steel, both of which resist fingerprints reasonably well. As shown in the product image, the silhouette is sleek and modern — this won't look out of place in a renovated kitchen.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The touchless sensor is the headline feature, and here's the honest breakdown: it's triggered by placing your hand or an object within 6–8 inches of the sensor — which is mounted at the base of the spout, not in the spray head. That's a meaningful difference from some pricier models. The sensor activates the water within a half-second, which is responsive enough to feel natural.

The pull-down sprayer is the real workhorse. It switches between a steady stream and a pressurized spray via a button on the head. The spray pattern is aggressive enough to blast stuck-on food off plates but not so violent that it splashes back at you. The weighted hose retracts cleanly — no tangling, no slapping against the cabinet walls.

Manual operation is straightforward: the handle controls temperature and flow, and it works independently of the sensor. That's a big deal — if the sensor's battery dies or the AC adapter fails, you're not left with a glorified paperweight.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The most consistent praise centers on the sprayer's reach and the magnetic dock. One reviewer noted, *"The spray head snaps back into place with a satisfying click — no more drooping wand."* Another commented on the sensor's reliability: *"It catches my hand every time, even when I'm in a hurry. My kids love it because they can get water without asking for help."*

The install is also a recurring positive. Multiple buyers report a straightforward DIY setup in under an hour, assuming standard 1- or 2-hole configurations. The included deck plate covers the extra hole if you're replacing a faucet with a separate side sprayer.

## Where It Falls Short

The sensor's fixed range is the biggest compromise. If you're filling a large stockpot, the pot's rim can sit right at the sensor's detection zone and trigger the water — or worse, block it entirely mid-fill. Several reviewers mention this: *"It's great for hands, but I have to be careful with my pasta pot."* There's no sensitivity adjustment, so you're stuck with the factory calibration.

Power is the second consideration. The faucet runs on either the included AC adapter (which needs an outlet under your sink) or 4 AA batteries. If you don't have an outlet under the sink, you'll be swapping batteries every 4–6 months depending on usage. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a planning factor.

The spray head itself is slightly bulkier than some competitors — it's not a problem in practice, but it's worth noting if you have a shallow sink.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're replacing a faucet in a family kitchen where hands are constantly dirty — raw meat, flour, garden dirt, kids' sticky fingers. The touchless feature genuinely reduces mess, and the dual-mode control means you're never fully dependent on the sensor.

**Skip it if** you regularly fill huge pots, want a fully hardwired installation, or need a sensor that can be tuned to your cooking habits. Also skip if you have an unusually deep sink — the high arc might not reach.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the sensor work through the spray head?**
No. The sensor is at the base of the spout neck. The spray head has no sensor — you operate it manually with the handle or the head's trigger.

**Q: Can I install this without an electrician?**
Yes, if you have an outlet under the sink. The AC adapter plugs in like any appliance. If you don't have an outlet, batteries work fine, but plan for periodic replacements.

**Q: Will this fit a 3-hole sink?**
Yes, the included deck plate covers the extra holes. It's designed for 1-hole installation, but the plate handles 2- and 3-hole setups cleanly.
