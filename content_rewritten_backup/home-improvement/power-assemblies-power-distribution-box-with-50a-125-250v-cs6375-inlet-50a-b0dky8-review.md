## What You're Getting

The box ships fully assembled with a CS6375 50A inlet, four NEMA 5-20R duplex receptacles, and a main breaker panel that's ready for your specific branch circuit configuration. The enclosure is 14-gauge welded steel with a powder coat finish — not the stamped sheet metal you see on budget units. The cover has a gasket seal that's actually functional, not decorative.

The split-phase configuration is the headline feature. You're getting true 125/250V capability from that CS6375 inlet, which means you can run both legs of a 50A service. That's the difference between powering a single 120V tool string and running a welder, an air compressor, and lighting simultaneously without tripping anything.

## Real-World Performance

I've seen these boxes mounted to job trailers, bolted to generator frames, and sitting in the bed of work trucks. The terminals are rated for continuous duty, and the internal bussing is copper — not the tin-plated aluminum you find in cheaper imports. Users consistently report zero heat issues even under sustained 40A+ loads, which is more than I can say for boxes at half this price point.

One contractor in the reviews put it plainly: "I've run my 50A welder and a 5HP compressor off this simultaneously for hours. The box doesn't even get warm to the touch." That's the kind of thermal headroom you want when you're on a jobsite and can't afford a shutdown.

## Where It Falls Short

**No GFCI protection built in.** If you're working in damp conditions, you'll need to add an inline GFCI breaker or a spa panel upstream. This is a notable omission for a box at this tier, especially since OSHA and most site safety officers require GFCI protection on 120V receptacles.

**Termination requires tools.** The inlet uses lugs, not a molded plug. You'll need a screwdriver and a basic understanding of split-phase wiring. If you're not comfortable landing 6-gauge wire, budget for an electrician or stick with a pre-wired unit.

**Weight.** At 11 pounds empty, it's not a carry-on. It's designed to be mounted, not lugged around. If you need portable, look at the lighter-duty 30A boxes instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with a standard 50A RV pedestal?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a CS6375-to-CS6364 pigtail adapter (typically 12-18 inches). The box itself is wired for 50A service and handles the full split-phase output.

**Q: Does it come with breakers installed?**
A: The main breaker is pre-installed, but branch breakers are not. You'll need to add breakers for each circuit you want to distribute — typically 20A single-pole for the duplex receptacles.

**Q: Is this legal for residential use?**
A: It's UL-listed for commercial and industrial use. For permanent residential installation, check with your local inspector — some jurisdictions require a different enclosure rating for dwelling units.
