**The WOWOW Pot Filler: A Kitchen Workhorse That Earns Its Spot (Mostly)**  

Let’s be honest: a pot filler faucet is a luxury. You’re paying for the convenience of not lugging a heavy stockpot from sink to stove, and that convenience only pays off if the faucet actually functions flawlessly every single day. The WOWOW Wall Mount Folding Faucet gets most of the way there — and at a price point that undercuts premium brands by a significant margin. But it’s not without compromises.

**What You Actually Get**  

The box contains the faucet body (already assembled), a chrome or brushed nickel finish (your choice — though that’s it, no other colorways), two 1/2-inch NPT supply lines with compression fittings, a mounting plate, and the hardware you’ll need for a standard stud or drywall installation. The instruction manual is surprisingly clear — a rarity at this price point. No specialty tools required beyond an adjustable wrench and Teflon tape.

**Build Quality That Surprises**  

Pick this thing up and you immediately notice the difference: it’s heavy. The brass body feels substantial — not the hollow, tinny weight you get with budget faucets. The folding arm operates on a double-jointed mechanism that extends to about 18.5 inches from the wall, which is enough to reach across a standard 30-inch range and fill a 12-quart pot sitting on the rear burner. The quarter-turn ceramic disc valve is smooth and precise, with no grinding or sticking. As one buyer put it: *“I was skeptical at this price, but the machining is clean, and the action feels like faucets twice the cost.”*

**Real-World Performance**  

Water flow is strong and even — no sputtering or aerator noise. The folding arm locks into position at multiple angles, so you can park it out of the way when not in use. That’s the whole point of a pot filler, and the WOWOW delivers. The swivel base rotates a full 360 degrees, which is handy if you ever need to angle it toward a sink on an adjacent wall. Hot and cold supply lines are clearly marked, and the included brass fittings sealed properly on the first try — no leaks in my test install.

**Where It Falls Short**  

Here’s the honest tradeoff. First, there’s no check valve built into the unit. Some municipalities require one for pot fillers to prevent backflow — you’ll need to buy an inline valve separately (~$10–15) if your inspector demands it. Factor that into your total cost.

Second, the handle tension is fixed at the factory. It’s tight when new, but a few buyers report it loosening after several months of heavy use. It doesn’t affect function — the arm still stays where you put it — but the lever may develop a little wobble. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Third, the finish options are disappointingly limited. Chrome and brushed nickel are fine, but if your kitchen is matte black or oil-rubbed bronze, you’re out of luck with this model.

**Who Should Buy This**  

- **The weekend DIYer** who wants a pot filler without paying plumber-install fees. The straightforward 1/2-inch NPT hookup and clear instructions make this a realistic weekend project.
- **The serious home cook** who regularly fills large pasta pots or canning batches. The extended reach genuinely saves you from awkward, heavy carries.
- **The budget-conscious renovator** who wants the look and function of a $300+ faucet without the price tag. This delivers 85% of the premium experience at roughly half the cost.

**Who Should Skip It**  

- **If your local plumbing code requires a check valve** and you don’t want to fiddle with additional parts, look elsewhere.
- **If you need a specific finish** to match your kitchen hardware — this isn’t it.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Is this faucet lead-free?**  
A: Yes, the brass is certified lead-free (NSF 61 compliant). Safe for potable water use.

**Q: Can I install this on a drywall wall without a stud?**  
A: The included mounting plate is designed for stud mounting. For drywall-only installations, you’ll need heavy-duty toggle bolts — not included — and the instructions recommend a qualified installer.

**Q: Does the arm stay extended while water is running?**  
A: Yes. The double-jointed arm is friction-locked at each joint. It stays put until you manually fold it back. The tension is sufficient for normal use, though you shouldn’t hang anything heavy off it.

**The Verdict**  

**Buy it if:** You want a solid, functional pot filler at a fair price and don’t mind a minor DIY addition (check valve) or a limited finish selection.

**Skip it if:** You need a specific color match, or you want zero compromises at any cost.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful kitchen upgrade that’s built better than its price suggests, held back only by a few minor oversights. For the money, it’s a smart buy.
