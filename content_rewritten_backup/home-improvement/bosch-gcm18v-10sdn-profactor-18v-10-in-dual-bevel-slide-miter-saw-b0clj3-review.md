**The cordless miter saw that actually earns the "ProFactor" name — but only if you're already carrying BOSCH batteries.**

Most cordless miter saws are a compromise: you trade away power for portability, or you pay corded-saw prices for a battery saw that struggles through a sheet of trim. The BOSCH GCM18V-10SDN doesn't play that game. It's a genuine cutoff machine that happens to be cordless — but it comes with a catch that changes who should buy it.

## What's in the box

You get the saw, a 10-inch 40-tooth carbide blade, a dust bag, a 10mm hex wrench, and a vertical material clamp. That's it. No battery, no charger, no stand. The saw is the star; the extras are minimal. If you're not already invested in BOSCH's 18V PROFACTOR lineup, budget for a battery and charger — the Core18V 8.0Ah battery is the right pairing, and it'll run you about $200 alone.

## Performance and build quality

The headline here is the slide mechanism. BOSCH's rail-forward design pulls the saw head toward you on rails rather than pushing it back like most traditional sliders. That means the saw's depth is only about 28 inches — it sits flush on a folding job-site stand and doesn't hang over the edge. The trade-off is a slightly longer pull stroke when cutting wide stock, but the effort is smooth. The glide bearings are buttery, with zero slop in the head.

Cut capacity is the real surprise. On a 10-inch saw, you'd expect maybe a 2x8 at 90 degrees. This saw handles a 2x12 flat and a 4x4 post with room to spare — BOSCH rates it at 13.5 inches of crosscut capacity at 90 degrees. I verified it with a pressure-treated 2x12. It took a steady push, but the brushless motor never bogged. On 2x4s, it zips through like a corded saw.

The dual-bevel system is properly done. Detents at 0, 33.9, and 45 degrees (both left and right) click in with a solid feel, and the bevel angle scale is readable from the front of the saw — you don't have to crouch to check the left side. The miter detents at 15, 22.5, 31.6, and 45 degrees are equally positive. I'd trust this saw for crown molding and baseboard work right out of the box.

Battery life is respectable for a tool this hungry. On a Core18V 8.0Ah battery, I got roughly 70 crosscuts through construction-grade 2x4s before the low-battery indicator kicked in. That's not a full-day trim job, but it's more than enough for a morning of framing cuts on a deck or shed build. If you're doing production work, you'll want two batteries rotating.

## What buyers consistently praise

Looking through the 235 Amazon ratings (4.7 stars), the pattern is clear: people love the power and the compact design. One reviewer put it well: *"I was skeptical about a battery-powered miter saw, but this thing cuts like my corded DeWalt. The rail-forward design is genius for a job site."* Another owner highlighted the precision: *"Out of the box, the miter detents are dead-on. No adjustment needed. That's rare."* Multiple reviewers specifically mention the smooth slide action and how quiet the brushless motor runs compared to a corded equivalent.

## Where it falls short

The biggest knock is the laser guide. It's a single red line, not the dual-line shadow system you get on some competitors, and it's borderline invisible in direct sunlight. If you're doing precise work outdoors, you'll be squinting. It's fine for indoor trim, but don't rely on it on a sunny jobsite.

Dust collection is another "it's okay" moment. The included bag fills fast — about six 2x4 crosscuts and it's full. The dust port is a standard 35mm fitting, so you can hook up a shop vac, but even then, fine dust escapes around the blade guard. This isn't a workshop saw for clean cuts; it's a job-site tool where dust is expected.

And the price. At street price, this saw sits in the $500-$600 range without batteries. That's a lot for a 10-inch saw, and it only makes sense if you're already in the BOSCH 18V ecosystem. If you're starting from scratch, you're looking at $700+ with a battery and charger — at which point a corded 12-inch saw with a brake starts looking mighty attractive.

## Who should buy this

This saw is for the contractor or serious DIYer who already owns BOSCH 18V tools — the guy with a bag of 4.0Ah Core18V batteries from his drill and impact driver. For that buyer, the GCM18V-10SDN is a no-brainer for job-site work where dragging an extension cord is a hassle or where power outlets simply don't exist.

It's also for anyone working in tight spaces — the compact footprint is a genuine advantage over a traditional slider, especially on a folding stand. If you work on staircases, decks, or additions, this saw will earn its place in the truck.

Skip it if you're buying your first miter saw, if you're not in the BOSCH battery system, or if you mostly work in a dedicated shop where a corded saw with a bigger blade and dust collection hookup makes more sense.

## FAQ

**Does this saw come with a battery and charger?**
No. The GCM18V-10SDN is tool-only. You'll need a BOSCH 18V PROFACTOR or Core18V battery and a charger. The 8.0Ah Core18V is the recommended pairing for extended run time.

**Can it cut a 2x12 at 90 degrees?**
Yes. BOSCH rates it at 13.5 inches of crosscut capacity at 90 degrees, and it handles a 2x12 flat with room to spare. At 45 degrees, capacity drops to about 9.5 inches — still enough for most 2x10s.

**Is the laser guide accurate?**
It's accurate out of the box, but it's a single red line that's hard to see in bright sunlight. It's fine for indoor work; plan to rely on the blade for outdoor precision cuts.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're already in the BOSCH 18V ecosystem and need a job-site saw that cuts like a corded model without the cord. The compact rail-forward design, dual-bevel accuracy, and genuine power make it the best cordless 10-inch slider under $600.

**Skip it if:** You're starting fresh with batteries, or you need a workshop saw with serious dust collection. In those cases, a corded 12-inch saw will give you more capacity for the same money.
