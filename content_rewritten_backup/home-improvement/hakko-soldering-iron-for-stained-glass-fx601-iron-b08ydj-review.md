If you've ever fought a cheap soldering iron that can't hold heat while you're working a long copper foil seam, you already know why the Hakko FX601 commands attention. This isn't a hobbyist's first iron — it's the upgrade you buy when you're tired of cold joints and rework.

## What You Actually Get

The FX601 arrives as just the iron itself — no stand, no sponge, no tip assortment. That's a deliberate choice by Hakko, since most stained glass artists already own a temperature-controlled station or an iron holder. What's in the box is a 120-watt workhorse with a dial temperature control on the barrel, a chisel tip pre-installed, and a heat-resistant cord that stays flexible even when the iron's been running hot for hours.

## Performance That Justifies the Hype

The headline feature here is the 120-watt heating element. For context, most entry-level irons run 60-80 watts and struggle to maintain temperature when you're laying down long beads of solder. The FX601 recovers heat almost instantly after each joint — you'll notice the difference within your first few minutes of work. The dial control covers roughly 660°F to 900°F, which is exactly the sweet spot for stained glass work where you need enough heat to flow 60/40 solder smoothly without burning through copper foil backing.

The barrel stays cool to the touch even after extended use, which isn't something you can say about cheaper irons. That's not just a comfort feature — it's a safety one when you're working with your non-dominant hand holding glass pieces in place.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects a pattern of satisfied glass artists. One reviewer noted, *"I've been doing stained glass for 15 years and this is the first iron that doesn't make me fight to keep my solder flowing on long seams."* Another highlighted the heat recovery specifically: *"Holds temperature better than any iron I've used under $150. Worth every penny."* Multiple reviewers mention that the lightweight design — just 4.6 ounces — makes a real difference during multi-hour sessions.

## Where It Falls Short

The stock chisel tip is serviceable but not ideal for everyone. If you do fine detail work or prefer a pointed tip for tight corners, budget an extra $15-20 for a replacement tip. The lack of a digital temperature readout is also worth noting — the dial markings are approximate, and you'll want to test your iron's actual output with a temperature probe if you're working on projects where precision matters. And while the no-stand packaging keeps the price down, it does mean an additional purchase if you're coming from a kit that included everything.

## Who Should Buy This

This iron is for the stained glass artist who's past the beginner stage. If you're doing copper foil work — sun catchers, panels, jewelry boxes — and you're frustrated by temperature inconsistency, this is your upgrade. It's also ideal for glass fusing hobbyists who need a reliable iron for tacking pieces together. Beginners might be better served by a complete kit that includes a stand and tips, but if you already have those accessories, the FX601 is a serious step up in capability.

## FAQ

**Is the Hakko FX601 compatible with other brand tips?**
Yes — it uses standard Hakko tips that are widely available on Amazon. You can also find compatible tips from brands like X-Tronic that fit the same thread pattern.

**Can this iron handle lead came work?**
Yes, though it's optimized for copper foil. The higher heat range works fine for lead came soldering, but you may want a wider tip for those larger seams.

**Is the cord long enough for working at a workbench?**
The cord is about 5 feet, which is standard. You'll want a nearby outlet or a power strip within reach.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a stained glass artist who's outgrown entry-level irons and needs consistent heat recovery for clean, professional solder lines. The 120-watt element and lightweight design make it a genuine upgrade that pays for itself in reduced rework.

**Skip it if:** You're just starting out and need a complete setup — you'll spend extra on a stand and tips. Or if you need precise digital temperature control for mixed-media work, look at the FX601's pricier digital siblings.
