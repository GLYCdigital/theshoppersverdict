**The $40 industrial lamp that actually looks like it costs more — but there's a catch**

Walk into any big-box home store and you'll find industrial cage lamps priced at $70–$90 that feel hollow and cheap when you pick them up. The G-SAFAVA 23.75'' Industrial Black Metal Table Lamp flips that script at roughly half the price. But before you hit buy, here's the honest breakdown of what 229 buyers (4.6 stars) are saying — and what they're not.

---

**What you actually get**

The box contains the lamp in two threaded sections, a weighted round base, a cage shade, and the hardware to join them. Assembly takes under five minutes — thread the pole into the base, thread on the cage, screw in your bulb. There's no wiring involved, no tools required beyond your hands.

The dimensions matter here: 23.75 inches tall with a 7.5-inch round base. That's compact enough for a 24-inch-wide nightstand or a narrow sofa end table, but substantial enough to read by. The matte black powder-coat finish is uniform — no runs, no thin spots, no silver showing through at the edges.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The headline feature is the all-metal construction. The base has real heft — you're not going to knock this over with a stray elbow. One buyer noted, "The base is heavy and sturdy. I have kids and pets, and this lamp hasn't budged." That's the experience most owners report: it feels planted.

The E26 socket accepts any standard bulb, which is where you can customize the experience. Want warm ambient light? Drop in a 2700K Edison bulb. Need task light for reading? A 60W equivalent LED works fine. The open cage design throws light in a full 360-degree pattern, which makes it better as a general room light than a focused reading lamp.

Here's where buyers consistently praise it: the aesthetic. "Looks way more expensive than it is," appears across multiple reviews. The industrial-steampunk vibe works in farmhouse, modern, and even transitional spaces. The black finish pairs well with wood tones and leather furniture.

**Where it falls short**

The pull-chain switch is the most common complaint. It's positioned on the socket, which means if the lamp sits against a wall or behind a chair, you're fishing for the chain. One reviewer put it bluntly: "The switch location is annoying. I wish it was on the cord."

The assembly joint is a second weak point. The pole threads together, but over several months, the connection can loosen. A quarter-turn tighten every few weeks solves it, but it's a maintenance quirk you should know about.

Finally, the open cage means dust. The bulb and interior of the cage collect dust visibly, and you'll need to wipe it down monthly to keep it looking sharp. Bare-bulb exposure also means you see the bulb's full glare — a frosted or vintage-style bulb is almost mandatory for comfortable lighting.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters and apartment dwellers** — it's lightweight enough to move, and the neutral black finish works in any room.
- **Farmhouse or industrial decor enthusiasts** — this lamp nails the aesthetic without the boutique markup.
- **Anyone needing a bedside or end-table lamp** — the 23.75-inch height is right for reading without towering over the furniture.

**Skip it if** you need a fully adjustable swing-arm lamp, want a built-in dimmer, or hate the idea of exposed bulbs. This is a fixed-position lamp with a simple on/off pull chain.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this lamp come with a bulb?**
A: No. You'll need a standard E26-base bulb. A vintage Edison bulb with a visible filament looks best with this cage design.

**Q: Can I use a smart bulb with it?**
A: Yes, any standard smart bulb (Philips Hue, Wyze, etc.) fits the E26 socket. Just note that the pull-chain must remain in the "on" position for the smart bulb to receive power.

**Q: How bright is it?**
A: That depends entirely on the bulb you install. With a 60W equivalent LED, it lights a typical 10x12 room adequately. The open design distributes light evenly in all directions.
