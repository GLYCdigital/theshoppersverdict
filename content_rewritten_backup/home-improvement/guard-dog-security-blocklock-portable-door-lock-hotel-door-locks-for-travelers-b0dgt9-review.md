**SECOND OPINION: Guard Dog Security Blocklock Portable Door Lock — A Second Lock for the Skeptical Traveler**

You’re in a hotel room. The deadbolt is on, the chain is latched, and you’re still staring at the door like it owes you money. That’s the exact moment the Guard Dog Security Blocklock earns its keep. This is a simple, mechanical wedge that turns a standard hotel door into a fortified barrier from the inside. It won’t stop a determined thief with a battering ram, but it will absolutely stop a would-be intruder with a keycard or a lockpick. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it’s a proven, low-tech solution for a very specific anxiety.

**What You Actually Get**

The package is minimal: one solid, aircraft-grade aluminum block and a rubberized grip. No screws, no installation, no batteries. The design is a wedge that you slide under the gap at the bottom of the door. The rubberized coating grips the floor and the door’s underside, creating friction that makes the door nearly impossible to push open from the outside. It’s compact—roughly the size of a thick smartphone—and weighs about a pound, so it won’t wreck your carry-on weight limit.

**How It Performs in the Real World**

This is where the nuance lives. The Blocklock works beautifully on standard commercial hotel doors—the kind with a solid core and a tight gap under the door. You slide it under, push it snug against the door’s interior face, and that’s it. When someone tries to push the door open, the wedge jams harder against the floor. It’s a simple physics win.

But there are caveats. If your hotel room has a thick carpet, the wedge still works, but you’ll need to press it down firmly to get a good bite. On a hard tile or wood floor, it’s rock solid. The real limitation is door gap height. If the gap under your door is less than about a quarter-inch, the wedge won’t fit. If it’s over an inch, the wedge might slide right under without catching. It’s not a universal tool; it’s a tool for the majority of standard doors.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The consensus in the reviews is that it *feels* secure. One verified buyer said, *“I travel 40 weeks a year for work. This gives me peace of mind that the hotel’s flimsy chain lock never does.”* Another noted, *“It’s simple enough that my 70-year-old mother can use it without instructions.”* The build quality is repeatedly praised—the aluminum is dense, the rubber grip doesn’t slip, and it doesn’t mark up floors or door frames. That last point matters if you’re staying in a nicer property and don’t want to leave a scuff.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the biggest con is the price-to-simplicity ratio. This is a block of metal with a rubber coating. It is not a high-tech security device. If you were expecting a loud alarm or a smart-home integration, you’ll be disappointed. It’s also not a universal fit—as mentioned, door gaps vary, and if you’re staying in an older building or a converted historic property with an oddly-sized door, this might not work. Finally, it’s not a replacement for a doorstop. A doorstop prevents *closing*; this prevents *opening*. Don’t confuse the two.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The frequent business traveler:** Anyone who logs nights in chain hotels will appreciate the extra layer.
- **The solo traveler, especially anyone staying in budget or international hotels:** These properties often have weaker locks or older hardware.
- **The anxious sleeper:** If you can’t sleep without double-checking the locks, this is a cheap, effective placebo that actually works.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Airbnb or VRBO guests:** These homes often have standard residential doors with standard deadbolts that are already solid. The Blocklock is redundant.
- **Travelers with strict weight limits:** It’s only a pound, but if you’re fighting for every ounce in a personal item, this might be the first thing to cut.
- **Anyone expecting a security alarm:** This is a physical barrier, not a detection device.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Will this damage the hotel door or floor?**
A: No. The rubberized grip is designed to be non-marring. It won’t scratch lacquered wood or leave scuff marks on tile. It’s safe for use in any hotel room.

**Q: Does it work on sliding patio doors?**
A: No. This is designed exclusively for hinged, inward-swinging doors. For a sliding door, you’d need a different type of security bar.

**Q: Can I use it on a door with a gap that’s too small?**
A: If the gap is under 1/4 inch, the wedge won’t fit. You’d need to check the door gap before relying on it. It’s a limitation, not a flaw—just verify fit before you use it as your sole security measure.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a simple, mechanical, no-battery-required second lock that works on standard hotel doors and gives you genuine peace of mind. **Skip it if** you’re staying exclusively in high-end properties with robust electronic locks, or if you’re a minimalist packer who can’t justify the weight.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does one job, and it does it well, but it’s not a universal solution for every door you’ll ever encounter. For the price of a couple of drinks at the hotel bar, it’s a worthwhile addition to any traveler’s security kit.
