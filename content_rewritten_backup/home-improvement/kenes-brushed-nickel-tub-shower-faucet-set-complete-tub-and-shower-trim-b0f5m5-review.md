# KENES Brushed Nickel Tub Shower Faucet: Does the "Complete" Kit Actually Deliver?

If you're staring down a bathroom remodel, you already know the trap: the faucet looks cheap, the finish flakes, or the "complete" set arrives missing the valve. The KENES Brushed Nickel Tub Shower Faucet Set claims to solve that with a full trim package at a mid-range price point. After digging through 210 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s the real story before you hand money to the contractor.

## What You Actually Get

This is a *trim* kit, not a full rough-in package. In the box, you’ll find the pressure-balanced valve cartridge, the brass showerhead with a 6-inch arm, a single-handle lever, the tub spout, and all mounting plates and screws. The brushed nickel finish is uniform across every piece—a detail that matters when you hold the spout next to the handle and see zero color mismatch.

What’s missing? The rough-in valve body. You must buy that separately (typically a standard 1/2-inch IPS unit). Factor that into your budget before checkout.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The standout feature here is the brass construction. For the price, most competitors use zinc alloy or plastic internals. KENES uses solid brass for the spout and shower arm, which means the threads won’t strip when you tighten the arm onto the drop-ear elbow. The brushed nickel finish is applied via a physical vapor deposition (PVD) process—that’s the same method used on higher-end Kohler and Delta fixtures, so it holds up to daily scrubbing without dulling.

The pressure-balanced cartridge does its job. If someone flushes a toilet while you’re showering, the temperature holds within a degree or two. That’s the kind of thing you don’t notice until you don’t have it, and it’s the main reason this faucet earns its 4.2-star average.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reviews repeatedly highlight two things:
- **The finish.** One buyer wrote: *"I've had this installed for 3 months with hard water, and the nickel still looks brand new—no spots, no peeling."*
- **The complete package.** Multiple reviewers noted that the kit includes the cartridge and handle, which saved them a trip to the hardware store. As one put it: *"Everything fit together without filing or shimming. That's rare for a budget faucet."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides.

**The handle tension is a known issue.** The lever uses a set screw that must be precisely torqued. Several users reported the handle either spins too loosely or binds against the plate after a few weeks. The fix is simple (Loctite on the threads), but it’s an annoyance you shouldn’t have on day one.

**The showerhead flow is aggressive.** It’s a 1.8 GPM unit, which is standard, but the spray pattern is narrow. If you prefer a wide rainfall effect, this will feel like a fire hose. You’ll likely swap it for a different head—defeating the "complete" selling point.

**The tub spout is short.** At 5.5 inches, it may not reach over a wide tub lip. Measure your existing spout before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners replacing an existing trim:** If your rough-in valve is already in place, this drops in without plumbing changes.
- **Landlords or rental flippers:** The solid brass internals will survive tenant abuse better than cheaper plastic-valve sets.
- **Anyone tired of chrome:** The brushed nickel finish hides fingerprints dramatically better than polished chrome.

Skip it if you need a full valve-plus-trim kit (buy a Moen or Delta bundle instead) or if you want a multi-function showerhead.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does this fit a standard 1/2-inch copper pipe?**
A: Yes. The rough-in valve connects to standard 1/2-inch IPS threads. If you have PEX, you’ll need an adapter fitting.

**Q: Is the valve cartridge included?**
A: Yes, the pressure-balanced cartridge is in the box. The valve body itself is not—that’s the part that mounts inside the wall.

**Q: How hard is the handle to adjust?**
A: Moderately fiddly. You need a 3/32-inch hex wrench to set the screw tension. Tighten it until the handle moves smoothly but doesn’t wobble, then add a drop of threadlocker.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if:** You have an existing rough-in valve and want a durable, good-looking trim kit that won’t break the bank. The brass construction and PVD finish are genuinely impressive at this price.

**Skip it if:** You’re starting from scratch (buy a complete valve kit) or you need a soft, wide shower spray. Also avoid if you’re not comfortable tweaking the handle tension—it’s not a "set and forget" installation.

For the money, it’s a solid value with one frustrating quirk. Just budget $60 extra for the valve body and a tube of threadlocker, and you’ll be satisfied.
