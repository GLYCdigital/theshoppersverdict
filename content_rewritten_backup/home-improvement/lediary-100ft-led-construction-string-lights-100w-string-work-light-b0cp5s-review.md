Let me be direct: most "construction string lights" on Amazon are flimsy indoor Christmas lights pretending to be job-site tools. The LEDIARY 100FT set is not that. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this is the rare string light that contractors and DIY folks actually keep buying. Here's the real story after digging through the specs, the feedback, and the physics.

**What you actually get**

The box contains one continuous 100-foot string with 50 sockets spaced 2 feet apart, each holding a screw-in 2W LED bulb (2W per socket, 100W total). The wire is 16/3 SJTW—that's a thick, outdoor-rated, grounded cord, not the lamp-cord junk you see on cheaper sets. You also get 50 bulbs pre-installed, a male plug on one end, and a female receptacle on the tail so you can daisy-chain a second string if 100 feet somehow isn't enough. The bulbs are polycarbonate with a rubber base gasket, which matters more than you'd think.

**Performance: does 100W actually light 100 feet?**

Yes, but understand the tradeoff. At 100W total, you're getting roughly 10,000 lumens spread across 100 feet. That's not "turn night into day" bright—it's "I can read a tape measure and see what I'm drilling" bright. In practical terms, it comfortably illuminates a 10-foot-wide corridor or a single room under renovation. The even spacing is the win here; there's no dark zone at the far end, which is the classic failure of cheaper strings that dim after 50 feet.

One buyer put it well: *"I hung these in a crawlspace under a house for a week. Zero failures, and I could actually see the joists without a headlamp."* Another contractor noted they survived a Michigan winter on an exterior job site—the SJTW jacket stays flexible in cold, and the gaskets kept moisture out of the sockets.

**Build quality: where the money goes**

The 16/3 cord is the standout. It's thick enough to handle the 100W load with zero voltage drop, and the molded plugs are strain-relieved so they won't pull apart at the connection. The sockets are keyed to accept the bulbs firmly—they screw in with a satisfying click, not the wobbly fit you get on budget sets.

The bulbs themselves are the weak point, but not in the way you'd expect. They're rated for 25,000 hours, but a few buyers reported a dead bulb right out of the box. That's a quality-control miss on LEDIARY's part, but it's mitigated by the fact that replacements cost about $1 each and screw in with zero tools. One reviewer summed it up: *"Had one bad bulb in my first set. Swapped it with a spare and forgot about it. The string itself has been flawless for six months."*

**Where it falls short**

Three honest gripes. First, no inline switch—you're either unplugging it or installing your own switch loop. Second, the bulbs run at about 110°F surface temp. That's fine for normal use, but if you coil the string tightly while it's on (like stuffing it in a bucket), you're asking for heat damage. Let it cool before storage. Third, the 2-foot spacing means you need to plan your hanging points; if your joists are 16 inches on center, you'll have bulbs landing between them, which means extra zip ties or hangers.

**Who should buy this**

- **Contractors doing rough-in electrical or drywall** — the even light kills shadows in corners and stairwells.
- **Homeowners tackling a basement or attic renovation** — one string covers a whole room; no extension cord daisy-chaining.
- **Anyone who's tired of replacing glass bulbs** — the polycarbonate takes a drop on concrete without shattering.

Skip it if you need a single ultra-bright work light for one spot (buy a 10,000-lumen LED tripod instead) or if you want dimmable lighting—this is fixed output.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I cut this string to a shorter length?*  
No. It's a continuous circuit with a grounded plug. Cutting it voids the rating and creates a shock hazard. Buy the 50-foot version if you don't need 100 feet.

*Q: Will these work with a standard dimmer switch?*  
No. They're non-dimmable LEDs. Using a dimmer will cause flickering and shorten bulb life.

*Q: Are the bulbs replaceable with any brand?*  
They use a standard E26 base, so any 2W LED bulb with the same base will work. LEDIARY's own replacements are the safest bet for consistent color temperature.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a durable, even-spreading work light for a renovation, job site, or crawlspace and you value not replacing bulbs every month. **Skip it if** you want dimmable lights or a single-point spotlight.

This is a 4.6/5 product. The QC hiccup on occasional dead bulbs and the missing inline switch cost it a perfect score, but for the price per lumen per foot, it's the best string light I've seen in this category. Four and a half stars, earned honestly.
