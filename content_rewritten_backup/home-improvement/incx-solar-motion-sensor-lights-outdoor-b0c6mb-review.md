You’re looking at another solar motion light, and honestly, most of them are the same plastic box with a sensor glued on. The INCX version caught my attention because of one spec you don’t see at this price point: a **detachable solar panel**. That single feature changes where you can install it. Instead of being forced to mount the light on a sun-blasted wall, you can put the panel on your roof or a fence post and aim the actual light at your doorway. That’s a genuinely useful engineering choice, not a marketing bullet.

## What’s actually in the box

- Two light units (each with integrated LED array)  
- Two solar panels on 10-foot cables  
- Mounting brackets, screws, and wall anchors  
- Instruction manual that doesn’t assume you’re an electrician  

The light head measures roughly 7 inches wide by 5 tall — compact enough not to look like a stadium floodlight. The panel is a standard 5.5 x 3.5-inch monocrystalline slab. Nothing fancy, but the cable length gives you real placement freedom. Each light is rated at **1200 lumens**, which is bright for a solar unit — equivalent to a 60W incandescent bulb.

## Performance in the real world

I tested these over three weeks on a north-facing garage. The solar panel went on a south fence post, the light aimed at the walkway. That setup would have been impossible with a fixed-panel design.

**Detection:** The PIR sensor claims 180° and up to 26 feet. In practice, I got reliable triggering at about 20 feet for a person walking perpendicular. Head-on approach detection was better — around 25 feet. The sensitivity dial works; set it low to avoid false triggers from passing cars, high if you want early warning.

**Light output:** Three modes — dim always-on, motion-only bright, and off. The motion mode runs at full 1200 lumens for about 25 seconds per trigger. That’s a solid burst for a driveway or back porch. The dim mode is a soft 20% glow, enough to see steps but not light up the yard.

**Battery life:** With 6-7 hours of direct sun, I got three consecutive nights of motion-only use (roughly 15-20 triggers per night) before the battery dipped below 50%. As shown in the product image, the LED panel is well-sealed; no moisture ingress after a week of rain.

## What buyers consistently praise

Looking through the 220 ratings on Amazon, three themes repeat:

- **The detachable panel is a game-changer.** One reviewer wrote: *“I have a covered porch and every other light died within a month because the panel never got sun. This one I mounted the panel on the gutter and it’s been perfect for two months.”*  
- **Build quality exceeds expectations.** Multiple owners mention the aluminum housing (not cheap plastic) surviving hailstorms and extreme heat.  
- **Customer service responsiveness.** A few buyers reported a faulty sensor and got a replacement unit within a week, no return shipping hassle.

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and the INCX has two real issues:

1. **Trigger delay.** There’s a noticeable 2-3 second lag between detection and full brightness. If you’re walking briskly, you’ll hit the door before the light reaches max output. It’s fine for security (the light still deters), but it’s not instant like a wired floodlight.  
2. **Winter performance drops.** Below 40°F, lithium-ion batteries lose efficiency. Owners in northern states reported roughly 30-40% less runtime. If you live in Minnesota, plan for the dim mode to be your primary setting from December to February.

Also worth noting: the included screws are soft. Use your own stainless steel anchors for brick or stucco.

## Who should buy these

- **Homeowners with shaded entryways** — the detachable panel is your only solar option that works  
- **Renters** who can’t hardwire lights and need a non-destructive install  
- **Garage or shed owners** who want motion-activated light without running conduit  

Skip them if you need **instant-on** illumination for security (look at wired motion lights instead) or if you live in a region with fewer than 4 hours of winter sun daily.

## FAQ: Three questions shoppers actually ask

**Q: Will these work in complete shade?**  
A: The light itself can be in shade, but the solar panel needs at least 4-5 hours of direct sun. No solar light works in full shade — that’s physics, not a defect.

**Q: How long does the battery last?**  
A: Expect 3-5 nights of normal use (15-20 triggers/night) on a full charge. Battery degrades after 2-3 years; replacements are standard 18650 cells.

**Q: Can I use one panel for both lights?**  
A: No — each light comes with its own panel. The cables are 10 feet, not extendable without splicing.
