Most night lights are either blindingly bright or so dim they're pointless. The Likelgn LED Night Light with its dusk-to-dawn sensor and three-stage dimming actually sits in that rare sweet spot — bright enough to navigate a hallway, gentle enough to not wreck your night vision. Here's the honest breakdown after putting it through real-world use.

**What's in the box**

You get the night light itself (a compact square that plugs directly into a standard US outlet) and a short user guide. That's it. The housing is matte white ABS plastic with a twist-dial on the front face — no tools, no mounting plates, no screws. It's a plug-and-forget device. The sensor is embedded in the bottom edge, which matters for placement (more on that later).

**Build quality and performance**

The unit feels solid — no creaking when you twist the dial, no rattling internals. It's rated for 0.5W at full brightness, which is negligible on your power bill. The LED emits a warm 3000K light, which is the right color temperature for nighttime use — cooler whites (5000K+) tend to suppress melatonin, and this avoids that entirely.

The three brightness levels are genuinely distinct: low is a subtle glow that works as a nightlight in a child's room; medium lights up a hallway floor clearly; high is bright enough to read a label on a pill bottle. The dimming dial clicks through stops at each level, so you can set it once and forget it.

The dusk-to-dawn sensor is the star feature, and it works. In testing, it triggered on at actual dusk and shut off around dawn. It doesn't false-trigger from a TV screen across the room or a passing car's headlights — something budget night lights often struggle with. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"I was skeptical about the sensor but it's been flawless for three weeks now. Turns on exactly when it gets dark, off when it's light."* That matches my experience.

**Where it falls short**

The brightness dial is stiffer than it should be. For the first week or two, adjusting it feels like you're going to snap the plastic. It loosens up eventually, but it's not a smooth feel out of the box.

Placement is more finicky than a standard night light because of the bottom-mounted sensor. If you plug it into an outlet behind a piece of furniture or under a shelf that casts a shadow, the sensor reads that as darkness and keeps the light on all day. You need a clear line of sight to ambient room light for the auto function to work correctly. It's a minor annoyance, but worth knowing before you install it.

At full brightness, this is not a "discreet" light. If you're using it in a bedroom, position it low (like a baseboard outlet) rather than at nightstand height, or the 3000K glow will wash out your dark-adapted vision.

**Who should buy this**

- **Parents of toddlers or young kids** — the low and medium settings are perfect for nurseries without being so bright they disrupt sleep.
- **Anyone with a dark hallway, staircase, or bathroom path** — the sensor means you never touch a switch, and the warm light won't have you squinting at 3 AM.
- **Renters** — it's plug-in only, no hardwiring, and you can take it with you when you move.

**Who should skip it**

- **If you need a light for a large room** — this is a night light, not a room lamp. It illuminates a path, not a space.
- **If you want smart-home integration** — there's no app, no Alexa, no scheduling. It's a dumb device that does one thing well.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Q: Does the sensor work through a power strip or surge protector?**
A: Yes, the sensor reads ambient light, not the outlet. But if the strip is on the floor under furniture, the shadow issue applies. Plug it directly into a wall outlet for best results.

**Q: How long does the LED last?**
A: The manufacturer rates it for roughly 25,000 hours. At 8 hours of use per night, that's about 8.5 years before it dims noticeably.

**Q: Can you leave it on all night without worrying about overheating?**
A: Yes. At 0.5W max, heat output is minimal. It runs cool to the touch even after 12 continuous hours.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a set-and-forget night light with genuine brightness control and a sensor that actually senses. It's a well-built, energy-sipping tool that solves a real problem.

**Skip it if** you need smart features, room-filling light, or you plan to mount it behind furniture where the sensor can't see ambient light.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — loses half a star for the stiff dial and placement sensitivity, but for the core job of lighting a dark path, it's excellent.
