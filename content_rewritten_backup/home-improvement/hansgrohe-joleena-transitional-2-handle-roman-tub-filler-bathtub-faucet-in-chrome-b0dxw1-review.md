**The hansgrohe Joleena is a $400+ faucet that looks like a million bucks but installs like a puzzle.** With 195 ratings averaging 3.9 stars, the split between glowing five-star reviews and frustrated one-stars tells you something important: this is a premium product with a specific installation learning curve. Let me break down whether it's worth your money.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the Roman tub filler body (the two-handle deck-mount unit), two lever handles, the spout, a hand shower with 59-inch hose, and the diverter mechanism. Notably absent: **supply stops** — the shut-off valves that connect your plumbing to the faucet. Multiple buyers flagged this. One reviewer wrote, "Nothing in the instructions tells you you need separate stops. Plumber had to run out mid-install." Budget an extra $30–60 for those.

The chrome finish is genuinely excellent. The valve body is solid brass, not that cheap zinc alloy you see at half the price. In hand, it feels like a $500 faucet. The lever handles operate with a satisfying, damped smoothness — that's the hansgrohe engineering DNA showing.

## Real-World Performance

At 1.75 GPM, this fills a standard 60-gallon tub in about 8–10 minutes. That's slower than an old-school 2.5 GPM filler but respectable for modern efficiency standards. The hand shower delivers a decent spray pattern for rinsing down after a bath, though it's not a pressure washer — expect typical shower-head pressure, not a massage jet.

The transitional design — rounded curves with clean lines — pairs well with both traditional and contemporary bathrooms. As shown in the product image, the chrome finish has a mirror-like depth that catches light nicely.

## What Buyers Love

The praise clusters around three themes:

- **Build quality**: "This thing is a tank. The brass is thick, the chrome is flawless after eight months." Recurring sentiment.
- **Looks**: "It's the centerpiece of my master bath remodel. Guests comment on it." The Joleena name carries weight in designer circles.
- **Hand shower utility**: The integrated sprayer gets consistent kudos for bathing kids and cleaning the tub.

## Where It Falls Short

The cons are real and repeated:

1. **Diverter knob frustration**. It's a small, cylindrical pull-knob on the spout base. Wet hands make it slippery. One buyer called it "my daily annoyance." Several report it sticking after a few months, requiring a firm yank.
2. **Installation complexity**. The rough-in valve is bulky, and the lack of included stops is a genuine oversight at this price point. If you're not handy with copper pipe, budget $200–300 for a plumber.
3. **Price-to-feature ratio**. You're paying for the hansgrohe name and finish quality, not groundbreaking features. No thermostatic control, no volume control beyond the handles.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're doing a serious master bath renovation, value heirloom-quality chrome finish, and either have plumbing skills or a plumber you trust. The hand shower is genuinely useful for families.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, want a straightforward DIY weekend install, or need a diverter you can operate with one soapy hand. Also skip if your tub deck is less than 5 inches deep — the valve body needs clearance.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this include the rough-in valve?**
A: Yes, the valve body is included. What's NOT included are the supply stops (shut-off valves) that connect to your home's water lines. You'll need two 1/2-inch stops, typically $15–30 each.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: If you've done a few faucet installations and own a basin wrench, yes — expect 2–3 hours. For first-timers, hire a plumber. The lack of stops and the bulky valve body trip up novices.

**Q: Does the hand shower have a pause feature?**
A: No. The diverter switches between spout and shower, but there's no flow-stop button on the hand piece. Minor, but worth knowing.
