**The Freestanding Tub Filler That Won't Wreck Your Remodel — American Standard 2764951.002 Review**

Let's be blunt: freestanding tub faucets are a pain point in most bathroom remodels. They're either overpriced designer pieces or cheap imports that wobble. The American Standard 2764951.002 sits firmly in the middle — and for many buyers, that's exactly the sweet spot. With a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, it's clearly doing something right, but it's not without quirks. Here's the unvarnished truth.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the freestanding floor-mount faucet body, a single-handle lever, the spout, and a handheld shower head with a 60-inch flexible hose. The finish is brushed nickel (the model number indicates this), which does an admirable job resisting fingerprints — a small but meaningful detail in a high-traffic bathroom. The faucet stands 43 inches tall, which is tall enough to clear the rim of most freestanding tubs without looking like a flagpole.

Installation requires a floor-mount rough-in. This is not a DIY-hour project; the valve is integrated, but you'll need access below the floor for supply lines. If you're replacing a wall-mount, budget for a plumber — this isn't a swap-in.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The one-handle lever controls volume and temperature with a smooth, progressive action. There's no sudden hot-water lurch, which is more than I can say for cheaper single-handle units. The spout reaches 8.5 inches forward, which clears the tub deck without splashing onto the floor — a common complaint with shorter freestanding units.

The integrated diverter switches between the tub spout and the handheld shower with a firm, positive click. The handheld itself is a standard 1.8 GPM unit with a rubberized spray face that's easy to wipe clean of hard-water scale.

The materials feel substantial — solid brass body, ceramic cartridge — and the whole unit weighs about 22 pounds. It doesn't feel hollow or cheap when you knock on it. The brushed nickel finish is consistent across all pieces, which is a detail buyers notice after a year, not on day one.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

One five-star reviewer noted: *"I was nervous about a floor-mount faucet in a small bathroom, but the footprint is compact and it doesn't interfere with cleaning around the tub."* That's a recurring theme — the narrow base (just 6 inches in diameter) makes floor cleaning manageable, unlike some bulky pedestal units.

Another common point of praise: *"The height is perfect — I don't have to hunch over to fill the tub, and the handheld reaches everywhere I need."* The 43-inch height and the long hose earn repeated mentions.

**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. The brushed nickel finish is attractive, but several buyers report it's a fingerprint magnet in warm, humid bathrooms — the opposite of my earlier point. One reviewer said it best: *"Looks great when clean, but it shows every water spot within an hour."*

The bigger issue is the installation flexibility. The rough-in valve is fixed at a specific height, so if your floor joists or existing plumbing don't align, you're looking at additional work. One two-star reviewer noted: *"The valve placement was non-negotiable — I had to cut into the subfloor to get it to fit my layout."* Measure your rough-in clearance before you buy, not after.

Finally, the handheld sprayer's hose is plastic-jacketed, not metal. It's functional, but it doesn't match the premium feel of the rest of the unit. If you're paying for a statement piece, this is a noticeable compromise.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The remodeler with a set floor plan:** If you're building a new bathroom or have full subfloor access, this faucet delivers 90% of the look and function of a $1,000 designer unit at a fraction of the cost.
- **The bather who fills deep tubs:** The tall spout and long hose make it genuinely useful for soaking tubs, not just decorative.
- **Skip it if:** You're trying to retrofit a freestanding faucet into an existing slab floor, or you demand a flawless, zero-maintenance finish.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Does this work with a standard 60-inch tub?**
Yes, the 43-inch height and 8.5-inch spout reach clear the rim of most standard freestanding tubs. It's designed for tubs with a deck height between 20 and 24 inches.

**Q: Is the handheld shower head pressure good?**
At 1.8 GPM, it's adequate for rinsing off but not a spa-grade shower. It's a tub filler with a convenience spray, not a replacement for your main shower.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
Only if you're comfortable with under-floor plumbing and have access to the joists. The valve is integrated and must be mounted before the floor is finished. This is a plumber's job for most homeowners.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're doing a full remodel with subfloor access and want a substantial, well-built freestanding faucet without the designer markup.

**Skip it if** you're on a slab foundation, need a flexible installation, or you're bothered by visible water spots.

The American Standard 2764951.002 is a solid 4.2-star product for a reason: it does the job, looks good, and won't make you weep when you see the invoice. Just don't expect it to be invisible to fingerprints.
