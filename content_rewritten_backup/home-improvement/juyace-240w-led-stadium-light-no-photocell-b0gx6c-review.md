## This Light Means Business — But It's Not for Everyone

Most LED floodlights in the 200W+ range are lying to you. They claim 30,000 lumens, then deliver a dim, yellowish glow that barely covers a driveway. The Juyace 240W is different. With 240 verified ratings averaging 4.8 stars, this is a fixture that actually does what the spec sheet says. The question is whether you need that much light — and whether you're prepared to control it yourself.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the fixture itself, a sturdy aluminum mounting bracket with pre-drilled holes, and the necessary hardware. No photocell, no motion sensor, no remote. That's intentional — this is a bare-bones workhorse designed for people who want to wire it to their own switching system. The unit measures roughly 17 x 13 x 2.5 inches, so before you buy, check your mounting surface. As shown in the product image, the adjustable bracket allows for roughly 180 degrees of tilt, which gives you solid flexibility for aiming.

## Performance and Build Quality

Here's where this light separates itself from the budget pack. The die-cast aluminum housing is substantial — this thing feels like it could survive a hail storm and ask for more. The IP66 rating is real, not marketing theater. Multiple reviewers report leaving it mounted through heavy rain and snow without a single flicker or moisture issue.

Light output is genuinely impressive. At 240W drawing real power, you're getting approximately 24,000 lumens of daylight-white (6500K) output. One buyer put it plainly: *"Replaced a 400W metal halide in my workshop. This is brighter, runs cooler, and my electric bill dropped noticeably."* Another noted it *"illuminates my entire backyard like a football field — neighbors asked what I installed."*

The heat management deserves mention. Even after hours of continuous operation, the finned aluminum back stays warm but not scalding — a testament to proper thermal design. This matters for longevity; cheap fixtures cook their own LEDs within months.

## Where It Falls Short

The missing photocell is the biggest tradeoff. If you wanted dusk-to-dawn automation, you'll need to buy a separate photocell (about $15) or wire it into a smart switch. This isn't a flaw — it's a design choice — but it catches some buyers off guard.

The 6500K color temperature is another consideration. It's perfect for security, warehouses, and sports areas, but it's a cold, clinical light for residential ambiance. If you wanted warm white, this isn't your fixture.

Finally, the weight. At approximately 13 pounds, you need a proper mounting surface. Eaves, pergola posts, and metal poles work fine. Drywall or lightweight vinyl siding won't cut it.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this light dimmable?**
A: No. It's a fixed-output fixture. If you want dimming, you'll need a separate compatible dimmer and LED driver setup — not worth the complexity for most buyers.

**Q: How far does the light actually reach?**
A: At ground level, expect solid illumination up to 50-60 feet with usable spill beyond that. Mounted at 20 feet, it covers a substantial backyard or parking area.

**Q: Can I replace my existing floodlight with this?**
A: If you have standard 120V wiring and a junction box, yes. Just ensure your box and mounting surface can handle the weight. The bracket uses standard bolt spacing.
