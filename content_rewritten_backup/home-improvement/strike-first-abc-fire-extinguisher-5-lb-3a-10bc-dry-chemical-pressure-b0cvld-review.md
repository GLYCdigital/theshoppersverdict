## What You Actually Get

The box contains the extinguisher, a safety pin with a tamper seal, and a pull-pin handle. That's it. No mounting bracket, no instruction manual beyond the basic label. The unit itself is 5 pounds of monoammonium phosphate dry chemical — the industry standard for ABC-rated fires. The tank is steel with a corrosion-resistant paint finish, and the valve assembly is all metal, not the pot-metal or plastic you'll find on cheaper units.

The pressure gauge is front-and-center and readable at a glance. As shown in the product image, the gauge needle sits in the green zone right out of the box. One buyer noted, "Arrived fully pressurized, gauge in the green. I checked it immediately." That's the kind of detail that matters when you're trusting your home to a safety device.

## Real-World Performance

The 3A:10BC rating is the spec that matters. The "3A" means it handles ordinary combustibles like wood, paper, and cloth — think garage fires and trash can blazes. The "10BC" covers flammable liquids and electrical fires, which is what you need for the kitchen, garage, or workshop. This is one of the highest ratings you'll find in a 5 lb portable unit.

Buyers consistently praise the discharge power. One reviewer wrote, "Tested it in the backyard per the instructions. The spray distance is impressive — I could stand well back and still hit the target." Another mentioned, "The handle is comfortable to squeeze, even with one hand. My wife could operate it easily." That's important because fire extinguishers are useless if the person grabbing it can't actually pull the trigger under stress.

The 5 lb size is the sweet spot for home and garage use. It's heavy enough to put out a real fire but light enough to carry quickly. The metal handle and trigger give you positive grip even with sweaty hands.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a mounting bracket is genuinely annoying. A fire extinguisher that's sitting in a closet or under a sink might as well not exist when you need it. You'll want to budget $10-15 for a proper wall bracket, which is a minor cost but an extra trip to the hardware store.

The dry chemical residue is the other real downside — and it's inherent to all ABC powder extinguishers, not specific to this brand. If you discharge this indoors, the powder will coat everything in a fine dust that's corrosive to electronics over time. You'll need to clean thoroughly after use. This isn't a Strike First problem; it's physics. But you should know what you're signing up for.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I need to recharge or replace it?**
A: Check the gauge monthly — it should stay in the green. The unit holds pressure for years, but you should replace it after any discharge, even partial. Also, shake it monthly to prevent the powder from settling and caking.

**Q: Is this safe for electrical fires?**
A: Yes. The 10BC rating covers live electrical equipment. The dry chemical is non-conductive, so you can safely discharge it on a plugged-in appliance. Just be prepared for the residue cleanup afterward.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
