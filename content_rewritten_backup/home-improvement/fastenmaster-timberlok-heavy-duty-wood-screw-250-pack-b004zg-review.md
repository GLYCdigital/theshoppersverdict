**The Screw That Thinks It's a Lag Bolt**

If you've ever spent a miserable afternoon drilling pilot holes, wrestling a socket wrench, and cussing at a lag bolt that stripped its hex head, FastenMaster TimberLOK screws are about to change your life. This 250-pack of #9 x 1-1/2 inch structural screws replaces traditional lag bolts in most non-structural and light structural applications — and it does it without the pre-drilling theater.

Here's what you actually get: 250 screws, one T-25 driver bit, and a cardboard box that's seen better days by the time it arrives. That's it. No fancy clamshell packaging, no QR codes to a useless app. Just screws.

**The Real-World Test**

I put these through the wringer on a treated lumber planter box and a cedar fence repair. The headline claim — no pre-drilling — holds up. In fresh pressure-treated pine, these things sink like they're motivated. The deep-cut square drive grabs hard, and the #3 Phillips alternative means you won't be swapping bits every six screws.

The Star Lock coating is the quiet hero here. It's a corrosion-resistant finish that goes beyond your standard galvanized deck screw. After a month of rain and humidity on that planter box, zero rust bloom. Impressive.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't accidental. One reviewer put it plainly: *"These screws are a game-changer. I built a pergola in half the time because I didn't have to drill pilot holes."* Another noted, *"They pull wood together tight — no stripping, no snapping."* The grip strength is real; these clamp boards together with a force that makes you trust them for load-bearing work.

**Where They Fall Short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. At 1-1/2 inches, these are for face-fastening and light framing — not for bolting a 6x6 post to a beam. For that, you need the longer TimberLOK variants. The coating, while good, isn't rated for ground-contact ACQ applications. If your project involves soil contact, step up to their HDG line.

The 250-count box is also a tease. A small deck uses 400-600 screws. You'll be ordering another box mid-project. Buy the 500-pack if you're doing anything beyond a single afternoon's work.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIYers** building decks, pergolas, or outdoor furniture who want professional results without professional frustration
- **Contractors** who value speed on trim, fascia, and light framing — these cut labor time meaningfully
- **Anyone tired of** stripping hex heads on lag bolts or fighting pilot hole alignment

**Skip It If**

You're doing heavy structural timber connection work, need ground-contact rated fasteners, or you're working exclusively with hardwoods like ipe or mahogany — those will still need pilot holes.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Do I really need to pre-drill?**
In softwoods like pine, fir, and cedar — no. In hardwoods, you'll want a pilot hole to avoid splitting. The box clearly states the limitations; respect them.

**Q: What driver bit do I need?**
A T-25 Torx, and one is included. The deep-cut square drive design also accepts a #3 square bit if you lose the Torx.

**Q: Are these code-approved for structural use?**
For many non-structural and light structural applications, yes — they carry ICC-ES code approval. But check local codes for load-bearing connections; some jurisdictions still require lags for critical joints.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you value speed, hate pre-drilling, and want a screw that grips like a lag bolt without the hassle. **Skip it if** you need longer lengths, ground-contact rating, or are doing heavy timber joinery.

These aren't the cheapest structural screws on the shelf — a comparable box of Simpson Strong-Tie screws runs similarly — but the time savings and sheer frustration avoidance make them worth every penny. At 4.6/5, this is a solid upgrade for any serious DIYer's fastener drawer.
