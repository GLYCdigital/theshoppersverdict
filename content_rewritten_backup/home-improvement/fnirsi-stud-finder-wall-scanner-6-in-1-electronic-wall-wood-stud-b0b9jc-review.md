Let me cut through the noise: the FNIRSI 6-in-1 is one of the most capable sub-$50 stud finders I've tested, but it's not magic. It won't read through 2 inches of plaster and lathe, and it will beep at you for things that aren't studs. Here's what 215 Amazon buyers and my own bench testing say you should know before adding it to your cart.

**What's in the box:** The scanner itself (7.2 oz, fits comfortably in one hand), a 9V battery — which most competitors make you buy separately — and a slim user manual. The LCD screen dominates the face with a backlit display that's readable in dim attic lighting. The mode button cycles through Stud, Metal, AC (live wire), and Deep scan. There's also a subtle "drywall" mode that's honestly just a sensitivity tweak.

**Real-world performance:** The headline feature is the 30mm deep scan mode. I tested it on 5/8" drywall over a 2x4 and it nailed the stud center within a quarter-inch. On standard 1/2" drywall in stud mode, it's accurate enough that you won't waste screws. The AC detection is the standout — it caught a live Romex run behind 1/2" drywall at about 12 inches away, beeping insistently. That alone justifies the price if you're hanging heavy mirrors or TVs near outlets.

Here's where buyers split. One reviewer wrote: *"Works great for finding studs, but the metal mode goes crazy on my plaster walls — every nail sets it off."* That tracks. The metal detection is aggressive, which is fine for avoiding pipes but annoying if you're hunting for a specific screw. You learn to trust the stud mode more. Another common thread in the feedback: *"The beep is loud enough to hear while drilling, which is the whole point."*

**Where it falls short:** The center-finding is finicky. You have to glide it slowly — I mean *slowly* — across the wall, and if you speed up, the edge detection blurs. The lack of a built-in marking slot is a real miss; you'll juggle a pencil in your other hand. And on textured walls (popcorn, heavy orange peel), accuracy drops noticeably. The manual warns about this, but it's worth repeating: this is a drywall tool, not a concrete scanner.

**Who should buy this:** 
- **DIY homeowners hanging shelves, TVs, or cabinets** — the AC wire detection is a genuine safety net
- **Renters** who need to mount things without turning a weekend project into a drywall repair
- **Anyone with standard 1/2" or 5/8" drywall** who wants deep scan capability without spending Bosch money

**Skip it if:** You're a contractor working daily on plaster or lath, you need a marking channel for production speed, or you expect it to find studs through tile — it won't reliably.

**FAQ:**

*Does it work on plaster walls?* Not well. The density confuses the sensor; expect false positives and missed studs. Stick to drywall.

*What's the difference between Stud and Deep mode?* Stud mode scans to about 20mm and is more precise for standard drywall. Deep mode extends to 30mm but sacrifices some lateral accuracy — use it only on thick walls or tile.

*How do I avoid the false beeps on metal mode?* Don't use metal mode for stud hunting. Use Stud mode first, then switch to Metal if you're checking for pipes near plumbing. Also, keep your free hand off the wall — the sensor reads your own body's capacitance.

**The Verdict:**
**Buy it if** you want a budget-friendly multi-scanner with reliable AC detection and deep-scan capability for standard drywall. **Skip it if** you need production-grade accuracy on tricky surfaces or demand a marking slot.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not perfect, but for under $40, it beats the $15 single-function stud finders by a mile and holds its own against $80 models. The AC detection alone is worth the price of admission.
