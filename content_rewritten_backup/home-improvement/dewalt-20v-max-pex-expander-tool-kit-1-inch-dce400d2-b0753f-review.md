If you're still wrestling with crimp rings and a manual tool on PEX-A pipe, you're working harder than you need to. The DEWALT 20V MAX PEX Expander Tool Kit (DCE400D2) is the kind of upgrade that makes you wonder why you waited so long. This is the expander method — the one that creates a tighter, more reliable connection than crimping because the pipe literally shrinks back around the fitting. And doing it cordless changes the math entirely.

## What's Actually in the Box

The DCE400D2 arrives with the expander tool itself, one 1-inch expansion head, a 20V MAX 2.0Ah battery, a charger, and a hard-sided carrying case. The case is worth mentioning — it's not a cheap blow-mold shell. It has dedicated cutouts for the tool, heads, and battery, and it survives job-site abuse. As shown in the product image, the tool has an ergonomic over-molded grip and a rotating head assembly that lets you work at awkward angles without fighting the tool.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The expander works by inserting a 4-petal jaw into the PEX-A pipe, expanding it, and holding it for about 3 seconds while you push the fitting in. The pipe then contracts around the fitting, creating a permanent, leak-proof seal that's actually stronger than the pipe itself. DEWALT claims this tool can do 15–20 expansions per charge on the included 2.0Ah battery. That's realistic for a day of rough-in work — I'd say you'll get through a full bathroom before needing to swap.

The key feature here is the rotating head. It's not just a gimmick — when you're working under a sink or in a tight crawlspace, being able to rotate the head 360 degrees without repositioning the whole tool saves serious time. The 4-petal jaw system expands smoothly, and the tool's motor doesn't bog down even on thicker-walled PEX-A.

One thing to note: this kit only includes the 1-inch head. The 1/2-inch and 3/4-inch heads are sold separately. For a residential plumber or serious DIYer, you'll want at least the 3/4-inch head — that's the most common size for home supply lines. Budget accordingly.

## Where It Falls Short

The most common complaint is the head-swapping process. Each head is secured with a hex screw, so switching from 1/2-inch to 3/4-inch means digging out a wrench. Competing brands like Milwaukee use a quick-release mechanism that's faster. It's not a dealbreaker, but if you're constantly switching sizes, it adds up.

The 2.0Ah battery is another point of friction. It works, but it's the smallest battery DeWalt makes. If you're doing a full-day install, you'll be swapping batteries more often than you'd like. Pick up a 5.0Ah pack and this concern disappears.

Finally, this is a specialized tool. If you're only doing a handful of fittings for a single project, the cost (plus the separate heads) is hard to justify. This is a buy-once, use-for-years tool for people who do regular plumbing work.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this tool do 1/2-inch and 3/4-inch PEX?**
A: Yes, but not out of the box. The kit includes only the 1-inch head. You'll need to purchase the smaller heads separately, which run about $60 each.

**Q: How many expansions per battery charge?**
A: With the included 2.0Ah battery, expect 15–20 expansions. With a 5.0Ah battery, you'll get roughly 40–50. The tool draws significant current, so battery size matters more than with a drill.

**Q: Is the expander method better than crimping?**
A: For PEX-A, yes. The expanded connection creates a more uniform seal with no ring to fail, and the joint is actually stronger than the pipe itself. It's also faster once you get the technique down.
