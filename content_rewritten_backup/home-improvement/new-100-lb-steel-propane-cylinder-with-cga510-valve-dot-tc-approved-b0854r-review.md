**What's in the box (and what it costs you)**

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The tank is DOT/TC approved, which means it meets US and Canadian safety standards for transport and refill. That's non-negotiable for legal refills, and this one has the stamping to prove it. The welded steel is thick — this isn't a thin-walled import that'll dent when you bump it. The powder-coat finish holds up well outdoors; multiple reviewers noted it survived a few seasons of sun and rain without rusting.

The CGA510 valve includes the mandated OPD (Overfill Protection Device), which prevents overfilling — you'll hear a distinctive click when the tank hits 80% capacity during filling. The valve also has a safety relief valve that vents pressure if things get too hot. Standard stuff, but well-executed here.

One thing to check: a few buyers reported the O-ring on the valve stem arriving slightly loose. It's a two-minute fix with a wrench, but worth inspecting before your first fill. Also, there's no fuel gauge. You'll either need to weigh the tank periodically (a bathroom scale works — subtract the empty weight) or install an inline gauge. Not a dealbreaker, but budget for it.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I transport this tank lying down in my truck?**
A: DOT regulations say you *should* transport propane cylinders upright and secured. Lying down is not recommended — the liquid propane can reach the relief valve. Get a tank rack or secure it upright.

**Q: How long will a 100 lb tank actually last?**
A: Depends on usage. A 30,000 BTU heater will run roughly 40–50 hours on a full tank. A 400 sq ft grill at high heat? You're looking at 15–20 hours per tank. It's roughly 23.6 gallons of propane in there.

**Q: Does this tank need to be recertified?**
A: Yes, standard DOT hydrostatic testing is required every 12 years after the manufacture date stamped on the collar. Plan for that cost down the road.
