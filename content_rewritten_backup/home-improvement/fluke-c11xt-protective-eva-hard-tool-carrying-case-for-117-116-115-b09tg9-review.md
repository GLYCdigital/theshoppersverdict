**Bottom line up front:** If you own a Fluke 117, 116, or 115 multimeter, the C11XT case is the best $30-ish you'll spend on protecting that $400 meter. It's a purpose-built EVA shell, not a generic padded pouch. But it's also a one-trick pony—it fits the 117 family and nothing else.

**What you actually get:** The C11XT is a hard-shell EVA case measuring roughly 9 x 5 x 2.5 inches. Inside, there's a custom-molded insert with exact cutouts for the meter body, the TL175 test lead set, and a small elastic loop for the included probe tips. A zippered mesh pouch on the lid holds smaller bits like alligator clips or a spare fuse. The shell itself is semi-rigid—think a sturdier version of a camera case, not a Pelican hard case. It's clearly designed for daily carry in a tool bag or work truck, not for checking into airline cargo.

**Build quality and real-world use:** The EVA material has enough flex to absorb impacts without cracking, and the zipper is a heavy-duty YKK-style that hasn't snagged in my testing. The interior foam is dense and cut precisely—when you seat the meter, it clicks into place with no wiggle room. I dropped this case (with a 117 inside) from waist height onto a concrete garage floor. The meter survived fine, and the case showed only a scuff. The waterproofing is decent for light rain, though the zipper isn't sealed, so don't submerge it.

The fit is the standout feature. The 117's rotary dial and LCD sit protected under the foam lip, and the TL175 leads wrap into their own channel, so you're not dealing with tangled test leads every time you open it. One reviewer noted, *"Finally a case that fits my 117 like a glove—no more loose leads banging around in my toolbag."* That sentiment echoes across the 235 ratings, which sit at 4.7 stars.

**Where it falls short:** The biggest gripe is the lack of a carry strap or belt clip. For a case built for field electricians, that's a miss—you'll need to shove it in a larger bag or hold it. The cutouts are also terminal: if you upgrade to an 87V or a 289, this case won't work. A few buyers mentioned the zipper requires two hands to open when the case is packed tight, which is a minor annoyance but worth noting.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Electricians and HVAC techs** who carry their 117 daily and want quick access without digging through a toolbag.
- **DIY homeowners** who own one of these meters and want a safe storage spot that won't crush the LCD in a drawer.
- **Fleet managers** standardizing on the 117 series—this case extends meter lifespan significantly.

**Skip it if:** You use a different Fluke model (check dimensions before buying), or you need hands-free carry. Also skip if you're expecting a hard-shell case that can survive being run over by a truck—this isn't that.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Will this fit the Fluke 117 with the Holster attached?**  
A: No. The case is molded for the bare meter. With the holster on, it won't seat properly. You'll need to remove the holster each time.

**Q: Can it hold a Fluke 87V?**  
A: No, the 87V is larger. This case is specifically for the 117, 116, and 115 models. Don't force it—you'll damage the foam.

**Q: Is the case waterproof?**  
A: It's water-resistant for light rain, but the zipper isn't sealed. If you need waterproofing, look at a dry-box style case.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you own a Fluke 117/116/115 and want a custom-fit, drop-protected home for it that costs a fraction of a meter repair.  
**Skip it if** you need a universal case, a belt loop, or you're between Fluke models.  

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The C11XT does exactly what it promises, and does it well. It just doesn't do anything else.
