# Blum 110 Degrees Screw On Self Closing Compact 33 Hinge: The Quiet Workhorse of Cabinet Hardware

If you've ever installed cheap cabinet hinges, you know the drill: misaligned doors, sagging after six months, and that annoying click-slam every time someone closes a cabinet. The Blum Compact 33 is the hinge that cabinet makers reach for when they want it done right the first time. And with a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, the market agrees.

## What's Actually in the Box

This listing ships **one hinge** — not a pair, not a set with mounting plates. You get the Compact 33 hinge body with the integrated soft-close mechanism. The screw-on mounting plate is sold separately (Blum part number for the plate varies by cabinet type). This caught several buyers off guard, so count your doors carefully before ordering.

The hinge itself is compact — roughly 2 inches long when closed — and features the classic Blum 110-degree opening angle. That extra 10 degrees over standard 100-degree hinges makes a real difference when you're reaching into deep cabinets or working around corner layouts.

## Real-World Performance

The Compact 33's party trick is the **integrated soft-close**. Unlike add-on dampers that stick out awkwardly, this mechanism is built into the hinge arm itself. Close the door from about 2 inches out and it glides shut with a whisper. No slam, no noise, no fingers caught. One reviewer put it simply: *"These are the only hinges I'll put in my house now. The soft close is buttery smooth and they've held up perfectly for two years."*

Installation requires a **35mm bore hole** — standard for European-style hinges, but a dealbreaker if you're retrofitting old American cabinets with 26mm holes. You'll need a Forstner bit and a steady hand, or a drill jig. The screw-on plate gives you **3D adjustment**: ±2mm vertical, ±2mm horizontal, and ±3mm depth. This is where Blum earns its reputation — you can fine-tune door alignment without loosening screws and praying.

Build quality is unmistakable. The zinc alloy body feels solid, the spring tension is consistent, and the hinge is rated for 100,000 cycles. That's roughly 27 years of opening a cabinet ten times a day. The included **lifetime warranty** backs that up.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides:

1. **The bore hole requirement is non-negotiable**. If you're not set up to drill 35mm holes cleanly, this hinge will frustrate you. Consider a drill guide or template.
2. **Face-frame cabinets need a special adapter plate**. The Compact 33 is designed for European frameless cabinets. If you have face-frame construction, you'll need the Blum face-frame mounting plate, which adds cost and complexity.
3. **Sold individually**. This is a minor annoyance, but when you're ordering for a whole kitchen, it's easy to miscalculate. Blum also sells multi-packs — check those listings before committing.
4. **No soft-close adjustment**. The closing speed is factory-set. It's well-tuned, but if you prefer a slower or faster close, you can't adjust it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Cabinet makers and serious DIYers** — this is your hinge. If you're building frameless cabinets, doing a full kitchen remodel, or replacing all the hinges in an IKEA-style cabinet system, the Blum Compact 33 is the professional choice.

**Skip it if** you're doing a quick hinge swap on existing face-frame cabinets, or if you're not prepared to drill precise 35mm holes. In those cases, look for Blum's clip-top face-frame hinges instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need the mounting plate or does it come with the hinge?**
A: The plate is **not included**. You need to order the matching Blum mounting plate separately (either screw-on or press-in, depending on your cabinet). The hinge and plate are sold as separate SKUs.

**Q: Will this work on my existing cabinet doors?**
A: Only if your doors are frameless (European-style) and you can drill a 35mm hole to the correct depth. Standard American cabinets with face frames and exposed hinges will need a different hinge type.

**Q: How many hinges do I need per door?**
A: For standard cabinet doors up to about 36 inches tall, use two hinges. For taller doors or heavy solid-wood doors, add a third hinge in the middle.
