**What You Actually Get**

Open the box and you're looking at a heavy-duty plastic case with a clear lid — the kind that survives being kicked around a job site. Inside, you'll find 2,000 self-tapping floor screws: 1,000 in 3mm and 1,000 in 1/8-inch. The 3mm size works for most standard hardwood installations; the 1/8-inch is ideal for thinner engineered planks or when you're fastening through existing flooring into a subfloor. The screws are case-hardened, which matters more than most buyers realize — soft screws shear off under impact driver torque, and that's a headache you don't want mid-install.

**Performance and Build Quality**

These screws earn their keep where it counts. The self-tapping design means you can drive them directly through the tongue of the floor plank without pre-drilling, which shaves real time off a large project. The hardened steel resists cam-out — that frustrating moment when the driver bit spins free and strips the screw head. I've seen cheaper bulk screws fail at exactly this point; the RTCs hold up.

One thing worth noting: you'll need to supply your own driver bit. There's no bit included in the kit, which is a small oversight for a product marketed as a complete installation solution. Most buyers will have a #2 Phillips or square drive on hand, but it's worth adding to your cart if you don't.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these work with a nail gun or only a screw gun?**
A: These are screws, not nails — they require a drill or impact driver with a #2 Phillips or square drive bit. They won't work in a nailer.

**Q: Can I use them on pre-finished hardwood?**
A: Yes, but drive them through the tongue at an angle as you would with standard floor screws. The self-tapping tip prevents splitting in most species.

**Q: Are the two sizes interchangeable?**
A: Not really. Use 1/8-inch for thinner planks (under 5/8-inch) and 3mm for standard 3/4-inch hardwood. Swapping them can cause splitting or insufficient holding power.
