## The 3-Second Tea Machine That's Almost Perfect

You know that moment when you're staring at a kettle, waiting for it to boil, wondering why it takes three minutes? The Delta Instant Hot Water Dispenser kills that entirely. As shown in the product image, this is a sleek, dedicated faucet that sits alongside your main kitchen faucet and delivers 200°F water on demand. It's not a gimmick — it's a genuine workflow changer for anyone who drinks tea, makes pour-over coffee, or needs boiling water for cooking.

But it's not without quirks. Here's the real story after digging through 210 ratings and owner experiences.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the faucet head, the under-sink tank (holds 0.5 gallons), all hoses, and the mounting hardware. Notably, there's **no electrical cord included for hardwiring** — you'll need to connect it to a dedicated circuit. The tank measures roughly 10" x 6" x 6", so it fits in most standard cabinets, but measure yours first. The faucet itself extends about 7 inches over the sink, which is shorter than most main faucets but perfectly adequate for filling mugs.

## Performance: Fast, Hot, and Consistent

The tank heats water to 200°F and holds it there. When you pull the handle, water flows immediately — no sputtering, no warm-up delay. Owners consistently report it delivers "hot enough to brew tea instantly" and maintains temperature even during back-to-back uses. The flow rate is modest (about 0.5 GPM), which is fine for filling a mug but frustrating if you're trying to fill a pasta pot. That's a tradeoff worth knowing.

One frequent praise point: the matte black finish. Multiple reviewers note it "doesn't show fingerprints" and "looks premium next to stainless sinks." As one buyer put it, "The black finish is why I chose this over the chrome version — it hides everything."

## Where It Falls Short

**The elephant in the room: installation.** This unit requires a dedicated 20-amp circuit per the manual. If your kitchen was built in the last decade, you might have one available for a dishwasher or disposal. If not, you're calling an electrician — expect $150–$300 extra. That's a real cost many buyers didn't anticipate.

**No temperature control.** It's fixed at 200°F. If you want 180°F for green tea or 160°F for formula, this isn't the unit for you. You'll be mixing with cold water, which defeats some of the convenience.

**Electricity draw.** The tank cycles on and off to maintain temperature. Owners estimate an extra $3–$5 per month on their bill. Not a dealbreaker, but it's not free to run.

**Scald risk.** 200°F water is genuinely dangerous. The handle requires two hands to operate (you push a safety button and pull), which helps, but if you have curious toddlers, this needs to be mounted out of reach.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a tea or coffee drinker who goes through multiple cups daily, you have a dedicated circuit available (or budget for an electrician), and you're okay with a fixed temperature. It's also great for families that make oatmeal, ramen, or need quick hot water for cooking.

**Skip it if:** You need variable temperatures, you're on a tight budget (the install cost can double your total), or you have young children who might access it.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it actually get hot enough for tea?**
A: Yes. 200°F is slightly below boiling (212°F) but hot enough for black, green, and herbal teas. Coffee lovers report it works well for pour-over, though you may want to let it sit 30 seconds for optimal brewing temp.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
A: The plumbing is straightforward (standard compression fittings), but the electrical work is not DIY-friendly unless you're comfortable wiring a dedicated circuit. If you don't have an available 20-amp line, budget for a professional.

**Q: How long does the tank take to reheat after heavy use?**
A: About 10 minutes to recover fully from empty to 200°F. For normal use (a few mugs at a time), you won't notice the recovery period.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful appliance that's held back by installation complexity and a fixed temperature.

**Buy it if:** You want instant boiling water daily and can handle (or afford) the electrical requirement.
