### The Schlage Touch Century: A Smart Lock for People Who Don't Want "Smart" Problems

Most electronic locks on Amazon scream "tech gadget." They have glowing rings, app logos, and voice assistants built in. The Schlage Touch Century takes the opposite approach. It looks like a classic, high-end mechanical lever—until you touch it and realize the entire front face is a hidden keypad. That design philosophy is both its biggest strength and its most significant limitation. Let’s dig into whether this "dumb" smart lock is the right call for your front door.

#### What You Actually Get

Opening the box reveals the standard hardware kit: the exterior keypad lever, the interior thumb-turn assembly, a latch, mounting screws, and four AA batteries. There is no physical key cylinder on the exterior—this is keypad-only. That means you need to store a backup key elsewhere or have a way to jump the battery terminals if the power dies. The build quality is typical Schlage: solid zinc alloy construction. It feels hefty in the hand, and the Century lever design (a smooth, curved handle) looks far more expensive than the sub-$200 price point suggests.

#### Performance and Real-World Use

Setting this up is a 15-minute job if you have a screwdriver. It fits standard 2-1/8" bore holes and handles door thicknesses from 1-3/8" to 1-3/4". Programming codes is done entirely on the keypad—no app required. You can store up to 19 unique codes, which is plenty for a family plus a dog walker.

The standout feature here is the built-in alarm. This is rare at this price. There are two sensors: one detects the door being forced open, and another detects someone trying to guess your code. When triggered, it emits a piercing 100-decibel siren. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"I didn't buy this for the alarm, but after a package thief tried my door handle, the siren scared him off instantly. It's loud."* That is real-world value.

The "One-Touch Locking" is also excellent. You press the Schlage logo on the front, and the deadbolt engages. It’s a small thing, but it prevents the "did I lock the door?" anxiety without requiring a phone app. The touchscreen is responsive; you don't need to press hard, just tap your code.

#### What Buyers Praise

Beyond the alarm, the consistent praise centers on the aesthetic and the keypad sensitivity. Shoppers repeatedly mention that it looks like a $300+ lock. One 5-star review states, *"Finally a keypad lock that doesn't look like a sci-fi prop. My 1920s craftsman door looks original with this on it."* The backlighting is also a hit—it only illuminates when you touch the screen, which saves battery and looks sleek at night. Users report getting 10-12 months out of a set of AAs with normal use.

#### Where It Falls Short

Here is the honest truth: this is not a "smart" lock. It has no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, and no app. If you want to check if you locked the door from work or unlock it for a guest remotely, you cannot do that with this unit. You would need to buy the separate Schlage Z-Wave or Wi-Fi adapter, which costs extra and adds complexity. If you are deep in the Apple HomeKit or Alexa ecosystem, this lock will feel archaic.

The other complaint is visibility. The touchscreen is high-gloss, which looks premium but is a fingerprint magnet. More importantly, the numbers are etched into the glass and backlit. In bright, direct sunlight, the contrast washes out, and you might have to shade the screen with your hand to see the numbers. It’s a minor annoyance, but a common one in reviews.

#### Who Should Buy This?

- **The Traditionalist:** You want keyless entry but refuse to put a plastic-looking gadget on your solid wood door. This is for you.
- **The Security-Conscious:** The built-in alarm is a genuine deterrent that most standalone keypads don't offer.
- **The Vacation Home Owner:** You need to give codes to cleaners or guests without handing out keys. The 19-code capacity is perfect.

**Skip it if:** You need remote access, auto-lock schedules, or integration with your smart speaker. For that, look at the Schlage Encode Plus or a similar Wi-Fi model.

#### FAQ

**Q: What happens if the batteries die?**
A: The unit has 9-volt battery terminals on the bottom exterior. Touch a 9V battery to the contacts to power the keypad temporarily and unlock the door. Keep one in your car.

**Q: Can I use my existing house key?**
A: No. This lock has no key cylinder. It is keypad-only. You must use a code or the interior thumb-turn.

**Q: Does the alarm reset automatically?**
A: Yes, but it's loud. The forced-entry alarm will sound for about 30 seconds before resetting. The tamper alarm resets after a minute. You can silence it by entering a valid code.

#### The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a rock-solid, good-looking keypad lock with a built-in security alarm and you don't care about app control. **Skip it if** you need remote locking or voice assistant integration.

This is a 4.0/5 product on Amazon, and that rating feels accurate. It loses points for the lack of connectivity and the smudge-prone screen, but it gains them back for sheer reliability and build quality. It’s a lock that will last a decade without a firmware update. Sometimes, that’s exactly what you want on your front door.
