If you've ever hand-filed a weld bead or spent twenty minutes chasing a burr with a Dremel, the DEWALT 20V MAX XTREME Cordless Bandfile is the tool that makes you wonder why you waited. This is a sub-compact belt sander that puts a 1/2-inch by 18-inch abrasive belt in a chassis that weighs about 2.5 pounds without the battery. It's not a replacement for a bench grinder, but it's the tool you'll grab first for deburring, blending, and detail work in spots a full-size sander can't reach.

## What's Actually in the Box

As shown in the product image, this is the bare tool configuration — just the bandfile, a 1/2-inch by 18-inch belt (one medium-grit belt is included), and the manual. No battery, no charger. If you're already running DEWALT 20V MAX tools, you're set. If not, factor in the cost of at least one 2Ah or 4Ah battery, because a 2Ah pack is the practical minimum for this tool.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The variable-speed dial is the headline feature. It ranges from 5,000 to 9,500 RPM, and unlike cheaper bandfiles that just have a trigger lock, this dial gives you genuine control. At the low end, you can finesse aluminum without loading the belt. Crank it up for steel and you'll chew through scale nicely. In practice, I found the sweet spot around 7,000 RPM for most stainless work.

The platen arm rotates 220 degrees, which is the real selling point. You can flip the nose down for flush sanding against a perpendicular surface — think blending a repair on a door edge — or pivot it up for working inside a box tube. The belt release is genuinely tool-free: pull the lever, swap the belt, close it up. Takes about ten seconds once you've done it twice.

One thing reviewers consistently mention is balance. The XTREME line's smaller motor housing puts the weight right over your grip, which makes a real difference when you're holding this at odd angles for extended periods. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I've used pneumatic bandfiles for years and this keeps up for light and medium work. The lack of a hose makes it worth the weight penalty."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 ratings, three themes dominate. First, the convenience of cordless operation — no compressor, no hose wrestling. Second, the speed control's usefulness for varying materials. Third, the build quality; DEWALT's 20V MAX tooling is generally reliable, and the XTREME line feels well-damped despite its light weight.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. The dust port is a 1-inch hose adapter that's more decorative than functional — you'll want a shop vac attached for anything beyond light sanding, and even then, fine dust escapes around the belt. The belt tracking can drift under aggressive side pressure; you'll be reaching for the adjustment knob more often than on a stationary sander. And the included belt is barely a starter — budget for a multi-pack of 80, 120, and 240 grit belts immediately.

Battery life is the other consideration. On a 4Ah battery, you'll get roughly 20–25 minutes of intermittent use. That's fine for detail work, but if you're planning a full day of weld blending, buy a second battery or keep a charger nearby.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Fabricators and metalworkers** who need a portable deburring tool for jobsite repairs and finish work.
- **Woodworkers** making jigs or shaping small parts where a router or file is too coarse.
- **Anyone already in the 20V MAX ecosystem** — the bare-tool price point makes this an easy add.
- Skip it if you do heavy, continuous stock removal; a corded belt sander or bench grinder will outperform this for less money.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this come with a battery and charger?**
No. It's a bare tool. You need a DEWALT 20V MAX battery (2Ah minimum, 4Ah recommended) and charger.

**Q: Can I use it on wood and metal?**
Yes. The variable speed makes it workable for both, but keep separate belts for each material — metal particles embedded in a belt will tear up wood fast.

**Q: What belt size does it take?**
1/2-inch by 18-inch belts. Standard size, widely available from 3M, Norton, and DEWALT.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a portable, precise finishing tool and you're already invested in DEWALT's battery system. The variable speed, rotating platen, and light weight make it a genuinely useful shop addition.

**Skip it if** you're starting from scratch without batteries, or if your work is mostly heavy stock removal — you'll get more value from a corded tool and a quality bench grinder.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It loses half a star for the token dust collection and the battery tax, but for its intended niche, this is the best cordless bandfile I've used.
