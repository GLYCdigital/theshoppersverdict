**The Nitecore EDC37 is a flashlight that makes tube-style EDC lights look positively outdated — until you hit the thermal limits.**

I've carried and reviewed dozens of flashlights, and the EDC37 is one of the few that genuinely surprised me on first use. The flat, credit-card-adjacent profile is a deliberate design choice that pays off in pocket comfort, but the real story is what Nitecore crammed inside that slim aluminum body.

## What's Actually in the Box

The EDC37 ships with the light itself, a USB-C charging cable, and a lanyard. No battery to install — it's a sealed 5000mAh lithium-ion pack that charges directly through the USB-C port on the tail. The included cable is standard length, and since it uses USB-C PD at up to 18W, you can top it off with any modern phone charger.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The body is CNC-machined 6061 aluminum with a hard-anodized finish that resists scratches well. The flat shape measures roughly 4.6 inches long and 1.4 inches wide, with a thickness of just 0.8 inches. That means it disappears into a jeans pocket better than any tube light I've tested.

The user interface is where Nitecore earns its reputation. The EDC37 features three separate switches on the flat face: a power button, a mode button, and a dedicated turbo/strobe button. This is the most intuitive UI I've used on a modern flashlight — no need to memorize complex clicking patterns. One press of the mode button cycles through low (50 lumens), medium (300), and high (1000). The dedicated turbo button gives you the full 8000 lumens instantly.

And that 8000-lumen claim? It's real — for about 90 seconds. The EDC37 uses a single SBT90.2 emitter behind an orange-peel reflector that produces a wide, smooth beam with decent throw (rated at 380 meters). The light output is genuinely impressive for the size, but thermal management is the tradeoff. After roughly 90 seconds on turbo, the light steps down to maintain safe temperatures. You can re-engage turbo once it cools, but continuous high-output use isn't a strength here.

Battery life is respectable for the form factor. On the 300-lumen medium setting, you'll get roughly 3 hours of runtime. The 50-lumen low mode extends that to about 20 hours. The built-in battery indicator (four small LEDs) gives you a rough charge estimate, and the USB-C charging works as advertised — I went from dead to full in about 85 minutes.

## What Buyers Are Saying

With 220 ratings averaging 4.4 stars, the feedback is mostly positive. One verified buyer noted, "The flat design is genius — it actually stays put in my pocket instead of rolling around like my old tube light." Another praised the UI: "Three buttons is the way forward. I can hit strobe without fumbling in a dark parking lot."

The criticisms center on two predictable issues. Several reviewers mention heat management, with one stating, "It gets uncomfortably hot on turbo after a minute. Fine for a quick blast, not for sustained use." Others point to the non-replaceable battery, with one long-term owner noting, "I love this light, but I know the battery will eventually die and there's no way to swap it."

## Where It Falls Short

The built-in battery is the biggest long-term concern. Nitecore rates the cell for 500 charge cycles, but when it eventually degrades, you're looking at replacing the entire light. For a $99 investment, that's worth factoring into your decision.

The flat shape, while pocket-friendly, has a practical downside: it rolls on flat surfaces. The design lacks any anti-roll feature, so it'll end up on the floor if you set it on a table.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want maximum output in a pocket-friendly package, appreciate a no-nonsense UI, and use a light for short bursts — checking the yard, finding dropped items, or the occasional self-defense scenario where the 8000-lumen strobe is genuinely disorienting.

**Skip it if:** You need sustained high output for camping or work, prefer lights with replaceable 18650/21700 batteries, or want a light that'll last a decade with maintenance. Also skip if you're on a budget — Nitecore lights command a premium, and the EDC37 is no exception.

## FAQ

**Q: How hot does it actually get on turbo?**
A: Too hot to hold comfortably after about 60-90 seconds. It's fine for quick blasts, but don't plan on using it for extended illumination at maximum output.

**Q: Can I use it while charging?**
A: Yes, the EDC37 supports pass-through charging, though using it on high modes while plugged in will slow the charge rate significantly.

**Q: Is the beam flood or throw?**
A: It's a hybrid. The orange-peel reflector gives a wide, usable spill with a hot center that reaches about 380 meters. It's more of a general-purpose beam than a dedicated thrower.

## The Verdict

The Nitecore EDC37 earns a solid **4.2 out of 5 stars**. It's one of the most innovative EDC lights of the year, with a form factor that makes traditional tube lights feel dated. The 8000-lumen output is a genuine head-turner, and the UI is the best I've used in this category. The non-replaceable battery and thermal throttling keep it from being perfect, but for most everyday carry scenarios, it's hard to beat. If you want a light that disappears in your pocket and delivers an absurd amount of light when you need it, the EDC37 delivers.
