## The Short Version

If you've ever fought with a manual rivet gun on a ladder, in a tight corner, or on a 50-rivet job, you already know why this tool exists. The DEWALT 20V MAX XR Cordless Rivet Tool eliminates the squeeze — literally. But at a price that's roughly triple a decent manual gun, the real question is whether your workflow justifies it. After digging through 225 buyer ratings and the spec sheet, here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The tool ships as a bare unit — no battery, no charger, no case. That's worth repeating because some buyers were surprised. You're getting the rivet gun itself, a 3.2mm nozzle, a 4.0mm nozzle, a 4.8mm nozzle, and a hex key. If you're already in the DEWALT 20V ecosystem, this is a no-brainer addition. If you're not, factor in the cost of a battery and charger before you get excited.

## Performance and Build Quality

The headline feature is the automatic rivet cycle. Pull the trigger, and the tool grips the mandrel, pulls it, snaps it, and ejects it — all without you manually cranking anything. The 18kN pulling force handles most standard aluminum and steel rivets up to 4.8mm without breaking a sweat. The XR motor delivers that force consistently, and the tool's runtime on a 5Ah battery is genuinely impressive for a job-site tool.

The anti-slip grip is well-executed. It's comfortable for extended use, which matters more than you'd think when you're doing overhead work or reaching into awkward spots. The LED work light isn't a gimmick — it actually illuminates the work area, which helps in dim crawl spaces and under cabinets.

What buyers consistently praise is the speed and consistency. One reviewer noted, "I riveted 200 rivets on a trailer rebuild and my hands didn't hurt at all. The old manual gun would have killed me." Another mentioned, "The automatic mandrel ejection is the feature I didn't know I needed. No more shaking rivets out of a manual gun."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this tool is heavy. At roughly 4.5 pounds with a battery attached, it's noticeably heavier than a manual rivet gun. For overhead work, that weight adds up fast. Some buyers also reported that the tool struggles with stainless steel rivets above 4.8mm — the pulling force is solid, but you'll want to step up to a hydraulic tool for heavy-gauge stainless work.

The other real downside is the price. This is a specialized tool, and DEWALT charges accordingly. If you're a DIYer who pops 20 rivets a year for gutter repairs, this is overkill. You'd be better served by a $30 manual gun.

A few buyers also mentioned that the 3.2mm nozzle can be slightly finicky to seat, and the tool has a tendency to occasionally skip the mandrel ejection cycle if you're using it at an extreme angle. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a professional fabricator, HVAC installer, or metalworker who uses rivets daily. The time savings and reduced hand fatigue pay for themselves quickly. Also a strong pick for anyone doing large-scale DIY projects — think metal roofing, trailer repair, or custom fabrication — where you're setting 100+ rivets per session.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual DIYer with occasional rivet needs, or if you're not already invested in the DEWALT 20V battery platform. The cost of entry is simply too high for light use.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with all DEWALT 20V batteries?**  
A: Yes, it's compatible with all DEWALT 20V MAX batteries, including the compact 2Ah and the extended 6Ah and 8Ah packs. The 5Ah is the sweet spot for runtime-to-weight balance.

**Q: Can it handle stainless steel rivets?**  
A: It handles stainless up to 4.8mm, but buyers report it works best with aluminum and mild steel. For heavy stainless, consider a hydraulic tool.

**Q: Is the tool covered by DEWALT's warranty?**  
A: Yes, it comes with DEWALT's standard 3-year limited warranty, plus a 90-day money-back guarantee and 1-year free service.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The DEWALT 20V MAX XR Cordless Rivet Tool is a genuine time-saver for anyone who sets rivets with any regularity. It's well-built, powerful enough for most jobs, and the automatic cycle is genuinely pleasant to use. The weight and price are real drawbacks, but they're the cost of admission for a professional-grade cordless tool.

**Buy it if** you're a pro or serious DIYer who's tired of manual rivet guns and already owns DEWALT batteries. **Skip it if** your rivet needs are occasional — a manual gun will do the job for a fraction of the price. This one earns its keep on the job site, not in the garage.
