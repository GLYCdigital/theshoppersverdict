If your garage hits 110°F by 2 p.m. in July, you already know the problem: that thin steel door is a radiant heat magnet. The NASA TECH White Reflective Foam Core kit is a straightforward fix, and at a 4.3-star average from 215 ratings, it's one of the more proven options in this category. But it's not magic — here's what actually happens when you put it on your door.

**What's in the box**

You get eight 5/16-inch-thick panels of closed-cell polyethylene foam, each laminated on both sides with reflective foil. The panels are sized to fit a standard 16-foot-wide, 7-foot-tall two-car door when installed between the horizontal rails. The kit includes double-sided adhesive tape strips to hold each panel in place. That's it — no cutting guide, no scoring tool, no spray adhesive. You supply scissors or a utility knife and about 45 minutes of patience.

**Real-world performance**

Here's the thing about reflective insulation: it doesn't slow conduction much — the foam is only 5/16 inch thick. What it does is reflect radiant heat, which is exactly what a sun-baked garage door radiates into your space. Several reviewers note temperature drops of 10–15°F on the garage side of the door after installation. One buyer in Texas said: *"My garage went from unbearable to tolerable in the summer. The door itself is cool to the touch on the inside now."* That tracks with how radiant barriers work — they stop the heat before it ever enters the air.

The foam also adds a meaningful layer of sound deadening. If you've got a door that rattles when the wind blows or you're tired of hearing every car pass by, this cuts that down noticeably. Reviewers with attached garages report less noise transfer into the house.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest complaint across reviews is the installation fit. The panels are sized for the *average* two-car door, but your door's panel dimensions may differ slightly — and when they don't match, you're trimming every single piece. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's tedious. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Wish they had pre-scored lines. I spent an hour measuring and cutting each one."*

The reflective surface is also fragile during installation. Drag a panel against the door's metal edge and you'll leave permanent white scuff marks. They don't affect performance, but they look sloppy on an otherwise clean install. Handle panels by the edges and cut on a flat, clean surface.

Also worth noting: this is not a solution for extreme climates. If you're in Minnesota and want to keep the garage above freezing in January, this alone won't do it. It's a radiant barrier, not a thick blanket of fiberglass. You'll want to pair it with weatherstripping and possibly a space heater for serious cold.

**Who should buy this**

- **The sun-baked garage owner** — if your garage is an oven from May to September, this is the single cheapest effective fix you'll find.
- **The attached-garage homeowner** — cutting radiant heat transfer means your HVAC isn't fighting the garage wall as hard.
- **The DIY beginner** — this is genuinely a one-hour project with basic tools. If you can cut a straight line, you can install this.

**Skip it if** you need serious thermal insulation for a workshop you heat year-round, or if your door has non-standard panel sizes that'll require extensive custom cutting.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work on a single-car garage door?**
A: Yes, but you'll have leftover panels. The kit is sized for two cars. Some buyers use extras on the garage's side walls or attic hatch. You can't really cut it down to fit a single door without wasting material — consider a single-car kit instead.

**Q: Will it cause my garage door opener to work harder?**
A: No. Each panel weighs about a pound. Total added weight is roughly 8 pounds for the full kit, which is negligible for any modern opener. Some reviewers noted it actually made their door *quieter* because the foam dampens panel vibration.

**Q: Does the reflective surface need to face a specific direction?**
A: The foil works on both sides, so orientation doesn't matter for heat reflection. However, the white side is meant to face into the garage for a cleaner look. It won't affect performance either way.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your garage turns into a sauna in summer, you want a quick, cheap noise reduction, or you're tired of your attached garage bleeding heat into your home. It's a solid 4.2/5 product for its intended use — radiant heat control.

**Skip it if** you need serious cold-weather insulation, have a non-standard door that'll require heavy trimming, or you want a zero-maintenance finish that won't scuff.

For the $50–70 price range, this is one of the better returns on investment in garage comfort you'll find. Just budget the extra hour for cutting, and handle the panels like they're made of eggshells until they're mounted.
