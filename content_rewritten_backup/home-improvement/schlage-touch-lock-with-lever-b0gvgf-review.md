It's not flashy — there's no Wi-Fi, no app, no voice assistant integration — and that's precisely why so many homeowners love it. At 4.5 stars across over 225 ratings, this is a proven, no-nonsense upgrade for anyone tired of fumbling for keys.

## What's Actually in the Box

SCHLAGE keeps the package refreshingly simple: the touchscreen lever lock (available in multiple finishes), a standard deadbolt mechanism, all mounting hardware, and a detailed instruction booklet. There's no hub, no bridge, no extra modules to buy. You get the lock, the keys, and a 9-volt battery that powers the touchscreen. If you've installed a standard Schlage deadbolt before, you can install this in about 20–30 minutes with just a Phillips screwdriver.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The core feature is the **touchscreen keypad** — you tap in a 4-to-8-digit code, and the lever turns smoothly. Response time is immediate; there's no lag between the last digit and the mechanical click. The backlit numbers are visible in low light but don't glow obnoxiously bright in a dark hallway.

The lock uses **Schlage's proprietary "Sit tight" security** — after five incorrect attempts, the keypad locks out for 15 seconds, then 5 minutes after ten attempts. That's a solid brute-force deterrent that many competitors at this price point skip entirely.

**Build quality is genuinely impressive.** The exterior is solid zinc alloy with a protective finish that resists fingerprints and smudges — a small but meaningful detail given how often you'll touch it. The lever itself has a substantial, damped action. It doesn't feel hollow or cheap like some sub-$100 smart locks.

The **mechanical key override** works flawlessly — it's a genuine Schlage C-keyway cylinder, so you can rekey it to match your existing house keys if you own other Schlage locks. That's a convenience that's easy to overlook until you need it.

## Where It Falls Short (Honestly)

**No smart home integration.** This is not a "smart" lock in the modern sense — there's no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or Z-Wave. You can't check who's coming and going from your phone, and you can't unlock it remotely. If you want app control or voice assistant compatibility, this isn't your lock.

**Battery life is conservative.** Schlage claims about 3 years of typical use, but heavy users (lots of daily code entries) may see closer to 18–24 months. It does have a low-battery warning, and the 9-volt emergency jump terminals on the bottom are a thoughtful backup — but you'll still need to keep a 9V battery handy.

**The lever design isn't for everyone.** If you have young kids who can reach the lever, they can potentially open the door even without a code — the lever itself is mechanical, after all. The included interior thumb turn is standard, but there's no deadbolt locking from the inside automatically. You'll need to manually engage it.

**No auto-lock feature.** This is a purely manual lock — you must remember to press the Schlage button on the exterior or use the interior thumb turn. Forgetting to lock your door is a common complaint in the reviews.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I use my existing Schlage keys with this lock?**
Yes. The lock uses a standard Schlage C-keyway cylinder. You can remove the cylinder and rekey it to match your existing Schlage keys in about five minutes. If you don't have Schlage keys, it comes with two standard keys.

**Q: How many user codes can I program?**
You can store up to 19 unique user codes. That's plenty for a family, a house cleaner, or a trusted neighbor — and each code can be deleted individually.

**Q: What happens if the battery dies completely?**
The lock will stop accepting codes but will still operate with the physical key. You can also use the 9-volt battery terminals on the bottom exterior as an emergency power source to enter a code and get in.
