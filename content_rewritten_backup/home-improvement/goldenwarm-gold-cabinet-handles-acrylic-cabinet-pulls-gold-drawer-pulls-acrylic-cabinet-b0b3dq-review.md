Here’s where these surprise you. The acrylic is thick—not flimsy—and the gold finish is electroplated onto the surface, not just painted on. That means it resists chipping and won’t flake off around the screw holes, a common failure point on cheaper metallic pulls. The weight is light, but that’s a benefit: no sagging on particleboard cabinet faces, and less torque on the screws over time.

I’m also impressed by the finish’s resilience. One buyer noted, *“I’ve had these for six months and they still look brand new, no fading or water spots.”* That tracks with my experience. **Where It Falls Short**

Let’s be direct about the downsides. The acrylic is scratch-prone. If you’re rough with them—metal rings, sharp utensils, aggressive cleaning with abrasive pads—you will see fine scratches. They’re more visible on the gold finish than on a brushed texture. One customer noted, *“I dropped a pan on the drawer front and it left a small nick. Metal would have dinked too, but the acrylic shows it more.”*

Second, these are not a match for true brass. If you have high-end, solid brass fixtures, the gold tone won’t match perfectly—it’s a hair cooler and more reflective. You’ll notice the difference if you put them side-by-side. Finally, the light weight might feel insubstantial to some. They aren’t hollow, but they don’t have that satisfying solid-metal thunk when you pull. It’s a minor thing, but worth knowing.
