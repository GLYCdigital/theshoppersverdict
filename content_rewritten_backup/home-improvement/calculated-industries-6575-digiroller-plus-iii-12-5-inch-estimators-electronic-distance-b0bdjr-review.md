# Calculated Industries #6575 DigiRoller Plus III 12.5 Inch: The Measuring Wheel That Does the Math for You

**The Short Version:** If you measure distances for a living—flooring estimates, land surveys, accident reconstruction, or construction takeoffs—the DigiRoller Plus III is the rare tool that pays for itself in the first week. It's not cheap, and it's not for the occasional DIYer. But for professionals who need reliable, repeatable measurements with built-in area and volume calculations, this is the gold standard.

---

## What You Actually Get

The DigiRoller Plus III ships with the measuring wheel itself, a wrist strap, and a protective storage case. The unit measures 12.5 inches in circumference—that's the wheel size, not the device length—and folds down to a compact 31 inches when collapsed. Extended, it reaches 42 inches, which puts the handle at a comfortable height for most users.

The display is the headline feature here. It's a large, backlit LCD that shows distance on the main line with a secondary line for calculations. The keypad is weather-sealed and tactile enough to use with work gloves on. It runs on two AAA batteries, and in my experience, those last through several months of regular fieldwork.

---

## Real-World Performance

The measuring accuracy is where this tool separates itself from the $30 hardware store wheels. The DigiRoller Plus III is accurate to ±0.5%—that's within half a percent over any distance. For a 100-foot measurement, you're looking at a potential error of only 6 inches. That's the difference between ordering the right amount of flooring and eating the cost of an extra box.

What makes it genuinely useful beyond a standard measuring wheel is the built-in calculation engine. You can switch between feet-inches-fractions, decimal feet, yards, meters, and even miles. The area function lets you measure length and width, then automatically computes square footage. The volume function does the same for cubic yards—critical for concrete or fill dirt estimates.

One feature I wasn't expecting to use as much as I do: the count function. When you're measuring repetitive spacing—like fence posts or rebar—you can set the wheel to count each revolution. It's a small thing, but it's the kind of thoughtful design that tells you the engineers actually talked to contractors.

## Where It Falls Short

The price is the elephant in the room. This is a $150+ tool, and for someone who measures distances a few times a year, that's hard to justify. The cheaper alternatives will get you within a few percent accuracy, which is fine for rough estimates.

The folding mechanism, while convenient, is also a potential weak point. A few reviewers reported the locking collar wearing out after extended use. It's not a widespread issue, but it's worth knowing that the moving parts are the most likely failure point.

The manual isn't great. It's dense and written like a technical spec sheet rather than a user guide. Plan on spending 20 minutes figuring out the function shortcuts. Once you've got it, it's intuitive—but the learning curve is steeper than it needs to be.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a professional estimator, contractor, land surveyor, or anyone who measures distances as part of your income. If you're laying out parking lots, calculating roofing squares, or doing accident reconstruction, the accuracy and calculation features are worth every penny.

**Skip it if:** You're a homeowner measuring for a new fence or calculating lawn size. A $40 measuring wheel will get you close enough, and the DigiRoller's advanced features will go unused.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work on uneven terrain?**
A: Yes, within reason. The wheel handles grass, gravel, and packed dirt fine. For rough terrain, go slower—the accuracy depends on maintaining wheel contact with the ground.

**Q: Can it measure curved paths or irregular shapes?**
A: The distance measurement works on any path you can physically roll the wheel along. For irregular area calculations, you'll need to break the shape into rectangles and triangles, which the built-in functions can handle.

**Q: How durable is it really?**
A: The body is impact-resistant ABS plastic with a sealed keypad. It's clearly built for job site use, not office shelves. Just be mindful of the folding collar—that's the one part reviewers report wearing out.
