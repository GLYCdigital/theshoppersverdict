If your water softener has been regenerating more often but softening less, the resin inside is likely exhausted — and the fix is cheaper than a new unit. The Purolite C100E is the workhorse refill that plumbers and DIYers consistently trust, and at 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it's earned that reputation. But is it the right resin for *your* system? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

The bag contains exactly 1.0 cubic foot of premium cation exchange resin — the standard fill for most residential 9" x 48" or 10" x 54" fiberglass tanks. That's roughly 40 pounds of damp, dark amber beads. Purolite doesn't pad the volume with gravel or shipping water; you get the full exchange capacity you paid for.

One thing to note: this is **not** a drop-in kit. There's no underbedding (gravel), no distributor tube, and no resin trap. If you're replacing resin in a tank that's never been opened, you'll need to order a separate gravel pack or reuse the existing underbed.

## Real-World Performance

The C100E uses 8% cross-linked polystyrene — that's the industry standard for durability. Higher cross-linking means the beads resist breaking down from chlorine, iron, and the physical stress of backwashing. I've seen budget resins turn to mush in two years; this one typically lasts 8-10 years in normal conditions.

Capacity is rated at 30,000 grains per cubic foot when regenerated with 9 pounds of salt. That's solid — not the highest on the market, but you'll rarely notice the difference in a typical household. What matters more is that the C100E has excellent kinetics: it grabs hardness ions quickly during service and releases them efficiently during regeneration. That translates to less salt waste and more consistent soft water between cycles.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides? Two things.

First, **no underbedding included**. If you're doing a full tank rebuild, you'll need to source gravel separately. It's a $10-15 add-on, but it's a common point of confusion for first-time DIYers.

Second, **the beads are damp, not dry**. That's normal — resin is shipped with moisture to prevent cracking — but it means the bag is heavier than you might expect, and there can be a few loose beads or a fine dust at the bottom. A quick rinse with a garden hose before loading solves this. Some buyers have also noted the bag can leak slightly in transit if punctured, so inspect it on arrival.

## FAQ

**Q: How often do I need to replace softener resin?**  
Typically every 8-10 years, depending on water hardness, chlorine levels, and iron content. If your softener regenerates but water feels hard again, test your hardness — if it's high while salt usage is normal, it's time.

**Q: Can I mix this with my existing resin?**  
Technically yes, but I don't recommend it. Old resin has reduced capacity and different physical properties. You'll get better results from a full replacement.

**Q: Does this work with all softeners?**  
It's designed for standard residential cation exchange systems. Check your tank's diameter and height — this 1 CF amount fits most 9x48 and 10x54 tanks. For larger systems, you'd need two bags.
