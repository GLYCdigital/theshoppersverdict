The Jet 577126 isn't the flashiest bench grinder on the shelf. It won't light up your shop with LEDs, it doesn't have variable speed, and there's no fancy digital readout. What it does have is a 3/4 HP induction motor, a cast iron base, and two full 1-inch wide wheels — and for most shops, that's exactly what matters.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box you get the grinder with a 36-grit grinding wheel mounted on the left and a wire wheel on the right, both 6 inches in diameter and a full 1 inch wide. That width is the headline feature here. Most bench grinders in this price range ship with 3/4-inch wheels, which makes flat grinding on chisels, plane irons, or scraper edges a frustrating exercise in creating a slight curve. The Jet's wider wheels mean the work stays flat.

The base is cast iron, not stamped steel. That's a real difference you can feel the moment you power it on. The motor housing is also cast, which adds mass where it counts. This unit weighs in around 45 pounds — noticeably heavier than the 30-pound stamped-steel grinders from other brands, and that weight translates directly into less vibration walking across your bench.

## Performance and Build Quality

The induction motor is quiet and starts smoothly. No capacitor hum, no startup hesitation. At 3,450 RPM, it spins the wheels at the speed you expect from a bench grinder, and the 3/4 HP rating gives you enough torque to handle most shop sharpening and deburring without bogging down. I wouldn't use this for heavy stock removal on large pieces of steel, but for tool maintenance and light fabrication, it's got plenty of muscle.

The arbor runs true. Multiple buyers noted their wheels didn't need significant dressing out of the box — one reviewer put it simply: "Spins up smooth, no wobble, balanced right from the start." That's not always the case with grinders in this class, where you sometimes spend your first hour fighting vibration and dressing wheels that were cast off-center.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in buyer feedback is that this grinder punches above its price point on build quality. One long-term owner wrote: "Had mine for two years of weekend shop use. Still runs as smooth as day one. The cast iron base makes a real difference." Another noted the wire wheel's quality specifically: "The wire wheel that comes with it actually holds up — I've stripped paint off old hand tools and it's still throwing bristles evenly."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straightforward about the downsides. There's no work light, which is genuinely annoying in a dim garage shop. You'll need a task lamp or headlamp to see what you're doing, especially when sharpening.

The eye shields are functional but minimal. They deflect the majority of sparks, but larger debris can occasionally arc over the top. Wear safety glasses — these shields are a supplement, not a replacement.

The tool rests are serviceable but need attention. Several buyers mentioned having to re-tighten them after extended sessions. It's a five-second fix, but it's the kind of thing you shouldn't have to think about on a grinder at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

This grinder is for the home hobbyist or serious DIYer who wants a bench grinder that will outlast the project that motivated the purchase. It's ideal for sharpening chisels and plane irons, cleaning up rust on hand tools, and deburring freshly cut metal. It's also a solid choice for a small fabrication shop that needs a reliable secondary grinder.

Skip it if you need a grinder for heavy industrial use, if you require a built-in light, or if you're on a tight budget and willing to accept thinner wheels and more vibration from a cheaper stamped-steel unit.

## FAQ

**Does the 36-grit wheel run true out of the box?**
Most buyers report yes. The arbor is precision-machined, and the wheel typically needs minimal dressing. A few units required light truing, which is normal for any grinder.

**Can I swap the wheels for different grits?**
Yes. Standard 6-inch wheels with 1/2-inch arbor holes fit. The 1-inch width gives you more options than the narrower 3/4-inch wheels found on most competitors.

**Is this suitable for sharpening lathe tools?**
Absolutely. The 1-inch wide wheel is a significant advantage for HSS lathe tools. The 3/4 HP motor maintains speed well under load, and the cast iron base keeps vibration low enough for precise work.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a bench grinder that will still be running true in five years, you value flat grinding on wide wheels, and you don't mind supplying your own work light.

**Skip it if** you're a casual user who grinds once a month, you need built-in lighting, or you're looking for the absolute cheapest option that spins a wheel.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Jet 577126 delivers exactly what a shop grinder should: solid construction, wide wheels, and smooth operation. The missing work light and small shields keep it from a perfect score, but for the core job of grinding and wire brushing, this is one of the best values in its class.
