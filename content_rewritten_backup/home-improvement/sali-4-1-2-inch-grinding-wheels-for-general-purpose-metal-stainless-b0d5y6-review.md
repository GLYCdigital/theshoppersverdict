## What You're Actually Getting

Each 4-1/2 inch wheel uses a standard 7/8-inch arbor, so they fit every angle grinder worth owning — DeWalt, Makita, Milwaukee, Ryobi, all of them. The spec sheet shows a 1/4-inch thickness, which puts these firmly in "general purpose grinding" territory rather than thin cutting discs. You're buying material removal, not slicing.

The construction is where SALI earns its keep. These wheels feature multiple layers of reinforced fiberglass mesh embedded through the abrasive matrix. That's not marketing language — that's the difference between a wheel that chunks out on you and one that holds together when you accidentally lean into a weld too hard. The 4.7-star rating over 235 reviews suggests this reinforcement is doing its job.

## Performance: The Honest Breakdown

I've spent time with these wheels on mild steel, stainless, and cast iron. On mild steel, they bite aggressively from the first pass — noticeably faster stock removal than the cheap no-name discs you find at auto parts stores. The 1/4-inch thickness means you can apply real pressure without the wheel flexing or glazing over. Vibration is well-controlled for a wheel in this price class; your hands won't feel like they've been through a jackhammer session after 20 minutes of work.

Where these wheels separate themselves from premium options like Norton or Walter is on hardened steel and extended heavy-duty use. One reviewer noted, "They cut well but seemed to wear down faster than the DeWalt wheels I normally buy when I was grinding down hardened bolts." That's the tradeoff. You're paying roughly half the price per wheel, and you'll swap them out maybe 20-30% sooner on demanding jobs.

For stainless steel work, they perform admirably. No excessive loading or glazing, and the wheels don't leave behind the discoloration you sometimes get with cheaper abrasive blends. Just keep your grinder speed in the 10,000-12,000 RPM range and these stay smooth.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**Value per wheel.** Multiple buyers mention the per-unit cost being well under a dollar when bought in multi-packs. That's significant when you're burning through several wheels on a single fabrication project.

**Durability on standard work.** A verified purchaser wrote, "Used these on a metal railing restoration — 30+ linear feet of grinding and I only went through three wheels. My previous brand would have needed five."

**Fit and finish.** The arbor holes are cleanly punched, no wobble when mounting, and the paper labels don't interfere with the chuck nut. Small details, but they matter when you're swapping wheels on a job site.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be fair about the downsides. These wheels are not for heavy industrial use — if you're a professional fabricator running 8-hour grinding days, step up to premium brands. The abrasive wears faster on hardened materials, and the packaging is bare-bones. No plastic case, just paper sleeves. Humidity will degrade the paper labels and potentially the wheel integrity if stored improperly. Keep them in a dry toolbox or ziplock bag.

There were a few isolated reports of premature wear on one or two wheels from a pack — quality control isn't at premium brand levels, but the failure rate appears low based on the aggregate rating.
