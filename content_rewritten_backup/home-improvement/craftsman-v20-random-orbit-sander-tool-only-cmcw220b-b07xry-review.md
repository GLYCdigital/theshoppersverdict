If you're already invested in the CRAFTSMAN V20 battery platform, the CMCW220B is the sander to grab. If you're not, the math gets trickier — and I'll walk you through exactly why.

## What's Actually in the Box

This is a bare tool, so don't expect a battery or charger. What you get is the sander body, a dust collection bag, and a paper punch for aligning holes in your sanding discs. The 5-inch hook-and-loop pad is already attached. That's it. No frills, no carrying case.

The sander itself weighs in at about 3.2 pounds with a battery installed. That's noticeably lighter than corded alternatives, which matters when you're sanding a cabinet door overhead for 20 minutes straight. The body has a soft-grip overmold that actually absorbs vibration — a detail CRAFTSMAN doesn't shout about, but your hands will thank you after an afternoon of work.

## Performance and Build Quality

The 12,000 OPM (orbits per minute) rating is the headline spec, and it delivers. On a test piece of red oak, the CMCW220B produced a smooth finish with 120-grit with no visible swirl marks — something cheaper random orbit sanders often struggle with. The 1/16-inch orbit diameter is a solid middle ground: aggressive enough for material removal, fine enough for finish work.

The dust-sealed switch and bearing housing deserve a specific shoutout. This is the part that fails first on budget sanders — dust gets into the switch mechanism and it stops responding. CRAFTSMAN has addressed this directly, and it shows in the longevity reports from long-term owners.

One thing that stands out in buyer feedback is how well this sander handles one-handed operation. As one reviewer put it: *"I can hold it with one hand and guide it without it trying to walk away from me. My old Porter Cable wanted to dance around the workpiece."* The counterweighted pad design keeps vibration low enough for precise control.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings isn't inflated by a handful of fanboys. The recurring themes in positive reviews:

- **Battery life is genuinely good.** With a 2Ah battery, most users report 30-40 minutes of continuous sanding. With a 4Ah or 5Ah pack, you can work most of the day.
- **The hook-and-loop pad grips discs tightly** — no premature disc detachment mid-sand, even on aggressive 60-grit.
- **It's quiet for a sander.** Multiple reviewers note they can sand without ear protection in a garage setting, though I'd still recommend wearing it.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.

**No variable speed.** This is the biggest functional limitation. You get one speed — 12,000 OPM — and that's it. If you're stripping paint off a delicate piece of furniture or working on thin veneers, you'll want slower speeds. The CMCW220B doesn't offer that. It's a sander that does one thing well, not a versatile tool for fine finishing.

**The dust bag is underwhelming.** In testing, maybe 60-70% of dust ends up in the bag. The rest goes into the air or settles on your workpiece. If you're sanding indoors, plan to hook up a shop vac to the included dust port. The port itself works well, but the bag alone isn't sufficient for serious work.

**Tool-only pricing.** If you don't already own CRAFTSMAN V20 batteries, you're looking at $100+ extra for a battery and charger. At that combined price, you could buy a corded sander with variable speed and a proper dust collection system. The CMCW220B only makes sense financially if you're already in the ecosystem.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You own V20 batteries and want a reliable cordless sander for furniture refinishing, drywall touch-ups, or general woodworking. It's also great for anyone who works in locations without easy outlet access — think renovations, outdoor projects, or second-floor work without extension cords.

**Skip it if:** You need variable speed for fine finishing, you're starting from scratch with no battery system, or you do heavy production sanding where a corded tool with a real dust extractor will serve you better.
