If you're standing in the kitchen aisle at a big-box store staring at a $79 sink, this review is for you. The Kraus Dex isn't that sink. It's the upgrade that makes you wonder why you ever considered the cheap one.

The Dex line sits in Kraus's mid-range tier, and the 4.5-star average across 225 Amazon ratings tells you it's doing something right. But ratings don't tell you whether the finish will scratch, whether the basin echoes like a gong, or whether the installation will make you curse. Let's dig into those details.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the sink itself — no faucet, no drain, no mounting clips for some configurations. That's standard for Kraus, but budget shoppers should know the total cost goes beyond the sticker price. You're getting a 16-gauge T304 stainless steel sink, which is the sweet spot for residential use. For reference: 18-gauge is thinner and cheaper, 14-gauge is commercial-grade overkill for most homes. The brushed finish hides water spots and minor scratches far better than mirror-polished alternatives.

## Performance and Build Quality

The first thing you'll notice is the weight. This is a substantial piece of metal — roughly 25 to 30 pounds depending on the exact Dex model you choose. That heft comes from the SoundGuard system: a thick undercoating plus rubber pads that deaden vibration. In real-world testing, dropping a cast-iron skillet produces a dull thud, not a metallic clang. Multiple Amazon reviewers echo this. One wrote, "The sound dampening is unreal. My old sink sounded like a church bell every time I set a dish down. This one is whisper quiet."

The zero-radius corners are a love-it-or-hate-it design choice. They give you noticeably more usable basin space than traditional rounded corners — you can fit a full sheet pan flat against the sides. The tradeoff? Food particles and grime collect in those 90-degree angles. You'll need to angle your sprayer deliberately to flush them out. A reviewer noted, "Looks sleek and modern, but I spend an extra minute each day getting rice out of the corners."

The brushed finish holds up well. After six months of daily use, most owners report no visible scratching from normal dishwashing. One reviewer mentioned, "Had it a year now. Still looks brand new. My kids aren't gentle with it."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The zero-radius corners are the main maintenance headache — they're not dealbreakers, but they require a habit adjustment. The sink also lacks a bottom grid in most configurations, so you'll want to buy a protective rack to prevent glassware from chipping against the stainless base. And if you're installing in a farmhouse or apron-front configuration, confirm you're getting the right mounting type; some buyers have reported confusion between top-mount and under-mount versions.

The biggest complaint across reviews? Shipping packaging. A handful of buyers reported dents and scratches on arrival, which Kraus handles through replacement but still adds friction to the purchase.

## Who Should Buy This

**The home cook who actually uses their kitchen.** If you're boiling pasta, prepping vegetables, and washing heavy cast-iron, the 16-gauge steel and sound dampening are worth every penny. **The remodeler who wants a premium look without the premium price tag.** The Dex delivers that high-end aesthetic at roughly half the cost of comparable Kohler or Blanco models. **Skip it if you're a landlord installing a rental property** — the maintenance demands of the zero-radius corners and the higher upfront cost make a cheaper rounded sink a smarter investment there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the Kraus Dex come with a faucet?**
No. You'll need to purchase a faucet separately. Kraus makes matching models, but any standard kitchen faucet will work.

**Will this fit my existing countertop cutout?**
Measure carefully. The Dex models come in specific dimensions, and the zero-radius corners mean you need a precise rectangular cutout. Kraus provides a template, but if you're replacing an existing sink with rounded corners, you may need to enlarge the cutout.

**How do I clean the zero-radius corners?**
A soft-bristled brush or a bottle brush works best. Avoid steel wool — it will scratch the finish. Most owners report that a daily rinse with the sprayer keeps buildup manageable.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want commercial-grade durability with genuinely effective noise reduction, and you're willing to adapt your cleaning routine to the sharp corners. **Skip it if** you want a zero-maintenance sink or you're working with a tight budget that can't absorb the extra cost of a faucet and drain.

The Kraus Dex earns its 4.6 out of 5. It's a well-engineered sink that delivers where it matters — build quality, noise dampening, and usable space — with only minor maintenance quirks holding it back from perfection. For the price, it's one of the best values in stainless steel kitchen sinks on Amazon right now.
