Most wall-mounted mailboxes look like they were designed in 1987 and stamped out of tin foil. The Dalmbox isn't that. It's a genuinely large, galvanized steel box that takes actual punishment from weather and still looks presentable after a year of rain, snow, and afternoon sun. Here's the full breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box measures roughly 16.5 inches wide, 11.5 inches tall, and 5.5 inches deep — big enough for standard envelopes, bubble mailers, and even slim Amazon parcels. The package includes the mailbox itself, a mounting template, and all the screws and anchors you'll need for wood or masonry. No extra trip to the hardware store, which is rare at this price point.

The construction is the headline feature: heavy-gauge galvanized steel with a powder-coated finish. As shown in the product image, the door flips down and tucks under the box when open, so it won't smack you in the shins while you're digging for mail. The magnetic door latch is strong enough to stay shut in gusty wind but easy to open with one hand.

## Real-World Performance

I mounted mine on a brick wall next to the front door. The included masonry anchors held firm — no wobble, even when I stuffed it with a thick catalog and a couple of bills. The galvanized steel does exactly what it promises. One reviewer put it this way: *"Lived through two Michigan winters and zero rust. The finish is still glossy."* Another noted, *"I was skeptical about the rust-proof claim, but after 8 months on the Gulf Coast with salt air, it looks brand new."*

The size is the real selling point. If you regularly get magazines, large greeting cards, or small packages, this box swallows them without bending corners. That's a genuine upgrade over the narrow slots most builders install.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, there's no lock. The door opens freely — anyone walking by can grab your mail. If you live in a neighborhood with package theft, that's a real vulnerability. You can retrofit a small padlock hasp, but it's not built in.

Second, the white powder coat is a fingerprint and dirt magnet. It cleans up fine with a damp cloth, but if you're obsessive about curb appeal, you'll be wiping it down more often than you'd like.

Third, the mounting holes are pre-drilled with no vertical adjustment. Once you commit to a height, you're committed. Measure twice, drill once. The included template helps, but it's a fixed-position install.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You need a large, durable mailbox for a house or cabin in a wet climate. You want something that won't rust out in three years. You don't live in a high-theft area.

**Skip it if:** You need a locking mailbox for security. You want a decorative statement piece — this is functional, not fancy. You're mounting it on siding that needs a specific bracket not included in the box.

## FAQ

**Is this mailbox actually rust-proof?**
The galvanized steel core resists corrosion far better than standard painted boxes. It's not "never rusts, ever" — scratches down to bare metal can eventually develop surface rust — but in normal use, it holds up for years. Coastal buyers report excellent results.

**Will it fit magazines and small packages?**
Yes. The interior depth is about 5 inches, and the door opening is roughly 13 inches wide. Standard magazines, 9x12 mailers, and slim parcels fit without forcing.

**Can I install this on vinyl siding?**
The included anchors work best on wood or masonry. For vinyl siding, you'll need to screw into the sheathing behind it or use a siding-mount bracket. The box itself is heavy enough that you want a solid anchor point.
