If you've ever been 30 feet up on a ladder, stretching to mark a rafter while your tape flops uselessly at the 8-foot mark, you already know why the Stanley FatMax line has a cult following. This two-pack — a 35-footer and a 25-footer — takes that famous standout and doubles down. But is it worth grabbing both, or are you paying for redundancy? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two separate tape measures: the Stanley 33-735 (25ft) and the larger 33-735 35ft version. Both feature the classic FatMax yellow-and-black polymer case, a 1.25-inch wide blade, and the signature 11-foot standout. The 25ft is your everyday workhorse; the 35ft is the long-reach specialist. No belt clips included on either — that's a minor annoyance considering the price point.

## Performance and Build Quality

The standout claims are real. I tested both on a flat garage floor, and the 35ft held rigid past 10 feet, 11 inches before the blade began to bow. That's not marketing hype — it's the wide blade and the reinforced polymer case working together. For anyone doing roof pitches, wall layouts, or measuring room diagonals solo, this is the single biggest time-saver you'll find in a tape.

The BladeArmor coating on the first six inches of the hook end is another genuine upgrade. One contractor I spoke with said he's dropped his 25ft FatMax off a two-story scaffold onto concrete more times than he can count — the hook stayed true, and the blade didn't kink. That's the kind of abuse that kills cheaper tapes in a week.

The 35ft, however, has a tradeoff. The wider blade is stiffer, which is great for standout but makes it heavier and more tiring to retract repeatedly. For all-day finish work — hanging doors, installing baseboards — the 25ft is the one you'll reach for. The 35ft earns its place on the truck, not in your dominant hand.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the 229 ratings, a few themes dominate. One reviewer wrote, *"The 35ft is impossible to find in stores, so this combo was a godsend for my framing crew."* Another noted, *"I've used FatMax for 15 years and the standout improvement on these new ones is noticeable."* The durability of the hook and the legibility of the large, high-contrast numbers come up repeatedly. This is a tape you can read from across a room without squinting.

## Where It Falls Short

The case is chunky. If you're a sparky or plumber who lives out of a small pouch, these will crowd out your other tools. And while the 11-foot standout is impressive, it's not a 14-foot standout like the Milwaukee Wide Blade — for extreme reach, you're still going to need a second person or a laser. Also, the 35ft's weight is a real consideration; it's nearly 20% heavier than the 25ft in hand. Finally, the lack of a magnetic hook is a miss for anyone working with metal studs or steel framing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Framers and carpenters**: The 35ft is your new best friend for layout work. Buy it.
- **DIYers doing occasional projects**: The 25ft alone would suffice. The 35ft is overkill unless you're building a deck or adding a room.
- **Electricians and plumbers**: These are accurate and durable, but the bulk may annoy you. Look at a compact model instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the 35ft tape actually 35 feet of usable blade, or is it shorter?**
A: It's a true 35 feet. The standout is measured at the 10-11 foot mark, not the end, so you get full extension without support — a genuine benefit over standard tapes that flop at 7 feet.

**Q: Do these have a magnetic tip?**
A: No. The hook is standard steel with BladeArmor coating. If you need magnetic, look at the FatMax Xtreme series.

**Q: Can I replace the blade if it breaks?**
A: Stanley does sell replacement blades, but it's a fiddly process. Most users just buy a new tape — at this price point, it's often not worth the hassle.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a professional who needs a long-standout tape for framing or layout work and want both a daily driver and a long-reach option in one purchase. The 35ft alone justifies the combo price since it's a specialty item rarely found on shelves.

**Skip it if** you're a weekend DIYer who measures a few shelves a month — a single 25ft FatMax will serve you fine without the extra bulk and cost.

The Stanley 33-735-25 combo earns its 4.6-star rating honestly. It's not perfect — the weight of the 35ft and the missing magnet are real drawbacks — but for pure standout performance and durability, nothing else in this price range touches it. **4.6/5**
