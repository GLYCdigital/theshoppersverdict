**The Megaflint keyless deadbolt is the anti-smart lock — and that's exactly why it works.**

Most keyless locks on Amazon are battery-hungry smart devices that demand Wi-Fi, an app, and a firmware update schedule. The Megaflint takes the opposite approach. It's a purely mechanical keypad deadbolt: you punch in a 4-digit code, turn the knob, and you're in. No batteries to die at 11 PM. No app to troubleshoot. No "smart home ecosystem" to integrate.

If that sounds refreshingly simple, it is. But it's not without tradeoffs. Here's the full breakdown after digging through 210 ratings (4.2 stars) and weighing the real-world feedback.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the exterior keypad assembly, the interior thumb-turn unit, a standard deadbolt latch, two keyed cylinders (one for each side of the door), mounting screws, and a template for drilling. The finish is a brushed stainless steel that looks more expensive than the price point suggests. As shown in the product image, the exterior keypad is compact — roughly 3 inches wide — and doesn't dominate the door like some bulkier units.

Installation is genuinely straightforward if you have a 2-1/8" bore hole and a 1" latch hole already drilled. If you're replacing an existing deadbolt, you're looking at a 15-minute swap with a Phillips screwdriver. First-time installers will need a hole saw and a steady hand, but the included template makes the process manageable.

## Performance and Build Quality

The star of the show is the mechanical mechanism. Each keypress rotates a set of internal discs — think of it like a combination lock on a briefcase, but engineered for a door. There's no motor, no solenoid, no electrical contact to corrode. One buyer summed it up: *"I've had smart locks die on me twice. This thing has been on my rental property for 8 months in Texas heat and humidity. Still clicks open every time."*

The zinc alloy body and stainless faceplate handle weather exposure well. Rain, sun, and temperature swings haven't produced visible rust or fading in the feedback I've seen. The keypad buttons themselves have a satisfying tactile click — you know you've pressed them correctly, which matters because there's no backlight. That's worth noting if your porch light is weak.

The interior thumb-turn is smooth and positive. The physical key override works as expected, which is more than you can say for some cheap keypad locks where the key cylinder feels like an afterthought.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The #1 theme in positive reviews is reliability. Multiple long-term owners report the lock hasn't skipped a beat after 6-12 months of daily use. One reviewer noted: *"I bought this for my beach house. Salt air kills everything. This lock hasn't corroded and the code still works perfectly."*

The second most-praised aspect is the lack of maintenance. No battery changes, no recalibration, no connectivity drops. For landlords, Airbnb hosts, or anyone managing a property remotely, this is a massive selling point — you never get a midnight call about a dead battery.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires flagging the limitations. First, there's no auto-lock. The deadbolt only engages when you physically turn the thumb-turn or key. If you're the type who forgets to lock the door, this lock won't save you. Several reviewers mentioned this as a minor annoyance.

Second, you're limited to a single 4-digit code. You can't program separate codes for family members, contractors, or guests. If you need to revoke access, you have to change the code for everyone. That's a real limitation for some use cases.

Third, entry is slightly slower than a smart lock. You must press the code deliberately, then turn the knob. In the dark or with gloves, it takes a few extra seconds. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a lock that will outlast your phone's battery. You manage a rental or vacation property and want zero-maintenance access control. You're replacing an old deadbolt and want keyless entry without the smart-home complexity. You live in harsh weather and need something that won't rust or seize.

**Skip it if:** You need multiple user codes, auto-lock, or remote access. You have mobility issues that make turning a knob difficult — the thumb-turn requires actual rotation, unlike a push-button smart lock.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I change the code if it's mechanical?**
A: Yes. The interior unit has a small reset button. Press it, enter a new 4-digit code, and you're done. Takes about 30 seconds.

**Q: Does it work with a standard door?**
A: It fits standard 2-1/8" bore holes and doors 1-3/8" to 2" thick. If you're replacing an existing deadbolt, it's nearly always a direct swap.

**Q: What happens if I forget the code?**
A: The physical key override always works. Keep a spare key somewhere safe, just like you would with a traditional lock.
