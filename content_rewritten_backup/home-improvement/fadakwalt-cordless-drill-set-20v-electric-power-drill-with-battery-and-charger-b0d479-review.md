Look, I've tested enough budget cordless drills to be skeptical when a brand name sounds like it was assembled from spare syllables. But the FADAKWALT 20V Cordless Drill Set has earned its 4.5-star rating across 225 Amazon reviews. Here's what you're actually getting before you spend your money.

**What's in the box**

This isn't a bare tool situation. The set ships with two 20V lithium-ion batteries, a rapid charger, and a 26-piece accessory kit that includes twist drill bits, driver bits, and socket adapters. As shown in the product image, everything packs into a hard-sided carry case with cutout foam — not the flimsy soft bag most budget sets cheap out with. The case alone would cost you $15-20 separately.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The drill itself has a surprising heft to it — 3.4 pounds with a battery installed. That's not a bad thing. The aluminum gear housing and reinforced nylon body feel like they can take a drop from a ladder. The variable speed trigger gives you decent modulation from 0 to 1,500 RPM, and the 18+1 torque clutch engages cleanly without that dreaded clicking-slip that cheaper drills do when you're driving screws into hardwood.

I drove 3-inch deck screws into pressure-treated lumber without the drill bogging down, and it handled a 1-inch spade bit through 2x4s with modest effort. You're not going to mix mortar with this thing, but for furniture assembly, drywall anchors, shelf installation, and light framing work, it's genuinely capable.

One thing buyers consistently mention: battery life. "I built a full 12x16 deck and only swapped batteries twice," one reviewer wrote. Another noted, "The second battery charges in about 40 minutes, so I never actually stopped working." The 2.0Ah cells aren't massive, but the two-battery system means you're never dead in the water.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The chuck is keyed, not keyless. Every bit change requires you to hunt for that little metal key — and if you lose it, you're stuck. At this price point, a keyless chuck is standard on most competitors, so this feels like a cost cut.

The battery indicator light on the back is optimistic at best. One reviewer put it plainly: "The light says 3 bars, then the drill dies thirty seconds later." It doesn't affect performance, but you can't trust it when you're mid-project.

No belt clip. The drill is compact enough that you'll want to hang it on your ladder or belt, but you'll be buying a third-party clip for $8.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the home DIYer who's tired of borrowing a drill from their neighbor and wants a complete kit that won't break the bank. It's also solid for apartment dwellers assembling IKEA furniture, renters hanging curtains and TV mounts, and first-time homeowners building a starter tool kit.

If you're a contractor using a drill daily on job sites, skip it. You want a brushless motor (this is brushed) and a warranty that covers abuse. But for weekend warriors, the FADAKWALT is more than enough.

**Buy it if:** You need a complete, ready-to-work drill kit for home projects and don't want to piece together accessories separately. The two-battery system and included case make it genuinely good value.

**Skip it if:** You're a pro who needs brushless power, keyless chucks, or a platform with expanding tool compatibility. You'll outgrow this within six months.
