**The tape measure that actually makes fractions readable — and takes a beating doing it.**

Most tape measures treat fractions like an afterthought. Tiny numbers crammed between hash marks, forcing you to squint, guess, and re-measure. CRAFTSMAN looked at that problem and printed the fractions right on the blade — both edges, actually. As shown in the product image, the blade is marked with clear fractions like 1/4, 1/2, and 3/4 directly on the tape, so you're not counting lines or doing mental math on a ladder. For $24, that's a feature that pays for itself in saved time and avoided mistakes.

**What's in the box**

You get the tape measure itself. Nothing fancy — no belt clip included (it has one built in), no extra accessories. Just a 25-foot, 1.25-inch wide blade in a reinforced ABS case. The manual-locking mechanism is a simple thumb slider that holds the blade firmly when engaged. It's straightforward, and after testing, that's exactly what you want from a workhorse tool.

**Real-world performance**

The blade extends a solid 9 feet without buckling — not the 11 or 12 feet you'll get from a $40 FATMAX, but respectable for the price. The standout (the hook at the end) is standard: it slides for inside measurements and has a small hole for hanging on a nail. It grabs drywall and wood studs fine, though I'll note it doesn't bite into metal studs as aggressively as some pricier models.

The case is where CRAFTSMAN clearly invested. The five-point reinforced frame absorbs impacts — I dropped it from shoulder height onto concrete twice and it didn't crack, dent, or misalign the blade. The blade coating resists wear; after two weeks of daily job-site use, the numbers remain crisp and legible.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The fraction markings are the headline feature, and reviewers agree. One buyer put it plainly: "I'm not great at reading tape measures quickly, and this thing makes it so I never have to think about it. The fractions are right there." Another reviewer noted the durability: "Dropped it off a roof, ran it over with a wheelbarrow, and it still reads perfectly." That's the kind of real-world feedback that matters.

**Where it falls short**

The manual lock is my biggest gripe. It works fine, but it requires two hands — one to hold the case, one to slide the lock. If you're used to auto-locking tapes, you'll find yourself fumbling one-handed when you're balancing a level or holding a pencil. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

Also, the fraction markings stop at 1/8-inch increments. For framing, drywall, and general construction, that's plenty. But if you're doing fine woodworking or cabinetry where 1/16 and 1/32 matter, you'll want a tape with those finer markings — this one won't help you there.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners** who want to measure accurately without doing fraction math in their head
- **Framers and rough carpenters** who need a durable tape that survives drops and reads fast
- **Beginners** learning to read a tape measure — the printed fractions are genuinely educational

**Skip it if:**

- You're a finish carpenter needing 1/32-inch precision
- You prefer auto-locking tapes for one-handed operation
- You need a standout that hooks onto metal studs aggressively
