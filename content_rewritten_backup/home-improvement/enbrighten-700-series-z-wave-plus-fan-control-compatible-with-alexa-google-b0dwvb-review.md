**The Short Version: Is This Fan Switch Worth Your Wall Space?**

If you're tired of getting up to pull chains or hunting for a lost remote, the Enbrighten 700 Series Z-Wave Plus Fan Control is a solid upgrade — but it comes with a catch. This is a *smart* switch, not a *Wi-Fi* switch. That distinction matters more than most shoppers realize.

---

**What You Actually Get**

Open the box and you'll find the fan control switch itself, a wire nut assortment, and a quick-start guide. The unit measures a standard 2.4 x 1.6 x 1.1 inches — nothing unusual. The front is a clean, gloss-white paddle with a small LED indicator. No separate faceplate included, which is standard for this category but worth knowing.

The key spec here is the Z-Wave Plus S2 security protocol with an 800-series radio. In plain English: it's got longer range and better encryption than older Z-Wave gear. If you already have a Z-Wave mesh network (via Hubitat, SmartThings, or a USB stick on Home Assistant), this will slot right in.

---

**Real-World Performance: Where It Shines**

Installation is genuinely straightforward. The switch supports both neutral and no-neutral wiring setups — that's a big deal for older homes where you don't have a neutral wire running to the wall box. The included wire nuts are decent quality, and the terminal screws hold stranded wire firmly.

Once powered up, pairing took me about 90 seconds. The switch uses a standard Z-Wave inclusion process — press the paddle three times quickly, and your hub sees it. No QR code scanning like some newer S2 devices require, which is actually a relief.

The fan control itself is the star. Unlike a dimmer that just throttles voltage (which can damage fan motors), this uses a true multi-speed control. You get four speeds: off, low, medium, and high. The low speed is genuinely low — not that annoying "barely spinning" fake low you get from cheap dimmers. It's quiet, the motor responds instantly, and the switch remembers your last speed setting even after power loss.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Scrolling through the 200+ ratings, a few themes pop up repeatedly. One buyer noted, *"Finally a fan switch that doesn't buzz on low. My old one hummed like a refrigerator."* Another mentioned, *"Works perfectly with my SmartThings hub — the speed control is actually intuitive, not buried in menus."*

The reliability of the Z-Wave connection gets consistent praise. Multiple users report the switch holding its mesh connection across two-story homes. That's the benefit of the 800-series radio — it's noticeably more stable than older 500-series devices I've tested.

---

**Where It Falls Short (Honest Talk)**

Here's the thing nobody tells you: **this is not Alexa-compatible out of the box.** The product title says "Compatible with Alexa," but that only works if you have a Z-Wave hub that bridges to Alexa. If you don't already own a hub, you're looking at an extra $50–100 investment. That's a significant hidden cost.

There's also the humming issue. A handful of reviews mention a low buzz at medium speed with certain motor types — particularly DC motors. It's not universal, but it's real. If you have a DC fan, buy from somewhere with a good return policy.

Finally, this switch controls the fan *only*. If your fan has a light kit, you'll need a second switch or a different product. The paddle controls speed, not light. That's a design choice, but it's a limitation worth flagging.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Does this work with a standard 3-speed ceiling fan?**
Yes. It's designed for AC motors with pull-chain speed selectors. Set the fan to high via the chain, then use the switch to control speed. Works reliably.

**Q: Will it pair with my Alexa/Google Home?**
Only through a Z-Wave hub. The hub exposes the switch to Alexa/Google via skill integration. You cannot pair this directly with Alexa.

**Q: Can I install it without a neutral wire?**
Yes. The switch includes a no-neutral configuration, but it requires a compatible hub for that mode. Check your hub's documentation before buying.

**The Verdict**

The Enbrighten 700 Series is a well-engineered Z-Wave fan control that does one thing and does it well. The 800-series radio is a genuine upgrade, the no-neutral support is rare at this price point, and the speed control is smooth and quiet.

But it's not for everyone. The hub requirement is a real barrier, and the DC motor humming issue means you should verify compatibility before tearing open your wall.

**Buy it if:** You have a Z-Wave hub and want rock-solid, local-controlled fan speed without cloud lag.

**Skip it if:** You're expecting plug-and-play Alexa setup or have a DC motor fan.
