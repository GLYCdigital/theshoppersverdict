The M12 FUEL Installation Drill Driver is the tool you buy when you're tired of owning four separate drills for four different jobs. It's a 4-in-1 system that collapses the right-angle driver, offset driver, standard drill, and a quick-release hex driver into one body. And for the most part, it works exactly as advertised.

## What's Actually in the Box

This is the **bare tool version** — no battery, no charger, no case. What Milwaukee gives you is the drill body plus four interchangeable chucks: a standard 3/8-inch keyless chuck, a right-angle chuck, an offset chuck, and a quick-release hex chuck. There's also a chuck wrench and a belt clip. If you're already invested in the M12 battery platform, this is the smart buy. If you're not, factor in roughly $100–$150 for a starter kit before you can actually use it.

## The Real-World Performance

The headline feature here is the **right-angle chuck**. It lets you drive screws into studs and joists where a standard drill physically won't fit — think toe-kicks, cabinet face frames, or electrical boxes between studs. The offset chuck handles the same job with a different geometry, giving you options depending on clearance.

What surprised me is the power. This is a 12V tool, but the M12 FUEL brushless motor punches above its weight class. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've driven hundreds of 3-inch deck screws with the right-angle attachment and it hasn't stalled once. My old 18V drill sits in the drawer now."* Another electrician noted, *"The compact head fits inside a 2x4 wall cavity where my impact driver never could."*

The standard chuck configuration handles typical drilling tasks — I ran 1-inch spade bits through pine studs without bogging down, and 3/8-inch pilot holes in hardwood were effortless. The clutch has 12 positions, which is plenty for cabinet work where over-driving screws strips out soft material.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. **The right-angle and offset chucks add significant length** to the tool. In truly confined spaces — inside a wall cavity or between closely-spaced joists — the added bulk can be the difference between fitting and not. Several reviewers mentioned this exact frustration.

The **chuck swap process** is also slower than it should be. You need the included wrench to loosen a collar, remove the chuck, and attach the next one. It takes about 20–30 seconds per change, which sounds trivial until you're switching between standard and right-angle chucks 15 times in a day. Milwaukee's own M18 version has a tool-free quick-release system; this one doesn't.

And again — **this is a bare tool**. Multiple one-star reviews on Amazon are from buyers who didn't realize no battery was included. Check your cart before checkout.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Electricians and low-voltage installers** who constantly work in tight spaces — this replaces a dedicated right-angle drill
- **Cabinet makers and finish carpenters** who need precision clutch control and compact size for hardware installation
- **DIYers already on the M12 platform** who want one versatile tool instead of three specialized ones

Skip it if you're buying your first cordless tool and don't own any M12 batteries, or if your work is all wide-open framing where a standard drill and impact driver pair covers everything.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this come with a battery?**  
No. The "No Charger, No Battery" in the title means exactly that — you're getting the tool body and chucks only. You need M12 batteries and a charger from a kit or separate purchase.

**Q: Is it powerful enough for drilling through metal?**  
Yes, with the right bits. The brushless motor handles up to 3/8-inch holes in mild steel using step bits or cobalt bits. It won't compete with an M18 for large hole saws, but for electrical boxes and conduit straps, it's plenty.

**Q: How does the right-angle chuck hold up under torque?**  
It's geared down, so you get high torque at low speed. The tradeoff is RPM — you won't be blasting through material. For driving screws and light drilling in tight spots, it's exactly right.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You work in tight spaces regularly, already own M12 batteries, and want one tool that replaces three. The build quality is typical Milwaukee — tough, well-balanced, and the 5-year warranty covers the tool itself.

**Skip it if:** You're starting fresh on the M12 platform (battery costs will sting), or you need the fastest possible chuck changes on a jobsite.
