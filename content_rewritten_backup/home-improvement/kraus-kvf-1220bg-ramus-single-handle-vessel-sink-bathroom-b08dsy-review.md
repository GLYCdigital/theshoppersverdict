The Kraus KVF-1220BG Ramus isn't a faucet you buy on a whim. It's a statement piece for a bathroom where the sink is the centerpiece — a vessel bowl perched on a vanity, demanding hardware that can clear its rim and complement its finish. With 220 ratings and a 4.4-star average, it's clearly doing something right. But is it worth the premium over Kraus's other finishes? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the faucet body, a single-lever handle, a pre-attached 22-inch supply line with a 3/8-inch compression fitting, a brass mounting nut and ring, a rubber gasket, and a plastic deck plate for three-hole installations. Missing from the box: a pop-up drain assembly. That's a $20–40 add-on you'll need to budget for, and it's a common gripe in the reviews.

The faucet itself is solid brass — not the zinc alloy you'll find at half the price. The PVD brushed gold finish is the star here. It's applied via physical vapor deposition, which means it's bonded at the molecular level rather than painted on. Multiple buyers who've owned it for 6–12 months report zero peeling or tarnishing, even with daily use and hard water.

## Performance and Build Quality

The Ramus stands 14.5 inches tall from the deck to the spout outlet, with a 6.5-inch reach. That height is deliberate — it's designed to clear vessel bowls that typically sit 4–6 inches above the counter. The water stream lands centered in most standard 16-inch round or 18-inch rectangular vessels, though you'll want to measure your sink's interior depth. If your bowl is shallower than 4 inches, you'll get splashing. That's physics, not a defect.

The ceramic cartridge is smooth and precise. Temperature and flow adjust with a quarter-turn of the lever, and there's no wobble or play in the handle. Owners consistently mention the drip-free performance after months of use. One reviewer noted: "Installed in March, zero drips by October. The finish still looks like day one despite daily toothbrushing splatter."

The aerator is a nice touch — it produces a laminar, non-splashing stream rather than the spray you get from cheaper faucets. It also has a flow rate of 1.2 gallons per minute, which meets WaterSense standards and keeps water bills in check.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 ratings, several themes emerge:

- **Finish durability**: The PVD brushed gold resists fingerprints, water spots, and cleaning products. This is the #1 reason people choose this over cheaper gold-look faucets.
- **Build weight**: At nearly 7 pounds, it feels substantial. You won't get that hollow, cheap resonance when you tap the body.
- **Easy installation**: The pre-attached supply line and clear instructions make it a 45–60 minute DIY job for most. One buyer said: "Took longer to unpack than to install. The mounting hardware is beefy and didn't strip."

## Where It Falls Short

The honest downsides:

- **Price premium**: The brushed gold version costs notably more than the chrome or matte black equivalents. If you're flexible on finish, save the money. If gold is non-negotiable, accept the upcharge.
- **No drain included**: This is a genuine annoyance at this price point. Kraus sells a matching pop-up drain separately, but it adds another $30–40.
- **Height limitations**: At 14.5 inches tall, this faucet won't fit under a mirror or medicine cabinet mounted low. Measure your clearance before buying.
- **Water spots on the gold**: While the PVD finish resists fingerprints, hard water can leave mineral deposits that require regular wiping. It's not self-cleaning.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if**: You have a vessel sink (or are installing one), you want a premium gold finish that will actually last, and you're comfortable with a DIY install. It's ideal for a guest bath or powder room where aesthetics matter.

**Skip it if**: You have a standard drop-in or undermount sink — the height is overkill. Also skip if you're on a tight budget; you can get a functional vessel faucet for half the price, but you'll sacrifice the brass body and finish quality.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this faucet fit a standard 3-hole sink?**
A: Yes, it includes a deck plate that covers all three holes, converting it to a single-hole mount. If you have a 1-hole sink, just skip the plate.

**Q: What's the spout reach?**
A: 6.5 inches from the center of the base to the water stream. For a standard 16–18 inch vessel bowl, the stream lands dead center. For larger bowls, you may need to position the faucet slightly forward.

**Q: Is the brushed gold finish durable?**
A: Yes. It's PVD-coated, which is the same process used on high-end kitchen fixtures. Multiple long-term reviewers confirm no peeling or fading after 6–12 months.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Kraus KVF-1220BG Ramus is a well-engineered, visually striking faucet that delivers on its premium promise. The brass construction, PVD finish, and smooth cartridge justify the price for anyone building a vessel-sink bathroom. The missing drain and height limitations are real considerations, but they're not dealbreakers for the right application.

**Buy it if** you want a gold finish that won't look cheap in six months. **Skip it if** you're installing over a standard sink or need to stay budget-conscious. For its intended use, it's one of the best options in its class.
