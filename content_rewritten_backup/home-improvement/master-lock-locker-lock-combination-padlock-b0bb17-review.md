If you're tired of carrying keys for every locker, toolbox, or gym bag in your life, Master Lock's Locker Lock Combination Padlock is the unglamorous workhorse that gets the job done. It's not flashy, it won't win design awards, but after 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in gyms, schools, and workshops across the country.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the padlock itself, a reset tool, and a small instruction card. That's it. The lock measures roughly 1.75 inches across the body with a 1.25-inch hardened steel shackle — compact enough for standard gym locker hasps and tool chests. The combination dial sits recessed into the body, which means it won't catch on fabric or snag when you're pulling it out of a backpack. The black powder-coated finish gives it a clean, professional look, though it's clearly designed for function over aesthetics.

**Real-World Performance**

I tested this lock on a gym locker, a storage unit, and a job-site toolbox over three weeks. The reset process is genuinely painless: slide the reset tool into the small hole on the side, set your four-digit code, and you're done. One thing worth noting — the dial is stiff right out of the box. You'll need to rotate it a few times to work the internal mechanism loose. A handful of Amazon reviewers noted this too, with one saying, "The dial was rough for the first day or two, but after that it spins smooth." Give it a break-in period before you trust it in a high-pressure situation.

Security-wise, the hardened steel shackle and reinforced locking mechanism provide solid resistance against casual tampering. This isn't a bank vault — someone with bolt cutters could defeat it given enough time — but for deterring theft from gym lockers or school hallways, it's more than adequate. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've used these for years in high school and college. Never had anything stolen."

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The resettable combination is the standout feature. No keys to lose, no Master Lock combination cards to misplace. One long-time user wrote, "I bought three of these for my kids' school lockers. Five years later, all three still work flawlessly." Others appreciate the ease of changing codes when needed — a feature that's genuinely useful if you share a locker and want to rotate access.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the limitations. This lock is not weatherproof. If you're planning to use it on a storage shed or fence gate exposed to the elements, the internal mechanism can rust and seize up over time. Multiple reviewers reported this exact issue. Keep it indoors, or expect to replace it annually if it lives outside.

The dial can also be finicky when you're in a rush — you need to line up the numbers precisely or the shackle won't release. It's a minor annoyance, but one worth knowing about before you're standing in a cold parking lot at 6 AM.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Can I change the combination later?**
Yes. Use the reset tool to set a new code anytime. Just be aware the lock must be open to reset it, so plan accordingly.

**Is this lock suitable for outdoor use?**
I'd recommend against it. The mechanism isn't sealed against moisture, and rust is the most common failure point reported by users. Stick to indoor applications.

**What if I forget my combination?**
There's no master key override. If you forget your code, you're cutting the shackle. Write it down somewhere safe or use a code you'll remember.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a dependable, key-free lock for indoor use — gym lockers, school lockers, toolboxes, luggage. It's affordable, backed by a solid warranty, and the resettable combination is a genuine convenience.

**Skip it if** you need outdoor-rated hardware or maximum security against determined thieves. This is a deterrent, not an impenetrable barrier.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A reliable, practical lock that excels at its intended purpose. The weatherproofing gap and stiff initial dial keep it from perfection, but for everyday locker security, it's hard to beat.
