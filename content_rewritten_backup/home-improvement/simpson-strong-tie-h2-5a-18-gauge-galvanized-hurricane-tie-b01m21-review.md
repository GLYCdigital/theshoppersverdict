**The H2.5A is the connector you stop thinking about after installation—which is exactly the point.**

If you're tying rafters to wall plates in a high-wind zone, this isn't the sexy part of the build. It's the part that keeps your roof attached when the weather gets serious. With 235 Amazon ratings averaging 4.7 stars, the Simpson Strong-Tie H2.5A has earned its reputation. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Each box contains 50 galvanized steel connectors, each measuring 1.5 inches in seat depth—the full rafter width. The 18-gauge G90 galvanized steel is stamped with the Simpson logo and the H2.5A designation, so inspectors can verify it at a glance. No fasteners included, so budget for Simpson's recommended 10d or 16d nails separately. The G90 coating means 90 grams of zinc per square meter, which is the industry baseline for exterior exposure. In coastal applications, consider the stainless steel version, but for most residential work, this is sufficient.

## Performance and Installation Reality

The H2.5A is designed to resist both uplift and lateral forces on rafter-to-wall connections. With the full complement of 10 nails, it's rated for 475 pounds of uplift—that's code-compliant in most hurricane-prone regions. The nail holes are pre-punched and align cleanly with standard 2x framing. One Amazon reviewer noted, "Went on fast with a palm nailer. No bending, no fighting the metal. Exactly what I needed for 40+ connections."

The seat depth is the differentiator here. The shallower H2.5 variant is a common substitute, but it doesn't fully engage the rafter, which reduces the load rating. The H2.5A's full seat means you get the maximum rated performance without having to calculate derating factors. For a few cents more per connector, it's the smarter choice.

The pattern in customer feedback is consistent: ease of installation and structural confidence. A contractor reviewing the product wrote, "Used these on a 2,400-square-foot roof in Florida. ## Where It Falls Short

The main gripe: you need all 10 nails for the rated load. Some buyers install with fewer fasteners and assume they're fine. They're not. The load rating assumes full nailing, and inspectors know to check. If you're in a high-wind zone, do it right the first time.

Also, this is not a truss-to-wall connector. The H2.5A is specifically for rafter-to-wall plate connections. If your framing plan calls for truss connections, you need a different Simpson product (like the H2.5T or H10A). Several reviewers made this mistake and had to return the box. Read your plans before ordering.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use these with pressure-treated lumber?**
A: Yes, but use stainless steel or hot-dip galvanized fasteners. The G90 coating is fine for the connector itself, but standard galvanized nails can corrode when in contact with treated lumber chemicals.

**Q: How many nails do I really need?**
A: For the full 475-pound uplift rating, all 10 nails. Simpson's load tables are based on complete nailing. Using fewer reduces capacity significantly.

**Q: Will these work with 2x6 rafters?**
A: Yes, the 1.5-inch seat depth matches standard 2x lumber. The connector is sized for the rafter width, not the depth.
