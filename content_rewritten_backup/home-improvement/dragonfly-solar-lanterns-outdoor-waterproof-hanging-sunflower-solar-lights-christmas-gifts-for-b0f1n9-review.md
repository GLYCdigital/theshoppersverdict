## What You're Actually Getting

Open the box and you'll find the hanging sunflower lanterns fully assembled — no tools, no frustration. Each unit has a metal stem hook that's substantial enough to hang from a pergola, fence, or tree branch without bending. The sunflower head measures roughly 6 inches across with a solar panel embedded in the center. As shown in the product image, the petals are a bright yellow plastic that catches light well during the day.

The battery compartment sits below the flower head, sealed with a rubber gasket. That gasket is why these qualify as truly waterproof rather than just "splash resistant" — the seal is snug, and one buyer noted, "Left mine out through two Nor'easters and they've never flickered once."

## Real-World Performance

Here's where these Dragonfly lanterns earn their keep. The warm amber LED inside the sunflower face creates a soft, honey-toned glow that actually looks like a lantern, not a cheap flashlight. In a dark garden, they cast enough light to define walkways and patio edges without blinding anyone sitting nearby.

Battery life is the standard solar playbook: full sun all day gets you 6-8 hours of evening glow. The auto on/off sensor is reasonably responsive — it triggers at genuine dusk, not when a cloud passes overhead. One reviewer captured it well: "They come on like clockwork at sunset and I don't think about them again until I see them glowing."

The build quality surprised me in a good way. The metal stem has real heft, and the hook mechanism is welded, not glued. This isn't the flimsy wire-and-plastic construction that usually sinks these seasonal solar products.

## Where They Fall Short

Let's be straight about the limitations. The solar panel is small — it's the center of the sunflower, after all — so you're completely dependent on direct sun. Shade a spot under a dense tree canopy and you'll get maybe two hours of weak light. One customer put it plainly: "Works great in my open yard, but the ones under my porch are basically decorations."

The plastic petals will fade. In high-UV climates (Arizona, Florida, Texas), expect noticeable yellowing after 8-12 months. They won't crack or break easily, but the color shift is real. And the battery compartment switch is positioned awkwardly — you'll need small fingers or a tool to flip it once the lantern is hung at height.

## Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Will these survive a full winter outdoors?**
Yes, but bring them inside if you can. The electronics are sealed, but extreme temperature swings can shorten battery life. Indoor storage over winter will extend their lifespan significantly.

**Q: How long do the batteries last before replacement?**
Most buyers report 1-2 seasons before noticeable dimming. The battery compartment is standard-size, so replacements are cheap and easy to find.

**Q: Can these be used indoors?**
Technically yes, but it's pointless — they need sunlight to charge. If you want the look indoors, put them near a south-facing window during the day.
