If you're tired of flickering fluorescent troffers that hum louder than your HVAC system, the FT Center LED panel is worth a serious look. This 2x2 drop-ceiling light skips the ballast headaches entirely and adds three brightness levels and three color temperatures in a single fixture. After combing through 235 ratings (4.7 stars), here's the real story for shoppers.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the LED panel itself, torsion springs pre-installed on the back, and a wire nut kit for hardwiring. No remote, no dimmer switch, no separate driver box — it's all integrated. The fixture measures a standard 23.8 x 23.8 inches, so it drops into any existing 2x2 ceiling grid. At 40W it pushes roughly 4,000 lumens, which is comparable to a 2-lamp fluorescent troffer but without the 15-minute warm-up or the buzz.

**The switch that makes this different**

Before you mount it, flip the two small switches on the back. One selects wattage (20/30/40W), the other picks your color temp (3000K warm, 4000K neutral, 5000K daylight). This is genuinely useful if you're outfitting a basement with mixed zones — 20W warm for a media corner, 40W daylight for a workbench. The tradeoff: once it's clipped into the grid, changing settings means pulling the whole panel down. Plan ahead.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average is backed by recurring themes. One reviewer wrote, "Replaced four fluorescent troffers in my garage — these are brighter, instant-on, and my electric bill dropped noticeably." Another noted, "The 5000K setting makes my workshop feel like daylight; no flicker even on my cheap dimmer." Multiple buyers mention how thin the profile is — about 1.5 inches — making it look cleaner than bulb-style retrofit kits.

**Where it falls short**

Three honest complaints surface repeatedly. First, the color/wattage switches being buried on the back is a real annoyance if you're indecisive. Second, at 40W in a dead-silent room, the driver emits a faint but noticeable hum — not deal-breaking for a garage or hallway, but annoying in a bedroom. Third, the back isn't enclosed, so if your local inspector is strict, you'll need to add a junction box for the wiring connections. That's an extra $8 and 15 minutes.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIY homeowners**: If you're comfortable with basic wiring (black, white, ground), this is a 20-minute install. Torsion springs hold it tight against the grid.
- **Garage/workshop owners**: The 40W daylight setting is excellent for detail work. Buy two and you'll light a two-car garage evenly.
- **Landlords or flippers**: The wattage switch lets you install one SKU across different room sizes — simplifying your ordering.

**Skip it if** you need a fully enclosed fixture for a damp location, or if you demand silent operation at max brightness in a bedroom.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with standard 2x4 drop ceilings?**
A: No, it's specifically sized for 2x2 grids. For 2x4 layouts, you'd need two units or a different model.

**Q: Can I use it on a dimmer switch?**
A: It's not rated for dimmers. Use a standard on/off switch — the color/wattage switches handle brightness control.

**Q: How do I wire it if I'm replacing a fluorescent troffer?**
A: Disconnect the ballast wiring, connect your line voltage (120V) directly to the fixture's black/white wires, and ground it. The driver is built in.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a bright, flicker-free troffer with flexible output and color options for under what a single fluorescent ballast replacement costs. **Skip it if** you need silent operation at max wattage or a fully enclosed back for code compliance.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely useful upgrade for drop-ceiling spaces, held back only by switch accessibility and a minor hum at top output.
