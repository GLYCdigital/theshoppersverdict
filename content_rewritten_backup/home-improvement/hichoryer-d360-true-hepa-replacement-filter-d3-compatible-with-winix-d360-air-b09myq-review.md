## What's in the box

Open the box and you get the main HEPA filter — a rigid, pleated unit wrapped in a thin activated-carbon layer — plus a separate washable pre-filter sheet that wraps around the outside. The dimensions match the Winix D360/D480 and 1150 series exactly, and the plastic frame is sturdy enough that you won't feel like you're installing a flimsy piece of cardboard.

The HEPA media itself is the real deal. Lab-tested at H13 grade, it claims 99.97% capture efficiency at 0.3 microns — the same spec as the OEM part. The carbon layer is pre-attached, so you don't have to fiddle with two separate components.

## Real-world performance

I tested this filter in a Winix D360 running continuously for three weeks in a 300-square-foot room with a dog and moderate dust levels. Airflow remained consistent — no whistling, no restriction that would make the fan strain. The pre-filter caught the visible dust bunnies, and the HEPA layer handled the invisible stuff.

What buyers consistently report matches my experience. One reviewer noted, *"Fits like the original, works perfectly — I honestly can't tell the difference from the Winix brand."* Another echoed: *"Half the price, same performance. I've bought three so far."* The consensus is that it's a genuine alternative, not a compromise.

The washable pre-filter is a smart touch. Every two weeks, I rinsed it, let it air-dry, and popped it back on. That alone should stretch the main HEPA element past the typical 12-month replacement cycle.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the carbon layer: it's thin. If you're running this in a kitchen or near a smoker, it will not handle odors the way a standalone carbon filter would. The HEPA part excels; the carbon part is a token gesture. For most living rooms and bedrooms, that's fine. For heavy odor environments, budget for a separate carbon filter or stick with OEM.

There's also no built-in replacement reminder. The OEM filter has that little tab that flips when it's time to swap. Here, you're on your own — set a calendar reminder at 12 months or when you notice airflow dropping.
