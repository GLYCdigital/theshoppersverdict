If you've ever stared at a box of recessed lights wondering which color temperature to commit to, the FREELICHT 4/6 Inch 5CCT LED Can Lights solve that with a simple toggle. But the real headline here is the integrated night light — a feature that sounds gimmicky until you actually need to stumble to the bathroom at 2 AM without blinding yourself.

I've tested dozens of retrofit LED can lights, and this one earns its 4.6-star rating from 229 buyers for a specific reason: it does two jobs with one fixture, and does both competently.

## What You Get

Each unit ships as a complete retrofit kit. You get the LED module, a twist-lock adapter ring, and a friction clip system that grips the inside of your existing recessed housing. No junction box wiring required — this is a direct replacement for your old bulb-and-trim setup.

The fixture accepts both 4-inch and 6-inch housings thanks to adjustable spring clips, which makes it a solid choice if you're swapping fixtures across multiple rooms with different can sizes. The trim is a clean, modern white that sits nearly flush against the ceiling, giving a low-profile look that doesn't collect dust.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Let's start with the primary light. The 5CCT switch on the side of the housing gives you 2700K (warm), 3000K (soft white), 3500K (neutral), 4000K (cool), and 5000K (daylight). That's the full range you'd need for anything from a cozy living room to a functional garage. Output is rated at 800 lumens for the 4-inch and 1100 lumens for the 6-inch — both sufficient for general room lighting.

The CRI is rated at 90+, which means colors render accurately. If you're lighting a kitchen where you prep food or a bathroom where you apply makeup, this matters. Skin tones look natural, not washed out or overly warm.

The night light mode is the standout feature. It runs at roughly 20% brightness with a warm, amber-ish glow that's enough to navigate by but not enough to disrupt your circadian rhythm. It's genuinely useful in hallways, kids' rooms, and bathrooms. The tradeoff: the night light activates via the wall switch, so you can't have the main light off and the night light on independently unless you're using a smart switch or dimmer that supports that logic.

## What Buyers Praise

The dominant theme in customer feedback is the ease of installation. One reviewer noted, "Took me 10 minutes to swap out all six of my old can lights. The twist-lock mechanism is foolproof." Another pointed out that the color temperature selection saved them from making multiple purchases: "I set 2700K in the bedroom and 4000K in the kitchen from the same box."

The night light feature gets consistent shout-outs. A verified buyer wrote, "The night light in the hallway is perfect — bright enough to see, dim enough to not wake me up fully."

## Where It Falls Short

The color temperature switch placement is the most common friction point. It's on the fixture body, which means you have to pull the light down from the ceiling to change it. Plan ahead — set your temperature before you lock the trim in place.

The night light limitation is worth repeating: it's tied to the wall switch. If you want independent control, you'll need to integrate this with a smart home system or accept the single-switch behavior.

Finally, the 4-inch version runs slightly warmer than the 6-inch at the same color temperature setting. It's subtle, but if you're mixing sizes in the same room, you might notice a minor mismatch.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're retrofitting recessed cans and want flexibility on color temperature, or you need low-level nighttime lighting in hallways, bathrooms, or kids' rooms. The dual-function design replaces two separate fixtures.

**Skip it if:** You require independent control of the night light, or if you're installing in a ceiling with unusual depth — the friction clips need a standard can housing to grip properly.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a special dimmer for these lights?**
A: They're rated as dimmable, but you'll want an LED-compatible dimmer. Standard trailing-edge dimmers work best. If you see flicker, swap the dimmer first.

**Q: Can I use these in insulated ceilings?**
A: Yes, they're IC-rated for direct contact with insulation. The housing is airtight, so no heat buildup concerns.

**Q: What's the lifespan?**
A: Rated at 50,000 hours. At average usage of 3 hours per day, that's roughly 45 years. The LED module is replaceable in the unlikely event of failure.
