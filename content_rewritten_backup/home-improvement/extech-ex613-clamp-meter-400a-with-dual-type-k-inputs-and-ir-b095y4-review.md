If you're an HVAC tech or commercial electrician, you already know the pain of carrying three tools to do one job — a clamp meter, a thermocouple thermometer, and an IR gun. The Extech EX613 tries to collapse all three into a single hand-friendly package. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The EX613 ships with the meter, a rubber holster, two Type K bead thermocouples, and a carrying case. The clamp opens to about 1.2 inches — enough for most 4/0 AWG wire but tight for parallel feeds. The dual Type K inputs are the headline feature: you can plug in both probes and read T1, T2, or the differential (T1-T2) directly on the display. That's not a gimmick — it's the difference between calculating superheat manually and just reading it.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The 400A AC/DC range covers residential and light commercial panels comfortably. True RMS means readings stay honest with variable-frequency drives and other non-sinusoidal loads — a place cheap averaging meters lie to you. The 0.01A resolution on the 40A range is genuinely useful for idle current draw checks on motors. Accuracy is rated at ±1.5% for AC current and ±2% for DC — solid for troubleshooting, not lab-grade calibration work.

The IR thermometer on the jaw is the convenience play. Point it at a breaker, get a surface temp read without breaking the circuit. But here's the catch: the 8:1 distance-to-spot ratio. At 8 inches away, you're measuring a 1-inch circle. For small components like fuses or tight breaker lugs, you need to be within 3 inches or you'll average in ambient temps. The laser guide helps, but it's not a substitute for a dedicated IR gun with a 12:1 or better ratio.

The backlit display is legible in dim mechanical rooms, though it's single-stage only — no dimmer. The dual thermocouple readings display simultaneously with the current or voltage measurement, which is a thoughtful touch for monitoring a motor's amp draw while watching its winding temp climb.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reviewers across the board hammer on the dual K inputs. One buyer put it plainly: *"Being able to see T1-T2 differential without doing mental math on a rooftop unit saved me real time."* Another noted the build quality: *"Dropped it off a ladder onto concrete — still calibrates fine."* The battery life also gets consistent love; the auto-off feature and low-power draw mean the included 9V lasts through heavy seasons.

**Where It Falls Short**

The IR thermometer is the weak link. It's a "good enough" tool, not a precision instrument. If you're checking for hot spots on a 200A busbar from a safe distance, the 8:1 ratio means you'll get vague averages. You'll want a dedicated IR gun for that.

There's no magnetic mount on the holster. For a meter aimed at HVAC techs who work alone on ladders, that's an odd omission. You'll be juggling it while you handle probes. The included thermocouples are bead-type — fine for air temp and surface contact, but you'll want a pipe clamp probe for accurate refrigerant line temps. That's an extra purchase.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **HVAC technicians** doing residential or light commercial service — the dual K inputs and differential temp reading alone justify the price
- **Electricians** who occasionally need temp data during panel work and want to drop one tool from the bag
- **Facilities maintenance staff** covering multiple trades with limited budget

**Skip it if** you're a refrigeration specialist doing critical superheat/subcooling on large systems — you'll want better thermocouple options and a higher-accuracy IR gun. Also skip if you need heavy-duty catenary clamps for busbar work.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can it measure inrush current for motor starts?**
A: No — this is not a peak-hold or inrush-capable meter. It reads continuously, so you'll catch running current, not startup spikes.

**Q: Does it come with a carrying case?**
A: Yes, a zippered soft case plus a rubber holster. The case has room for the probes and the meter, but it's snug with both thermocouples attached.

**Q: Is the dual temperature reading simultaneous with the clamp reading?**
A: Yes. The display shows the primary electrical measurement and both T1 and T2 (or the differential) at the same time.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuine all-in-one diagnostic tool for HVAC and electrical troubleshooting and you're willing to live with the IR's limited reach. **Skip it if** you need precision IR measurement or primarily work on high-amperage commercial gear.

The EX613 earns a 4.2/5. It's not the best at any single function, but it's the best at combining three jobs into one rugged, field-tested package — and for the tech who carries their own bag, that's real value.
