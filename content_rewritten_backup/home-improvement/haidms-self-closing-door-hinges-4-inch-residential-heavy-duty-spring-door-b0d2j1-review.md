# Haidms Self Closing Door Hinges: The 4-Inch Spring Hinge That Actually Works

Most self-closing hinges are either flimsy stamped steel that bends in a month or industrial monsters that slam your door like a jail cell. The Haidms 4-inch residential grade version sits somewhere smarter — in the middle. And after digging through 225+ ratings and the engineering specs, here's what you need to know before buying.

## What You're Actually Getting

This is a 4-inch (101.6mm) hinge with a built-in spring mechanism — no separate door closer arm, no ugly hydraulic cylinder mounted to your frame. Each hinge is zinc alloy construction with a matte finish that looks like brushed nickel or oil-rubbed bronze depending on the variant you pick. The key spec: an adjustable tension spring controlled by a hex key (included) on the hinge's side pin.

You get three hinges per set, which is the correct number for a standard 80-inch residential door. The pin is non-removable, which is a double-edged sword that I'll get to in the cons.

## Performance in Real Homes

The spring mechanism does its job quietly — no loud pneumatic hiss, no metal-on-metal squeak. One buyer noted, "Installed these on my basement door and the spring tension was perfect out of the box. Door closes smoothly every time without slamming." That tracks with the design: the tension adjustment range is generous enough to handle everything from a hollow-core bedroom door to a heavier 1¾-inch pre-hung unit.

The hinge barrels rotate on what feels like a hardened steel pin, and the leaf thickness (roughly 2.5mm) gives it a solid, non-flexing feel when the door is mid-swing. The finish resists fingerprints better than most brass hinges I've tested.

## What 225+ Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming theme in positive reviews is simplicity — no door closer arm, no drilling extra holes, no hydraulic fluid to leak. One verified purchase said, "Replaced three old hinges with these and the door now closes itself perfectly. Took 15 minutes total." Another highlighted the adjustability: "Being able to dial in the tension with the included hex key was huge. My door doesn't slam, it just gently closes."

The left/right hinge compatibility is also a genuine selling point — you don't need to figure out your door handing before ordering. Both configurations work out of the box.

## Where It Falls Short (Honest Tradeoffs)

The spring tension has a ceiling. If you're hanging a solid oak door or anything over roughly 20 pounds, these hinges won't consistently pull it shut — you'll need a traditional surface-mounted door closer. One reviewer flagged this directly: "Worked great on my hollow door, but my solid pine door needs a push to close fully."

The included screws are the weakest link. Several buyers reported stripping them when driving into hardwood jambs. Replace them with quality #8-32 screws and you'll avoid the headache. Also — the non-removable pin means you can't lift the door off the hinges for painting or removal without unscrewing the hinge plates entirely.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You have standard residential interior doors (hollow-core or light solid), you want automatic closing without an ugly arm on the frame, or you're installing in a rental or Airbnb where guests never close doors properly.

**Skip them if:** You have heavy solid-core doors, commercial-grade traffic (they're rated residential only), or you need quick door removal for frequent access. For those cases, a traditional hinge with a separate spring closer is the better call.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Will these work on a 36-inch wide door?**
Yes, but only if the door is standard weight (under 20 lbs). The 4-inch size is correct for 32-inch and 36-inch residential doors — just make sure your door isn't solid-core.

**Q: Can I adjust the tension after installation?**
Yes. The hex key adjusts a screw on the side of the hinge knuckle. Loosen to reduce tension, tighten to increase. Turn the screw in small increments — a quarter turn makes a noticeable difference.

**Q: Do these work on metal door frames?**
They can, but use the included screws only if your frame is wood. For metal frames, you'll need self-tapping machine screws — the included ones are wood screws.
