If you've ever fought with string lights that tangle into a knot the size of a grapefruit, these curtain lights from the 16 Colors Changing line will feel like a small miracle. The drops come pre-spaced and vertically aligned on two separate panels, so you're not untangling anything — you're just hanging and plugging in.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains two curtain panels, each measuring roughly 5 feet wide by 6.5 feet tall, with 8 drops per panel and 10 bulbs per drop. That's 160 individual LEDs total, which covers a standard sliding glass door or large bedroom window completely. You also get the 12V adapter, the IR remote, and mounting clips — though honestly, the clips are the weakest part of the package. They're the same thin plastic hooks you've seen on a hundred other light sets. For outdoor installation or anything permanent, budget for Command hooks or small nails instead.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The headline feature is the 16-color palette, and it delivers. You're not getting 16 shades of the same hue — the controller actually mixes RGB values, so the "orange" is genuinely orange and the "pink" isn't just washed-out red. The 7 modes cycle through static colors, fading, flashing, and a twinkle mode that looks surprisingly organic, not like a strobe light at a rave.

The remote is refreshingly simple: color buttons on the left, mode buttons on the right, brightness up/down, and a timer button. It worked from across my living room (about 18 feet) and through a bedroom door. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical about the remote since it's IR, but it works from across the room perfectly. No complaints."

The 12V adapter runs cool even after 6+ hours of use, which matters more than most people think — cheap LED sets often run hot adapters that become fire hazards in dry rooms. That's not an issue here.

**Where It Falls Short**

The honest tradeoffs: the wires are thin. Not dangerously so, but the soldered joints where drops connect to the main bus are fragile. One reviewer reported, "One of the drops stopped working after two weeks — the connection point came loose when I was adjusting the height." If you're installing these permanently, don't pull on the drops to adjust them. Unhook and re-hook instead.

The timer resets if the power blips. It's a minor annoyance, but if you're relying on the timer to turn them off at 11 PM nightly, expect to reprogram it after every storm or outage.

**Who Should Buy These**

These are ideal for three specific use cases:

- **Apartment dwellers** who want ambient window lighting without permanent installation — no drilling required, just hanging clips.
- **Event hosts** who need fast, colorful backdrop lighting for parties or holiday gatherings — the 12V adapter is safe for indoor use, and the remote makes switching scenes effortless mid-party.
- **Anyone covering a large window on a budget** — 160 LEDs for under $25 is genuinely good value. A comparable set with this color range and remote typically runs $35–$45.

Skip these if you need outdoor-rated lights (these are indoor-only), or if you're looking for smart-home integration — there's no Wi-Fi, no Alexa, no app. The remote is the only control method.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be shortened?**
A: Yes, but carefully. Each drop is a separate circuit, so you can snip individual drops at the main bus wire. Just cap the exposed end with electrical tape or heat shrink. Don't cut the main bus itself.

**Q: How long is the power cord?**
A: The adapter cord is about 6 feet, which is enough for most ceiling-to-outlet runs. If you need longer, use a standard extension cord — the adapter draws minimal wattage.

**Q: Do they work with a dimmer switch?**
A: No. The brightness is controlled only via the remote. Plugging them into a wall dimmer will cause flickering and may damage the controller.
