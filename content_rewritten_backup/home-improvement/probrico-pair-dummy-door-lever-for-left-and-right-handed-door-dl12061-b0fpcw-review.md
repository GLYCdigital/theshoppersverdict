Dummy door levers are the quiet workhorses of home improvement—nobody notices them until they're wrong. The Probrico pair solves the most common headache right out of the box: it ships with both a left-handed and a right-handed lever, so you're not stuck with two of the same orientation. That alone saves a return trip to the hardware store.

**What's actually in the box**

You get two complete lever sets, each with mounting plates, screws, and the lever itself. The finish options include brushed nickel, oil-rubbed bronze, and satin black—all of which match Probrico's larger door hardware line if you're doing a whole-house refresh. The levers are zinc alloy, not the hollow pot-metal you find on budget builder-grade sets. As shown in the product image, the rosettes are compact and modern, about 2.5 inches in diameter, which suits both interior passage doors and closet bifolds.

**Build quality and installation**

The levers have a satisfying heft—around 0.6 pounds per pair—and the finish is electroplated, not painted. That means it won't chip or peel when you accidentally whack it with a vacuum cleaner. One buyer noted, "The brushed nickel actually looks like brushed nickel, not shiny chrome pretending." That's a fair assessment; the texture is fine-grained and consistent across both levers.

Installation is genuinely tool-simple. Since these are dummy levers, there's no latch mechanism, strike plate, or spindle to align. You slide the rosette over the existing hole, insert the lever, and tighten the set screw. Most users report a 5-10 minute job per door. The included screws are standard #8-32, so replacements are easy to find if you lose one.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews reflects a pattern of satisfied customers. The most repeated compliments center on three things: the matched left/right pair, the quality of the finish, and the price point. Another reviewer mentioned, "I bought these for a pantry and a hallway closet. They look like they cost three times more than they did." That sentiment appears repeatedly—people are surprised by the perceived value.

**Where it falls short**

Dummy levers are decorative by design. If you're expecting them to actually latch a door shut, you're buying the wrong product. They work great for closets, pantries, and non-latching interior doors, but they won't hold a door closed against a draft or keep a toddler out of the garage.

The more legitimate complaint involves the set screw. A handful of reviewers report that the lever can work loose over several months, especially on doors that get heavy daily use. The fix is simple: apply a drop of blue Loctite to the set screw during installation. It's a 30-second preventive step that eliminates the issue entirely. Also note that the screw heads are Phillips, not Torx, and they're small—use a properly sized driver to avoid stripping.

**Who should buy this**

- **DIYers updating closet and pantry doors** — the matched pair covers both swing directions, and installation is a weekend-morning project.
- **Landlords or flippers** — at this price point, you can outfit an entire property without blowing the budget.
- **Anyone replacing mismatched builder-grade levers** — the finish options blend well with existing Schlage or Kwikset hardware.

Skip it if you need a functional latch, if your door has an unusual non-standard bore, or if you're installing on exterior doors that require a locking mechanism.

**FAQ**

**Q: Do these work on doors with existing latch holes?**  
A: Yes. The rosette covers the standard 2-1/8" bore hole and the 2-3/8" latch hole. You just remove the old hardware and mount the dummy lever over the existing opening.

**Q: Are the left and right levers clearly marked?**  
A: Each lever is individually bagged and labeled in the package. The orientation is determined by which side the lever extends from the rosette—the packaging makes it obvious.

**Q: Can I use these on a door that currently has a working latch?**  
A: Technically yes, but you'd be removing the latch mechanism. That's wasteful. These are best for doors that never had functional latching, like closets or bifolds.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need matched, decent-looking dummy levers for interior doors and don't want to overpay. **Skip it if** you require a latching function or expect commercial-grade durability.

The Probrico pair delivers exactly what it promises: two correctly oriented, well-finished levers that install in minutes. The set screw issue is minor and preventable. At 4.6 out of 5, this is a solid value purchase that looks better than its price tag suggests.
