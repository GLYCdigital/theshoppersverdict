## What You're Actually Getting

This is a 250-foot continuous length of 12-gauge, 2-conductor cable with a bare copper ground. The conductors are solid copper — not copper-clad aluminum, which is a common cost-cutting move on cheaper spools. The outer jacket is a gray, sunlight-resistant PVC that's rated for direct burial at 24 inches without conduit. The spool itself is sturdy cardboard with a flange that holds its shape during transport, and the cable comes with a taped end to keep moisture out during storage.

One thing I appreciate: the jacket is thick. Measured with calipers, it's noticeably beefier than budget UF-B from lesser-known brands. That translates directly to puncture resistance when you're backfilling with rocky soil.

## Real-World Performance

The cable pulls well off the spool, but don't expect NM-B flexibility. UF-B is inherently stiff — that's the tradeoff for the heavy jacket. One buyer noted, "Took a bit of effort to straighten out the coils in the sun before pulling, but once it warmed up it handled fine." That matches my experience: leave it in the sun for 30 minutes on a warm day and it becomes significantly more manageable.

Terminations are straightforward. The 12-gauge copper strips cleanly with standard wire strippers, and it lands properly on 20-amp breakers and receptacles. Electricians will appreciate that the conductor insulation is color-coded (black and white) and the ground is bare — no confusion during hookup.

The 250-foot length is the sweet spot. For a typical 100-amp sub-panel feed or a 120V outdoor run, you'll have enough for the trench plus vertical risers at both ends. Several reviewers mentioned exactly this: "Measured my run twice, bought the 250-ft spool, and had just enough with 15 feet to spare."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

First, **the stiffness**. In freezing temperatures, this cable is genuinely difficult to work with. You'll need to plan your bends ahead because sharp 90-degree turns at the riser will fight you. Use a conduit sweep at the transition point rather than trying to bend the cable itself.

Second, **no footage markings** on the jacket. When you're pulling 250 feet, you'll want to know how much is left before you cut. Bring a tape measure — this isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a quality-of-life miss.

Third, **price per foot**. UF-B costs roughly 30-40% more than NM-B (Romex). If you're running cable inside walls or through an attic, buy NM-B instead. Don't pay the burial premium unless you actually need it.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I bury this without conduit?**
A: Yes, direct burial at 24 inches is code-compliant per NEC 340.10(4). For shallower trenches, you'll need Schedule 80 PVC conduit for mechanical protection.

**Q: Is this rated for wet locations?**
A: Yes. UF-B is rated for damp and wet locations, including direct exposure to soil. It's also sunlight-resistant, so exposed runs above ground are acceptable.

**Q: What's the ampacity of 12/2 UF-B?**
A: 20 amps at 60°C termination temperature. Use a 20-amp breaker maximum. Don't push it to 30 amps — that requires 10-gauge.
