Let’s be honest: you’ve kissed your garage wall at least once. That scrape, that thud, that sinking feeling. The LED Stop Sign Parking Assistant won’t undo past damage, but it might save your bumper — and your drywall — from the next one. This is a niche tool for a very specific frustration, and for the right buyer, it works. For everyone else, well, read on.

**What’s actually in the box**  
You get the main unit — a 4.5-inch circular LED sign with a bold red STOP graphic — plus a wired sensor strip, a small power adapter (DC 12V, 1A), and mounting screws with double-sided tape. No batteries, no Wi-Fi, no app. This is deliberately dumb tech, which is both its charm and its limitation. The sensor strip runs about 3 feet from the sign, so you’ll mount the sensor low on the wall and the sign at eye level in your rearview mirror’s line of sight.

**How it performs in the real world**  
Installation took me 15 minutes with a drill and a screwdriver. The sensor uses an ultrasonic pulse — it measures distance from your bumper to the wall and triggers the flashing red LED when you’re within roughly 18 inches. The flash is bright and unmistakable, even in daylight. One buyer summed it up: *“I stopped worrying about my wife’s parking job. The light goes off, she stops, done.”* That’s the core value: it removes guesswork.

But here’s the catch. The trigger distance is fixed. You can’t dial it closer or further. If your garage is tight and you need to pull in until the bumper is 6 inches from the wall, the sign will start flashing way too early — you’ll be creeping forward with a strobe light in your mirror the whole time. The workaround is mounting the sensor higher or lower to change the angle, but that’s trial-and-error, not precision.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
- Reliability: The ultrasonic sensor doesn’t care about lighting conditions, unlike camera-based systems.  
- Simplicity: No pairing, no calibration, no phone. One buyer said, *“It’s the most useful $30 I’ve spent on the garage. My kids can park straight now.”*  
- Visibility: The flashing pattern draws your eye instantly, even when you’re focused on side mirrors.

**Where it falls short**  
The plastic casing feels light, almost toy-like. It won’t survive a direct hit from a ladder or a carelessly swung door. The included screws are short — if your drywall has a thick air gap, use your own anchors. Also, the power adapter is wall-wart style, so it blocks the second outlet in a duplex. Minor, but annoying. And if you have a garage door opener mounted near the ceiling, make sure the sensor isn’t in its path — the door’s motion can false-trigger the sign.

**Who should buy this**  
- The driver who shares a garage with a spouse or teen who “just doesn’t feel” the bumper distance.  
- Anyone with a tight single-car garage where an inch matters.  
- People who want a no-nonsense warning without smart-home integration.

**Who should skip it**  
- If you need adjustable trigger distance, look elsewhere.  
- If you have a finished garage with thick insulation, the sensor may not read through it — you’ll need to mount on the surface, not recessed.  
- If you already have a backup camera with a distance grid, this is redundant.

**FAQ**  
*Q: Does it work with any vehicle?*  
A: Yes, since it measures distance from the sensor to your bumper, not a specific car. SUVs and sedans both trigger it fine.

*Q: Can I use it outdoors?*  
A: No. It’s rated for indoor use only. Moisture will kill the sensor.

*Q: How do I change the trigger distance?*  
A: You don’t. The only way to adjust is by repositioning the sensor — tilt it up to shorten the range, down to lengthen it. It’s a physical hack, not a setting.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a cheap, reliable “stop here” warning that anyone in the family can understand. **Skip it if** you need fine-tuned distance control or a rugged housing. At 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, it’s a solid niche tool that does exactly one job — and does it well.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a practical fix with a few rough edges.
