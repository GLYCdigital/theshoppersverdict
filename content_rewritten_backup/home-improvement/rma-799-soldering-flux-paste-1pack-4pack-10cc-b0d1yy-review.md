## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: either a single 10cc syringe or a 4-pack of the same. Each syringe comes with a metal dispensing needle pre-attached, ready to apply. The flux itself is a tan-colored paste with a rosin-based RMA (Rosin Mildly Activated) formulation. As shown in the product image, the syringe design is compact — about the size of a standard glue stick — which makes it easy to keep in a drawer or tool roll.

This is a no-clean flux, which means it's designed to leave minimal residue that won't corrode your board over time. For most hobbyist and repair work, you can skip the post-solder IPA bath. That's a genuine time-saver.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this on a mix of through-hole and SMD work: tinning old speaker wire, reflowing a damaged USB connector, and soldering fresh headers onto a Raspberry Pi Pico. The paste dispenses in a controlled ribbon — not too runny, not stiff. It holds its shape when applied, which matters when you're placing it precisely on a pad before reflow.

The RMA formulation does its job. On lightly oxidized pads that would normally reject solder, one application of RMA-799 was enough to get a clean, shiny joint. It activates quickly under heat — you'll see it bubble and turn clear within a second or two of iron contact. The residue is light amber and slightly tacky, but it's non-conductive and won't eat your board over time.

One thing I appreciate: this flux works well for drag soldering fine-pitch QFP pins. The paste stays put instead of wicking away under the iron, which is a common complaint with liquid fluxes.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations.

**Volume.** 10cc is not a lot. If you're doing a full PCB assembly with dozens of joints, you'll burn through a syringe in one session. The 4-pack is the better buy for anyone who solders more than once a month.

**Needle clogging.** The metal tip can clog if you leave the cap off — the paste dries at the tip and blocks flow. You need to develop the habit of capping it immediately. In hot, dry climates, this is more of an issue. A quick poke with a fine wire clears it, but it's an annoyance.

**Not for extreme rework.** If you're dealing with heavily corroded boards or lead-free solder that's been sitting for years, RMA-799 is good but not magic. You'll want a stronger acid-based flux for those jobs — this is a rosin flux and stays in its lane.

## FAQ

**Q: Is RMA-799 safe for all PCB types?**
A: Yes. RMA flux is specifically designed for electronics. It's mildly activated, so it won't damage components or board traces with proper use. It's safe for through-hole, SMD, and even sensitive components like ICs.

**Q: Do I really need to clean the residue afterward?**
A: For most applications, no. It's a no-clean flux. However, if the board will be exposed to high humidity or you're working on high-impedance circuits, a quick wipe with isopropyl alcohol is cheap insurance.

**Q: How long does a single 10cc syringe last?**
A: For a typical hobbyist doing a few boards a week, expect 2–3 months. For heavy repair work, one syringe might last a single weekend. That's why the 4-pack exists.
