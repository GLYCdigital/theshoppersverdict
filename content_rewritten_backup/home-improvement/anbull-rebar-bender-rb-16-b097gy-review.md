**Bottom Line Up Front:** The Anbull RB-16 is a legit workhorse for anyone bending rebar regularly, but it's not a set-and-forget tool. It demands a little technique and a lot of elbow grease on thicker bar. Here's the full picture.

---

**What You're Actually Getting**

The Anbull RB-16 is a manual lever-style rebar bender rated for #3 through #5 bar (3/8" to 5/8" diameter). Out of the box, you get the bender itself, two bending pins (one short, one long), and the mounting hardware. The unit weighs in around 40 pounds — heavy enough to stay planted during use, light enough to toss in a truck bed. As shown in the product image, the frame is welded box steel with a reinforced pivot point, and the handle is a solid steel bar with a rubber grip.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The frame is genuinely stout. I've seen cheaper benders flex and twist under load on #4 bar — this one doesn't. The pivot bushing is machined, not cast, which means smooth rotation and minimal play. That's where the 4.6-star average comes from: buyers consistently mention the build quality. One reviewer put it simply: *"This thing is a tank. I've bent hundreds of pieces of 1/2" rebar and it hasn't budged."*

The dual-pin system is the standout feature. The short pin gives you tight 90° stirrups and hooks; the long pin handles gradual curves and larger radius bends. Switching between them takes about 30 seconds with a wrench. That versatility is why DIYers and contractors both gravitate toward it.

**Where It Falls Short**

The lack of an angle stop is the biggest annoyance. If you're bending 50 identical L-shaped anchors, you'll be eyeballing each one or clamping a stop block to the frame. It's doable, but it slows production work. One buyer noted: *"Wish it had a degree gauge. I ended up marking my own reference lines with a Sharpie."*

On #5 bar, the lever arm gets long and heavy. It'll do the job, but you'll be putting your whole body into it. A cheater pipe over the handle helps, but the rubber grip makes that a tight fit. Also, the included mounting bolts are too small for permanent installation — plan on grabbing your own hardware if you're bolting it to a workbench.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **DIYers building foundations, retaining walls, or concrete slabs** — you'll bend a few dozen pieces per project, and this handles it effortlessly.
- **Small contractors and rebar installers** doing light-to-medium production work — the speed and clean bends justify the price.
- **Anyone who's tired of the bolt-cutter-and-vise method** — this turns a 20-minute chore into a 2-minute one.

Skip it if you're a weekend warrior doing one-off projects with a handful of bends — a manual hickey bar will suffice. Also skip it if you work exclusively with #6 or larger rebar; this isn't rated for that.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this bend #4 rebar without kinking?**
A: Yes. The die is sized correctly, so the bar compresses evenly without the outer edge cracking or flattening. That's the main reason people upgrade from cheaper benders.

**Q: Can I mount it permanently to a workbench?**
A: Yes, there are four bolt holes in the base. Just use your own 3/8" or 1/2" bolts — the included ones are too small for a secure permanent mount.

**Q: How long does the pivot hold up?**
A: The bushing is replaceable, and several long-term reviews report thousands of bends without needing service. Keep it greased and it'll outlast your project.

**The Verdict**

The Anbull RB-16 earns its 4.6-star rating honestly. It's not the cheapest bender on Amazon, but it's the one that won't leave you frustrated mid-project. The build quality is exceptional for the price, the dual-pin system handles real-world bending needs, and it's portable enough to move around a job site.

**Buy it if** you're serious about rebar work and want a tool that'll last through years of projects.

**Skip it if** you're a light-duty user who can't justify the cost — or if you need automatic angle repeatability for high-volume production.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a solid, dependable tool held back only by the missing angle stop and the physical effort required on thicker bar.
