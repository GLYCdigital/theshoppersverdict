The UY CHAN TS101 isn't just another USB-C soldering iron. It's the answer to a specific question: do you need a full station for occasional board work, or do you want something that heats up before you finish plugging it in? At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this pocket-sized iron has clearly found its audience. Here's who that audience is — and isn't.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the iron itself, a 1.2m USB-C to USB-C cable, a TS-I01 conical tip (installed), and a basic manual. No power adapter — you supply your own USB-C PD charger. That's either a smart cost cut or an annoyance depending on what you own. If you've got a 65W laptop charger lying around, you're set. If not, factor in another $20-30 for a proper PD adapter.

The iron itself is compact: roughly 180mm long and 34g without the cable. It feels like a quality pen, not a toy. The OLED screen is the standout feature — it displays current temperature and wattage draw in real time, which is genuinely useful for diagnosing thermal performance on heavy joints.

**Performance: the headline numbers**

With a 65W PD adapter, the TS101 hits 350°C in about 5 seconds. That's not marketing hype — it's the same heating core used in the Miniware TS101, and the UY CHAN is a licensed/upgraded variant. The 40W USB-C mode (standard PD) still gets you to soldering temperature in under 10 seconds, which beats most $60+ stations that need 30-60 seconds to stabilize.

Temperature control is accurate to ±5°C, and the iron recovers quickly on large ground planes — a common weakness in cheap irons. The firmware supports temperature adjustment in 1°C increments from 100°C to 400°C, and you can program it via the PC UI if you're into that level of control.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is the "grab and go" factor. One verified buyer wrote: *"I replaced my 60W station with this for 90% of my work. Heats up faster than my station, and I can toss it in a drawer when done."* Another noted: *"The OLED display showing wattage is brilliant — you can see exactly when the tip is struggling."* Multiple reviewers mention using it with power banks for field repairs, which is a legit use case no station iron can match.

**Where it falls short**

The 1.2m cable is genuinely short. On a bench with your PD charger on the floor, you'll feel the constraint. Buy a 2m USB-C extension or mount the charger on your desk.

The stock conical tip is fine for SMD and fine-pitch work, but it struggles with through-hole soldering on larger pads. A TS-I chisel tip (about $8) fixes this immediately — just budget for it.

No sleep stand is included. The iron has an auto-sleep after 10 minutes idle, but you'll want something to rest it on during active use. The silicone mat sold separately is $15+; a cheap metal stand works just as well.

**Who should buy this**

- **Drone/RC hobbyists** who solder in the field — this pairs perfectly with a 65W power bank
- **Electronics students** who need one iron for dorms, labs, and home — it replaces three tools
- **Anyone with limited bench space** — it's smaller than a pencil case

**Skip it if** you do heavy through-hole work daily (like appliance repair) or you want a full station with hot air. This is a precision iron, not a workhorse.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can it run off a standard 5V USB port?*
A: No. It needs USB-C PD at 12V/20V minimum. A phone charger won't work.

*Q: Does it work with TS100 tips?*
A: No — TS101 uses TS-I series tips. They're not cross-compatible with the TS100's TS-C series. Buy accordingly.

*Q: Is the OLED screen durable?*
A: Yes, it's recessed and protected by the aluminum body. No reported failures in the review sample set.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely portable, fast-heating iron that handles 90% of electronics work and disappears into a drawer when done.

**Skip it if** you need a dedicated station with hot air or do heavy production soldering daily.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The TS101 nails portability and speed with real firmware smarts. It's not perfect (that cable!), but at this price, it's the best pocket iron you can buy. Pair it with a chisel tip and a 65W charger, and you've got a kit that punches far above its size.
