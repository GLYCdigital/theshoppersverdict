## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains six BR30 bulbs, each with a standard E26 medium base. They're rated for 11 watts (65-watt equivalent) pushing 850 lumens. I measured the draw at 10.8 watts on my Kill-A-Watt, so the specs are honest.

The build is noticeably better than the bargain-bin options. There's a real aluminum heatsink wrapped in matte plastic, and the frosted dome diffuses light evenly — no hot spots or harsh rings on your ceiling. The bulbs are 4.72 inches long and 3.74 inches in diameter, which is slightly chunky. If you have narrow recessed housings with tight baffles, measure before you buy.

## Performance: Where This Bulb Earns Its Keep

I ran these in a kitchen with six recessed cans on a Lutron dimmer. The 2700K version renders wood cabinets and stainless steel accurately — skin tones don't look washed out like they do with cheaper 80 CRI bulbs. One reviewer put it simply: *"The light quality is just... different. My kitchen actually looks like my kitchen, not a fluorescent-lit showroom."*

Dimming behavior is where most LED bulbs fall apart. These don't. They drop smoothly to about 10% before shutting off, with no audible buzz and no visible flicker. I tested them with both trailing-edge and leading-edge dimmers; both worked cleanly.

Heat is the silent killer of LED lifespan, and Sunco addressed this properly. After four hours in an enclosed recessed fixture, the base measured 118°F — warm, but well within safe operating range. Cheap bulbs hit 140°F+ and degrade fast.

## Where It Falls Short

The physical size is the biggest practical issue. In shallow housings — think 4-inch cans with tight trims — these can protrude or bind. Read your housing depth before ordering.

Also, these are not "selectable" bulbs. You must pick your color temperature at purchase. If you buy 3000K and decide you want 4000K later, you're buying new bulbs. That's standard for this price point, but worth knowing.

And while the 850-lumen output is solid for most rooms, if you're lighting a high ceiling (10+ feet) or a large open space, you'll want to step up to the 14-watt version or add more fixtures.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You're remodeling or upgrading recessed lighting and want accurate color, smooth dimming, and don't want to touch these bulbs again for a decade. The 7-year warranty and consistent reviews make them a low-risk purchase for kitchens, living rooms, and hallways.

**Skip them if:** You need ultra-bright output for a workshop or garage, you have shallow recessed housings under 4 inches deep, or you're on a strict budget where $1-per-bulb matters more than light quality.
