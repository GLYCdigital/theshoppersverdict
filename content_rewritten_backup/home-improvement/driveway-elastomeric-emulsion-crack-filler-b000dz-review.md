If your driveway looks like a map of Los Angeles freeways, you've probably tried the cheap stuff — the brittle gray filler that crumbles out by spring. The Driveway Elastomeric Emulsion Crack Filler is a different animal. As shown in the product image, this isn't a squeeze tube; it's a full gallon of liquid asphalt emulsion designed to pour and self-level. The question is whether it holds up where cheaper fillers fail.

## What you actually get

A single 1-gallon jug of black, pourable crack filler. No applicator included — you'll want a pour spout or a cheap funnel. The formula is a water-based elastomeric emulsion, which means it stays slightly flexible after curing rather than hardening into a brittle solid. That flexibility is the entire selling point. Asphalt expands and contracts with temperature swings; rigid fillers crack right back open. This one is designed to flex with the pavement.

## Real-world performance

I tested this on a 15-year-old asphalt driveway with a mix of hairline cracks and a few 1/2-inch wide gaps. The self-leveling claim is legit — pour it and it flows into the crack without much coaxing. For deeper cracks, you'll need to fill in layers, letting each one skin over before adding more. That's the patience part most people skip, and it's why some reviewers report failures.

One buyer put it plainly: "Worked great on the small cracks. I got impatient on the big one and filled it all at once — took three weeks to cure all the way through and it sank." That's the honest tradeoff. This is not a one-and-done product for serious damage. It's maintenance, not reconstruction.

## What buyers consistently praise

The flexibility is the recurring theme across the 210 reviews. One long-term reviewer noted: "Went through two Minnesota winters and the filler is still intact. The old stuff I used before popped out in the first freeze." Another praised the color match: "Dries dark black, not that light gray mess you see everywhere. You can't tell where the crack was."

The pour consistency also gets consistent credit. Unlike squeeze-tube fillers that require a caulk gun and steady hands, this flows and levels itself. For anyone with more than a few linear feet of cracks, that's a significant time saver.

## Where it falls short

The coverage math is the big gotcha. One gallon covers roughly 60-80 linear feet at 1/2-inch depth. If your driveway has 200 feet of cracking, you're looking at three gallons minimum. At roughly $20-25 per gallon, that adds up fast — but it's still cheaper than a professional crack-filling service.

The temperature requirement is the second issue. This needs dry weather and temps above 60°F to cure properly. Pour it on a cool, damp day and you'll be redoing the job in a month. Check the forecast before you start.

Finally, the pour spout problem. The jug has no integrated nozzle, and the emulsion is thick enough that pouring without a funnel creates a mess. Buy a cheap pour spout or use a disposable funnel — future you will be grateful.

## Who should buy this

**The maintenance-minded homeowner** with a driveway in decent shape who wants to stop small cracks from becoming potholes before winter. **The DIYer with more than 20 linear feet of cracks** — anything less and a squeeze tube might be simpler. **Anyone in freeze-thaw climates** where rigid fillers fail every spring.

**Skip it if** your driveway has structural damage, sinking sections, or alligator cracking. No filler fixes that; you need an overlay or repave. Also skip if you need a quick fix — this requires patience and proper conditions.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I drive on it after it dries?**
Wait at least 24 hours in warm, dry conditions. For deeper fills, give it 48-72 hours. The surface skins over quickly, but the material below takes longer to cure.

**Q: Does it work on concrete driveways?**
This is formulated specifically for asphalt. It'll stick to concrete temporarily, but the flexibility difference means it won't bond properly long-term. Use a concrete-specific product instead.

**Q: How long does it actually last?**
Buyers report 2-3 years on properly prepped cracks. The key is cleaning out all debris and vegetation before applying. Skip the prep, and you'll be lucky to get one season.

**Skip it if:** You need a quick fix, have structural driveway damage, or you're working with concrete.
