## What You Actually Get

The WORKPRO is a magnetic torpedo level built specifically for conduit work. It features the essential 45-degree and 90-degree vial angles that electricians need for EMT and rigid conduit bends, plus a standard center bubble for general leveling. The magnetic base is strong enough to snap onto metal conduit, studs, and junction boxes without you having to hold it in place.

Dimensions are compact—roughly 8 inches in length—which makes it pocketable and easy to maneuver in tight spaces. It's not a 48-inch box level, and it doesn't try to be.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this level earns its keep. The vials are embedded in a solid aluminum body with a protective frame around the glass. In practice, that means you can drop it off a ladder and it won't crack the vials—a complaint you'll see with cheaper levels that use exposed plastic. The 45-degree and 90-degree markings are clearly visible without magnification, which matters when you're crouched under a panel with a flashlight in your mouth.

The magnets are the standout feature. They're not just decorative; they genuinely grip 1/2-inch and 3/4-inch conduit firmly enough to stay put while you adjust your bender. As one reviewer noted, "The magnet strength surprised me—it holds tight to the pipe without sliding, even on a vertical run."

Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**Accuracy:** Multiple buyers confirmed the vials are true out of the box. One electrician wrote, "Checked it against my $80 Stabila and it was dead on. No need to 'learn' this level's quirks."

**Durability:** The aluminum frame and reinforced vials have survived job-site abuse. A reviewer mentioned, "Dropped it off a scissor lift onto concrete. Picked it up, checked it, still accurate. Bought a second one for my apprentice."

**Value:** At this price point, buyers consistently note it performs like tools costing twice as much.

## Where It Falls Short

No tool is perfect. The WORKPRO's primary weakness is the vial clarity in low light. The bubbles aren't backlit, so if you're working in a dim crawlspace or after sunset, you'll need a headlamp. It's a minor annoyance, but worth knowing if you often work in dark conditions.

The level also lacks a plumb (vertical) vial on the end—only the side-mounted one. For conduit work this isn't a dealbreaker, but if you need simultaneous horizontal and vertical reference, you'll be flipping the tool.

A few reviewers noted the paint on the frame scratches off with heavy use, leaving bare aluminum. Cosmetic only, but it happens faster than you'd expect.
