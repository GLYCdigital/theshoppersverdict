**The Sali #29 flap disc doesn't try to be fancy — it just removes metal fast and keeps removing it.** That's what 229 Amazon reviewers found, averaging 4.6 stars. After spending a week on mild steel, stainless, and a stubborn weld seam, here's the real story.

## What You're Actually Buying

The box contains one 4.5-inch disc with a standard 7/8-inch arbor — fits any standard angle grinder, no adapter needed. The #29 bevel designation means the flaps are angled back from the edge, giving you a conical working face. As shown in the product image, this isn't a flat sanding disc; the taper lets you work both flat against the surface and at a 15-30 degree angle for aggressive stock removal.

The abrasive is zirconia alumina — a step up from cheap aluminum oxide. That's the key spec. Zirconia self-sharpens as it wears, which is why these discs keep cutting long after budget options go dull.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

On carbon steel, this disc removes mill scale and light rust without bogging down. I ran it at 11,000 RPM on a standard DeWalt grinder — smooth, no vibration, no wobble at the arbor. The #29 geometry lets you blend a weld joint flat in one pass, then tip the disc to knock down the crown. That's the advantage over a flat #27 disc: more versatility in one tool.

On stainless, it left a clean, consistent finish — not mirror-polished, but uniform enough for fabrication work. Heat buildup was manageable; the zirconia grain sheds heat better than aluminum oxide.

Here's the honest limitation: this is a metal disc. Try it on wood or aluminum and you'll glaze the flaps within minutes. One reviewer noted, "Great on steel, but I grab a different disc for non-ferrous." That matches my experience exactly.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in customer feedback is value-for-performance. One verified buyer wrote, "I've used Diablo and Norton for years — this holds up just as long at half the price." Another mentioned, "No shedding of flaps mid-job, which I've had happen with cheaper discs."

The durability point is worth emphasizing. I pushed one disc through roughly 45 minutes of continuous grinding on 1/4-inch plate. The flap wear was even — no chunks flying off, no premature structural failure. For a disc in this price range, that's genuinely impressive.

## Where It Falls Short

Two real issues. First, single-disc packaging. At this price point, most pros expect a multi-pack. You're paying a slight premium per unit versus bulk options. For a one-off project, that's fine. For daily shop use, you'll want to buy several or look for a 5-pack.

Second, the #29 bevel has a learning curve if you're used to flat discs. The cone shape means you can't just lay it flat and push — you need to work the angle. New users might find it aggressive at first. It's not a flaw, but it's a real adjustment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home hobbyists** doing occasional fabrication or rust removal — one disc, no commitment
- **Welding students** learning bead blending — the #29 geometry is exactly what you'll use professionally
- **DIYers with a single grinder** who want one disc that handles both flat sanding and edge work

**Skip it if** you're a production shop needing bulk pricing, or you exclusively work with wood, plastic, or aluminum.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my grinder?**
A: Any angle grinder accepting 4.5-inch discs with a 7/8-inch arbor — that's virtually all standard models from DeWalt, Makita, Milwaukee, Bosch, and Ryobi.

**Q: How long does one disc last?**
A: On mild steel, expect 30-60 minutes of continuous grinding depending on pressure and RPM. Aggressive angle work wears faster than flat sanding.

**Q: Can I use this on aluminum?**
A: Technically yes, but it will load up and glaze quickly. Get a dedicated non-ferrous disc for aluminum work.
