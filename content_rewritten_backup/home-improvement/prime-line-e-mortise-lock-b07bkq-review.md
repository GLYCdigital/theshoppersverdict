If your door's mortise lock has finally given up after a decade of daily abuse, you're staring down a surprisingly confusing purchase. Mortise locks aren't like standard cylindrical knobs—they need to fit a specific pocket cut into the door edge, and getting that wrong means a trip back to the hardware store. The Prime-Line E Mortise Lock aims to be the straightforward replacement for the most common residential setup. Here's the real story after digging through what 220 buyers actually experienced.

**What's Actually in the Box**

Prime-Line ships this as a complete unit: the mortise body with both latch and deadbolt functions, two levers (one for each side), two rosettes, a strike plate, and all mounting hardware. The body measures a standard 2-3/8" backset with a 2-1/2" case height, which is the configuration found on the vast majority of interior and exterior doors built in the last 40 years. As shown in the product image, the finish is a brushed satin nickel that doesn't scream "cheap replacement"—it has a subtle sheen that matches most modern door hardware. You also get two keys, which is standard but appreciated.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The first thing you notice when you lift this lock is the weight. The housing is zinc die-cast, not the flimsy stamped steel you find on $20 entry-level locks. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've installed a lot of locks over the years, and this one feels like it's machined, not stamped out of a tin can."* The levers are spring-loaded on both sides, which is a detail that matters—cheaper mortise locks let the handle droop after a few months, and that doesn't happen here.

Installation is where this lock earns its keep. The mortise body slides into the existing pocket with minimal filing needed if your door was originally cut for a similar unit. Several reviewers noted it was a direct drop-in replacement for older Schlage and Kwikset mortise locks. One customer wrote: *"Took me 20 minutes total, including removing the old unit. The screw holes lined up perfectly with my existing door prep."* That's the experience most people have—no door modifications required.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated compliment is the smooth action. The deadbolt throws cleanly with a satisfying click, and the latch retracts without that gritty, grinding feel you get on budget locks. The finish also holds up—multiple reviewers who installed it on exterior doors report no tarnishing or peeling after a year of weather exposure.

Security-wise, the deadbolt uses a standard 1" throw, and the internal mechanism has anti-pick pins. It's not a high-security lock, but it's a solid step up from the builder-grade original it's replacing.

**Where It Falls Short**

The set screws that hold the levers onto the spindle are small—2mm Allen heads—and they're easy to strip if you overtighten. Use a quality Allen wrench, not the cheap L-key included in the package, and stop at snug. One reviewer learned this the hard way: *"I gorilla-tightened one screw and rounded it out. Had to buy a replacement screw set."*

The included strike plate screws are also on the short side. If your door has thick weatherstripping or you're mounting into a metal frame, you'll want to grab #8 x 1" screws from your local hardware store. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the homeowner who needs a reliable replacement without reconfiguring their door. It's ideal if you have an existing mortise pocket and want a drop-in fix that will last 5–10 years. It's also a good choice for landlords who want something durable enough for tenant turnover but not so expensive that theft becomes a concern. If you're building a new door from scratch or have a non-standard backset, you'll need to look elsewhere.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my 1960s-era door?**
Most likely yes, if your current lock has a 2-3/8" backset. Measure from the door edge to the center of the knob hole. If that's 2-3/8", this will fit. If it's 2-3/4", you need the longer version.

**Q: Does the deadbolt function independently from the handle?**
Yes. The deadbolt throws separately, and the handle operates the latch. You can lock the deadbolt and still use the handle from the inside—a standard and expected feature.

**Q: Is this suitable for an exterior door?**
Yes, the finish is weather-resistant and the internal components are steel. Just make sure you use the included weatherstripping gasket and consider upgrading the strike plate screws for a tighter fit.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a straightforward, durable replacement for a standard mortise lock and don't want to pay custom lock shop prices.

**Skip it if:** You have a non-standard backset, need a high-security lock with bump resistance, or you're the type who overtightens screws with reckless abandon.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not flashy, but it does the job reliably, fits where it should, and costs far less than a locksmith visit. The minor hardware quibbles are easily solved with a trip to the hardware store.
