A $175 tub and shower faucet that doesn't feel like a compromise. That's the short version of this S3230 review. But before you add it to your cart, let's talk about what's actually in the box and whether it's the right fixture for your remodel.

## What You Actually Get

The S3230 arrives as a complete kit: the rough-in valve body, trim plate, single-handle lever, showerhead with a 60-inch stainless steel hose, tub spout, and all mounting hardware. The valve uses a 1/2-inch IPS connection, which is standard for most residential plumbing in North America. The showerhead is a 2.5 GPM unit — code-compliant in most states, though some water-restricted regions like California and Colorado will want to check local limits.

The first thing you notice handling the components is the weight. The valve body is solid brass, not the zinc alloy you find at this price point from lesser brands. The lever action is smooth with a positive stop, and the diverter on the tub spout pulls up and engages with a firm click. It feels like a $300 fixture, full stop.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is where this faucet earns its keep — or tests your patience. The rough-in valve is designed for a 1/2-inch copper or PEX supply, and the body fits into a standard 2x4 wall cavity, but barely. You'll want the valve positioned so the plaster guard sits flush with your finished wall surface. Get that depth wrong and the trim plate won't seat properly. If you're comfortable sweating copper or crimping PEX, this is a DIY-friendly project. If not, budget $200–$350 for a plumber.

Once installed, the pressure-balanced valve does its job. Run the kitchen sink or flush a toilet while someone's in the shower, and the temperature holds steady within a degree or two. That's the difference between a cheap fixture and one with a real anti-scald cartridge. The lever controls temperature and volume with a single hand, and the rotation range is generous enough for fine adjustments.

The showerhead delivers a solid, even spray pattern — not a rainfall experience, but a consistent 2.5 GPM flow that rinses shampoo without feeling weak. The tub spout has a standard 1/2-inch thread and a 6-inch reach, which clears the edge of most standard tubs.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Scrolling through the 210 ratings, a few themes come up again and again. One verified buyer put it simply: *"Heavy, solid construction. The brass valve body is the real deal."* Another noted the finish: *"The brushed nickel looks way more expensive than what I paid."*

But the praise isn't universal. Several reviews mention the finish showing water spots, with one buyer writing, *"Looks great for the first week, then you're wiping it down constantly."* That's the tradeoff with brushed nickel — it hides fingerprints better than chrome but still shows hard water deposits if you have mineral-heavy supply.

Another common thread: the installation instructions are minimal. One reviewer said, *"The diagrams are small and not super clear. I've installed faucets before and I was fine, but a first-timer might struggle."* Fair warning if this is your first rough-in valve.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the tight fit and finish maintenance, there are two honest downsides worth flagging. First, the trim plate uses a single set screw to hold it against the wall — if your finished wall isn't perfectly flat, you'll see a small gap on one side. It's cosmetic, but it's there. Second, the cartridge is proprietary to this brand, not a universal replacement like a Moen or Delta. That means when it eventually wears out in 10–15 years, you'll be hunting for a specific replacement part rather than grabbing one at the local hardware store. That's a real long-term consideration.

## Who Should Buy This

This faucet hits the sweet spot for a few specific buyers:

- **DIY renovators** who want a premium-feeling fixture without paying premium prices and don't mind a Saturday project
- **Rental property owners** who need something durable that tenants won't destroy — the solid brass body will outlast cheap alternatives
- **Anyone replacing an old two-handle setup** who wants a modern single-handle look without a full wall reconfiguration

Skip it if you need a universal cartridge system, if you have a non-standard rough-in depth, or if you're the type who never wants to wipe down a shower fixture.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this faucet come with a shower arm, or do I need to buy one separately?**
A: It includes the showerhead and hose, but not the wall-mounted shower arm. You'll need a standard 1/2-inch shower arm with flange — about $10–15 at any hardware store.

**Q: Will this work with PEX plumbing?**
A: Yes, the 1/2-inch IPS connections work with PEX via crimp or push-fit adapters. Just make sure your rough-in depth accounts for the adapter length.

**Q: Is the cartridge replaceable without tearing out the wall?**
A: Yes, the cartridge is accessible from the front by removing the handle and trim plate — no wall access needed. But the replacement part is brand-specific, so keep your model number handy.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You want a substantial, well-built fixture for a mid-range remodel budget and you're comfortable with a moderately challenging install.

**Skip it if:** You need universal parts compatibility, have a non-standard wall depth, or want a finish that shrugs off hard water without daily wiping.
