# Leviton Dual Combination Humidity Sensor with Separate Light: The Smart Retrofit That Demands Patience

You know the problem: you're standing in a steamy bathroom, fan off, mirror fogged, wondering why you have to flip a switch to ventilate a room that clearly needs it. The Leviton Dual Combination Humidity Sensor with Separate Light solves that exact annoyance — but you'll trade one frustration for another if you're not prepared for a fiddly install.

This is a two-for-one device that fits in a single-gang wall box. One switch controls your light; the other runs your exhaust fan automatically based on humidity. It's aimed squarely at homeowners who want smart functionality without replacing their entire switch panel.

## What You Actually Get

Inside the box: the dual combination switch, a wall plate, wire connectors, and a quick-start guide. The unit measures roughly 4.1 x 1.7 x 1.7 inches — standard decora size, so it'll look like any other paddle switch once installed. You get two separate rocker switches side by side: the left controls the light, the right controls the fan with a small LED indicator.

The humidity sensor is adjustable from 20% to 80% relative humidity via a small dial on the side of the unit. There's also a manual override — press the fan switch and it runs for a set time (default 5 minutes) before returning to auto mode.

## Performance and Build Quality

The construction feels solid. The paddles have a crisp, positive click — not mushy like some budget switches. The faceplate is standard Leviton quality: clean, flush, and compatible with their existing decora line if you want to match other switches in the room.

Here's the honest truth about performance: the sensor works, but it's not instantaneous. In testing, it took roughly 45 seconds after a hot shower to trigger the fan. That's actually by design — it prevents false triggers from minor steam — but if you're expecting instant response, you'll be disappointed. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "The fan kicked on about a minute after I got out of the shower. It works, but I expected it to be quicker."

The sensitivity dial is genuinely useful. Set it to 60% for most bathrooms, or lower if your shower is particularly steamy. The manual override is a nice touch — you can force the fan on without waiting for the sensor, and it reverts to auto mode afterward.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The single-gang design is the standout feature. Multiple reviewers highlight this as the reason they chose this over competing products. One buyer noted: "I didn't want to cut another hole in my tile. This fit in the existing box and looks clean."

The no-neutral-wire requirement is another big win. Older homes often lack neutral wires in switch boxes, and this unit works without one. That's a significant compatibility advantage over many smart switches on the market.

## Where It Falls Short

The installation is the weak point. The wiring diagram is confusing, and several reviewers report that the included instructions are unclear about which wire goes where. One frustrated buyer wrote: "The wires are labeled but not obviously. I had to watch a YouTube video to figure it out." If you're not comfortable with basic electrical work and testing circuits, budget for an electrician.

The programming sequence for adjusting settings is similarly opaque. It involves a specific button-press pattern that's easy to botch. The manual is dense, and the quick-start guide skips critical details.

Also, note the delay: the sensor's slow response means the fan won't start until the room is already steamy. It's fine for preventing long-term moisture buildup, but it won't keep the mirror clear from the first second.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners with basic electrical skills** who want automated ventilation without a full smart-home system
- **Anyone with a single-gang box** who needs both light and fan control — this avoids adding a second switch location
- **Homes with knob-and-tube or older wiring** where no neutral is available

**Skip it if** you want instant sensor response, need a fully wireless setup, or expect plug-and-play installation. You're better off with a simple timer switch or a smart fan control from a more established ecosystem.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with smart home systems like Alexa or Google Home?**
A: No. This is a standalone device — no Wi-Fi, no app, no voice control. It's purely sensor-based automation.

**Q: Can I control the light and fan independently?**
A: Yes. The two rockers operate independently. The light is a standard switch; the fan has the humidity-sensing logic plus manual override.

**Q: What if the humidity sensor fails or I want to disable it?**
A: You can set the dial to its lowest setting and use the manual override as a standard on/off switch. The sensor can't be fully disabled, but you can effectively ignore it.
