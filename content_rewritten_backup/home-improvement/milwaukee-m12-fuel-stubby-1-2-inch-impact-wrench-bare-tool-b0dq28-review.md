If you've ever wedged a full-size impact wrench between a strut tower and engine block, only to realize it doesn't fit, you already know why the Milwaukee M12 FUEL Stubby exists. This thing crams genuine impact torque into a housing shorter than a standard ratchet, and at 4.8/5 stars across 240 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in pro toolboxes. But it's not a magic wand — let's talk about where it shines and where it comes up short.

**What you actually get**

This is the bare tool — no battery, no charger, no case. Just the wrench itself, weighing 2.4 pounds with a compact 5.4-inch length. You're paying for the head and the electronics, not the accessories. If you're already invested in M12 batteries, this is the smart buy. If you're starting from scratch, factor in the cost of a battery and charger before you commit.

**Real-world performance**

The headline numbers are 250 ft-lbs of fastening torque and 450 ft-lbs of nut-busting torque. In practice, that translates to zipping off most passenger car lug nuts without breaking a sweat — even ones torqued to spec by a shop with a pneumatic gun. The 4-mode drive control is the sleeper feature here: you can dial down to a low-torque setting for smaller fasteners, and the auto-shutoff mode prevents you from snapping off a 10mm bolt because you got carried away.

The M12 FUEL motor delivers that characteristic Milwaukee punch — it's not the smooth, linear pull of the M18, but it's got a sharp, hammering bite that breaks free stubborn bolts. The ½-inch friction ring keeps sockets locked on, and the hog ring design means no detent pin to lose in the dirt.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in reviews is the size-to-power ratio. As one verified buyer put it: *"I kept waiting for it to struggle, but it hasn't. It's replaced my 3/8 air ratchet for 80% of what I do."* Another noted: *"Reaches the caliper bracket bolts on my F-150 that I used to have to do by hand with a breaker bar."* That's the real story here — it's not about replacing your big impact, it's about eliminating the hand-tool fallback for tight spaces.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limits. This is not an M18 FUEL. It will not reliably break loose a rusted crank pulley bolt on a 20-year-old truck, and it'll struggle with heavy-duty truck lugs torqued to 150+ ft-lbs that have been sitting for years. Some reviewers note it stalls on stubborn suspension bolts that a 3/8 drive air impact handles easily. It's also worth flagging that the M12 platform drains batteries faster under heavy load — expect roughly 40-60 lug nuts per 2.0Ah battery before you feel it slowing.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the home mechanic who's tired of fighting tight spaces, the mobile tech who needs a compact kit, and the pro who wants a second impact for quick work without dragging out the air compressor. It's also a natural upgrade for anyone who has an M12 drill/driver and wants to expand the platform. Skip it if your primary work is heavy trucks, farm equipment, or anything with serious corrosion — you need the M18 FUEL for that.

**FAQ**

**Will this remove lug nuts from my car?**
Yes, in the vast majority of cases. Most passenger car lugs are torqued to 80-100 ft-lbs, and this handles that easily — even ones that have been over-torqued by a shop. It'll spin them off in about a second per lug.

**Does it fit in tight engine bays?**
That's its entire reason for existing. At 5.4 inches long, it's shorter than most 3/8 ratchets. It'll reach alternator bolts, caliper brackets, and starter bolts that full-size impacts can't physically fit.

**Is the bare tool worth it, or should I get a kit?**
If you already own M12 batteries, the bare tool is the smart play. If you don't, buying a kit with a battery and charger is usually cheaper than piecing it together separately. Watch for deals on the combo kits.

**The verdict**

The Milwaukee M12 FUEL Stubby is one of those tools that changes how you work. It doesn't replace your full-size impact — it replaces the breaker bar and swearing that used to happen in tight spots. At 4.6/5, it's not perfect, but for the home mechanic and pro alike, it earns its spot in the box.

**Buy it if** you're already in the M12 platform, you work on cars or light trucks, and you're tired of fighting for access.
**Skip it if** you need serious torque for heavy-duty or rusted fasteners, or you're starting from scratch with no M12 batteries.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a genuinely great tool with honest limitations.
