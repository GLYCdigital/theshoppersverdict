**Bottom line:** The BLS motion sensor light solves a genuinely annoying problem — fumbling for light switches in dark closets, under cabinets, or on stair landings — without hardwiring or replacing fixtures. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, it's earned its reputation as a reliable, no-fuss lighting upgrade. But it's not perfect, and knowing the quirks before you buy saves frustration.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the light bar itself, a magnetic mounting plate with 3M adhesive backing, a USB-C charging cable, and two small screws if you prefer permanent mounting. The unit measures roughly 8 inches long and about 1.5 inches wide — compact enough to tuck under cabinet lips or along closet shelves without looking obtrusive. The built-in battery is rated for weeks of typical use between charges, though that depends heavily on how often the sensor triggers.

## Real-World Performance

The motion detection is the star here. With a 120° field of view and about 10 feet of range, it catches you approaching from a hallway or reaching into a deep shelf. Response time is quick — under a second — which matters when you're carrying laundry or groceries. The three brightness modes (dim, medium, bright) give you flexibility: dim for nighttime hallway navigation, bright for finding specific items in a dark closet.

One detail that stands out: the light remembers your last brightness setting between charges. That sounds minor, but cheap competitors reset to default every time. Set it to dim for a nursery or bright for a workshop shelf, and it stays put.

The magnetic mount is genuinely convenient. The light clicks off the plate for charging, and the USB-C port means you're not hunting for a proprietary cable. Recharging takes about 2-3 hours for a full charge.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint involves the adhesive plate on textured surfaces. If your walls have orange-peel or knockdown texture, the 3M tape won't grip well, and the light can drop. The included screws solve this, but only if you're willing to drill. Renters may not be.

Also, the "always-on" mode keeps the sensor active even in daylight, which drains the battery faster. You'll want to use the auto mode (sensor only in darkness) for most applications. Some users report the sensor occasionally triggers on pets — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

Skip it if you need a light for a large room or if your walls are heavily textured and you refuse to drill.

## FAQ

**How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
With normal motion-triggered use (about 10-15 activations per day), expect 3-4 weeks. Always-on mode cuts that to roughly 5-7 days.

**Can I use this outdoors?**
No. It's rated for indoor use only. Moisture will damage the electronics.

**Does the sensor work through glass or plastic?**
Passive infrared sensors detect heat, not motion through barriers. It needs a clear line of sight.
