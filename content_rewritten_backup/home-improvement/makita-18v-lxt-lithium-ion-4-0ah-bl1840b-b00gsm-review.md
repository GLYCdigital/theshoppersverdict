Let's cut through the noise: the MAKITA BL1840B is the battery that most 18V LXT owners eventually buy, whether as a spare or a replacement for the older 3.0Ah packs. With 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly doing something right. But at roughly $80–$90 street price, is it actually worth it? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains a single BL1840B battery pack — no charger, no tool. At 4.0Ah capacity, this sits in the sweet spot of Makita's LXT lineup: above the entry-level 3.0Ah, below the heavy 5.0Ah and 6.0Ah packs. It uses 18650 lithium-ion cells arranged in a 10-cell, 2-parallel configuration, giving you 18V nominal output.

Physically, it's compact — measuring about 4.5 inches tall and weighing just 1.4 pounds. That's only 2 ounces heavier than the 3.0Ah version, which is impressive given the extra capacity. It slides onto any LXT tool with that satisfying, firm click. No wobble, no loose fit.

## Real-World Performance

The runtime jump from 3.0Ah to 4.0Ah is genuinely noticeable. On a circular saw, you'll get roughly 25% more cuts per charge. On an impact driver, expect to drive 30–40% more screws before the tool slows. The Star Protection electronics are the real differentiator here — they monitor cell temperature and voltage in real time, cutting power before the battery can be damaged by overheating or deep discharge.

One thing I appreciate: this battery doesn't suffer from the "memory effect" that plagued older NiCd packs. You can top it off mid-project without reducing long-term capacity. The cells also hold their charge well in storage — expect minimal self-discharge over several months.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The Amazon reviews tell a consistent story. One verified buyer noted, "I've had mine for two years of daily framing work and it still holds a full charge." Another mentioned, "Works flawlessly in my impact and circular saw — no noticeable power drop until the last few minutes."

The most common positive themes: reliability, compatibility across the LXT line, and the fact that it charges quickly — about 40 minutes on a standard 18V charger, faster on the dual-port rapid charger.

## Where It Falls Short

No battery is perfect, and the BL1840B has two clear drawbacks. First, there's no charge indicator on the pack itself. You'll need to pop it into a tool or charger to check remaining capacity — annoying when you're mid-project. Second, the price. You can find third-party 4.0Ah batteries for half the cost, but you sacrifice the Star Protection circuitry and Makita's build quality. If you're a professional, the premium is worth it. For a weekend DIYer, maybe not.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You already own Makita 18V LXT tools and need reliable runtime for medium-heavyduty work. You're a contractor or serious DIYer who uses tools daily and can't afford a battery failure on a job site. You want genuine Makita quality with warranty support (3-year limited).

**Skip it if:** You're a casual user who only drills a few holes a month — the cheaper 3.0Ah will suffice. Or if you're on a tight budget and willing to risk third-party batteries for your occasional projects.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Will this fit my older Makita 18V tools?**
A: Yes, it's fully backward compatible with all LXT tools, which have used the same battery interface since 2005. The slide-in design is universal across the line.

**Q: How long does it take to charge?**
A: On a standard DC18RC charger, roughly 40 minutes. With the rapid charger (DC18RD), about 25 minutes. Both are sold separately.

**Q: How does it compare to the 5.0Ah battery?**
A: The 5.0Ah gives about 25% more runtime but weighs 20% more and costs significantly more. For most users, the 4.0Ah is the better balance of weight, power, and price.
