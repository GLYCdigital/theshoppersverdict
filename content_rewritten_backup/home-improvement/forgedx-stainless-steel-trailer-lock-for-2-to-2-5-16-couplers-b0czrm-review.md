**If you tow anything more valuable than a rusted yard cart, you need a coupler lock that actually slows down a thief.** Most cheap locks are made of pot metal that shatters with one hammer hit. The FORGEDX stainless steel trailer lock is a different animal — but it's not without quirks.

## What You Get

The box contains the lock body, a 5/16-inch hardened steel locking pin, and two keys. That's it. No adapters, no mounting hardware, no nonsense. The lock body is machined from 304 stainless steel — not stamped, not coated, not painted. As shown in the product image, the design is a classic coupler pin lock: the pin slides through the coupler's pinhole, and the stainless body covers the opening so a thief can't access the pin with a pry bar.

The fit range is 2" to 2-5/16", which covers the vast majority of trailers, boat trailers, and RVs on the road today.

## Real-World Performance

**The stainless steel construction is the headline feature, and it delivers.** Multiple buyers note that this lock has survived harsh coastal salt air without a speck of rust. One reviewer wrote, *"I've gone through three locks in two years due to corrosion. This one has been on my boat trailer since March and still looks new."* That's the 304 stainless doing its job.

The 5/16-inch hardened pin is thick enough to resist bolt cutters, and the shrouded keyway design means a thief can't easily get a grip on the cylinder to twist it off. The locking mechanism itself feels solid — positive click, no slop, no wobble when engaged.

**Where it gets less rosy:** the key cylinder is not sealed. If you tow on gravel roads or in dusty conditions, grit will get into the keyway and make the key stick. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's a maintenance item. A shot of graphite powder every few months keeps it turning smoothly.

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings is genuinely earned. The most common positive threads in customer feedback:

## Where It Falls Short

**Fit tolerance is the real issue here.** The lock is machined to tight tolerances, which is great for security but bad for compatibility with couplers that have thick powder-coated surfaces. Several buyers report the pin won't slide through their 2-5/16" coupler without persuasion. One customer noted, *"Had to file down the hole on my new trailer — the paint was too thick. Once I did that, it fit perfectly."*

The other complaint is the key sticking issue, which I mentioned above. It's not a design flaw per se, but it's a known annoyance.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Boat owners** — if your trailer sees salt water or salt air, this is the lock to buy. Zinc locks will fail within a season.
- **RV owners** — the universal fit works for both standard ball sizes, so you're covered if you upgrade trailers.
- **Anyone who parks outside** — the stainless body won't develop the ugly rust streaks that painted locks do.

**Skip it if:** you have a coupler with an unusually thick powder coat finish, or if you want a combination lock (this is key-only). Also skip if you're on a tight budget — this costs more than the zinc alternatives, and you're paying for the material upgrade.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this lock fit a 2-5/16" coupler with a grease zerk?**
A: Usually yes, but the grease fitting can interfere with the pin path on some couplers. Check that your zerk doesn't protrude into the pinhole area before buying.

**Q: Can the keys be duplicated at a hardware store?**
A: Yes, they're standard key blanks. Get a spare made — losing the only key to a trailer lock is a nightmare.

**Q: Will this fit a "bullet" style coupler?**
A: No. This is designed for standard ball couplers with a horizontal pinhole. Bullet couplers use a completely different locking mechanism.

## The Verdict
