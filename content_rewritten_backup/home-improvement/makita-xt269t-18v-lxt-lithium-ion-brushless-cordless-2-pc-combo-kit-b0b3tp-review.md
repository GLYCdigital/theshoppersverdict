Let me be blunt: the Makita XT269T is not the cheapest 18V combo kit on Amazon. It's not even the flashiest. But if you're a serious DIYer or a working professional who's tired of burning through brushed motors, this two-piece set is the one to beat.

I've put both tools through drywall, studs, and a concrete block wall. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

Makita doesn't skimp here. The XT269T includes:

- **XPH14Z** — 18V LXT Brushless Hammer Drill (0-2,000 RPM, 0-28,000 BPM, 530 in.lbs. torque)
- **XDT16Z** — 18V LXT Brushless Impact Driver (0-3,400 RPM, 0-3,600 IPM, 1,090 in.lbs. torque)
- Two **5.0Ah batteries** (not the weak 1.5Ah starters you see in budget kits)
- **DC18RC** rapid charger (charges a 5.0Ah pack in about 45 minutes)
- Belt clip, driver bit, and a basic contractor bag

The missing piece: no hard case. For the price point, that's a minor annoyance, but the included soft bag is thin. Plan on buying a separate tool box if you're hard on gear.

## Performance: Where These Tools Earn Their Keep

The **XDT16 impact driver** is the star. With 1,090 in.lbs. of torque, it drove 3-inch GRK screws into pressure-treated lumber without a hint of bogging down. The brushless motor stays cool even after 100+ fasteners. The variable speed trigger has real finesse — I could feather it for delicate cabinet screws without stripping heads.

The **XPH14 hammer drill** is a different beast. It's heavier — around 4.2 pounds with the 5.0Ah battery — and the hammer mode vibrates more than I'd like for fine work. But in drill mode, it punched 1/2-inch spade bits through a 2x4 like butter. In hammer mode, a 1/4-inch masonry bit went through a concrete block in under 10 seconds. It's not a rotary hammer, but for occasional masonry it gets the job done.

One thing to know: both tools have **Star Protection** electronics. This means the tool communicates with the battery to prevent overload, overheating, and deep discharge. The downside? You're locked into Makita's proprietary battery system. Third-party batteries won't trigger the full safety suite, and you'll lose performance. That's the price of reliability.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws.

**First, the bulk.** The XPH14 is a full-size hammer drill. If you're doing overhead drywall anchors or working in tight attic spaces, you'll feel the weight. Makita makes the compact XFD131 if that's your priority — but you'll sacrifice hammer function.

**Second, the price.** This isn't a budget kit. You're paying for brushless technology, two 5.0Ah batteries, and a rapid charger. If you only need a drill for hanging pictures, save your money and get a cheaper brushed combo.

**Third, no case.** At this price, a hard case feels expected. The soft bag is functional but won't protect tools in a truck bed.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The serious DIYer** tackling decks, framing, or renovations — you'll use both tools enough to justify the cost
- **The professional** who needs reliability on a jobsite and doesn't want to replace tools yearly
- **The Makita loyalist** already invested in the LXT battery platform — this adds two workhorses to your lineup

**Skip it if** you're a weekend warrior with a $150 budget, or if you only need one tool, not a pair.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: Does this come with a charger?**
Yes — the DC18RC rapid charger is included, plus two 5.0Ah batteries. It'll top off a depleted pack in about 45 minutes.

**Q: Can the hammer drill handle concrete?**
For occasional work — yes. The XPH14 will drill anchor holes in block or brick up to 1/2-inch. For continuous slab drilling, rent a rotary hammer.

**Q: Are these the same tools sold separately?**
Yes, the XPH14 and XDT16 are both available individually. The kit saves you roughly 15-20% versus buying separately, plus you get the two-battery bundle.
