If you've ever stared at a game console, appliance, or office chair screw with that weird pin in the middle, you know the frustration. The NEIKO 10048A doesn't just solve that problem — it nukes it with 100 bits across 12 drive types. For under $25 (price varies), this is the kind of kit that turns a "can I fix this?" moment into a 30-second job.

**What's actually in the box**  
You get 100 individual 1-inch bits, organized in a clear plastic case with labeled slots. The breakdown: security Torx (T8H through T40H), hex (3/32" to 3/8"), spanner (4, 6, 8, 10), tri-wing (1 through 5), plus Phillips, slotted, square, and Pozidriv. Missing from the set: Torq-set and 12-point, but those are rare outside aerospace work. Each bit is stamped with its size — a small touch that saves real time when you're digging through a tray.

The bits are made from S2 steel, which is the same alloy used in higher-end professional sets. In practice, that means they resist cam-out on rusty screws and don't round off on tight Torx fasteners. I tested the T20H on a laptop with thread-locked screws; it seated fully and turned without slipping.

**Performance and build quality**  
The 1-inch length is the sweet spot for most work — long enough to reach recessed screws in electronics, short enough to fit in a standard magnetic bit holder. I used these with a ratcheting screwdriver and a drill chuck; both worked fine. The fit on security Torx bits is snug, not sloppy, which is critical when you're torquing on a stripped screw.

One thing to note: there's no bit holder in the kit. You'll need your own 1/4-inch driver or a drill with a chuck. This isn't a dealbreaker, but it's an extra cost if you're starting from scratch.

**FAQ**  
**1. Will these bits fit a standard impact driver?**  
Yes — they're 1/4-inch hex shanks. Use them in an impact driver or drill, but keep the speed low on small screws to avoid cam-out.

**2. Are the security Torx bits hollow?**  
Yes, they have the center hole for pin-in-Torx fasteners. That's what makes them "security" bits — they'll also work on standard Torx screws without the pin.

**3. What's the difference between tri-wing and Phillips?**  
Tri-wing has three curved slots instead of four. It's used on some game consoles and electronics. This set includes sizes 1-5, which covers most consumer devices.
