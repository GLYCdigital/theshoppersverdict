## Performance and Real-World Use

The Permex material matters. Standard rubber diaphragms degrade, harden, and lose their seal in 3-5 years, especially if your water has high chlorine or mineral content. Permex resists that breakdown significantly longer — facilities managers routinely report 7-10 year service life between swaps. For a part that costs less than a tank of gas, that's exceptional value.

Installation is genuinely simple. Turn off the water supply, remove the cap from the flushometer body (the large brass or chrome nut on top), lift out the old assembly, drop in the new one, and hand-tighten the cap. The filter screen catches sediment before it reaches the diaphragm, which is the leading cause of premature failure. If you've replaced one of these before, you'll have it done in five minutes. If you haven't, a YouTube video and ten minutes will get you there.

## FAQ

**Q: How do I know if my flushometer needs a new diaphragm?**
A: Symptoms include continuous running, random ghost flushing, weak flushes that don't clear the bowl, or water leaking from the cap area. If you have any of these, the diaphragm is the first thing to replace.

**Q: How long will this diaphragm last?**
A: With normal municipal water, expect 5-7 years. With hard water or high chlorine, you'll get closer to 3-5 years. The Permex material resists degradation far better than standard rubber.

**Q: Do I need any special tools?**
A: No. A flathead screwdriver to remove the cap is all that's required. The assembly drops in place and hand-tightening the cap is sufficient — do not overtighten, as that can warp the seal.
