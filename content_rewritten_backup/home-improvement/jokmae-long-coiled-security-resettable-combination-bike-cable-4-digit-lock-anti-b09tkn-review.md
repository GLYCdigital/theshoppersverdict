Let's be honest about bike locks: a cable lock isn't a U-lock, and it never will be. But the Jokmae Long Coiled Security Cable Lock doesn't pretend to be one. With a 4.3-star average across 215 ratings, it's carving out a specific niche — and doing it well. Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The Jokmae is a 6-foot coiled cable lock that stretches out to give you real flexibility. When coiled, it's compact enough to wrap around your seat post or stuff in a pannier. The cable itself is braided steel with a vinyl coating — the coating isn't just for looks; it protects your frame's paint from scratches, which is more than many budget locks bother with.

The headline feature is the resettable 4-digit combination. That's 10,000 possible combinations versus the 1,000 you get with a 3-digit lock. You set it via a small button on the lock body — it's a bit fiddly the first time, but you'll only do it once. The lock head is die-cast zinc, which feels more substantial than the cheap plastic you'll find on $10 locks.

## Real-World Performance

I've seen enough locks to know the difference between marketing specs and actual use. The Jokmae's coiled design is the standout feature here. It stretches to secure your frame and front wheel to a rack, or thread through both wheels and around a post. That 6-foot reach matters more than you'd think — it's the difference between locking to a bike rack properly and awkwardly trying to reach a signpost.

The 4-digit dials rotate smoothly, and the reset mechanism is genuinely functional. One buyer noted, *"I've had this lock for three months and use it daily. The combination hasn't stuck or jammed once, even after riding in light rain."* That tracks — the dials are sealed well enough to shrug off typical weather, though it's not rated for full submersion.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Digging through the feedback, three themes keep coming up:

- **The coiled design's practicality.** *"Being able to stretch it out to lock my helmet and both wheels without disconnecting anything is a game-changer,"* one reviewer wrote. The spring-back coiling means no tangling — a real problem with straight cables.
- **The resettable combo.** Buyers who've had fixed-code locks stolen appreciate the control. *"The previous lock had a code I didn't choose. This one is mine,"* noted a commuter.
- **Value for the price.** At this price point, buyers expect flimsy. The Jokmae's braided steel and zinc head exceed those expectations for most.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's not sugarcoat it. This is a **deterrent lock, not a theft-proof lock**. Cable locks — even braided steel ones — can be cut with bolt cutters in under a minute. That's physics, not a manufacturing flaw. If you're parking overnight in a high-theft urban area, pair this with a U-lock for the frame.

Two other honest gripes from the feedback:

- **The reset button is stiff.** Several users reported needing pliers or significant thumb pressure the first time. It loosens up, but it's annoying out of the box.
- **Length is listed as 6 feet, but the coiled measurement is misleading.** Fully stretched, it's closer to 5.5 feet of usable cable between lock heads. Still workable, but know what you're getting.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:**
- You're a casual commuter or errand-runner who locks up for 30 minutes at a time in low-to-moderate risk areas
- You want a secondary lock to secure your wheels or saddle while a U-lock handles the frame
- You hate carrying keys and want a resettable combo you'll actually remember

**Skip it if:**
- You park overnight in a city with known bike theft rings — you need hardened steel, not cable
- You expect a tool-free setup experience — the initial reset requires some elbow grease
- You need something for a high-value e-bike as your primary lock — don't risk it

## FAQ

**Q: Can I reset the combination to anything I want?**
A: Yes, any 4-digit code from 0000 to 9999. Use the small reset button on the lock body. It's stiff initially — use a pen or small screwdriver for leverage.

**Q: Will this fit around a thick bike rack post?**
A: The coiled cable stretches to about 5.5-6 feet, so it'll wrap around most standard racks and posts. For oversized posts, you might need to loop through your wheel instead of around the post.

**Q: Is it weather-resistant?**
A: The vinyl coating and sealed dials handle rain and road spray fine. It's not designed for submersion or saltwater exposure, so don't leave it out all winter in coastal areas.
