**What’s in the Box**

You get two complete flashlights, each with a rechargeable battery, a USB charging cable, and a lanyard. The build is aluminum alloy with a knurled grip — it feels solid, not like a cheap disposable. Each unit is roughly 6.5 inches long and weighs about a pound. That’s not pocket-carry territory; these are workhorse lights designed for camping, automotive work, or emergency use.

**Performance and Real-World Brightness**

In practice, the MILAOSHU puts out a genuinely useful beam. The LED array is bright enough to light up a backyard or illuminate under a car hood clearly. The zoom function adjusts from a wide flood to a tight spot, and it works smoothly. The advertised 12-hour runtime is technically possible, but only on the lowest setting. On max brightness, you’re looking at more like 2–3 hours before the battery drains. That’s a common gap between marketing and reality, but worth knowing before you rely on it for an overnight power outage.

The USB charging port is covered by a rubber flap — a nice touch for weather resistance, though it’s not fully waterproof. You can use it in light rain, but don’t submerge it.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The two-pack value is the headline. Multiple reviewers note that having a backup light is the real selling point. **Where It Falls Short**

The biggest issue is the lumen inflation. Expect somewhere in the 1,500–2,500 real-lumen range, not 90,000. That’s still solid for most tasks, but if you’re buying this specifically for extreme brightness, you’ll be let down. The zoom mechanism uses a moving lens that can collect dust over time — a few long-term reviewers mention it getting stiff after months of use. The battery indicator is a simple LED that goes from green to red; there’s no percentage readout, so you won’t know exactly how much juice is left. Finally, the included USB cable is short and basic — plan to use your own.
