**Bottom Line Up Front:** The Intermatic DT200LT is the workhorse of landscape lighting timers. It doesn't have an app, it won't sync to your phone, and the programming button sequence will make you consult the manual twice. But if you need reliable, astronomically-correct dusk-to-dawn control for outdoor lighting — without dependence on a Wi-Fi network — this metal-bodied timer delivers for years. Given its 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews, buyers consistently confirm it earns its place.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the DT200LT timer itself, pre-mounted in a rugged die-cast aluminum enclosure with a clear, gasketed cover. The unit measures roughly 7.5 x 5 x 4 inches and weighs a solid 3.5 pounds — this is not a flimsy plastic box. The package includes a comprehensive instruction manual, mounting hardware, and knockouts for standard 1/2-inch conduit fittings. Notably, there's no external transformer needed; the timer runs on 120-277 VAC directly.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The standout feature is the astronomic function. Once you set your latitude and longitude (or use the pre-programmed US/Canada zone table), the timer calculates daily sunrise and sunset times and adjusts your lighting schedule automatically. This means your lights come on at 6:52 PM in July and 4:38 PM in December without you touching a button. It's genuinely set-and-forget.

The 40-amp contactor rating is the unsung hero. Most competing timers max out at 15 amps, which forces you to buy a separate contactor for large transformer loads. Here, you can wire up to two 20-amp circuits directly. One reviewer noted, *"I'm running a 900-watt transformer and a 500-watt pump off this single unit. No issues in two years."* That tracks with the build quality — the internal relay is a proper magnetic contactor, not a cheap solid-state switch.

The digital display is bright and readable in daylight, though you'll need to open the clear cover to press the programming buttons. The unit holds its schedule through power outages thanks to a built-in capacitor backup — though that backup lasts roughly 4 hours, so a prolonged outage will require reprogramming. A handful of reviewers mentioned this as a minor annoyance.

## What Buyers Praise

The consistency of the astronomic tracking earns near-universal applause. One verified buyer wrote, *"I've had cheap timers drift by 20 minutes over a season. This thing is within a minute of actual sunset, every day."* Multiple reviewers with seasonal lighting in northern climates (Minnesota, Washington) confirmed the auto-adjustment handles the dramatic day-length swings without issue.

The weatherproofing is the second most-cited positive. The metal housing with gasketed door has survived coastal salt air, direct rain, and freezing temperatures per multiple long-term reviewers. It's UL-listed for wet locations, which is rare at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

The programming interface is the biggest friction point. The DT200LT uses a multi-button sequence (SET, +, −, and MODE) with a menu system that requires scrolling through options. It's not intuitive. Expect to reference the manual for the first few programming sessions. Several reviewers noted the button response can feel sluggish if you press too quickly.

There's no battery backup beyond that 4-hour capacitor, so a multi-day outage wipes your settings. And if you're expecting smart-home integration — no Wi-Fi, no Z-Wave, no app. This is strictly a dumb-but-reliable device.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want dependable astronomic scheduling for landscape lights, pool pumps, or signage without relying on home Wi-Fi or cloud services. It's ideal for homeowners who program once and want zero maintenance for years.

**Skip it if:** You need phone control, voice assistant integration, or you're uncomfortable with basic electrical wiring. Also skip if your setup draws less than 15 amps and you want a cheaper, simpler plug-in timer.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it work with LED landscape lighting transformers?**
A: Yes — the timer switches the line-voltage side (120V) feeding your transformer. Since the contactor is mechanical (not a TRIAC), it handles LED transformers' inrush current without issue.

**Q: How accurate is the astronomic calculation?**
A: Within a few minutes of actual civil twilight, provided your latitude/longitude is set correctly. The unit updates daily, so it stays accurate year-round.

**Q: Can it control 240V loads?**
A: Yes, the DT200LT is rated for 120-277 VAC, so it handles split-phase 240V circuits for pool pumps or irrigation systems.
