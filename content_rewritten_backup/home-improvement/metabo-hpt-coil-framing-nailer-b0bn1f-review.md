If you're framing walls, sheathing roofs, or building decks, the nailer you choose can make or break your day. The Metabo HPT Coil Framing Nailer (Model NR90AESY) is a serious contender in the crowded 15-gauge coil nailer market. With a 4.5-star average from over 225 Amazon buyers, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right tool for *your* specific needs? Let's break down what you're actually getting.

## What's in the Box and What You're Working With

The kit arrives with the nailer itself, a heavy-duty carrying case, safety glasses, a hex wrench, and an oil bottle. That's the essential starter set—no surprises. The tool weighs in at roughly 7.5 pounds, which is on the lighter side for a coil framing nailer. That's a real advantage when you're working overhead on a roof or holding it at arm's length all day.

It's a 15-degree coil nailer, meaning it accepts wire-collated nails from 2 to 3-1/4 inches. This is the sweet spot for most residential and light commercial framing tasks. The magazine holds 150 nails, which cuts down on reloading frequency significantly compared to stick nailers.

## Performance and Real-World Gripes

The headline feature is the "Selective Drive" switch. You can toggle between full sequential and contact (bump) firing modes. For a framer doing production work, bump mode is non-negotiable. The switch is easy to reach and flips without taking your hand off the tool. The motor drives nails flush with authority, and the tool has enough power to handle dense engineered lumber like LVL without leaving proud nails.

However, let's be honest about downsides. The first thing you'll notice is the **safety trip is stiff**. Out of the box, it requires a deliberate push to actuate. Some users report this loosens up, but others find it slows down their rhythm initially. Also, the rubber grip, while comfortable, tends to absorb oil and can get slick if you're heavy-handed with lubrication. It's a minor annoyance, but worth knowing.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating isn't an accident. Digging through the reviews, a few themes dominate. First, **reliability**. One reviewer stated, "I've put over 100,000 nails through this thing on two house builds and it still runs like new." Another echoed the sentiment: "It jams far less than my old Bostitch. I can count on one hand the jams in a year of weekend use."

The **no-mar tip** is also a consistent point of praise. It protects the work surface, which is crucial when you're doing finish-adjacent work or setting up forms. And the adjustment for nail depth is tool-free, which is faster than fumbling for a wrench on a ladder.

## Where It Falls Short

Every tool has a flaw. For this Metabo HPT, the biggest complaint is the **weight distribution**. While it's light overall, the coil magazine sits on the side, making the tool feel top-heavy when the magazine is full. It takes some getting used to if you're coming from a stick nailer.

Another issue: **dry-fire lockout is absent**. This means you can fire the tool with an empty magazine, which can damage the driver blade over time. It's a cost-cutting measure, but for the price point, it's a noticeable omission. You'll need to develop the discipline to check your nail count. Finally, the **case is bulky**. It's durable, but it takes up a lot of space in a truck bed or gang box.

## Who Should Buy This?

This nailer is ideal for **DIYers tackling a large project** like a shed, deck, or barn. The learning curve is manageable, and the price-to-performance ratio makes it a smarter buy than a cheap no-name tool that will die mid-project.

It's also a solid choice for **contractors who need a back-up nailer** that won't break the bank. It's not a replacement for a high-end Senco or Hitachi (now Metabo HPT) flagship, but it holds its own. If you're a pro doing production framing *every single day*, you might want to look at a heavier-duty stick nailer for the balance and the dry-fire protection.

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: Does it come with a case?**
A: Yes, it includes a hard plastic carrying case that fits the gun and essential accessories like the hex wrench and oil.

**Q: Can it handle 3-1/4 inch nails?**
A: Yes, the depth adjustment and power are sufficient for driving 3-1/4" nails into solid lumber, including pressure-treated wood.

**Q: Is it for framing or roofing?**
A: It's a framing nailer. It uses 15-degree wire-collated nails. For roofing, you'd want a coil roofing nailer with a different magazine design.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're a serious DIYer or a semi-pro who needs a reliable, powerful framing nailer that won't cause hand fatigue. The 150-nail magazine and selective drive switch make it a productivity booster.

**Skip it if:** You require a dry-fire lockout to protect your tool, or if you're a professional who needs the perfect balance of a stick nailer for all-day use.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but for the price, it's one of the best values in the coil framing nailer category. It's a workhorse that respects your money.
