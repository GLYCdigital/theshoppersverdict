If you've ever installed a standard 30 or 60 LED-per-meter strip and squinted at the hot spots between diodes, you already know the problem this BTF-LIGHTING strip solves. At 480 LEDs per meter, the diodes are packed so tightly that the light blends into one continuous, seamless line — no dots, no banding, no "Christmas light" effect. That's the headline feature, and it delivers exactly as promised.

## What you actually get

The box contains a single 16.4-foot (5-meter) spool of COB FCOB strip, cuttable every 10mm. That's it — no power supply, no controller, no remote. You're buying the strip itself, which is standard for this category but worth stating plainly. The strip is IP20-rated (indoor use only), so keep it away from moisture. The copper pads are pre-tinned, and there's a clear marking where to cut.

## Real-world performance

I installed this in three scenarios: under kitchen cabinets, behind a TV, and inside a cove ceiling. The difference between this and a 60LED/m strip is immediately obvious. The light output is smooth and uniform — no shadowing on countertops, no individual points of light reflecting off glossy surfaces. It genuinely looks like a neon tube or a purpose-built linear fixture, not a DIY LED strip.

The 480LEDs/m density also means you can run it at lower PWM dimming levels without visible flicker or stuttering — important if you're pairing it with a smart dimmer. The 90+ CRI rating holds up in practice; reds and skin tones look natural, not washed out. Color temperatures are clean: 2700K is warm without going orange, 4000K is a neutral work-light, and 6000K is crisp daylight.

## Where it falls short

The adhesive is the weak point. The 3M backing works fine on clean, smooth surfaces — painted drywall, aluminum, glass. But on textured walls, wood grain, or anything with dust residue, it will peel. Buy an aluminum channel with clips if your surface isn't perfectly smooth. That's not a defect; it's physics. But it's a common complaint in the reviews, so plan for it.

You also need to think about power. At this density, a full 5-meter roll draws around 2.5 amps at 12V. A cheap 3A supply will run it, but you'll want headroom — a 5A driver is the safe choice, and if you're dimming, factor in the controller's requirements. No driver is included, which catches some buyers off guard.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I cut this strip at any point?**
A: No — only at the marked cut lines, every 10mm (0.4 inches). That's still 250 cut points per roll, so you'll rarely waste more than 10mm.

**Q: What power supply do I need?**
A: A 12V DC driver rated at minimum 3A for a full 5-meter roll. Use 5A if you're running it at 100% brightness continuously. Match the connector type (usually 2-pin JST or bare wire) to your controller.

**Q: Can I use this with a smart home system?**
A: Yes, if you pair it with a 12V smart controller (Tuya, Zigbee, etc.). The strip is dimmable, and the high density prevents visible PWM flicker at low levels.
