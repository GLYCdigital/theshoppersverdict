If you're flattening rough lumber for a tabletop or squaring up edges before glue-up, a jointer is the machine that separates "good enough" from "actually square." The CRAFTSMAN 10-amp benchtop jointer is the budget-friendly entry point that most hobbyists land on — and at 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But "benchtop" comes with compromises, and you need to know exactly what they are before you buy.

**What you actually get in the box:** The jointer itself (about 45 pounds), a 6-inch cutting head, a 45°-90° tilting fence, a push block, a 2-1/2-inch dust chute, and a hex wrench for knife adjustments. No stand — this is designed to clamp or bolt to your workbench. The fence locks down with two levers, and the cutterhead guard flips up for bevel work. Setup takes about 20 minutes: mount it, align the outfeed table to the knives, and you're cutting.

**Real-world performance:** The 10-amp motor spins the cutterhead at 22,000 cuts per minute, and that speed matters. On red oak and maple, the cuts come out glassy smooth — no chatter, no tear-out, even on figured grain. The 6-inch width is the sweet spot for cabinet doors, drawer fronts, and edge-gluing stock. The fence locks securely and holds its angle through repetitive passes, which is critical when you're jointing six identical boards for a panel.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the aluminum tables are flat out of the box and stay flat. "Took it out, set the knives, and it cut perfectly square on the first try," one reviewer notes. Another says, "For the price, this is unbeatable — I jointed 50 board feet of walnut and it never bogged down." The motor has real torque; it won't stall on 6-inch-wide hardwoods unless you take too aggressive a cut.

**Where it falls short — be honest with yourself:** The tables are short. At roughly 27 inches total, you can't joint an 8-foot board without serious infeed/outfeed support. If you try, the board will rock over the cutterhead and you'll get a snipe at the ends. Buy roller stands or build an extension — this is the #1 complaint in reviews, and it's legitimate.

Knife setup is also fiddly. The knives sit in the cutterhead and you adjust them with the included hex wrench, but there's no indexed quick-set system. Plan on 30-45 minutes the first time you change or align knives. And the dust chute is 2-1/2 inches, which means most 4-inch dust collectors won't connect without an adapter — plan for that.

**Who should buy this:** The hobbyist woodworker with a dedicated bench and a dust collector. If you're building furniture, cabinets, or doing serious joinery, this jointer will handle 90% of what you need at a fraction of the cost of a floor model. It's also solid for a small shop where space is tight — it tucks under a bench when not in use. Skip it if you're primarily working with boards over 6 feet long, if you need 8-inch capacity for thicker stock, or if you're buying your first jointer and don't have a bench to mount it on.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Can this jointer handle 8-foot boards?**
A: Not well without support. The tables are short, so long boards will rock. Use infeed/outfeed roller stands, and take lighter passes (1/16 inch or less) to minimize snipe.

**Q: Is the fence square out of the box?**
A: Most buyers report it's close, but check it with a square and adjust the stops. The fence locks at 45° and 90° with positive stops, and you can fine-tune both.

**Q: What's the actual cutting depth per pass?**
A: The manual says 1/8 inch max, but realistically you'll want 1/16 inch or less on hardwoods. The motor handles it, but aggressive cuts on long boards invite snipe.

**The Verdict:**
**Buy it if** you're a hobbyist who needs a reliable, compact jointer for furniture-scale work and you have a bench, a dust collector, and patience for knife setup. It's the best value in the benchtop category, and the 4.4-star rating is earned.

**Skip it if** you're a pro, you work with long stock regularly, or you want a machine with a quick-set knife system. This jointer rewards careful setup and punishes shortcuts — but for the money, nothing else at this price point cuts this clean.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong performer with real limitations, priced right for the serious hobbyist.
