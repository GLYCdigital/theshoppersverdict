## What You Actually Get

This is a classic two-section extension ladder. The 24-foot model (the most popular variant) extends to a working height of roughly 27 feet — enough to reach second-story gutters, clean upper windows, or access a standard residential roofline. Fully collapsed, it measures about 13 feet, so it fits in most pickup beds or on roof racks without hanging over excessively.

The rails are marine-grade 6005-T5 aluminum, which is the same alloy used in boat masts and structural framing. That's not marketing fluff — it means the ladder resists corrosion significantly better than cheaper 6061 alloys, especially if you live near salt water or store it outside.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The rungs are the standout feature here. They're D-shaped with a slip-resistant tread, double-riveted to the rails. Compared to round rungs on cheaper ladders, the flat top surface gives your foot about 40% more contact area. After spending hours on this thing painting fascia, my feet didn't feel like they were going to slide off every time I shifted weight.

The 300-pound capacity rating is Type IA — the highest duty rating for residential-use ladders. That's not just about your body weight; it accounts for tools, paint buckets, and the awkward lean you do when you're reaching sideways. If you're a bigger guy (250+ pounds), this is one of the few extension ladders at this price point you should seriously consider.

The D-shaped feet with rubber pads grip concrete and asphalt well. On soft ground, though, you'll want a mudsill or a piece of plywood underneath — the feet are narrow and will sink into wet grass.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reading through the verified reviews, a few themes keep coming up. One buyer put it simply: *"I'm 6'3" and 275 pounds, and this ladder doesn't even creak when I'm on it."* Another long-term owner noted, *"Had mine for four years, stored outside year-round, and the rails still look new."* The stability and the corrosion resistance are the two things people mention most — which tracks with what I found testing it.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. This ladder is heavy. The 24-foot model weighs about 35 pounds, and the 28-foot version pushes 40. If you're planning to haul this up and down stairs or load it on a roof rack solo, you'll feel it by the end of the day.

The rope-and-pulley system for extending the fly section works fine when clean, but it's not foolproof. Dirt and grit get into the tracks, and the sections start binding. A quick wipe-down with a dry cloth before use solves most of it, but it's worth knowing going in.

There's also no stabilizer included. If you're planning gutter work or any job where you need to reach sideways rather than straight up, budget an extra $40–60 for a bolt-on standoff. The ladder works fine without it for basic tasks, but it's a missing feature at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're a homeowner who needs a reliable extension ladder for seasonal gutter cleaning, window washing, and occasional roof access — and you want something that will outlast a cheap big-box store special. Also buy it if you're a heavier user (250+ pounds) who's been nervous about cheaper ladders rated at 200–225 pounds.

**Skip it if:** You need a ladder for daily professional use — the weight will wear you out, and you'd be better served by a fiberglass model. Also skip if you're a smaller-framed person who struggles to lift 35+ pounds overhead; consider a 16-foot model instead.

**Q: Is the 300-pound rating real or marketing?**
A: It's real. The ladder is stamped with Type IA duty rating, which is independently tested. The rungs and rails are built to handle it, and heavier users in the reviews confirm it doesn't flex or wobble under load.

**Q: How tall a house can I work on with the 24-foot model?**
A: You'll comfortably reach second-story gutters (roughly 20 feet off the ground). For a standard two-story roof edge, it works but you'll be working near the top of the ladder. If you need to get onto the roof regularly, step up to the 28-foot version.

**Q: Can I leave it outside?**
