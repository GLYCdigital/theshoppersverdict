The base footprint is compact — about 7 by 5 inches — so it won't swallow your desk real estate. Setup takes under two minutes: plug in the adapter, position the arm, press the touch controls.

**Performance and build quality**

The Sunturalux branding isn't marketing fluff. This lamp uses a proprietary LED array designed to minimize blue light spikes and reduce PWM flicker — the invisible strobing that cheap LEDs use for dimming, which triggers headaches and eye fatigue. In practical terms, the light feels steady and natural even at lower brightness levels. That's a genuine differentiator against $25 Amazon basics lamps that pulse at 30% brightness.

Brightness control runs from a dim 10% up to a solid 100%, and you get three color temperature presets: warm (3000K), neutral (4000K), and cool (6000K). The warm setting works well for winding down after work; the cool setting mimics daylight for detail work. The touch controls on the base respond instantly — no lag, no accidental double-taps.

Build quality is better than most desk lamps in this category. The aluminum arm holds its position without drooping, and the pivot joints feel tight. The base has a rubberized bottom that grips most surfaces, though it's not weighted heavily. If your desk shakes when you type aggressively, the lamp can shift slightly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the eye-care feature actually reduce eye strain?**
A: It reduces two known culprits: blue light intensity and PWM flicker. Both are linked to eye fatigue and headaches. It won't fix poor posture or screen glare, but it's a meaningful improvement over standard LED lamps.

**Q: Can this lamp replace a ring light for video calls?**
A: Not really. The directional head is designed for task lighting, not flattering face illumination. You'd need to bounce it off a wall for usable video call light, and even then it's not ideal.

**Q: How does it compare to a generic LED desk lamp?**
A: The difference is in the light quality. Generic lamps often use cheap LEDs with visible flicker and harsh color rendering. The H6 uses a higher-grade array with steadier output and better color accuracy — you notice it within the first hour of use.
