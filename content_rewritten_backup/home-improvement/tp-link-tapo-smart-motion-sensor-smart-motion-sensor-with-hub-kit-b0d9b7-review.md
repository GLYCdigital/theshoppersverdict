# TP-Link Tapo Smart Motion Sensor Review: The Missing Piece in Your Tapo Setup

If you've already committed to the Tapo ecosystem, this motion sensor is a no-brainer addition. If you haven't, the hub requirement changes the math entirely. Here's what 220 buyers at a 4.4-star average are actually saying — and what they're not telling you.

## What's Actually in the Box

The package includes the sensor unit, the Tapo H100 Smart Hub (which doubles as a Wi-Fi range extender), a USB power cable for the hub, mounting tape, and mounting screws. The sensor itself is compact — roughly 4.3 × 4.3 × 4.3 cm — and weighs just 76 grams. You also get a magnetic ball-joint mount that lets you tilt and rotate the sensor about 30 degrees in any direction after it's mounted.

Important: this is a *kit* with the hub. If you already own a Tapo H100 hub, you can buy the sensor standalone for about half the price. Check which version you're ordering.

## Performance: Range That Actually Delivers

The headline spec is 9 meters of detection range with a 120° horizontal field of view. In real-world testing, that's not marketing fluff. One reviewer noted, "I mounted it in the corner of my living room and it catches my dog walking across the far side of the room — about 8 meters away. Impressive for the price."

The sensor uses passive infrared (PIR) technology with a sensitivity setting you can adjust in the app from 1 (low) to 10 (high). At default sensitivity, it reliably detects human movement but ignores small pets under 15 kg. Crank it to 10 and you'll get triggered by your cat — and potentially by heat vents or direct sunlight. Start at 5 and adjust.

Detection speed matters for automations. The sensor polls at roughly 1-second intervals, which means lights trigger almost instantly — no noticeable lag when walking into a dark room. The hub processes automations locally, so even if your internet drops, your lights still come on.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The battery life is the standout feature. Powered by two CR2450 coin cells, the sensor runs for up to 18 months per set. One long-term reviewer confirmed: "Had mine for 14 months, still at 80% battery in the app. No false triggers, no connection drops."

Setup takes under five minutes using the Tapo app — pair the hub, scan the QR code on the sensor, mount it. The app also shows a battery percentage, signal strength, and a live "last triggered" timestamp, which helps you fine-tune placement.

The magnetic mount earns consistent praise. You can detach the sensor to re-aim it without pulling the base off the wall, and the included 3M tape holds firmly on painted drywall. Screws are included for more permanent mounting.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint across reviews is the hub requirement. One frustrated buyer put it plainly: "Wish I'd known I needed the H100 hub. Bought this thinking it worked with Wi-Fi directly. Now I have to spend another $40."

There's no ambient light sensor built in. So if you want "turn on lights only when it's dark," you'll need the separate Tapo T100 light sensor ($15) or you'll need to program time-based schedules in the app.

Detection range drops to about 4–5 meters if you mount the sensor high on a wall (above 2.5 meters) because the PIR lens is angled downward. For full 9-meter coverage, mount it at chest height, which is less ideal if you have kids or pets.

Finally, the sensor is limited to Tapo's ecosystem. It won't work with Alexa as a standalone trigger for non-Tapo devices, and there's no HomeKit support. If you're mixing ecosystems, this isn't your sensor.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You already have a Tapo H100 hub or plan to build out a Tapo smart home. You want reliable, low-latency motion detection for lighting automations, security alerts, or triggering cameras. You'll be mounting it at chest height for maximum range.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a standalone Wi-Fi motion sensor, you need HomeKit or Zigbee compatibility, or you want daylight-aware automations without buying extra sensors.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work without the Tapo hub?**
No. The sensor communicates over the sub-GHz protocol to the H100 hub, not directly to Wi-Fi. You must have the hub.

**Q: Can I use this to trigger an Amazon Echo or smart speaker?**
Only indirectly. The sensor can trigger Tapo smart plugs, lights, and cameras. It can expose a virtual switch to Alexa, but setup is clunky and requires the Tapo skill to be linked.

**Q: How many sensors can one hub support?**
Up to 64 Tapo sensors and devices per H100 hub. You won't hit that limit in a typical home.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** ⭐⭐⭐⭐

The TP-Link Tapo Smart Motion Sensor is a well-built, responsive sensor with genuinely impressive range and battery life — *if* you're already in the Tapo ecosystem. The hub requirement and lack of a light sensor are real friction points, but for the price, it outperforms comparable Zigbee sensors that cost twice as much. Buy it as part of a Tapo starter kit, not as a standalone experiment.
