**The Short Version**

If you're tired of lugging a sloshing, heavy stockpot from the sink to the stove, a pot filler isn't a luxury—it's a back-saver. The Delta Brushed Nickel Modern Pot Filler is a solid mid-tier option that gets the job done, but it comes with a few installation caveats you need to know before you cut into your tile backsplash.

**What You're Actually Getting**

This is a wall-mounted, dual-joint faucet designed to swing over your cooktop. In the box you get the main faucet body, the dual-arm swing joint, a brass male adapter, a stainless steel braided supply line, mounting hardware, and an Allen wrench for the set screws. The brushed nickel finish is applied over a zinc die-cast body—not solid brass—which is worth noting at this price point. The spout measures 22.5 inches from the wall plate to the tip, with a 10-inch vertical clearance, which comfortably clears most stockpots and even a 12-inch Dutch oven.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The brushed nickel finish is the standout feature here. It resists fingerprints far better than chrome, and it matches Delta's other Brushed Nickel fixtures almost perfectly—important if you're coordinating finishes across the kitchen. The dual-joint swing arm moves smoothly with a detent that holds position, so the spout doesn't drift while you're filling. Flow rate is capped at a sensible 1.8 GPM, which fills a 7-quart pot in roughly 40 seconds. That's not blazing fast, but it's practical.

The quarter-turn handle is ergonomic, with a smooth, precise action. One thing buyers consistently note: it does NOT come with a separate valve shutoff at the wall. The faucet itself is the shutoff. That means if the unit ever drips, you're turning off water at the main supply. That's a design choice, not a defect, but it's worth knowing.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated praise in the 200+ reviews centers on the finish and the swing action. One verified buyer wrote: *"The brushed nickel is flawless—no fingerprints, no water spots. The arm glides and stays exactly where I put it."* Another noted the installation was straightforward *"if you have basic plumbing skills,"* particularly appreciating the included braided supply line that eliminated a trip to the hardware store. Several reviewers with gas ranges specifically noted the spout clears the burner grates without issue.

**Where It Falls Short**

Now the honest part. The most common complaint is the set screw placement. The Allen screws that hold the spout to the wall plate are on the underside and require a tiny 3/32" wrench. If you have thick fingers or limited clearance between the faucet and the backsplash, this is fiddly. One reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great faucet, but plan 15 extra minutes just for those two set screws."*

The second issue is the lack of a swivel joint at the base. The entire arm swings as one unit, which means you can't angle the spout independently of the arm. It's fine for the standard pot-on-rear-burner position, but if you have a wide griddle or a stockpot on a front burner, you'll find the reach slightly short. The 22.5-inch reach is decent, but it's not the 27-inch reach you'd get from a premium model like the Brizo or even some Delta's higher-end pro lines.

Finally, the zinc body. It's not a durability concern for a fixture that's rarely touched, but if you're the type who bangs heavy pots into the spout, you'll want to be careful. A solid brass unit will laugh that off; this one could dent.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The Weekend DIYer** with basic plumbing experience. The included parts and standard 1/2-inch NPT connections make this a manageable afternoon project.
- **The Gas Range Owner** who does serious stockpot cooking (soups, broths, pasta water) and wants the spout to swing over the front or rear burners.
- **The Finish Matcher** who already has Delta Brushed Nickel elsewhere and wants a perfect color match.

**Skip It If:**

- You need a long reach for oversized cookware. Look for a model with a 26-inch or longer spout.
- You want a dedicated shutoff valve at the wall. This faucet doesn't have one.
- You have a tight clearance between the backsplash and the wall—the set screws are a pain.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this faucet come with a shutoff valve?**
A: No. The faucet handle is the only flow control. If you want to isolate the water supply for maintenance, you'll need to install a separate valve in the wall before the supply line.

**Q: Will this fit on a 1/2-inch copper pipe?**
A: Yes, the included male adapter threads onto standard 1/2-inch NPT or 1/2-inch copper stub-out. For PEX, you'll need a PEX-to-NPT adapter.

**Q: What's the actual spout height and reach?**
A: The spout extends 22.5 inches from the wall plate and sits 10 inches above the counter surface. It clears most stockpots and standard griddles.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, good-looking pot filler that matches Delta's Brushed Nickel line, and you're comfortable with a basic DIY install. **Skip it if** you need a longer reach, want a wall shutoff valve, or prefer a solid brass body for heavy daily abuse.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A strong performer with a few installation quirks and a reach that won't satisfy everyone, but at this price, it's a smart buy for most home cooks.
