If you've ever stood in a bathroom that smells like a gym locker room an hour after a shower, you know the problem isn't the exhaust fan's motor — it's the CFM rating. Most builders install 50–80 CFM units that are underpowered for the space. Panasonic's WhisperGreen Select fixes that with a dial on the motor housing that lets you choose 110, 130, or 150 cubic feet per minute. That's the headline feature, and for once, it's not marketing fluff.

**What's in the box**  
You get the fan body (galvanized steel housing), a blower wheel assembly, a grille (white, paintable), and a mounting bracket. The unit ships with both the SmartAction sensors pre-wired — a humidity sensor and a motion sensor — plus a standard wall switch connection. No light kit, no nightlight, no Bluetooth. This is a pure ventilation appliance. The housing measures 13.5" x 11.5" — check your joist spacing before you cut drywall, because it's not a 9" round retrofit.

**Performance and real-world use**  
On the 110 CFM setting, this fan moves air at a whisper — 0.3 sones, which is quieter than a refrigerator hum. Crank it to 150 CFM and you get 1.0 sone, still conversation-level quiet. Compare that to a typical builder-grade fan at 3–4 sones, and you'll understand why people pay the premium. The DC motor is the real deal: it draws about 8 watts on low, which is roughly 75% less energy than an equivalent AC motor.

The humidity sensor is the standout. Set it to trigger at 60% relative humidity, and the fan runs until the room dries out — no timer guessing, no leaving it on for 20 minutes and hoping. The motion sensor is a nice perk for guests who forget to flip the switch, though it does add a 5-second delay on activation that some buyers find annoying.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
Scrolling through the 210 ratings (4.2 average), the recurring theme is "quiet" and "effective." One reviewer wrote: *"I replaced a 15-year-old Nutone that sounded like a jet engine. This thing is silent on low — I actually checked twice to make sure it was running."* Another noted: *"The humidity sensor alone is worth the upgrade. My mirror stays clear now, no towel over the vent trick needed."* Multiple installers commented on the solid build — thicker gauge steel than competitors, and the blower wheel doesn't wobble at high speed.

**Where it falls short**  
The installation is not beginner-friendly. The housing has a tight clearance for the sensor wiring, and the mounting bracket requires precise alignment. If you're retrofitting an existing 4" duct, you'll need to adapt — the included connector is 4" round, but the housing itself is designed for new construction or open-access attics. One frustrated buyer said: *"Took me 4 hours because the bracket instructions were unclear. Not a weekend DIY project unless you've done fans before."* Also, the lack of a built-in light is a miss at this price point — you'll spend another $50–100 on a separate fixture if your bathroom doesn't already have one.

**Who should buy this**  
- **The moisture-conscious homeowner**: If you have mold issues or constantly fogged mirrors, the humidity sensor pays for itself in prevention.  
- **The master bath remodeler**: You're already spending $15K on tile — the $150–200 for this fan is a rounding error.  
- **The sound-sensitive sleeper**: If the fan is near a bedroom, the 0.3 sone low setting is a blessing.  

**Who should skip it**  
- **The budget renovator**: A basic Panasonic WhisperCeiling (non-select) at half the price moves 110 CFM at 1.0 sone. You lose the sensors and adjustability, but you save $80.  
- **DIY novices**: If you've never wired a fan or cut drywall, hire a pro — the sensor wiring and bracket alignment will frustrate you.

**FAQ**  

**Q: Can I use this on a standard wall switch, or does it need a smart switch?**  
A: Standard switch works fine. The sensors are built-in and activate automatically. You can also wire it to run continuously at low speed and ramp up on humidity — a nice feature for stuffy closets.

**Q: Does the 150 CFM setting require a bigger duct?**  
A: No — it's designed for 4" round duct up to 150 CFM. But if your duct run exceeds 15 feet, bump to 6" duct for best performance.

**Q: How loud is the motion sensor click?**  
A: There's a soft click when the sensor engages (about as loud as a light switch). Some buyers noticed it; most didn't care.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you want a bathroom fan you'll never think about again — quiet, smart, and efficient. The sensor automation and adjustable CFM are genuinely useful, not gimmicks.  
**Skip it if** you're on a strict budget or need a light fixture combo. The WhisperCeiling line offers 90% of the performance at 60% of the price.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Loses half a star for the frustrating install and missing light option. But for performance and quiet operation, it's the best ceiling fan in its class. If you're handy or willing to pay for installation, this is a buy-it-once product.
