## The cordless grinder that finally killed the extension cord

I've tested dozens of cordless grinders over the years, and most feel like toys compared to their corded counterparts. The Milwaukee M18 Fuel 4-1/2/5 with electronic braking is the first one that made me genuinely consider ditching my corded DeWalt. It's not perfect — nothing is — but the gap between cordless and corded just closed in a meaningful way.

## What's in the box

You're getting the bare tool: the grinder itself, a side handle, a wrench, and a guard. No battery, no charger. If you're not already in the M18 ecosystem, factor in the cost of at least one High Output battery — this grinder is hungry, and the standard 5.0 batteries will leave you frustrated. The 5/8-11 spindle is standard, so your existing wheels and discs fit without adapters.

## Performance: The real story

The headline feature here is the electronic brake. Pull the trigger, release it, and the wheel stops in under two seconds. That doesn't sound dramatic until you've used a grinder that coasts for 10+ seconds after every cut. It's a safety feature that also speeds up your workflow — no waiting, no accidental wheel contact when you set the tool down.

The variable speed dial is the sleeper feature. You get 2,800 to 8,500 RPM, and unlike some grinders where the low end is gutless, this one maintains torque. Wire brushing stainless steel at 3,500 RPM without the wheel bogging down? That works here. Flap disc finishing at 4,000 RPM? Also works. This isn't just a marketing dial — it genuinely expands what the tool can do.

Power-wise, the POWERSTATE brushless motor punches above its weight class. On 1/4-inch steel with a cutoff wheel, it's not quite a corded 13-amp grinder, but it's close enough that you'll stop noticing the difference after the first few cuts. With a 12.0 High Output battery, I got through 15+ minutes of continuous cutting before the power started to fade.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't an accident. Owners repeatedly mention the brake as a game-changer. One reviewer wrote: *"The brake alone is worth the price. I've had grinders take forever to spin down and accidentally gouge workpieces. This stops instantly."* Another noted: *"I've used this professionally for 6 months. It keeps up with my corded Makita on 90% of jobs."* Runtime with the bigger batteries gets consistent shoutouts too.

## Where it falls short

The weight. At 5.3 pounds with a 12.0 battery, this is a hefty tool. Overhead grinding or working on ceiling tracks will fatigue your arms faster than a corded unit. It's the price you pay for the power.

Battery hunger is real. With the included 5.0 batteries, you'll get maybe 8-10 minutes of heavy cutting. You need the High Output line — the 8.0 or 12.0 — to get respectable runtime. That's an additional $200+ investment if you're starting from scratch.

The paddle switch has a separate lock-on button rather than the slide-switch design some pros prefer. It's a matter of taste, but if you're used to one-handed lock-on operation, the learning curve is annoying.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're a professional or serious DIYer already in the M18 system, you do enough grinding to hate extension cords, or you value the electronic brake for safety in tight spaces.

**Skip it if:** You're a casual user doing occasional cuts — the battery investment isn't justified. Or if you're doing heavy production grinding all day, where a corded 15-amp grinder is still the right tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with standard 4-1/2 inch wheels?**
A: Yes, the 5/8-11 arbor is standard. You can also remove the guard and use 5-inch wheels, though the tool is rated for 4-1/2-inch officially.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a 5.0 vs 12.0?**
A: With a 5.0, expect 8-12 minutes of active cutting. A 12.0 High Output gives you 30-40 minutes of mixed use. The difference is dramatic.

**Q: Is the brake really necessary?**
A: It's not necessary, but it's transformative. It prevents accidental damage to workpieces, reduces sparks when you set the tool down, and makes the grinder feel more precise.

## The Verdict

The Milwaukee M18 Fuel 4-1/2/5 is the best cordless grinder I've tested, period. The brake, variable speed, and genuine cutting power justify the premium price — if you're already in the M18 ecosystem. If you're not, the total cost of entry is steep, but you're getting a tool that performs within shouting distance of corded equivalents.

**Buy it if** you're a pro or serious DIYer who needs cordless grinding performance and values the safety and precision of an electronic brake. **Skip it if** you're a casual user who grinds a few times a year — a corded grinder at a third of the price will serve you fine.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — deducting half a star for the battery requirement and weight. Otherwise, this is the new benchmark.
