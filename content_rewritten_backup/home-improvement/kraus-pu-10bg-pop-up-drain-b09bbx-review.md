Most pop-up drains are an afterthought — a cheap chrome cylinder you forget until it jams, leaks, or rusts. The Kraus PU-10BG is not that drain. This is the upgrade you buy when you're tired of fighting flimsy linkage rods and corroded finishes. After 235 Amazon ratings holding a 4.7 average, it's clear this isn't a niche product — it's a legitimately better mousetrap. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the drain body, a spring-loaded pop-up stopper, a threaded tailpiece, rubber gaskets, and a lift rod assembly. The key detail: the entire body is **machined solid brass**, not the zinc alloy or plastic you'll find on most $15 drains. The finish is PVD (physical vapor deposition), which is the same process used on high-end faucets — it doesn't flake or tarnish like electroplated chrome.

One thing you won't find is a traditional pivot rod and clevis. Kraus replaced that whole fussy linkage system with a spring-loaded stopper that pushes down to close and pushes again to open. That's a major simplification for anyone who's ever fought with a misaligned rod under the sink.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is genuinely tool-free. You drop the drain into the sink opening, tighten the locknut by hand, and attach the tailpiece. For a standard 1-1/4 inch sink opening, it's a 10-minute job — and if you're replacing an old drain, the included gaskets accommodate slightly different basin thicknesses.

The spring mechanism works smoothly in daily use. Push to close, push to open. No jiggling, no partial seals. The stopper seats flush with the drain lip, which means water pools properly and drains fully when open.

Here's where buyers consistently agree: the finish holds up. Multiple reviewers note that after a year of daily use, the brushed gold and matte black finishes show no wear. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've replaced cheap drains every 18 months for a decade. This one looks brand new after two years."*

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations before you buy.

**No overflow compatibility** — this is the big one. If your sink has an overflow hole, the spring-loaded stopper won't form a watertight seal. You'll get a slow leak into the overflow channel. Check your sink before ordering.

**Cleaning required** — the spring mechanism is exposed, and hair wraps around it. You'll need to pull the stopper out periodically to clear debris. It's a 30-second task, but it's not as set-and-forget as a standard lift rod.

**Not for every sink** — the tailpiece is threaded for standard plumbing, but if you have an unusual drain configuration or a vessel sink with a specific depth, measure carefully. The stopper sits slightly proud of the sink floor, which is fine for most basins but noticeable in ultra-shallow designs.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my bathroom sink?**
A: If your sink has a standard 1-1/4 inch drain opening (which is nearly universal for residential bathroom sinks), yes. Just confirm you don't have an overflow hole.

**Q: How does the spring stopper latch?**
A: Push down firmly to close, push again to open. There's no half-open position — it's a positive detent both ways, so it won't slowly sink or stick.

**Q: Does the finish really hold up?**
A: Yes. The PVD finish is baked onto the brass at a molecular level, unlike plating that sits on the surface. Buyers report no peeling or discoloration even after years of daily use.
