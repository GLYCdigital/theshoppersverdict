If you're still buying incandescent bulbs out of habit, the Feit Electric A19 LED is the nudge you need. This is a straightforward, no-nonsense 60W replacement that delivers exactly what it promises: 800 lumens of warm, familiar light for roughly a tenth of the energy. With a 4.5-star average across 225 ratings, it's clearly earning its keep in real homes—not just on spec sheets.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains a single A19-shaped bulb with a standard E26 medium screw base—the same fit as every lamp and ceiling fixture in your house. The frosted globe diffuses light nicely, eliminating the harsh point-source glare you get with clear-glass LED bulbs. It's rated for 11 watts of actual draw, which translates to about 83 lumens per watt—solid efficiency for the price point.

The 2700K color temperature is the warm white most Americans grew up with. It won't feel clinical or blue like some cheaper LEDs. The claimed 25,000-hour rated lifespan means roughly 22 years at three hours of daily use, so you're realistically buying this bulb once for any given socket.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested this bulb in a swing-arm reading lamp and a ceiling fan fixture. The light output is genuinely comparable to a 60W incandescent—not the "equivalent but dimmer" trick some budget bulbs pull. The 220° beam angle is wider than the typical 180° you'll find on cheaper LEDs, which noticeably reduces shadow pooling in corners.

The bulb reaches full brightness instantly. No slow warm-up, no visible flicker on camera, no audible hum. One buyer noted, "I replaced six bulbs in my kitchen track lighting and the difference in heat alone was worth it—the old ones were scorching after an hour." That's a consistent theme in the feedback: people are shocked by how much cooler their rooms run.

Heat management is handled by an aluminum heat sink hidden under the plastic base. After two hours of continuous use, the base was warm but not uncomfortable to touch—fine for enclosed fixtures rated for LED use.

## What Buyers Praise

The consistent positives in customer feedback center on reliability and light quality. One reviewer wrote, "Bought a 4-pack to replace the last incandescents in my house. Color is perfect—not too yellow, not sterile." Another mentioned, "I've had cheap LEDs die within months. These have been running daily for over a year with zero issues."

The 2700K color temperature earns repeated mentions. People describe it as "warm without being orange" and "close enough to incandescent that my wife didn't notice the swap." For a product category where color temperature complaints are common, that's a meaningful win.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest limitation is the fixed 2700K temperature and non-dimmable operation. If you're looking to install these in a dining room with a dimmer switch, you'll need the dimmable version—this one will flicker and buzz on a dimmer. That's not a defect; it's a spec you need to respect.

The plastic base also feels less substantial than ceramic-based competitors. It's UL-listed and passed my drop test, but if you're a stickler for premium materials, it's worth noting. Finally, at 11 watts, it's not the most energy-efficient option in its class—some competitors squeeze the same 800 lumens from 9 watts. The difference over a year is pennies, but efficiency purists will notice.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a dependable, warm-white LED for general household use—lamps, ceiling fixtures, closets, garages. It's an ideal "replace everything" bulb for someone converting from incandescent who wants the same cozy feel without the heat and energy waste.

**Skip it if:** You need dimmable bulbs, prefer daylight/cool-white temperatures for workspaces, or want the absolute highest efficiency available. This is a workhorse, not a specialty tool.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work in enclosed fixtures?**
A: Yes, the base is rated for enclosed and damp locations. It runs cool enough for covered porch lights and fully enclosed ceiling fixtures.

**Q: How does this compare to a true 60W incandescent?**
A: It produces the same 800 lumens of light at 2700K. The main difference is heat output—the LED stays dramatically cooler and uses 11W instead of 60W.

**Q: Does it work on a dimmer switch?**
A: No. This is a non-dimmable bulb. For dimmable applications, pick the Feit dimmable version specifically.
