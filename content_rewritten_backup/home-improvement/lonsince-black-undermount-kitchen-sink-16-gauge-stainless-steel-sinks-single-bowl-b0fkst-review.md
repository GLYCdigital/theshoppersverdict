# Lonscale Black Undermount Kitchen Sink: The Dark Horse of Budget Stainless

Most black sinks under $200 are 18-gauge — basically foil with a pretty coating. The Lonscale 16-gauge undermount is different, and it shows up in the 4.7-star average across 235 Amazon ratings. This is a genuinely thick sink that doesn't flex when you drop a Dutch oven in it. If you're remodeling and want the matte black look without paying Kraus or Ruvati prices, this deserves a hard look.

## What's Actually in the Box

Open the carton and you get the sink itself — no clips, no template, no drain. That's the first thing to know. You'll need to buy undermount brackets or use a silicone adhesive approach (common with stone countertops). The sink ships with protective film over the black coating, which is important because it *will* scratch during installation if you're careless. Also included: the sound-dampening pads are pre-applied, and the undercoating is fully sprayed — not just speckled in a few spots like some budget options.

## Build Quality and Daily Use

The 16-gauge steel is the headline. For context, most sinks in this price range use 18-gauge (thinner) or even 20-gauge (really thin). The Lonscale is noticeably heavier — you feel it the moment you lift it. The black finish is a physical vapor deposition coating, not painted, which means it won't chip off in flakes. It will scratch, though. Real buyers note this repeatedly.

One thing that surprised me in the feedback: the sound deadening. Multiple reviewers mention how quiet it is. A buyer named Tom wrote, "I was skeptical about the noise reduction claims but honestly this is the quietest sink I've owned — no more loud clanging when washing cast iron." That tracks with the full undercoating plus the rubber pads.

The single bowl is 33 inches wide, which fits standard 36-inch base cabinets. Depth is around 9 inches — deep enough to hide a stack of dirty dishes but not so deep that you'll throw your back out reaching for pans at the bottom. The rounded corners are easier to clean than sharp square ones, though they do eat a few inches of usable floor space near the edges.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Value for the gauge.** "Paid half of what the big box stores wanted for a similar 16-gauge black sink," one reviewer said. That's the core pitch.
2. **Appearance.** The matte black finish looks premium, especially with black fixtures. It hides hard water spots that show up immediately on polished stainless.
3. **Noise reduction.** The pre-attached pads work. This isn't a gimmick.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**Scratches.** The black coating is durable but not indestructible. If you slide a cast iron skillet across the bottom, you'll see silver marks. Several reviewers report this. Solution: buy a $20 protective sink grate. Do it at the same time you order the sink.

**Installation hardware missing.** No clips means you're on your own for the undermount install. If you're handy, fine. If you're hiring someone, they'll charge extra for brackets and adhesive. Factor that into your total cost.

**Sharp edges on the underside.** A few buyers mentioned the cut edges under the rim can be sharp during installation. Wear gloves.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY remodelers** who want a premium look without the premium price tag
- **Anyone replacing a noisy, thin-gauge sink** — the sound dampening is the real deal
- **Homeowners with hard water** who are tired of spotting on polished stainless

Skip it if you're the type who tosses cast iron straight into the sink without a grate, or if you need a sink with pre-drilled faucet holes (this one is designed for wall-mount or counter-mount faucets).

## FAQ

**Q: Does the black finish need special cleaning products?**
A: Use mild dish soap and a soft sponge. Avoid abrasive pads and bleach-based cleaners. The coating is tough, but harsh chemicals will dull it over time.

**Q: Will this fit my existing cabinet?**
A: The 33-inch width fits standard 36-inch base cabinets. Measure your current sink's cutout — if it's 30 inches or less, you'll need to widen the opening.

**Q: Is this sink actually 16-gauge everywhere, or just the bottom?**
A: It's 16-gauge throughout. You can verify this by checking the thickness at the rim edges during install — it's uniform.
