The JORGENSEN No.4 is the kind of tool that makes you ask why you waited so long to ditch power sanders. At 10 inches long with a 2-inch blade, this is a classic smoothing plane — the one tool every furniture maker keeps within arm's reach. But here's the thing about budget-priced planes: they're either diamond rough or hidden gems. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, this one leans hard toward gem territory, though it's not without quirks.

**What you actually get**

Open the box and you'll find the plane itself, a blade/cap iron assembly already installed, and a thin layer of protective grease you'll want to wipe off before use. No manual worth reading, no blade honing guide — just the tool and a basic cardboard box. The casting is clean with no visible sand holes or rough edges, which is more than I can say for planes at this price point a decade ago. The rosewood handles are a genuinely nice touch; they're shaped properly with no sharp corners digging into your palm.

**Performance and real-world use**

Out of the box, the blade will take shavings, but they'll be rough. The factory edge is "sharpened" in the loosest sense of the word. Plan on spending time on a fine diamond stone or water stone before this plane performs at its potential. Once you get that edge right — and the cast-iron body's 4.5 pounds of mass working with you — the JORGENSEN cuts like a much more expensive tool. The adjustable mouth closes down tight enough to handle figured maple without tear-out, which is the real test of any smoothing plane.

The adjuster knob turns smoothly, and the frog sits solidly once locked. I found the chip breaker's mating surface needed a quick flattening to get the perfect seal against the blade — a 10-minute job with 400-grit paper on a flat surface. Do that, and you'll eliminate the clogging issues some reviewers mention. As one buyer put it: "After flattening the chip breaker and honing the iron, this plane performs like my friend's Lie-Nielsen." That's high praise, but fair once set up properly.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition dominates the feedback. Buyers repeatedly mention that this plane handles 90% of what the $200+ options do at a fraction of the cost. The ergonomics get consistent praise — the rosewood handles genuinely fit the hand well, and the weight distribution feels balanced for extended use. Several reviewers note it works beautifully right out of the box for softer woods like pine and poplar, with only hardwoods demanding the sharpening session.

**Where it falls short**

The lateral adjustment lever is the weak point. It has noticeable slop, meaning you'll occasionally find the blade slightly skewed mid-project. It's not a dealbreaker — a quick tap fixes it — but it's annoying when you're chasing a perfect finish. The blade itself is decent A2 tool steel, not the premium O1 or PM-V11 you'd find on high-end planes. Also, the factory grind is uneven, so if you're new to sharpening freehand, budget for a honing guide or practice before trusting this plane on your best walnut board.

**Who should buy this**

This plane is ideal for the woodworker who wants to learn hand-tool techniques without dropping $300 on a premium plane. It's also perfect for the hobbyist who needs a reliable smoother for weekend projects and doesn't mind the initial setup ritual. If you're a professional using a plane daily for hours, the lateral adjuster play will drive you nuts — spend more. If you're a beginner, buy this, watch a sharpening tutorial, and you'll be ahead of 80% of hobbyists.

**FAQ**

**Does this plane work out of the box?**
It cuts, but not great. The blade needs honing (20–30 minutes on stones) and the chip breaker benefits from flattening. Set it up properly and it rivals planes costing three times as much.

**What's the difference between a No. 4 and a No. 5 plane?**
The No. 4 is a smoother — shorter (10") and designed for final finish passes. The No. 5 is a jack plane — longer (14") and more versatile for rough stock removal. For final smoothing, the No. 4 is correct.

**Is the blade replacement-friendly?**
Yes. It uses standard 2" × 2-3/8" plane blades, widely available from multiple brands. You can upgrade to a higher-grade blade later if you want better edge retention.

**The Verdict**

The JORGENSEN No.4 is the smart buy for anyone wanting a serious smoothing plane without the serious price tag. It demands 30 minutes of setup and occasional lateral adjustment checks, but the performance you get for the cost is exceptional.

**Skip it if:** You expect perfection out of the box, need a plane for daily professional use, or aren't willing to learn basic sharpening.
