If your water softener has been acting up — not regenerating properly, leaving that salty sludge at the bottom of the brine tank, or just eating through salt faster than it should — the problem might not be your unit. It’s probably the salt you’re feeding it. The Morton Salt 1501 Clean Protect System is engineered to tackle exactly that issue, and it’s been a steady 4.4-star performer across 220 Amazon ratings. Here’s the real story.

## What you actually get

This is a 50-pound bag of evaporated salt pellets, not rock salt. The key differentiator is the "Clean Protect" additive — a blend that helps keep the brine tank cleaner by reducing the mushy, undissolved salt buildup that plagues many softeners. You get a sturdy plastic bag with a handle, and the pellets are uniform in size, which means less dust and fewer small fragments that clog the brine valve. It’s compatible with all major softener brands, including high-efficiency and on-demand models.

## Performance and build quality

The pellets themselves are dense and 99.1% pure, which matters more than most people realize. Impure salt leaves behind insoluble matter that accumulates in the tank, reducing efficiency and shortening the life of your softener. In real-world use, the Clean Protect system does what it promises: the brine tank stays noticeably cleaner between refills, and users report fewer "salt bridges" — that hard crust that forms above the water level and stops the softener from drawing brine.

One thing I noticed in the feedback: the blue-tinted pellets (the additive) can leave a faint blue or white residue in the tank over time. It’s cosmetic, not functional, and it doesn’t affect water quality. But if you’re someone who likes to see a spotless tank, it’s worth knowing.

## What buyers consistently praise

The recurring theme in the reviews is reliability and cleanliness. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I’ve used Morton's regular salt for years, but this keeps my tank free of that slimy mess. Worth the extra dollar."* Another noted, *"My softener regenerates more consistently since switching to this. No more mushy salt at the bottom."* The 50-pound bag size is also a hit — it’s heavy but manageable, and the handle makes pouring into the tank simpler than wrestling with a flimsy bag.

## Where it falls short

The honest downside is price. This costs noticeably more per pound than Morton’s standard white crystal salt. If you have a basic softener and don’t struggle with buildup, you’re paying for a benefit you might not fully use. Also, a handful of reviewers mentioned the pellets can be slightly dusty when you open the bag — not a dealbreaker, but you’ll want to pour carefully over the tank opening.

## Who should buy this

- **Homeowners with older softeners** that have a history of salt bridging or sludge buildup — this will extend the time between cleanings.
- **Anyone using hard water with high iron or sediment** — the cleaner brine tank means fewer service calls.
- **Busy households** that don’t want to babysit the brine tank every month.

Skip it if you already use a high-end resin cleaner and your tank stays spotless, or if you’re strictly budget-focused — plain Morton salt will still soften your water, just with more maintenance.
