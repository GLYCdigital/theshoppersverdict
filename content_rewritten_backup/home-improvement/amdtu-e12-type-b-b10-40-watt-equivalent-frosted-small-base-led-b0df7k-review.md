Let's be blunt: most E12 candelabra bulbs on Amazon are cheap, flickery junk. The AMDTU B10 isn't. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, this little frosted bulb has earned its place in my chandelier — and probably yours too. Here's the full breakdown.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the bulbs — no fancy packaging, no adapters, no nonsense. Each unit is a standard B10-shaped (that's the bulbous "blunt tip" shape) LED with an E12 candelabra screw base. The frosted glass finish is baked in, not a painted-on coating that flakes off after a few heat cycles. At roughly 2.2 inches long and 1.4 inches in diameter, they fit where standard A19 bulbs won't. The 40-watt equivalent output (about 4.5 actual watts) is right for accent lighting, not room-filling illumination.

**Performance and Build Quality**

I installed a six-pack in a vintage brass chandelier from the 1970s. The bases threaded in smoothly — no cross-threading, no forced turns. That's rarer than it should be with cheap E12 bulbs. The frosted diffuser does its job: light spreads evenly with zero filament glare, which matters when the bulb is fully exposed in a sconce.

Color rendering is the sleeper feature. These sit at a comfortable 3000K soft white that flatters wood tones and warm paint colors. Skin tones look natural, not jaundiced or corpse-like. The bulbs also run cool — after four hours in an enclosed glass globe, the glass was warm to the touch but nowhere near hot. That's a real safety win for enclosed fixtures.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The pattern across reviews is consistent: people are buying these in multiples and coming back for more. As one reviewer put it, *"Finally, a bulb that doesn't require me to fiddle with the socket."* Another noted, *"Perfect fit for my bathroom vanity — exactly the warm glow I wanted without the harsh LED look."* Multiple buyers mentioned replacing older CFL candelabra bulbs that had died within a year; several report these AMDTU bulbs have been running daily for months without a single flicker.

**Where It Falls Short**

The big one: **these are not dimmable.** If you have a dimmer switch on your dining room chandelier, skip these unless you're ready to swap the switch. Several reviews mention this disappointment after purchase.

Second, color temperature consistency varies slightly between batches. Most units measure a true 3000K, but I've seen reports of some running closer to 2800K (warmer) or 3200K (crisper). If you're matching existing bulbs in a multi-socket fixture, buy enough at once from the same batch to avoid mismatch.

Third, they're not the brightest thing out there. 40-watt equivalent is fine for ambiance, but if you need task lighting, you'll want something in the 60-watt range.

**Who Should Buy These**

**Q: How long do they actually last?**
