Let's be honest: nobody gets excited about buying a water heater connector. You're here because the old one is rusted, leaking, or you're replacing the tank entirely. But this is one of those rare plumbing parts where spending an extra $5 over the bargain-bin option actually matters. ## What's actually in the box

You get one pre-assembled flexible connector, 12 inches long, with 3/4-inch male threaded ends on both sides. The brass hex nuts come already attached with rubber gaskets seated inside. That's it — no Teflon tape, no extra washers, no instruction booklet. The packaging is minimal, which is fine because this isn't a product that needs a manual.

The build quality is immediately apparent when you handle it. The stainless steel corrugation is thick and substantial — not the flimsy foil-like material you see on cheap import lines. The brass fittings are machined cleanly, and the whole assembly has a reassuring heft.

## Real-world performance

Installation took me about eight minutes on a standard 40-gallon gas water heater. The flexibility is the standout feature here. In my utility closet, the water heater sits tight against a stud wall with less than six inches of clearance. The TT FLEX bent into a sharp 90-degree angle without kinking or collapsing — something the rigid copper lines I've used before simply cannot do.

The pre-attached brass nuts with rubber washers are a genuine time-saver. No wrestling with Teflon tape or pipe dope, no worrying about getting the thread alignment perfect. Hand-tighten, then give it one full turn with a wrench — that's the entire installation process. The rubber gasket does the sealing work, and it does it well.

One thing worth noting: this connector is rated for both hot and cold water supply lines, and the stainless construction handles the temperature swings without any expansion noise or creaking — something I've noticed with cheaper braided lines.

## Where it falls short

The fixed 12-inch length is the biggest limitation. If your water heater sits farther than 12 inches from the supply lines, you'll need to buy a longer version or add an extension — neither is ideal. Measure your gap before ordering.

The rubber washers are single-use. If you disconnect this line for maintenance or a move, you'll want to replace the washers before reinstalling. They compress permanently during the initial tightening, and reusing them risks a slow weep at the connection point. Pick up a pack of replacement gaskets when you order.

Some buyers with older homes have noted that the 3/4-inch NPT threads may not match older 1/2-inch supply lines. Check your existing connection size before buying — this connector is strictly 3/4-inch on both ends.
