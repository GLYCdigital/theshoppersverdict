## What You Actually Get

The box contains the floor-mounted Roman tub filler assembly, a handheld shower wand with a 59-inch stainless steel hose, a wall-mounted hand shower bracket, and all necessary mounting hardware. The unit stands 48 inches tall with a 9-inch reach from the floor mount to the spout—dimensions that matter if you're working with a smaller footprint. The finish is a brushed nickel that resists fingerprints reasonably well, and the dual cross handles control hot and cold independently, which means you'll need to set your preferred temperature manually each bath. There's no thermostatic valve here; this is a manual mixer.

## Real-World Performance and Build

The body is solid brass with a lead-free interior, and at 16 pounds, this thing feels substantial. Buyers consistently note the weight and material quality. One verified purchaser wrote, "The brass construction is obvious the moment you unbox it. No cheap thin metal here." That sentiment repeats across reviews—people are pleasantly surprised by the heft and the quality of the brushed finish.

Flow rate is a strong point. The spout delivers a generous, even stream that fills a standard 60-gallon tub without the narrow, high-pressure jet you sometimes get from cheaper units. The handheld shower is genuinely useful, not an afterthought. The wand has a rubber nozzle for easy cleaning, and the hose swivels at the connection point, so it doesn't kink when you're rinsing the tub walls. The diverter toggle on the spout is stiff but precise—you'll know when you've switched from spout to shower.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reading through the 229 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **The finish holds up.** Multiple reviewers with months of use report no spotting or peeling. One wrote, "Six months of daily use and the brushed nickel still looks brand new."
2. **Installation flexibility.** The floor mount plate has adjustable slots, which helps when your rough-in isn't perfectly centered. A reviewer noted, "I had about an inch of play, and that saved my tile job."
3. **The handheld is actually usable.** For rinsing the tub or washing a dog or a kid, the wand's reach and flow rate get consistent positive mentions.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the installation requirements. This is a floor-mount unit, which means the plumbing comes up through your subfloor. If your rough-in is already set for a wall-mount faucet, this isn't a swap you can make without redoing pipes. The manual also assumes a decent level of DIY competence—one reviewer mentioned hiring a plumber after realizing their floor joists interfered with the supply lines.

The cross handles are functional but not ergonomic for everyone. If you have arthritis or limited grip strength, the small metal crosses can be awkward to turn, especially when you're adjusting temperature mid-bath. It's a classic design choice, but it's not for everyone.

There's also no integrated thermostatic control. You'll set hot and cold independently every time, and if you're filling a deep tub, you'll be reaching over the edge to fine-tune. Not a dealbreaker, but know it going in.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a freestanding tub with the floor space to accommodate a 9-inch reach, you're comfortable with (or budgeting for) professional installation, and you want a solid brass fixture with a genuinely functional handheld shower. It's an especially strong pick if you're doing a modern or transitional bathroom and want the floor-mount look without paying boutique showroom prices.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight DIY timeline, your plumbing rough-in is already set for a wall mount, or you want thermostatic temperature control. Also, if your tub is positioned with less than 12 inches of clearance behind it, the floor-mount plate will be a tight squeeze.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit a standard freestanding tub?**
Yes, the 48-inch height and 9-inch spout reach work with most standard freestanding tubs. Measure your tub's deck height and distance from the floor mount rough-in to the tub center before buying.

**Q: Is the handheld shower powerful enough for rinsing the tub?**
Yes. The wand produces a solid spray with good pressure, though it's not a high-pressure massage shower. It's designed for utility, not a shower replacement.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
If you're comfortable with copper or PEX supply lines and have access below the floor, yes. If your joists block the supply path or you're unsure about the rough-in, budget $200–400 for a plumber.
