Let me get this out of the way first: the Festool CT Midi I costs more than most full-size shop vacuums, and it's the smallest model in the pro-grade lineup. But after 240 Amazon reviewers averaged out to 4.8 stars, the conversation isn't about whether it's expensive — it's about whether it's worth it for *your* work. Here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the box**

You get the CT Midi I unit itself (roughly 19" tall, 14" deep, 14" wide — about the footprint of a milk crate), a 3.5m (11.5 ft) anti-static suction hose with 27mm/35mm stepped end, a HEPA filter pre-installed, a disposable collection bag, and the remote battery and receiver. Notable: the main filter is a cartridge-style HEPA, not a flimsy paper bag. The unit weighs 15.4 lbs, which is manageable for carrying by the integrated handle.

**Performance and real-world use**

The suction is rated at 134 CFM with a 95" water lift. In practical terms, this thing pulls dust out of a router table like it's angry at it. The Midi I has an auto-start feature via the Bluetooth remote — you pair it with a Festool tool (or an adapter for other brands), and the vac kicks on when you pull the trigger. No more bending over to hit a switch, no more leaving the vac running for five extra minutes after you set the tool down.

The HEPA filtration here is the real story. It's certified for 99.97% efficiency at 0.3 microns. If you're cutting MDF, drywall, or any silica-containing material, this makes your shop measurably safer. One reviewer put it simply: "I can run my track saw on MDF all day and the air in my garage stays clean enough that my wife doesn't complain."

The hose anti-static design is another detail that matters — no zaps when you're moving it around, and less dust clinging to the hose walls. The cord and hose both store neatly on the unit when not in use.

**What buyers consistently praise**

From the Amazon reviews, three themes stand out:

- **The Bluetooth remote is a game-changer.** One reviewer wrote: "I was skeptical about the remote feature. Now I can't imagine going back to a manual switch. It's the difference between actually using dust collection and skipping it because it's a hassle."
- **The suction doesn't fade.** Multiple reviewers note the filter design keeps airflow strong even as the bag fills. As one put it: "My old Shop-Vac would lose suction halfway through a job. This one pulls the same from start to finish."
- **Build quality is genuinely pro-grade.** Latches, hinges, and the Systainer interface feel like they'll survive a decade of job-site abuse.

**Where it falls short**

The price is the obvious one. At roughly $500-600 street price, you could buy three capable shop vacuums and have money left over. That's not a value play; it's a professional tool investment.

Second, the Bluetooth auto-start is locked to Festool's ecosystem unless you buy the separate adapter (around $40-50) for non-Festool tools. That's a deliberate ecosystem play, and it's annoying if you own Dewalt, Milwaukee, or Makita gear.

Third, the 3.5m hose is decent but not generous. If you're working in a large room, you'll want the optional 5m hose. Also, the collection bag is a consumable — factor in ongoing costs.

**Who should buy this**

- **Professional cabinet makers and finish carpenters** who cut MDF or plywood daily and need genuinely clean air
- **Flooring contractors** doing dust-free sanding where HEPA certification is a spec requirement
- **Serious hobbyists** who've outgrown two-stage shop vacs and want a single, compact, truly portable solution
- **Anyone with dust allergies** who works in a home shop and doesn't want to breathe fine particulates

**Who should skip it**

- Budget-conscious DIYers — a $150 Rigid or Craftsman with a decent filter will handle weekend projects fine
- Anyone who only does rough work — framing, demo, concrete cleanup — where fine dust isn't a concern
- If you never use dust collection now, this won't magically change your habits at this price point

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Bluetooth remote work with non-Festool tools?**
A: Not out of the box. You need the Festool Bluetooth adapter (part 204491) that plugs into the tool's dust port. It's about $40-50 extra. Without it, you're manually switching the vac on/off.

**Q: How often do I need to replace the HEPA filter?**
A: The cartridge filter itself lasts a long time — typically a year or more for daily pro use — but you'll replace the collection bag far more often. Festool claims bag changes every 40-60 gallons of debris, and you'll need to knock the filter clean occasionally to maintain airflow.

**Q: Is this actually a HEPA vacuum, or is it marketing?**
A: It's certified. Festool provides test documentation showing 99.97% efficiency at 0.3 microns. This matters if you're cutting materials that produce crystalline silica — it's an OSHA-compliance-grade filtration system.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a professional or serious enthusiast who works with fine dust daily, values air quality, and wants a compact unit that doesn't compromise on power. The Bluetooth feature genuinely changes how often you use dust collection — and that alone can be worth the premium.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget, work mostly outdoors, or only need occasional cleanup. A conventional shop vac will do the job for a fraction of the cost.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — docked a half-point for the proprietary adapter situation and the consumable bag costs. But for the right buyer, this is the last dust extractor you'll ever need to purchase.
