Let me be upfront: most "farmhouse" lighting on Amazon is the same matte-black-and-wood cookie cutter. The 360 Lighting Romeo is not that. At 23.5 inches tall, this jar-style sconce has actual presence. It reads more like a small lantern than a wall light, and that changes how you use it.

## What you're actually getting

The Romeo ships as a single wall sconce — not a pair, which is worth noting since most people end up buying two. The frame is a rust-resistant black finish with a natural wood top cap. The jar itself is seeded glass, which gives that slightly wavy, hand-blown look without the hand-blown price. The dimensions matter here: 23.5" high, roughly 7" wide, extending about 7.5" from the wall. This is a substantial piece.

Inside the box you get the fixture, mounting hardware, and instructions. Nothing fancy, but the hardware is decent gauge — not the flimsy stamped stuff you see at this price point. The wood cap is real wood, not plastic printed to look like wood, which is the first thing I check on "rustic" fixtures.

## How it performs on the wall

The seeded glass does exactly what it should: it diffuses the bulb enough to kill harsh shadows while staying clear enough to show the filament. If you use a vintage-style Edison bulb, you get that warm, amber glow that makes this fixture work. If you throw a standard daylight LED in there, it'll look like a garage light. That's on you, not the fixture.

Installation is straightforward if you've hung a light before. One catch: it needs a 4-inch round junction box. The canopy is a fixed size and it will not cover a 3-inch box gap. I've seen multiple reviewers hit this. One said, "Beautiful fixture but I had to replace the junction box — the canopy is smaller than it looks in photos." That's accurate. Plan for it.

The damp rating is legitimate. People are mounting these on covered porches and in bathrooms without issues. The finish is powder-coated, not painted, so it resists chipping and fading better than I expected in direct sun exposure.

## What buyers consistently say

The praise clusters around two things: the scale and the craftsmanship. As one reviewer put it, "Looks way more expensive than it is. The wood top and seeded glass make it feel custom." Another noted, "I put these on either side of my fireplace and they completely changed the room. Everyone asks where they're from."

The complaints are fewer but worth hearing. "The glass jar is attached with a screw from underneath — it's secure but you can't easily swap the bulb without loosening it." That's accurate. It's a two-minute job, not a 10-second one. Also, because the jar is fully clear, dust shows on the glass quickly. If you're the type who never dusts light fixtures, this will collect visible grime.

## Where it falls short

The bulb exposure is the real design tradeoff. The Romeo is a glass jar with nothing inside but the socket. That means the bulb is the entire aesthetic. Buy cheap bulbs, get a cheap look. Budget for quality filament bulbs — it's another $15–20 per fixture.

Also, this is a directional light. It throws light out the sides and front but not up or down much. If you need ambient wash lighting, this isn't the fixture. It's task-adjacent and accent lighting only.

## Who should buy this

- **Porch or entryway owners** who want something that reads as substantial from the street
- **Farmhouse renovators** matching real wood tones, not gray-washed everything
- **Anyone tired of tiny sconces** that look like afterthoughts — this one fills wall space properly

Skip it if you need a tiny hallway light, or if you're working with standard 3-inch junction boxes and don't want to swap them.

## FAQ

**Does this come in a pair?**
No, it's sold individually. Buy two if you need matching lights — the listing doesn't offer a bundle discount, so factor that into your total.

**Can this go outside in the rain?**
It's damp-rated, not wet-rated. Covered porch, yes. Fully exposed to driving rain, no.

**What bulb does it take?**
Standard medium base (E26). Use a 60W-equivalent vintage filament LED. Not a standard frosted bulb — it will look wrong.

## The verdict

**Buy it if** you want a statement piece with real presence and don't mind planning the electrical box ahead of time. **Skip it if** you need a subtle accent light or want a quick swap without fixture prep.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Romeo delivers exactly what the photos promise, with honest construction and a finish that holds up. The junction box quirk and bulb sensitivity cost it a few points, but for the price, this is one of the better farmhouse fixtures on Amazon.
