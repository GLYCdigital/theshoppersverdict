## What Zero TDS Actually Means

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) measures everything in your water that isn't H2O — minerals, salts, metals, organic compounds. Most pitcher filters reduce TDS by 20-40%. ZeroWater's 5-stage filtration targets it all: sediment, carbon, ion exchange, and two proprietary stages that strip dissolved solids down to the FDA standard for purified bottled water.

This matters most for specific use cases. A five-year-long Singapore reviewer explains it best: "For my purpose, I need a very low TDS to remineralise water (using third wave water) to make coffee. Thus this filter is much better for that purpose." Coffee and tea enthusiasts who want a blank-slate water profile will find ZeroWater indispensable.

It's also the go-to for areas with genuinely bad tap water. Another five-year customer writes: "I do not live in the US. For all the other reviews where they say it's bad, I think it's the water supply in their area issue." When the incoming water is heavy with minerals or contaminants, ZeroWater's aggressive filtration makes a visible difference.

## The Speed and Longevity Trade-Off

The same reviewer who praised ZeroWater for coffee also noted the catch: "Downside is that this is much slower than the Brita so you should really buy the filter/system fit for your purpose." ZeroWater filters are deliberately dense to achieve that 99.6% reduction. That density means slower flow and shorter filter life.

Multiple Singapore-based users confirm this. One buyer said it "works great but does not last as long as I expect." A 4-star reviewer summed it up simply: "Only expensive." At $44.99 for a 2-pack, the per-gallon cost is meaningfully higher than standard carbon filters.

For households filtering primarily for taste — where tap water is already safe but tastes chlorinated — a standard Brita or PUR filter delivers 90% of the value at 30% of the ongoing cost. For households that want RO-quality purity without the under-sink installation, ZeroWater is the only game in town.
