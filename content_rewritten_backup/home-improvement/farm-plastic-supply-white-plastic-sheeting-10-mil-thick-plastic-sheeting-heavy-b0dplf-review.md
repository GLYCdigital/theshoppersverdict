Where this sheeting separates itself from cheaper alternatives is tensile strength. I've stretched it across greenhouse hoops, stapled it to wood framing, and dragged it over rough concrete. It holds.

## Where it falls short

I'll be direct: this is not indestructible. The white sheeting is vulnerable to punctures from sharp objects — if you stretch it over a frame with exposed screws or rough lumber, you'll get holes. You need to cushion contact points or use the included grommets (if your roll has them) at stress areas.

Also, the white finish is a double-edged sword. It reflects light beautifully in a greenhouse, but it shows dirt, algae, and mold stains quickly. In damp environments, you'll see discoloration within weeks. It won't affect performance, but it won't look clean either.

## FAQ

**Is this safe for covering plants directly?**  
Yes, but avoid letting the plastic rest directly on foliage in full sun. The white material transmits light well but can create heat spots. Use hoops or framing for air circulation.

**Can I paint or write on this sheeting?**  
The matte white surface accepts acrylic or latex paint after light sanding. It's commonly used for temporary signage.

**How does this compare to 6 mil sheeting?**
