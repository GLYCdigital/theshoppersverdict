**The Aolemi Floor Mount Bathtub Faucet isn't the cheapest freestanding filler on Amazon, and it's not the most expensive either. It sits right in the sweet spot where buyers expect real metal, real flow, and real durability. After digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet, here's what you're actually getting.**

## What's in the Box

You get the floor-mounted spout assembly, a hand shower with a 59-inch stainless steel hose, a diverter valve, the floor flange and mounting hardware, and the cartridge. The finish is a brushed nickel that photographs well and hides water spots better than chrome. The unit stands 41 inches tall with a 7-inch spout reach — measure your tub deck clearance before ordering, because this is not a compact fixture.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

This is where the Aolemi earns its 4.3-star average. The body is solid brass with a PVD finish, not the zinc alloy you'll find on budget fillers. Multiple buyers describe the weight as "substantial" and note it feels anchored once installed. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've installed three of these for clients. The valve cartridge feels smooth, and the brass body is the real deal."*

The high-flow spout delivers a genuine 8-10 gallons per minute depending on your supply pressure. That's the difference between a spa-like fill and standing around waiting. The diverter switches between spout and hand shower cleanly, and the hand shower itself is a functional bonus for rinsing the tub or washing hair.

**Installation note:** This is a floor-mount unit, so you need access below the floor or a solid concrete slab. The flange covers a 2-inch rough-in. Buyers consistently advise using thread sealant on the supply connection and thread locker on the flange screws — several report the screws backing out after a few months if skipped.

## Where It Falls Short

The exposed flange screws are the most common complaint. They're visible and can loosen with thermal cycling. It's a minor issue with a $3 tube of thread locker, but it's worth knowing before install.

There's no integrated shut-off valve. If you need to service the cartridge or the hand shower, you're cutting water to the whole bathroom unless you add an inline valve. Plan for that during rough-in.

The 59-inch hand shower hose is workable for a standard 60-inch tub, but if you have a longer soaking tub, you'll be reaching. A 72-inch hose would have been a better call at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You're installing a freestanding tub and want a floor-mounted filler that looks like a $1,200 piece but costs a third of that. You're comfortable with basic plumbing and don't mind a half-day install. You want a hand shower for practical tub cleaning.

**Skip it if:** You have a tight alcove — the 7-inch reach may not center over a narrow tub. You want a wall-mounted unit instead. Or you expect a fully tool-free, maintenance-free fixture. This is real hardware that rewards careful installation.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit a standard 60-inch freestanding tub?**
Yes. The 41-inch height clears most tub rims, and the 7-inch spout reach centers over a standard 60-inch tub. Measure your tub's rim-to-rim width to confirm.

**Q: Is the finish durable?**
The PVD brushed nickel finish is a step above electroplated chrome. Buyers report no peeling or tarnishing after 6-12 months of daily use. It wipes clean with a damp cloth.

**Q: Can I install this myself?**
If you have access below the floor and basic soldering or PEX skills, yes. Budget 3-5 hours. If you're not comfortable with supply lines, hire a plumber — the floor flange must be sealed properly to avoid leaks.
