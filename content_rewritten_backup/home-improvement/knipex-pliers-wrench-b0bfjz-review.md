## Performance: Where It Earns the 4.8 Stars

The headline feature is the parallel jaw movement. Traditional adjustable wrenches angle the jaws slightly when you apply torque, concentrating force on two corners of the fastener. The KNIPEX keeps jaws perfectly parallel, so it grips all six points of a hex bolt. In practice, this means you can apply serious torque without rounding off the head — I've used it to break loose corroded brake caliper bolts that a standard wrench couldn't hold.

The push-button slide mechanism is genuinely one-handed. Press the button, slide the jaw to the fastener, release, and it locks into one of 10+ positions. There's no ratcheting action — you lift and reposition like a normal wrench — but the grip is so solid that you won't miss it. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I've owned Snap-On and Mac tools for 25 years. This is the only tool I'd replace immediately if lost." Another verified buyer said, "It grabs like a crescent wrench on steroids."

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the cons are about tradeoffs, not defects. The 10-inch model weighs 0.9 pounds — noticeably heavier than a comparable 10-inch Crescent (0.6 lb). If you're working overhead on a car or doing repetitive tasks, that extra weight adds up.

The lack of a jaw measurement scale is a genuine oversight. With a traditional adjustable wrench, you can roughly preset the opening to a known size. Here, you're adjusting blind and relying on feel. It's minor, but it slows down repetitive work.

And the price. You're paying $50+ for a tool that does what a $15 wrench does — just better. If you're a weekend warrior who changes a faucet twice a year, the cost is hard to justify.
