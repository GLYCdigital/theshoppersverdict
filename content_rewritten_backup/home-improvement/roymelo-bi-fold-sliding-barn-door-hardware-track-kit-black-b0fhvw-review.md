# ROYMELO Bi-fold Sliding Barn Door Hardware Track Kit, Black — Does the Space-Saving Design Deliver?

Most barn door kits assume you have five feet of empty wall to slide into. The ROYMELO bi-fold kit doesn't. If you're staring at a narrow hallway, a tight laundry closet, or a bathroom opening where a swinging door won't work, this kit splits the door into two folding panels that stack against each other — cutting required wall clearance roughly in half.

That's the headline feature. But is the hardware itself worth your money? After digging through 220 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the honest breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit includes the steel track (length varies by option), two hangers with ball bearings, pivot brackets, floor guides, end caps, rubber stoppers, and all necessary mounting hardware. The black finish is a matte powder coat — not glossy, not cheap-looking. The track itself is heavy-gauge steel that doesn't flex when you hang a solid-core door.

One thing I appreciate: the floor guides are adjustable, which matters more than most people realize. If your floor isn't perfectly level — and most aren't — those guides keep the door from swinging outward and catching on the track.

## Performance and Build Quality

The ball-bearing hangers are the star here. They roll smoothly along the track with minimal noise — a common failure point on cheaper kits that use nylon bushings. Multiple buyers confirmed this, with one noting: *"Glides like it's on rollers, not dragging like my old one did."*

The bi-fold mechanism itself is where this kit earns its keep. Each panel folds on pivot hinges, and the hardware includes a guide rail at the bottom to keep the panels aligned. When installed correctly, the doors fold flat against each other — not perfectly flush, but close enough that you won't lose much space.

Installation is the tradeoff. This is **not** a beginner-friendly kit. The included template is printed on paper and doesn't account for uneven walls or non-standard framing. Several reviewers mentioned needing to re-drill holes after the first attempt. One said: *"The template was useless. I ended up measuring everything myself and it took twice as long."* That's consistent with what I see in the design — the hardware is solid, but the instructions assume you've hung a barn door before.

## Where It Falls Short

The mounting bolts are standard for wood framing, but if you're attaching to concrete or brick, you'll need to supply your own masonry anchors — the kit doesn't include them. That's a minor annoyance, but worth knowing before you start.

Also, the track comes in fixed lengths. If your opening is wider than the track you order, you're out of luck — there's no way to extend it. Measure your opening carefully and order accordingly. The kit supports door weights up to roughly 220 pounds, which covers most solid wood doors, but not heavy steel or custom oversized panels.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Homeowners with tight spaces**: If you're converting a closet or small bathroom, the bi-fold design is a genuine space-saver
- **DIYers with moderate experience**: You've hung a door or installed hardware before; you're comfortable measuring and drilling
- **Anyone replacing an old bi-fold bypass door**: The hardware upgrade will make your doors glide like new

Skip it if you're a first-time installer looking for a plug-and-play project. The learning curve is real, and you'll likely need to buy extra anchors or make a second trip to the hardware store.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the track work with uneven floors?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to adjust the floor guides. They're height-adjustable, so you can compensate for slopes up to about 1/4 inch. Beyond that, you'll need to shim the door itself.

**Q: Can I use this with a 36-inch opening?**
A: Yes, but you need to order the correct track length. The track should be roughly 1.5 times the width of your opening for the bi-fold to stack properly. Measure your exact opening before ordering.

**Q: Is the black finish durable or does it scratch easily?**
A: The powder coat is reasonably durable for interior use. It will show scratches if you drag tools across it during installation, but normal use won't mark it. One reviewer noted minor chips after a year of heavy use — nothing that would affect function.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you need a space-saving barn door solution and have intermediate DIY skills. The hardware quality justifies the price, and the smooth operation will make you forget the installation headache.

**Skip it if** you're a novice installer or need concrete anchors included in the box. This kit rewards patience and precision — rush it and you'll be re-drilling holes.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The hardware itself is excellent, but the installation experience drags the score down. With better instructions and included masonry anchors, this would be a no-brainer at 4.5+. As it stands, it's a solid choice for the right buyer.
