**Review Body**

If you have ever had a cheap blue tarp shred in a windstorm or watched a standard 6 mil poly sheet rip from a single staple pull, you understand the appeal of string-reinforced plastic. The Farm Plastic Supply Dura Skrim is the upgrade you buy when you are tired of patching holes. This is not a vapor barrier for a crawlspace; this is a working material for greenhouses, temporary shelters, and construction enclosures.

**What you are actually getting**

The Dura Skrim is a 6 mil clear polyethylene sheet laminated with a crosshatched fiberglass string grid. That grid is the entire point. When you cut it, the edges do not want to split. When you staple it, the reinforced webbing catches the staple and holds. The clear film lets in roughly 85-90% of available light, which makes it a legitimate option for overwintering plants or extending a growing season.

I have seen buyers use this for everything from covering hay bales to lining a chicken coop. As shown in the product image, it comes on a compact roll, but do not let the packaging fool you—unrolling a 100-foot section of this stuff is a workout. It is significantly stiffer and heavier than a standard 6 mil sheet.

**Real-world performance**

The string reinforcement is not just marketing. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I used this to cover a hoop house and had 40 mph winds. The plastic held, but the wood frame gave way." That is the exact scenario where this product earns its keep. Standard clear poly would have shredded at the corners. The Dura Skrim flexes, and when it does tear, the rip stops at the first string intersection instead of racing across the sheet.

Cold weather performance is better than expected. I have seen reports of this staying pliable down to around 20°F without cracking when handled carefully. It is not as cold-weather friendly as a true greenhouse film, but it beats standard hardware store poly by a wide margin.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The durability feedback is overwhelmingly positive. Reviewers repeatedly mention that it survives seasons rather than weekends. One long-term user wrote: "I've had a section up for two years in the Pacific Northwest. It's yellowed a bit but still structurally sound." That aligns with my experience—this is a product that rewards careful installation.

The edge quality is also worth noting. The reinforced border takes staples without tearing, and if you install grommets, they hold tight. This is a huge quality-of-life improvement over flimsy poly that rips at the first gust of wind.

**Where it falls short**

Let me be direct: this is not a permanent outdoor solution. The packaging and product descriptions are honest about this, but some buyers miss it. This is not UV-stabilized. In full sun, expect 6 to 9 months of reliable service before the plastic starts to yellow and stiffen. If you need a 5-year greenhouse film, buy a 5-year greenhouse film.

The weight is also a factor. Standard 6 mil poly is manageable; this stuff is not. A 20-foot by 100-foot roll weighs around 45 pounds and has zero give. You need a second person for installation, and you need to account for the stiffness when laying it over curved frames. It wants to fight you.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hobby greenhouse owners**: If you build a PVC hoop house every spring, this is your material. It lasts through the season and then some.
- **Contractors needing temporary enclosures**: The tear resistance means you can staple, screw, and even nail it without destroying it.
- **Homesteaders covering equipment or feed**: The string grid keeps small punctures from becoming catastrophic rips.

**Skip it if** you need a permanent greenhouse glazing, if you are installing it solo, or if you are looking for cheap vapor barrier. This is a premium temporary material, priced accordingly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use this for a greenhouse in a windy area?**
A: Yes, with proper framing. The string reinforcement handles wind load far better than standard poly. But you must fasten it every 12-18 inches—the plastic will not fail, but your frame might.

**Q: How long does it really last outdoors?**
A: In direct sun, 6-9 months before it yellows and stiffens. In shaded or partial-sun applications, expect 12-18 months. It is not a permanent solution.

**Q: Can I tape or glue this, or do I need staples?**
A: Tape and adhesive do not bond well to the string grid. Use staples, screws with washers, or grommets. If you need a seal, double-sided greenhouse tape works at the seams.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a tough, tear-resistant temporary cover that survives wind, staples, and cold snaps. **Skip it if** you need UV-stabilized long-term exposure or you are working solo.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Dura Skrim is a specialized tool that does its job exceptionally well. It loses points only for the lack of UV protection and the physical effort required to install it. For the right application, it is the best clear poly sheeting you can buy at this price point.
