## What You Get Out of the Box

The unit ships mostly assembled, which is a relief. You'll attach the handle assembly, the winch crank, and the stabilizing legs. The frame is steel—not the thin-walled tubing you see on budget units—and the powder coat is evenly applied. The foldable design is a genuine space-saver: when collapsed, it stands roughly 6.5 feet tall but tucks into a corner with a footprint smaller than a standard office chair. The lift forks are 27 inches long, which fits a standard half-pallet (48x40) but will overhang on full-size pallets.

The winch is a dual-gear ratchet system with a 2,200 lb rated capacity. The steel cable is 3/32" galvanized, and the hook has a safety latch that actually engages properly. The base includes two rigid and two swivel casters—the swivels lock, which is critical for keeping the load stable during winching.

## Real-World Performance: The Good

The winching mechanism is where this unit earns its keep. The 2:1 gear ratio means you’re not fighting the load—a 400 lb pallet of tile cranks up with moderate effort, not back-breaking strain. The ratchet pawl engages crisply, and the brake holds the load at any height without creeping. I tested it with 800 lbs of bagged concrete, and the frame did not flex or twist. That’s the hallmark of a properly gusseted steel weld.

Buyers consistently mention the build quality. One verified purchaser wrote, *"I've gone through two cheaper stackers in three years. This one feels like it's built by a company that actually understands load dynamics. The welds are clean, and the caster mounting plate is 3/8" thick—not stamped sheet metal."* That tracks with what I saw. The fork tines are 2.5" wide and rated for a 42" load center, which is honest engineering for the class.

## Where It Falls Short: The Honest Tradeoffs

First, the "heavy duty" label is doing heavy lifting. The 2,200 lb rating is the *static* winch capacity, not the dynamic lifting capacity. Realistically, you should keep loads under 1,200 lbs for safe, controlled lifting. At full rated capacity, the crank effort becomes significant, and the frame—while sturdy—will sway if you don't lock the swivel casters.

Second, the manual operation is slow. A full lift from floor to maximum height (about 63 inches) takes 45-60 seconds of steady cranking. If you're moving dozens of loads a day, your shoulder will feel it. This is not a replacement for an electric pallet jack.

Third, the foot pedal for lowering is stiff out of the box. Several reviewers noted it requires a solid stomp to release the hydraulic descent valve. It loosens after about 20 cycles, but it's a minor annoyance initially.

## Who Should Buy This (and Who Shouldn't)

**Buy it if:** You're a home workshop owner, small farm operator, or light commercial user who needs to lift 500-1,000 lbs occasionally. You value a compact footprint and don't want to spend $1,500+ on a powered stacker. The foldable design makes it ideal for a garage where floor space is premium.

**Skip it if:** You're running a busy shipping dock moving pallets all day. The manual winch will exhaust your team, and the 27" forks won't handle full 48" pallets safely. Also skip if you need to lift above 63 inches—this unit tops out there.

**Q: Can I lift a full 48x40 pallet with this?**
A: Technically yes, but the forks are 27 inches long. You'll have significant overhang, which shifts the load center and reduces safe capacity. Stick to half-pallets or custom skids for best results.

**Q: Does the winch lock automatically?**

**Q: How much floor space does it take when folded?**
A: About 24" x 30" footprint and roughly 6.5 feet tall. It rolls on its casters, so you can wheel it to a wall or corner without lifting.
