## What You Actually Get

The box contains four individual dolly units—not a single welded frame. Each unit is a solid steel caster with a rubber-coated wheel and a threaded post that screws into the snowmobile’s existing mounting holes (typically on the skis and rear tunnel). The 500-pound-per-caster rating means a 1,000+ pound sled is supported without breaking a sweat. The wheels are roughly 4 inches in diameter, which is the sweet spot for rolling over garage floor cracks and small debris without catching.

## Real-World Performance

The Rover 360’s genius is its 360-degree swivel design. Unlike fixed casters that force you to wrestle the sled in straight lines, these pivot freely, letting you spin a 700-pound machine in place to face the door. The rubber-coated wheels grip concrete well but don’t mar epoxy or painted floors—a detail that matters if your garage doubles as a workspace.

Installation is genuinely tool-free: remove the factory skis or tunnel bolts, thread in the dolly posts, and you’re done. Most buyers report a 10-minute setup for the whole set. The threads are machined to match standard snowmobile bolt patterns (Ski-Doo, Polaris, Arctic Cat, Yamaha), though you should verify your model’s spacing before ordering—some newer sleds use metric threads that may require an adapter.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs exist. First, these are **not** meant for off-garage use. The small wheels sink into gravel, snow, or soft dirt instantly—this is strictly a hard-surface tool. Second, the rubber coating on the wheels can leave faint scuff marks on very light-colored, polished concrete after heavy use. Third, the posts are fixed-length; if your sled sits low, you may need to jack it up slightly to get the dollies threaded in. Finally, a few reviewers mentioned the included hardware is zinc-plated, which is fine indoors but will corrode if you leave the dollies on during wet, salty rides.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You store your sled indoors or in an enclosed trailer and want to move it solo without a winch or a second person. It’s ideal for garage organizers who need to park multiple sleds side-by-side or nose-in.

**Skip it if:** You park on gravel or dirt, or you leave your sled outside in winter weather. Also skip if your sled’s undercarriage has non-standard bolt spacing that won’t accept these posts.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my 2023 Polaris RMK?**  
A: Most late-model Polaris, Ski-Doo, and Arctic Cat models use the standard 5.5-inch center spacing on the rear tunnel and 6-inch on the skis. Measure your bolt holes before ordering—if they’re off by more than a quarter inch, you’ll need adapters.

**Q: Can I leave these on while riding?**  
A: No. They’re for storage and trailering only. Riding with them attached will damage the casters and could cause a crash.

**Q: Do I need all four, or can I use just two?**  
A: Use all four for stability. Two on the rear only leaves the front skis dragging, which scratches floors and makes steering difficult.
