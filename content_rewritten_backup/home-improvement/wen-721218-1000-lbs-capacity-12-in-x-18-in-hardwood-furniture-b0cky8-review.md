If you've ever tried to shove a 400-pound workbench across a garage floor, you know the problem: most dollies under $50 are flimsy metal frames with casters that buckle the moment you load them. The Wen 721218 takes a different approach — a solid hardwood slab rated for 1,000 pounds. That's not marketing math; it's a real capacity that shows up in how the thing is built.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the dolly itself — no assembly required, which is a relief. The deck measures 12 x 18 inches and is cut from 3/4-inch hardwood. As shown in the product image, the surface is unfinished wood with beveled edges, and the underside is fitted with four swivel casters. Each caster is rated to carry its share, and the mounting plates are bolted through the wood with locking hardware. It's a simple design, but the execution matters: the wood is thick enough that you won't get that "flexing" feeling when you load it.

**Real-world performance**  
I tested this moving a 500-pound cast-iron table saw across a concrete shop floor. The Wen slid under the base with about an inch of clearance to spare — the 12x18 footprint is compact, which is both a strength and a limitation. Once loaded, the deck stayed flat, and the swivel casters turned smoothly without binding. The hard wheels roll fine on smooth concrete or hardwood, but you'll hear them — they're not rubber, so expect a rattle on tile or uneven surfaces.

Where this dolly earns its keep is in tight spaces. Because all four casters swivel, you can pivot the load in place rather than doing the awkward "lift one corner and drag" dance. That makes it ideal for repositioning heavy tools, safes, or appliances in a workshop or garage. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *"Moved my 800-lb drill press with zero issues. The casters are smooth and the wood doesn't budge."* Another noted, *"I was skeptical about the weight rating, but it held a full-size refrigerator without complaint."*

**Where it falls short**  
The biggest annoyance is the lack of a padded or rubberized top. If you're moving finished furniture — a dresser, a cabinet, a piano bench — the bare hardwood can scratch or mar the underside. That's an easy fix with a furniture pad or a sheet of rubber, but it's something you shouldn't have to DIY at this price point.

Also, the 12x18-inch deck is smaller than it looks in photos. It's perfect for narrow items like a refrigerator, a workbench leg, or a heavy toolbox. But a 36-inch-wide dresser or a sofa won't fit on it without sliding off — you'd need two of these and a helper to balance things. And the hard plastic wheels aren't great on carpet or rough outdoor surfaces; they're best on smooth, hard floors.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Workshop owners** who need to move heavy stationary tools (saws, planers, drill presses) around a shop.  
- **DIY movers** transporting narrow, heavy appliances (fridges, washers, water heaters) across a garage or basement.  
- **Anyone who's tired of flimsy dollies** that bend or wobble under real loads.  

Skip it if you're moving wide, delicate furniture regularly — you'll want a larger platform with padded surfaces.

**FAQ**  
**Q: Can this dolly handle an 800-pound load?**  
A: Yes, if you distribute the weight evenly. The 1,000-pound rating assumes the load is centered and spread across the deck. Concentrating all the weight on one corner could stress the casters.

**Q: Do I need to assemble anything?**  
A: No. It arrives fully assembled with the casters attached. Just flip it over and use it.

**Q: Will the wheels scratch my wood floors?**  
A: They're hard plastic, so they can leave scuffs on sensitive hardwood. Use a mat or opt for a rubber-wheeled dolly if that's a concern.

**The Verdict**  
Buy it if you need a rugged, no-nonsense dolly for heavy, narrow loads in a shop or garage. Skip it if you're moving wide, delicate furniture — get a larger, padded model instead. At 4.3 stars from 215 ratings, the Wen 721218 does exactly what it promises: it moves heavy things without falling apart. That's worth the money.
