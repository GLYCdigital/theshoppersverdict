The Roravilila 18-inch outdoor wall light doesn’t try to reinvent the fixture. It just executes a classic design exceptionally well, and that’s exactly what most homeowners need.

**What’s in the box**

You get two complete wall lights (this listing is for a pair), each with the die-cast housing, 12 clear glass side panels, a top cap, mounting plate, and the standard complement of screws and wire nuts. Each fixture measures 18 inches tall with a 7.5-inch width and 8.5-inch projection from the wall. That projection matters—it clears the siding and gives the 12-sided glass room to throw light outward rather than hugging the wall.

The glass is real, not plastic. Each of the 12 panels is individually seated into the frame, which is both a design strength and a maintenance consideration. The die-cast aluminum body has a matte black finish that looks better in person than in the product photos—it has a subtle texture that hides fingerprints.

**Build quality and performance**

The housing is substantial. At 6.4 pounds per fixture, these feel solid when you lift them out of the box. The finish is baked on, not painted over bare metal, so it should hold up through a few seasons of weather without chalking or peeling. Multiple Amazon reviewers mention installation in coastal areas, and one buyer noted, “I live 300 feet from the ocean and these have been up for eight months with zero corrosion.”

The 12-sided clear glass does real work here. Unlike flat-panel fixtures that throw a narrow beam, this design produces a 360-degree wash of light. With a standard 60W equivalent LED bulb installed, it illuminates a full porch swing and both flanking columns. The open bottom also lets light spill downward onto the door, which is where you actually need it for unlocking at night.

The E26 base accepts any standard bulb. That’s a plus for buyers who want to install smart bulbs or vintage-style Edison bulbs for a warmer aesthetic. No integrated LED means you can replace bulbs as technology improves, rather than replacing the whole fixture.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across the 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, three themes repeat. First, the look: “These look like they cost three times the price,” one reviewer wrote. The faceted glass gives off a lantern quality that reads as high-end from the street. Second, the light spread—people consistently mention how evenly the light distributes across the whole porch, not just a hotspot directly below. Third, installation ease: standard wiring, clear instructions, and a mounting plate that can be oriented horizontally or vertically depending on your junction box.

**Where it falls short**

The glass is the tradeoff. Clear panels mean every smudge, water spot, and dead bug is visible from ten feet away. If you’re the type who never climbs a ladder to clean fixtures, choose a seeded or frosted glass version instead. Buyers in dusty areas have also noted the glass needs a wipe-down every few weeks to maintain that crisp lantern look.

The included mounting screws are soft and strip easily if you overtighten. Plan to swap them for #8 stainless screws from your local hardware store before installation. Also, there’s no photocell or motion sensor built in. That’s not a flaw at this price point, but budget for smart bulbs if automation matters to you.

**Who should buy this**

Two specific buyer profiles fit best. First, homeowners replacing dated bronze or brass fixtures who want a modern-craftsman aesthetic without paying boutique lighting store prices. Second, anyone with a wide porch or entryway—the 360-degree light spread covers a 10-foot span far better than standard flat fixtures. Skip it if you want smart-home integration out of the box or if you live in a heavy-pollen area and refuse to clean exterior glass.

**FAQ**

**Q: What bulb type should I use?**
A: Standard E26 base. A 60W equivalent LED in warm white (2700K) gives the best balance of brightness and ambiance. Avoid 100W equivalents—the fixture isn’t rated for that heat output.

**Q: Can these be installed on stucco or brick?**
A: Yes, but you’ll need a masonry bit and appropriate anchors. The included mounting plate works with standard junction boxes, but brick requires pilot holes.

**Q: Are they safe for direct rain exposure?**
A: The listing says weatherproof, and the gasketed glass panels back that up. That said, the open bottom design means water can enter if mounted where rain blows straight up—install under an eave or overhang for best results.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want premium looks, wide light coverage, and a fixture that won’t rust or fade within a few seasons. **Skip it if** you refuse to clean glass or require built-in sensors.

At 4.6 out of 5, this is one of the better value wall lights in its class. The build quality justifies the price, and the 12-sided glass sets it apart from the generic flat fixtures at big-box stores. Just budget ten minutes and better screws for installation.
