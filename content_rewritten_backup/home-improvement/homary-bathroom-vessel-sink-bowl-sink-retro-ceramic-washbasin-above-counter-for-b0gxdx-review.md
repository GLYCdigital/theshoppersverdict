Let's be blunt: most vessel sinks under $150 look like cheap hotel fixtures bolted to a slab of granite. The Homary Retro Ceramic Washbasin isn't that. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, this one's earned its place in the top tier of above-counter sinks — but only if you understand what you're buying.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the ceramic bowl itself, a pop-up drain assembly, and an overflow ring. That's it. No faucet, no mounting ring, no silicone sealant. The sink measures roughly 15.75 inches in diameter and stands about 5.5 inches tall — standard vessel dimensions, but the weight surprises you. This is dense, solid ceramic, not the hollow-feeling stuff you find at big-box stores. One reviewer put it simply: "I was shocked at the quality for the price. It's heavy, glazed perfectly, and feels like a $300 sink."

**How it performs in real bathrooms**

Installation is straightforward if you've mounted a vessel sink before. You'll want a countertop with pre-drilled holes for the drain and faucet, or a drill with a diamond bit. The pop-up drain threads on cleanly and seals without needing five rounds of plumber's tape. The overflow is functional, not decorative — a detail many budget sinks skip entirely.

The glaze is where this thing shines. It's thick, glossy, and remarkably uniform. Coffee rings, toothpaste residue, hard water spots — they all wipe off with a damp cloth. The bowl's curved interior means no sharp corners trapping grime. Cleaning takes about 30 seconds.

The retro shape deserves a callout. It's not a perfect circle — there's a subtle oval taper that gives it a vintage, art-deco feel. It photographs beautifully and looks genuinely expensive on a walnut vanity or white marble top.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The overwhelming theme in the reviews is value. "Looks way more expensive than it is," appears in various forms throughout the feedback. Another recurring point: the ceramic doesn't scratch easily. One buyer mentioned dropping a glass bottle into the bowl — the bottle shattered, the sink didn't have a mark.

**Where it falls short**

The glossy finish is a double-edged sword. It shows every water spot and splatter. If you have hard water, plan on wiping the bowl down after each use or you'll see mineral buildup within a week. That's not a defect — it's the nature of high-gloss ceramic — but it's worth knowing.

The bigger issue: faucet compatibility. Because the sink sits above the counter, your faucet needs a tall enough reach to clear the bowl's rim. Standard bathroom faucets won't work. You'll need a vessel-style faucet with at least a 6-inch height. That's an additional $50–$150 depending on what you choose. The product page shows the sink with a faucet, but that's not included — a point that's caused some buyer confusion.

**Who should buy this**

This sink is ideal for three types of shoppers: anyone renovating a guest bathroom on a budget, someone building a powder room with a vintage or farmhouse aesthetic, and DIYers who want a statement piece without a contractor's markup. It's a poor fit if you need a fully integrated countertop sink, want a matte finish to hide water marks, or expect a complete kit with faucet included.

**FAQ**

*Does the sink work with any countertop material?*
Yes, as long as the surface is flat and can support the weight. Solid wood, marble, quartz, and concrete all work. Avoid thin laminate or hollow-core vanities — the weight could crack them.

*Is the drain metal or plastic?*
The pop-up assembly is metal with a ceramic-coated stopper. The overflow ring is metal too. No cheap plastic parts in the drain path.

*Can I install this myself or do I need a plumber?*
If you're comfortable with basic tools and have access to the drain pipe below, you can DIY it in under an hour. The hardest part is getting the silicone sealant level around the base. If you've never done plumbing work, pay a handyman $50–$75 to avoid a leaky mess.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a heavy, well-glazed vessel sink with vintage character and don't mind spending extra on a compatible faucet. You're okay wiping down the glossy surface regularly. You appreciate getting $300 quality at roughly half the price.

**Skip it if:** You want a complete solution with no additional purchases, prefer a low-maintenance matte finish, or need something that hides water spots. Also skip if your countertop is flimsy or you're not prepared to handle the faucet height requirement.

The Homary Retro Ceramic Washbasin is one of the better value plays in the vessel sink market right now — just budget for the faucet and keep a microfiber cloth handy. 4.6/5.
