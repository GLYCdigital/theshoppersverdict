Let's cut through the cordless sander hype. The Milwaukee M12 Fuel 6 Random Orbital Sander with 3/16-inch orbit has been out for a bit now, and with 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, buyers are mostly happy — but not universally. Here's the real story.

## What's actually in the box

You get the bare tool only — no battery, no charger. That's typical for Milwaukee M12 Fuel line, but worth flagging if you're new to the platform. The sander itself features a 6-inch hook-and-loop backing pad, a variable speed dial (7,000–11,000 OPM), and a built-in dust canister with a clear window so you can see when it's full. There's also a vacuum adapter port if you prefer hooking it to a shop vac instead of using the canister.

## Performance: The surprise is real

Here's what surprised me — and what most buyers echo in their reviews: this little 12-volt sander punches above its weight. The brushless Fuel motor delivers enough torque to do real material removal, not just finish sanding. One buyer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical a 12V could do 80-grit work, but it ate through old paint on a deck like my corded Ridgid used to."*

The 3/16-inch orbit is the sweet spot for most DIY and pro work. It's aggressive enough for paint and varnish removal but not so harsh that it leaves deep swirls. The variable speed dial matters — drop it to 4-5 for finish passes and it behaves much smoother.

Dust collection genuinely works. The canister captures maybe 85-90% of the fine dust, which is dramatically better than the bag-style collectors on cheaper sanders. You'll still get dust in the air, but your lungs and workshop floor will thank you.

## Battery life: The honest tradeoff

This is where the 4.3-star average starts making sense. On a standard 4.0Ah M12 battery, you're looking at roughly 15-20 minutes of continuous sanding. That's fine for a small project or a couple of touch-up panels, but it's not a production tool. Buyers consistently mention this. One review summed it up: *"Great sander, but I burn through batteries fast. Buy the 6.0Ah pack or keep two 4.0s charged."*

If you're already invested in M12 batteries, this is a no-brainer. If you're starting fresh, factor in the cost of a charger and at least one high-capacity battery — it changes the value equation.

## Where it falls short

Beyond battery life, the 6-inch pad can feel cumbersome for tight spots or curved surfaces. It's not a detail sander. Also, the hook-and-loop backing pad is decent quality but not premium — some buyers reported the adhesive failing after several months of heavy use, requiring a pad replacement (about $20).

Finally, it's not the quietest sander. It's not deafening, but it's noisier than premium corded models. Wear hearing protection.

## Who should buy this

- **M12 platform loyalists** — if you already have batteries, this is an easy buy.
- **Contractors and serious DIYers** who need cordless convenience for sanding cabinets, trim, drywall patches, or small furniture projects.
- **Anyone tired of dragging a cord and a shop vac around** — the canister works well enough that you can skip the vac for smaller jobs.

**Skip it if** you're doing production sanding all day, or if you need a do-everything sander for large surfaces like full tabletops and doors. In those cases, grab a corded 6-inch with a 5mm orbit instead.

## FAQ: What shoppers actually ask

**Q: Does it come with batteries?**
No, this is the bare tool. You'll need M12 batteries and a charger. If you don't have them, buy the kit version or factor in the cost.

**Q: Can it handle 80-grit for stripping paint?**
Yes, with the 3/16 orbit it does surprisingly well. Keep the speed dial high (6-7) and let the tool's weight do the work.

**Q: Is the dust canister worth using, or should I hook up a vac?**
For small jobs, the canister works fine. For extended sanding, hooking it to a shop vac improves collection and keeps the canister from filling mid-project.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're on the M12 platform and need a genuinely capable cordless sander for finish work, small projects, and anything where dragging a cord is a pain.

**Skip it if:** You need all-day runtime, do large-scale sanding, or are starting fresh without any M12 batteries — the total cost might be better spent on a corded model.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely impressive compact sander held back only by battery limitations and a pad that could be more durable. For the right buyer, it's a game-changer. For the wrong one, it's frustrating. Know which you are before you buy.
