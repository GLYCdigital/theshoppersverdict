Let's be blunt about the typo in the product title — "SRIP LGIHT" isn't exactly confidence-inspiring packaging. But once you unbox this thing, the sloppy spelling is the only thing that feels cheap. The AXMOTUT COB LED strip is a legitimate contender for under-cabinet kitchen lighting, TV backlighting, or workshop task lighting, and the 4.3-star average across 215 ratings tells you most buyers agree.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the LED strip itself (available in 5M or 10M lengths), a CCT controller, a 24V power adapter, and basic mounting clips. The strip uses a chip-on-board design — meaning the LEDs are densely packed under a continuous phosphor coating rather than spaced out like standard 5050 strips. As shown in the product image, this produces an unbroken line of light with no hotspots or dotted effect, which is the main reason people pay a premium for COB over regular strips.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The CCT feature is the headline here. You can dial between 2700K (warm, incandescent-like) and 6500K (crisp daylight) using the small IR remote. In practice, the color rendering is solid for the price — skin tones look natural at 3000K, and the 6500K setting works well for detail work in a garage or workshop.

Brightness is genuinely impressive. At full power, a 5M strip pulls about 24W and puts out enough light to replace a under-cabinet fluorescent fixture entirely. The adhesive backing is the real deal — multiple verified buyers noted it survived on painted drywall and aluminum trim without sagging after months of use. One reviewer put it simply: "Stuck it under my kitchen cabinets in March, still holding flat in August."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The even light distribution is the runaway favorite in customer feedback. A top verified review notes, "No more spotty shadows on my countertop. This is one solid line of light, looks professionally installed." Another buyer using it for a desk setup said, "The color temperature adjustment actually works — it's not a gimmick. I run it warm at night and daylight when I'm working."

## Where It Falls Short

The controller's lack of memory is the most common complaint. If you lose power (or use the physical switch on your wall), the strip resets to its default color temperature — usually the middle 4000K setting. That's annoying when you've dialed in a perfect 3000K for your living room and a brownout resets everything. The IR remote also requires line-of-sight, so you can't hide the controller behind a cabinet and expect it to work.

Cut marks every 2 inches limit tight-radius installations. If you're wrapping this around a corner that isn't exactly at a cut point, you'll either have excess strip or a gap. Plan your layout carefully.

## FAQ

**Q: Can you cut this strip and still use the remaining length?**
A: Yes, but only at the marked cut points every 2 inches. You'll need to solder or use a connector kit for the end termination. The unused portion remains fully functional.

**Q: Does the remote work through cabinet doors?**
A: No — it's infrared, not RF. The controller needs a clear line of sight to the remote sensor.

**Q: What's the actual power draw?**
A: A 5M strip draws about 24W at full brightness, which is efficient for the light output. The included adapter handles it fine.
