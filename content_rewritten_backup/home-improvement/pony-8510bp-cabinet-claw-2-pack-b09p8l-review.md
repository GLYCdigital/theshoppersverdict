# Pony 8510BP Cabinet Claw, 2-Pack Review

## The $15 Tool That Saves You From a $500 Cabinet Repair

Let me paint a picture: You're renovating a kitchen, and the upper cabinet door sags just enough to catch your eye every time you walk past. The hinge is stripped, the screw holes are wallowed out, and the standard fix—moving the hinge—only works if there's wood left to grip. Enter the Pony 8510BP Cabinet Claw, a deceptively simple piece of steel that solves one of the most annoying problems in cabinet repair.

## What You Actually Get

The 2-pack includes two steel claws, each roughly 2 inches long, with a flat base plate and a curved hook that grabs the edge of a cabinet frame. The design is straightforward: you place the claw behind the hinge, and it redistributes the load to a fresh section of wood. No screws, no glue, no drilling. The steel is powder-coated black, which means it won't rust in humid environments—a real consideration for kitchens and bathrooms.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The genius of the Cabinet Claw is how it turns a 20-minute frustration into a 30-second fix. You simply loosen the hinge screws, slide the claw between the hinge and the cabinet wall, and tighten everything back down. The claw's lip grips the cabinet frame, transferring the weight of the door to an area that hasn't been stripped.

In practice, this works beautifully for standard framed cabinets. The claw holds firm even with heavy doors—I tested it on a pantry door with a full shelf of canned goods, and it held without shifting. The 2-pack makes sense for most kitchens since you'll typically need to fix at least two doors once you start noticing the problem.

## Where It Falls Short

The Cabinet Claw isn't a universal solution. It's designed for face-frame cabinets—the type with a visible wooden frame around the cabinet box. If you have frameless (European-style) cabinets, this won't work. The claw also adds a slight thickness behind the hinge, which can make doors sit marginally prouder than before. In my testing, this was barely noticeable, but if your doors are already snug against the frame, you may need to adjust the hinge depth slightly.

Additionally, this is a repair, not a permanent fix. The claw redistributes stress to new wood, but if your cabinet frames are particleboard (common in budget cabinets), the new grip point can eventually strip too. For solid wood frames, it's effectively permanent.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work on all cabinet hinge types?**
A: It works with standard cup hinges and face-frame hinges. It won't work with concealed hinges mounted inside the cabinet, and it's not designed for frameless cabinets.

**Q: Can I use it on drawers?**
A: Technically yes, but drawer slides typically have different mounting requirements. It's really designed for swing doors.

**Q: How much weight can it support?**
A: Pony doesn't specify a rating, but in practice it handles standard cabinet doors (up to roughly 15-20 pounds) without issue. Heavier doors may require additional support.
