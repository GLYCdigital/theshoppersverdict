The installation procedure is straightforward: shut off the water supply, remove the old diaphragm bonnet, swap in this assembly, and reattach. ## Where it falls short

The price is the biggest friction point. Aftermarket diaphragm kits run roughly half the cost, and if you're only dealing with a worn rubber diaphragm (not a clogged filter or failing solenoid), a generic replacement can work fine. However, you sacrifice the exact calibration and material quality that Sloan builds in.

The dual filters require periodic maintenance. In hard water areas, mineral buildup can restrict flow over time. It's not difficult to clean — just remove the filter housing and rinse the screens — but it's an ongoing task that a single-screen system doesn't demand as frequently.
