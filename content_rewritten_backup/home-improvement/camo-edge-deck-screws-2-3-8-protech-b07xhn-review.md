If you've ever stared at a deck covered in countersunk screw heads, wood filler, and sanding dust, you already know the pain. The CAMO Edge system eliminates that entire mess — and these 2-3/8" ProTech screws are the consumable that makes it work. At 4.8 stars across 240 ratings, they're clearly doing something right. But they're not magic. Here's what you need to know before you buy.

## What's in the box

You get a 500-count box of T20 star-drive screws, 2-3/8" long, with a #8 gauge shaft. The ProTech coating is a grayish-green corrosion-resistant finish specifically formulated for ACQ-treated lumber — the stuff that eats plain galvanized screws alive. The head is flat and thin, designed to slide into the hidden channel of the CAMO Edge jig. There's no square drive, no Phillips, no combo. Just T20.

## How it actually performs

The genius (and limitation) of these screws is that they're only half the system. You need the CAMO Edge jig — a tool that clamps onto the board edge and guides the screw at a precise angle into the side of the deck board. The result: zero fasteners visible on the walking surface.

In practice, the star drive is the standout feature. It engages cleanly, and even with an impact driver on full power, cam-out is rare. The self-tapping point bites into pressure-treated pine, cedar, and even Ipe (with pilot holes) without splitting — assuming you're not driving too close to the board end.

One buyer put it simply: *"These screws are the real deal. I built a 400 sq ft deck with zero stripped heads."* Another noted the coating held up: *"After a full season in the Pacific Northwest rain, no rust spots on the screw heads where they meet the joists."*

The hidden-fastener design also means you can flip boards over if one side gets damaged or warped — a flexibility you don't get with face-screwed decks.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. First, you're locked into the CAMO Edge ecosystem. These screws won't work with any other jig, and if you lose or break the jig mid-project, you're dead in the water until a replacement arrives. That's a real dependency.

Second, the 2-3/8" length is perfect for 1x and 5/4 decking, but if you're building with 2x6 or 2x4 boards — common in some regions — you'll need the longer 2-3/4" version. Check your material before ordering.

Third, the ProTech coating is good, but it's not stainless steel. On coastal decks with constant salt spray, some users report surface staining over time. Not structural failure, but cosmetic. One reviewer warned: *"Fine for inland decks. If you live on the water, spend the extra money on the stainless version."*

## Who should buy this

**Buy these if:** You're building a new deck or re-decking an existing one with 1x or 5/4 boards, and you want a clean, fastener-free surface without the labor of plugging and sanding. You already own (or are willing to buy) the CAMO Edge jig. You're working with pressure-treated lumber.

**Skip these if:** You're just replacing a few loose boards on an existing deck — you'd need the jig for a handful of screws, which isn't cost-effective. Or if you're working with 2x material, in which case get the longer screws. And if you're building within a mile of salt water, consider the stainless variant.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need the CAMO Edge jig to use these screws?**
A: Yes. These screws are designed specifically for the CAMO Edge hidden-fastener system. They won't work as general-purpose deck screws. The jig costs extra, but it's a one-time investment.

**Q: Can I use these with composite decking?**
A: The ProTech screws are intended for wood. For composite, use the CAMO Edge composite screws, which have a different thread design and are approved by major composite manufacturers.

**Q: How many screws do I need per board?**
A: Typically two screws per joist location — one on each side of the board. A 12-foot board over joists at 16" on center takes about 16 screws per board. A 500-count box covers roughly 30 boards.
