Let me be blunt: the Kaba 1011 looks like it belongs on a school hallway or an office supply closet, and that's exactly the point. This is a mechanical pushbutton lock that's been a contractor favorite for decades because it does one thing extremely well — it lets trusted people in without keys while keeping everyone else out. But "trusted" is doing a lot of work here, so let's dig into what you're actually getting for your money.

**What's in the box**

You get the complete cylindrical lockset: the outside knob with the pushbutton keypad, the inside knob with the turn piece, the latch assembly, mounting hardware, and a strike plate. No batteries, no wires, no app, no smart-home integration to fiddle with. The US26D satin chrome finish shown in the product image is the bright, polished option — it's the same finish you see on commercial-grade hardware in office buildings, and it holds up well against fingerprints compared to brass or oil-rubbed bronze.

**How it actually works**

The concept is simple: press your 3-to-7 digit code, then turn the knob. The lock uses a mechanical pushbutton mechanism — no electronics to fail, no solenoid to jam. That's the biggest selling point here. When the power goes out, when it's -10°F outside, when your smart lock's Wi-Fi drops — this thing still works. The key override takes a Schlage C keyway, which means you can rekey it to match your existing house key if you're already on that system.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The reliability theme comes up constantly in reviews. One buyer put it this way: "Installed this on a rental property two years ago. Zero issues. The tenants can't lose keys they don't have." Another noted, "The mechanical mechanism means no dead batteries at the worst possible moment." That's the core value proposition — this lock removes the "forgot my key" problem entirely for day-to-day use.

**Where it falls short**

Now the honest part. First, the factory default code is 3-4-5-6, and it's printed right in the manual. If you install this and don't change the code immediately, you've essentially installed a decorative knob with extra steps. It takes about 30 seconds to change, but it's a real security gap.

Second, the pushbutton resistance is stiff. Several reviewers note that you need a firm press to register each digit, and it can be finicky for kids, elderly users, or anyone with arthritis. It does loosen up with use, but expect a break-in period.

Third — and this matters if you're thinking about commercial use — this is not a Grade 1 lock. It's rated for light commercial and residential applications. A heavy-traffic office door will wear this out faster than you'd like. The latch is solid, but the internal mechanism isn't built for thousands of cycles per month.

**Who should buy this**

This lock is ideal for three specific scenarios: rental properties where you don't want to rekey between tenants, interior security doors (office, storage, utility rooms) where you need code access without wiring, and vacation homes where you want to give contractors or cleaners temporary codes. If you're looking for a front-door smart lock with app control, auto-lock scheduling, or remote access — this isn't it. This is the analog workhorse, not the digital showpiece.

**FAQ**

*Can I change the code without removing the lock?*
Yes. There's a small access hole on the interior mount — insert a paperclip, enter the current code, then your new code. Takes about 30 seconds. The manual walks you through it clearly.

*Does this fit standard residential doors?*
It fits standard 2-1/8" bore holes and adjusts for door thicknesses from 1-3/8" to 1-3/4". Standard residential doors are covered.

*What if I forget the code?*
Use the key override. That's why it's there. If you lose the key and forget the code, you're calling a locksmith — there's no backdoor reset.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a keyless entry lock that will never leave you locked out due to a dead battery, and you're okay with the mechanical simplicity. It's a proven, durable design that earns its 4.2-star rating through sheer reliability.

**Skip it if** you need smart-home integration, auto-lock features, or you're installing on a high-traffic commercial door. For that, look at Grade 1 rated electronic locks.

Rating: 4.2/5 — a solid, dependable mechanical lock with a few quirks that don't outweigh its core value.
