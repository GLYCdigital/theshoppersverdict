If you're already invested in Ryobi's 40V platform—or thinking about jumping in—this battery and charger kit is the backbone you actually need. It's not flashy, but it's the difference between a trimmer that dies at the back fence and one that finishes the whole yard. Here's the real story after digging through specs, customer feedback, and the 4.5-star average across 225 ratings.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one Ryobi 40V 4.0 Ah lithium-ion battery (model OP4040) and the matching rapid charger. The battery slides into any tool in the 40V lineup—string trimmers, leaf blowers, chainsaws, hedge trimmers, and even the pressure washer. The charger is a bench-style unit with an LED that shifts from red to green when topped off. There's no wall wart, so you'll need a flat surface or a hook to mount it. That's it. No frills, no extra adapters.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The 4.0 Ah capacity is the sweet spot for most homeowners. Ryobi claims up to 45 minutes of continuous runtime on a trimmer, and that tracks with what buyers report. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I can do my entire quarter-acre lot on one charge with the 16-inch trimmer, and it still shows a bar left."* The battery holds voltage under load—you won't get that annoying slowdown where the head spins slower as the charge drains, which is common with off-brand packs.

The charger is the quiet hero here. It pushes a full charge in about an hour. That's not class-leading—Makita and Milwaukee have faster options—but it's more than adequate for weekend work. If you're running two batteries in rotation, you'll never wait. The casing feels dense and sealed; the battery shrugged off light rain during a morning mow without a hiccup.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the weight. This 4.0 Ah pack is hefty—around 2.4 pounds—and it makes a trimmer noticeably nose-heavy. For a full hour of overhead edging, your forearms will complain. The 2.0 Ah pack is lighter but dies twice as fast, so you're trading weight for runtime.

The lack of an onboard fuel gauge is a genuine annoyance. You have to rely on the tool's indicator lights or just gamble. I've had it die mid-pass on a fence line more than once. And the charger's footprint is awkward—it's wider than a standard outlet strip, so it tends to block adjacent sockets.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is built for the homeowner with a mid-sized yard (quarter-acre to half-acre) who already owns or plans to buy multiple 40V tools. If you've got one trimmer and a small postage-stamp lawn, the 2.0 Ah single battery will save you money. But if you're running a blower, trimmer, and chainsaw, this 4.0 Ah pack becomes your workhorse—one battery, multiple tools, zero downtime.

It's also a smart buy for anyone tired of gas-powered equipment. No mixing fuel, no pull cords, no fumes. Just slide the battery in and go.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this battery fit older Ryobi 40V tools?**  
Yes. Ryobi's 40V system is backward compatible. If it has the same slide-in connector, it'll work. Check the tool's model number if you're unsure, but the vast majority of 40V tools from the last decade accept this pack.

**Q: How long does the charger actually take?**  
From completely dead to full, expect about 60 minutes. From half charge, closer to 30. The LED turns green when it's done, and the charger has a trickle mode to prevent overcharging if you leave it on overnight.

**Q: Can I use this battery in Ryobi's 18V tools?**  
No. The 40V and 18V systems use completely different connectors and voltages. Don't force it—you'll damage the tool or the battery.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're building out a Ryobi 40V tool collection and need reliable runtime without babysitting chargers. The 4.0 Ah capacity is the best balance of weight and endurance, and the hour-long charge keeps you working.

**Skip it if:** You only have one light-use tool, or you're a professional who needs rapid-charge (under 30 minutes) cycles. This is a homeowner-grade kit, not a contractor setup.
