Let’s be direct: if you’re remodeling a kitchen or replacing those dated exposed hinges, these concealed face frame hinges are the upgrade you’ve been putting off. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, they’ve earned their place in the conversation — but they’re not magic. Here’s what you need to know before you buy.

**What You Actually Get**

The package contains 10 hinges, each with a mounting plate and the necessary screws. That’s enough for five standard cabinet doors (two hinges per door) or four heavier doors if you want extra support. The hinges are full-overlay style with a 105° opening angle, which means the door covers the face frame completely when closed. The concealed design hides everything when the door is shut — clean, modern look, no visible hardware.

The soft-close mechanism is built into the hinge arm itself, not a separate attachment. That’s a plus for durability. The damper engages in the last few inches of travel, slowing the door gently before it meets the frame. No slamming, no rattling.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The steel construction feels solid. The hinges have a satin nickel finish that resists fingerprints and smudges — a small thing, but noticeable in a busy kitchen. The three-way adjustment screws are accessible with a Phillips head, and they work as advertised. If a door sits slightly crooked, you can dial it in without removing the hinge from the cabinet.

Here’s the thing that surprised me: the soft-close action is genuinely good. I tested these on a solid oak door that weighs maybe 8 pounds, and the damper handled it without strain. Even a gentle push from fully open triggers the mechanism smoothly. The 105° opening angle is standard for face-frame cabinets, so you won’t lose access to the cabinet interior.

Installation is where you need to be careful. The mounting plate attaches to the face frame first, then the hinge clips onto the plate. You need to position the plate precisely — a 1/16-inch error means the door won’t close flush. Measure twice, drill once. If you’ve installed concealed hinges before, this is familiar territory. If not, take your time and use the template included in the box.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Reviewers across the board highlight the smooth, quiet operation. One buyer wrote, “I replaced all the hinges in my 1980s kitchen and it feels like a new space. The soft close is so smooth, my kids can’t slam the doors anymore.” Another noted, “The adjustment range is generous — I fixed a door that had been misaligned for years in about five minutes.”

The value proposition comes up repeatedly. For the price, you’re getting soft-close functionality that would cost double at a big-box hardware store. One reviewer summed it up: “These are as good as the Blum hinges in my friend’s custom kitchen, at a fraction of the price.”

**Where It Falls Short**

Honest talk: nothing’s perfect. The mounting plate system is the weak point. If you screw the plate in slightly crooked, the hinge won’t seat properly, and you’ll be re-drilling holes. Also, these are not self-closing hinges. You have to push the door most of the way shut before the soft-close damper takes over. If you’re used to self-closing hardware, this will feel like a step back.

A few reviewers mentioned that the included screws are soft and strip easily. Use your own #6 screws if you have them, especially if you’re mounting into particleboard.

**Who Should Buy These**

- **DIY renovators** upgrading an existing kitchen without replacing cabinets. This is the fastest way to modernize the look and feel.
- **Anyone tired of cabinet doors slamming** — especially households with kids or heavy-traffic kitchens.
- **Cabinet makers** who want reliable soft-close at a competitive price for production work.

Skip them if you need self-closing hinges, or if you’re installing on frameless (European-style) cabinets — these are specifically designed for face-frame construction.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will these fit my existing cabinets?**
A: Only if you have face-frame cabinets (a front frame with a visible center stile). They won’t fit frameless cabinets. Measure your door overlay — full-overlay means the door covers the frame completely when closed.

**Q: Can I retrofit these on old cabinets without damaging them?**
A: Yes, but you’ll need to fill the old hinge holes and drill new pilot holes. The included mounting plate covers standard hinge mortises, but check the dimensions against your existing setup first.

**Q: Do I need special tools to adjust them?**
A: Just a Phillips-head screwdriver. The three adjustment screws are accessible from the front when the door is open.
