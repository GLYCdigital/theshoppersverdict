## The $30 Path Light That Doesn't Look Like It Costs $30

Most low-voltage path lights in this price bracket are plastic impostors that yellow, crack, and tilt after one winter. The hykolity ORB LED stands apart because it's built from die-cast aluminum with an oil-rubbed bronze finish that actually looks like it cost three times the price. That's the first thing buyers notice when unboxing — the weight. This thing has heft. It feels substantial in hand.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get four complete fixtures, each standing 19 inches tall with a 6.5-inch diameter base. The crackled glass shade sits atop a tapered aluminum body with a classic orb silhouette. The box includes:

- 4 assembled path lights with 18-inch lead wires
- 6-3 waterproof wire connectors (the push-in type)
- Ground stakes with anti-tip support
- No transformer — you'll need a separate low-voltage power supply (12-15V AC)

The pre-assembled design means zero construction. Pull the stake from the housing, connect your buried wire, push the stake in, and seat the light head. Ten minutes per fixture if you're slow.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The 2700K LEDs produce 120 lumens each — not blinding, but perfect for defining a walkway edge or garden border. The crackled glass shade is the standout feature. It scatters light into a soft, diffused pattern rather than a focused beam, which means you get ambient illumination without the harsh glare that plagues clear-glass fixtures. Walking past these at night won't leave you squinting.

Heat management is solid. The aluminum body acts as a heat sink, keeping the LED driver cool enough that the fixtures run barely warm to the touch even after hours of operation. That matters for longevity — heat is the #1 killer of LED fixtures.

One buyer noted: *"I've had these installed for 14 months through two Michigan winters and they still look brand new. No corrosion, no fading, no water inside the glass."* That sentiment repeats throughout the review history. The gasket seal around the glass and the rubber plug at the wire entry point do their job.

## Where It Falls Short

The included 6-3 push connectors are the weakest link. Several reviewers report intermittent flickering traced back to loose connections at the connector. The fix is simple — strip your wire a bit longer, push firmly until you hear the click, and tug-test each connection — but it's an extra step that shouldn't be necessary. If you're comfortable with wire nuts and waterproof tape, swap them out.

Second, at 19 inches tall, these sit low. In a bed of tall grasses or dense shrubs, they'll disappear. They're best for open walkways and borders where the fixture itself has room to be seen.

Finally, the LEDs are non-replaceable. When the board eventually fails — expect 5-7 years of daily use — you're replacing the whole fixture head, not just a bulb.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy these if:** You want the look of premium architectural lighting without paying $60+ per fixture, you have a defined walkway or border edge that needs warm accent lighting, and you're comfortable with basic low-voltage wiring.

**Skip these if:** You need tall, statement fixtures that stand above dense planting, or you want replaceable bulbs for long-term serviceability.

## FAQ

**Q: What transformer size do I need?**
Each fixture draws about 1.6 watts. A 60-watt transformer comfortably runs 20+ of these. For four fixtures, even a 30-watt unit is overkill. Just confirm your transformer outputs 12V AC.

**Q: Can these be installed without a transformer?**
No. They require 12V low-voltage input. Running line voltage (120V) will destroy the LEDs instantly. If you don't have an existing low-voltage system, budget for a transformer ($25-40).

**Q: How durable are they in freezing climates?**
The aluminum housing and sealed glass handle freeze-thaw cycles well. The stake is galvanized steel. The main risk is the wire connectors — use waterproof connectors and bury connections properly to prevent corrosion.
