If you've been window-shopping plasma cutters, you've probably noticed a wall of no-name Chinese units promising 50 amps for $200. The FLARING 65A sits above that crowd, and the 4.4-star average across 220 ratings suggests it's earning its keep. But is it a genuine workshop tool or just a more expensive gamble? Let's cut through the marketing.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the cutter unit itself, a hand torch with pilot arc (the PT-31 style), an 8-ft ground cable with clamp, a regulator with gauge, and a basic consumables kit — a few tips and electrodes to get you started. No air filter, no spare swirl rings, no cart. The unit measures roughly 14" x 6" x 11" and weighs about 18 lbs, so it's portable enough for hauling to a job site but not something you'll want in a backpack.

**Real-world performance**

This is where the FLARING earns its score. The dual voltage functionality is genuinely seamless — plug into a standard 120V household outlet and you'll get enough power for clean cuts through 1/4" steel. Move to a 240V circuit and it wakes up properly, slicing through 1/2" plate with minimal dross and a narrow kerf. The pilot arc is the standout feature here: it starts on rusty, painted, or mill-scaled metal without you having to grind a clean contact point first. That's a real time-saver on salvage work.

The duty cycle is rated at 60% at 65A, which means about six minutes of continuous cutting before a cooldown. For hobby use and light fabrication, that's fine. For production work, you'll be waiting on the thermal overload light more than you'd like.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The recurring theme in positive reviews is reliability out of the box. One buyer noted, "I've had cheap plasma cutters die within a month. This one has cut through 1/2-inch plate every day for three months without a hiccup." Another mentioned the customer service: "Had a question about the pilot arc gap, they walked me through the adjustment on video chat." That level of support is rare at this price point.

**Where it falls short**

The proprietary consumables are the biggest long-term cost. PT-31 style torches are common, but FLARING's specific tip and electrode geometry aren't interchangeable with generic parts. You'll pay roughly 20-30% more per set than you would for a Hypertherm-compatible consumable. Over a year of regular use, that difference adds up.

The 12-ft torch lead is another limitation. On a car chassis or a large steel table, you'll find yourself repositioning the unit constantly. And if you live somewhere humid, plan on adding a moisture separator to your air line — the built-in regulator doesn't remove water, and wet air will make your cuts rough and eat consumables quickly.

**Who should buy this**

The FLARING 65A is ideal for the serious hobbyist, the farm or ranch owner doing equipment repairs, and the small fabrication shop that needs a dependable backup unit. It's also a solid choice for someone upgrading from a 40A inverter who wants more cutting capacity without jumping to a 3-phase industrial machine. Skip it if you're a production fabricator cutting 8 hours a day, or if you need to cut material thicker than 3/4" regularly.

**FAQ**

*Does it really run on 120V?* Yes, but with reduced capacity. On 120V, expect clean cuts through 1/4" steel. For anything thicker, you want the 240V circuit.

*Can I use it with a generator?* Yes, but the generator needs to produce clean, stable power. A 6500-watt unit minimum is recommended for 240V operation.

*What air pressure do I need?* Set the regulator to 70 PSI with adequate CFM — your compressor should deliver at least 5 CFM at that pressure for consistent cuts.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a versatile, dual-voltage plasma cutter for hobby or light professional work and value pilot arc reliability over raw cutting speed.

**Skip it if:** You're on a strict budget, do production cutting daily, or want the freedom to use off-the-shelf consumables.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely capable machine with honest specs and good support, held back only by proprietary consumables and a short torch lead.
