## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the drawbacks.

First, the seal height is slightly shorter than the OEM part. If your door has a gap larger than 1/2 inch at the bottom—say, from a settling concrete floor—this won't bridge it. You'll still get a draft. Measure your gap before ordering.

Second, there's no pre-cut option. You get one continuous length and you trim it yourself. For a standard 16-foot door, that's fine. But if your door is an odd width, you'll waste a bit of material. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Third, a few buyers noted the seal is a touch softer than the original. That's actually a benefit for sealing, but it means the bulb may compress more under the door's weight. If your door is heavily used, you might need to replace it a bit sooner than the OEM part—say 3 years instead of 4.

**Q: Will this fit my Wayne Dalton model?**
A: If your door uses the 154-style T-track (the standard for Wayne Dalton), yes. Check your existing seal's profile—if it has a T-shaped bead that slides into a track, this will work. If you have a different style, look for the 156 or 158 versions.

**Q: How do I remove the old seal?**
A: Pull the old seal out of the track starting from one end. It should slide out with steady pressure. If it's stubborn, use a flathead screwdriver to pry it free. Clean the track with a rag before sliding in the new one.

**Q: Can I use this on a non-Wayne Dalton door?**
