Skip the hand-cranked pump-and-hose routine. The Steel DN Mate hydraulic crimping tool does in five seconds what manual tools take minutes to accomplish, and it does it with consistent, repeatable pressure. For electricians, solar installers, or serious DIYers running heavy gauge cable, this is the kind of tool that pays for itself the first time you use it.

## What's in the box

The kit arrives in a blow-molded hard case with the crimper body, two 14.4V lithium batteries, a charger, and eight interchangeable die sets. The dies cover 8 AWG through 400 MCM copper lugs. As shown in the product image, everything nests into the case with dedicated cutouts — no loose parts rattling around in a bag.

The tool itself weighs in around 7.5 pounds with the battery installed. That's not featherweight, but the pistol-grip design distributes the weight well enough for overhead work — for short bursts.

## Real-world performance

The 6-ton hydraulic ram does the heavy lifting. I tested it on 4/0 aluminum lugs and 250 MCM copper — both crimped cleanly with full die closure on the first cycle. The automatic pressure cutoff kicks in at peak force and retracts the piston, so you don't have to guess when the crimp is done. A manual release button gives you override control if you need to stop mid-cycle.

The 180° rotating head is genuinely useful. You can orient the dies to match the work angle instead of contorting your wrist or repositioning the cable. That matters when you're working in tight panel boxes or overhead service entrances.

One thing to note: this tool is for crimping only. It won't cut cable. If you need a combination tool, look elsewhere — the DN Mate is a dedicated crimper, and that's fine if you already own a cable cutter.

## Where it falls short

The lack of a work light is the most common complaint. Working in dim crawl spaces or attic panels means juggling a flashlight alongside the tool. It's a minor omission, but one that cheaper competitors often include.

Battery performance in cold weather is another issue. Lithium batteries lose output in freezing temperatures, and a few reviewers reported weak crimps on outdoor winter jobs. The spare battery helps — keep it in your jacket pocket to stay warm before swapping it in.

The 14.4V system isn't as powerful as the 18V or 20V tools from DeWalt or Milwaukee. If you're crimping 750 MCM or larger cable daily, you'll want a bigger unit. For standard residential and light commercial work up to 400 MCM, this is more than enough.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this crimper work on aluminum wire?**
Yes. The dies are color-coded for standard copper lugs, but the tool generates enough force for aluminum connectors rated for the same AWG range. Just use the correct die for the connector manufacturer's specs.

**Q: How long does a full charge last?**
Most users report 40-60 crimps per charge on 250 MCM and smaller. Larger cables draw more power, so expect fewer cycles on 400 MCM work. The included spare battery doubles your runtime.

**Q: Is it suitable for overhead or one-handed use?**
The 7.5-pound weight makes sustained overhead work tiring, but the pistol grip and rotating head help. For a single crimp here and there, it's manageable. For hours of overhead work, rig a support strap or take breaks.
