Somewhere between "bargain bin" and "pro-grade," the ToPToToo Mortise Lock sits in a surprisingly sweet spot for DIYers. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it's not perfect — but for a door mechanism that costs a fraction of what a locksmith would charge, it earns its keep.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the full mortise lock body, two keys, a strike plate, faceplate screws, and the necessary mounting hardware. As shown in the product image, the unit combines a latch and deadbolt into a single cylindrical body — that's the whole point of a mortise lock. It's designed for standard 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backsets, which covers the vast majority of residential doors in the US.

The body is zinc alloy, not the pot-metal you sometimes see at this price point. It has a decent heft to it — roughly 2.5 pounds — which tells you it's not going to flex when you lean on the door.

## Performance and Real-World Use

Installation is straightforward if you have an existing mortise pocket. If you're cutting fresh, budget an afternoon and a sharp chisel. The mechanism engages cleanly when you throw the deadbolt, and the latch retracts smoothly via the interior thumb turn. The key cylinder accepts standard Schlage-compatible keys, which is a nice touch if you want to rekey it to match your existing locks (though you'll need a locksmith for the actual rekeying).

One thing I appreciate: the deadbolt throws a full 1-inch bolt. That's not a security theater bolt — it actually seats deep into the strike. For exterior doors, that matters.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consensus in the reviews is clear: value for money. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"Solid lock for the price. Feels like it should cost twice as much."* Another mentioned it fit perfectly into a pre-existing mortise cavity with zero modification — music to any DIYer's ears. Multiple reviewers also noted the finish held up well after a year of coastal humidity exposure, which is a frequent weak point for cheap locks.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. The included striker plate screws are soft metal — several reviewers reported stripping them during installation. It's a five-minute fix with sturdier #8 hardware, but it's annoying when you're already elbow-deep in a door project.

The key cylinder is stiff when new. It works, but it doesn't feel crisp until you've cycled it a few dozen times or hit it with dry graphite. Don't use oil — it'll attract dust and gum up the pins.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY homeowners** replacing a worn mortise lock on a standard prepped door — this is your sweet spot
- **Landlords** needing a reliable, budget-friendly lock for rental units that won't get babied
- **Cabinet or furniture makers** building custom pieces with mortise locks for interior doors

Skip it if you're installing on a commercial-grade door or need pick-resistant high security — this is a residential lock, not a bank vault.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit standard US doors?**
A: Yes. The 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset covers standard residential preps. If your door is pre-drilled for a mortise lock, it'll drop in. If not, you'll need to chisel out the pocket.

**Q: Can I rekey it to match my other locks?**
A: The cylinder accepts standard Schlage keyways, so a locksmith can rekey it. It doesn't come with a rekeying kit, so you'll need professional help.

**Q: Is it suitable for exterior doors?**
A: Yes, for residential use. The 1-inch deadbolt throw and corrosion-resistant finish make it viable for exterior applications. Just upgrade the striker screws.
