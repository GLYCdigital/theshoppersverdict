**What You Actually Get**

The box contains two complete pendant fixtures — that's the headline here. Each unit includes a matte black metal cage shade, a ceiling canopy, and an adjustable cord that extends up to 59 inches. That's a generous drop range. Whether you're hanging these 12 inches above a counter-height island or letting them fall 40 inches from a vaulted ceiling, you have room to work.

All mounting hardware ships in the box: mounting brackets, screws, wire nuts. There's no separate trip to the hardware store for a part that didn't make it in. The fixtures are rated for standard US junction boxes and work with 120V wiring. If you've swapped a light fixture before, you can handle this install in roughly 20-30 minutes per light.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The metal construction is the standout feature. The cage shades are welded — not glued — and the matte black finish is applied evenly, no drips or thin spots. One reviewer put it plainly: *"These are surprisingly heavy for the price. The metal is thick, not like the cheap tin stuff you see in big box stores."* That weight translates to durability; these won't warp or discolor from heat when you run higher-wattage bulbs.

The cord adjustment mechanism works well — loosen the thumb screw, slide to your desired length, tighten. No fiddly set screws that strip out after two adjustments.

One thing buyers consistently mention is dimmer compatibility. The fixtures themselves don't include dimming circuitry, but they're fully compatible with standard dimmer switches **provided you use dimmable bulbs**. Multiple verified reviews confirm this works smoothly, which gives you real control over the ambience. That's a meaningful advantage over cheaper fixtures that flicker when dimmed.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the bulbs. Ludil does not include bulbs, so you'll need to budget for six Edison-style or LED bulbs (two per light, since these are double-socket fixtures). That's an extra $15-30 depending on your bulb choice. Go with LED filament bulbs — they give the vintage look without the heat of incandescent.

The open-bottom design means dust and cooking grease will settle on the bulbs and interior of the cage. In a kitchen with heavy frying, you'll want to wipe these down every few weeks. It's a minor chore, but it's recurring.

Also note: the black finish is matte, which looks great but shows fingerprints and smudges more readily than brushed nickel or oil-rubbed bronze. Handle the shades during installation with a cloth if you're particular about that sort of thing.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Can these be installed on a sloped or vaulted ceiling?**
Yes. The 59-inch adjustable cord and swivel canopy accommodate angled ceilings up to roughly 45 degrees. Just make sure your mounting bracket is rated for the angle.

**Q: What bulb type and wattage should I use?**
Use E26-base bulbs. LED filament bulbs in 60W equivalent (about 800 lumens) are the sweet spot — bright enough for task lighting over a counter, but warm and dimmable. Don't exceed 60W incandescent equivalent to avoid heat buildup.

**Q: Do these require a special dimmer switch?**
No. Any standard dimmer works, but you must use dimmable bulbs. Non-dimmable LEDs will flicker or hum regardless of the fixture quality.
