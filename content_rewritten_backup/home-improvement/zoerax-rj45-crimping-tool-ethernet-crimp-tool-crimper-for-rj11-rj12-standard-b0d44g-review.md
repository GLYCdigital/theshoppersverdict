If you're terminating your own Ethernet cables, you already know the pain: a cheap crimper that smashes the connector sideways, or one that leaves the gold pins barely kissing the wire. The **ZOERAX RJ45 Crimping Tool** sits in the budget tier at around $20, but it brings a feature that usually costs double — a full ratcheting action. The question is whether it delivers clean terminations or just another frustration. I've run it through a weekend of cable builds. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Box

The ZOERAX ships as a single, self-contained unit. There's no carry case and no spare parts — just the tool itself. What you get is a 9-inch crimper with three integrated functions:

- **Crimp die** with labeled cavities for RJ45 (8P8C), RJ11 (6P6C), and RJ12 (6P4C)
- **Wire stripper** built into the handle, with a small gauge window
- **Cable cutter** near the pivot point

The body is a steel frame with a rubberized grip. It's not featherweight at 1.2 pounds, but it's balanced enough for one-handed use.

## Performance and Build Quality

The ratcheting mechanism is the headline here. When you squeeze, the handle locks into a gear and forces the die down in a controlled, even stroke. This is the single biggest difference between a $20 tool and a $10 no-name — the ratchet prevents you from releasing early, which is exactly what causes those "half-crimped" connectors that slide off the cable jacket.

I terminated 30 RJ45 connectors with this tool on a mix of 23 AWG solid copper and 24 AWG stranded Cat6. The results:

- **Solid copper:** 28 out of 30 passed a continuity and wiremap test on the first try. The two failures were my own error — I didn't push the wires fully into the connector.
- **Stranded cable:** 15 out of 15 passed cleanly. The die seats the pins flush without crushing the jacket.
- **RJ11/RJ12:** Tested on a phone line cable — the smaller cavities align properly, and the crimp depth is correct.

The built-in stripper has a flaw worth noting. The blade is aggressive. On 23 AWG solid copper, it's easy to nick the wire if you squeeze too hard. You have to rotate the cable gently and listen for the jacket to pop. Press too hard and you'll score the copper, which creates a weak point that breaks when you bend the wire into the connector. It's workable, but it's not the tool's strongest feature. For clean strips, I'd rather use a dedicated stripper.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest tradeoffs:

1. **No pass-through support.** If you want to use pass-through RJ45 connectors (which let wires poke through the front for easier alignment), this tool won't do it. The die is shaped for standard 8P8C connectors only.
2. **Stripper blade sensitivity.** As mentioned, it's easy to nick wire. This is the most common complaint in the reviews — one user said, *"The stripper takes practice. I ruined three wires before I figured out the right pressure."*
3. **Tension adjustment is hidden.** The ratchet tension screw sits under the rubber grip. If you need to loosen the action for lighter crimps (e.g., RJ11), you have to peel the grip back. Annoying, but a one-time adjustment.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY home network builders** running Cat6 drops through walls — the ratchet ensures clean terminations when you're working in awkward spaces.
- **IT pros who need a backup tool** in a service bag — it's rugged enough for occasional field use, though not a daily-driver replacement for a $60+ professional crimper.
- **First-time crimpers** who want to avoid the frustration of cheap tools — the ratchet removes the guesswork.

Skip it if you need pass-through connector support or if you're crimping hundreds of cables weekly — at that volume, a professional-grade tool with replaceable dies is worth the extra cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this crimper work with Cat6 and Cat6a cable?**
A: Yes, for both solid and stranded conductors, as long as you're using standard 8P8C RJ45 connectors. It handles 23 AWG and 24 AWG without issue. For Cat6a, use a proper connector rated for the thicker jacket.

**Q: Can I use it for RJ45 pass-through connectors?**
A: No. The die is shaped for standard connectors. Pass-through plugs require a separate crimper with a flat front plate. If you prefer pass-through, look at the Klein Tools VDV226-110 instead.

**Q: Will the ratchet release if I need to abort a crimp?**
A: Yes. There's a small release lever near the pivot. You can disengage the ratchet mid-stroke to free a misaligned connector, though you'll likely ruin that connector anyway.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, budget-friendly crimper for occasional home or office network work. The ratcheting mechanism alone is worth the price — it eliminates the most common failure mode of cheap crimpers.

**Skip it if** you need pass-through support or plan to terminate cables professionally every day. For that, invest in a higher-end tool with user-serviceable dies.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid performer that punches above its price class, held back only by a finicky stripper and missing pass-through support.
