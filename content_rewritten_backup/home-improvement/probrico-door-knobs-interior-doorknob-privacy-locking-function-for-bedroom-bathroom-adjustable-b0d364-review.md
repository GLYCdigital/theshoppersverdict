## What You're Actually Getting

The Probrico is a **privacy lock**, not a keyed entry lock. That means it's designed for bedrooms, bathrooms, and interior doors where you want to keep people out but don't need a key. The package includes the two knobs, the latch mechanism, the strike plate, and all mounting screws. You're not getting any fancy smart features or a key — and you shouldn't expect to at this price point.

As shown in the product image, the knob has a clean, modern line with a slightly rounded face. The finish options include brushed nickel and oil-rubbed bronze, both of which look significantly more expensive than they are. The twist-lock mechanism is on the interior knob — you push and turn the small thumb-turn to engage the lock, and there's a tiny release button on the exterior side for emergencies. That's a thoughtful touch for bathrooms with small children or anyone who might accidentally lock themselves in.

## Installation: The 15-Minute Project

Here's where Probrico earns its keep. The knob uses a **standard 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" adjustable latch**, which means it fits virtually every pre-drilled interior door in North America. The installation process is genuinely tool-light: you need a Phillips screwdriver and maybe five minutes. The latch is reversible, so you don't need to fuss with flipping it for left- or right-handed doors. Multiple reviewers mentioned the same thing — one said, "I replaced three knobs in under 20 minutes, and I'm not handy at all." That's the experience you should expect.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings isn't an accident. Recurring themes in the feedback:

- **Solid feel:** Multiple customers explicitly noted that the knob doesn't feel hollow or cheap. One reviewer wrote, "I was skeptical at this price, but the weight and finish compare to the $20+ knobs at the big-box store."
- **Quiet operation:** The latch mechanism is smooth, and the knob doesn't rattle. That matters when you're trying to sneak out of a sleeping toddler's room.
- **Consistent finish:** No paint flakes, no uneven plating. The brushed nickel matches other fixtures in the same finish family without looking mismatched.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and Probrico has some real tradeoffs. **The interior lock is a thumb-turn, not a push-button** — some people prefer the push-button style found on Schlage or Kwikset because it's faster to engage. Also, the emergency release on the exterior side is a small flathead slot, not a coin slot. You'll need a tiny screwdriver or a paperclip to unlock it from the outside, which is less convenient than a coin catch.

A few reviewers noted that the latch can feel slightly loose on doors with thicker-than-standard frames. It's not a dealbreaker, but if your door is a solid-core model, you might need to adjust the strike plate position slightly. Finally, the finish, while attractive, is not as scratch-resistant as the higher-end brands. If you have pets that scratch doors or kids that bang into them, you'll see wear within a year.

## Who Should Buy This

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on a bathroom door?**
A: Yes, but know that the privacy lock is not a true keyed lock. The exterior release requires a small flathead screwdriver or paperclip. It's fine for privacy, but it won't keep out a determined person.

**Q: Does it fit all standard doors?**
A: It fits doors with a 2-3/8" or 2-3/4" backset, which covers 95% of residential interior doors. Measure from the edge of the door to the center of the bore hole before ordering.

**Q: Is the lock easy to override in an emergency?**
A: Yes, the exterior has a small slot for a screwdriver. It's not as convenient as a coin slot, but it works. For households with young children, this is a safety positive.
