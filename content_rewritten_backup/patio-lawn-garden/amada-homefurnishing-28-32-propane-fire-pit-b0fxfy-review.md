There's no propane tank included — you'll need to supply your own standard 20-pound tank. The build is heavy-gauge steel with a powder-coated finish. It's not flimsy. The lid seals properly, which matters more than most shoppers realize — a loose lid lets rain pool inside and rust out the burner over time. This one fits snugly.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. The base and tank housing require two people to align properly — one to hold the body while the other secures bolts. Budget 45-60 minutes. It's not hard, just awkward.

Changing the propane tank is also a mild hassle. The tank sits inside the base, so you're reaching through the side door and wrestling with the regulator connection in a tight space. You'll figure out your own technique, but the first few swaps are frustrating.

There's no safety timer or auto-shutoff. If you tend to fall asleep on the patio (no judgment), this pit will keep burning until you turn it off or the tank runs dry. Set a reminder.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you want real heat output for cool evenings, value sturdiness over portability, and don't mind a two-person assembly once. It suits homeowners with a dedicated patio space who want a permanent-looking fixture without the cost of built-in installation.

**Skip it if** you need something portable for camping or tailgating — this is heavy and stays put. Also skip if you're solo and physically limited; the assembly genuinely requires two people for best results.
