**Skip the low-profile sprinklers.** If you're watering anything taller than a freshly mowed fescue lawn, the Hourleey Extra Tall Tripod Sprinkler solves a problem most sprinklers ignore: getting water *over* the plants, not into them.

Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 210 ratings, the spec sheet, and how this thing actually behaves in the yard.

## What You're Actually Getting

The box contains the tripod assembly, the brass impact head, and the hose connector. No tools needed — the legs lock into place with a simple collar mechanism, and the sprinkler head threads on by hand. Assembly takes under two minutes.

The headline feature is the height. Extended, this unit reaches roughly 40 inches. That's the difference between soaking your tomatoes from above versus blasting the bottom leaves and splashing soil onto the fruit. The legs telescope independently, so you can level it on slopes without shoving rocks under the feet.

The brass impact head is the star. It's a classic impulse design — the kind that goes *click-clack* and throws a heavy, arcing stream. It covers a claimed radius of up to 80 feet at full pressure. In practice, expect a solid 50–65 feet of effective soak before the droplets get too fine to matter.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is powder-coated steel, not the thin aluminum you see on budget tripods. It feels solid when you pick it up. The brass head is machined, not stamped, and the adjustment knobs are metal — that's where most cheap impact sprinklers fail within a season. This one should hold up.

One thing buyers consistently mention is the **stability**. Here's a real quote from a verified purchase: *"Finally a sprinkler that doesn't tip over when the hose drags. The wide legs handle my uneven backyard without me having to babysit it."* That matches the design — the footprint is generous, and the weight distribution keeps the center of gravity low even at full extension.

Water distribution is even and consistent, which matters more than raw throw distance. The brass head breaks the stream into large droplets that resist wind drift. You won't get the fine mist that evaporates before it hits the ground or blows sideways onto your patio.

## What Buyers Praise (With Receipts)

- **Coverage area:** Multiple reviewers note it covers "the entire yard" in one position, cutting down on moving the sprinkler every 15 minutes.
- **Height versatility:** *"I use it for my corn and sunflowers — nothing else gets over them. This is the only sprinkler that actually waters the tops."*
- **Durability:** Several buyers report owning it for over a year with zero leaks or breakage, which is rare for this price tier.
- **Adjustability:** The head can be set to a partial arc, so you're not soaking your fence or driveway.

## Where It Falls Short

The base is **hollow plastic**. Out of the box, it's light enough that high water pressure can cause the whole unit to hop, especially on hard, dry ground. The fix is simple — fill the legs with sand or water — but it's an added step that should've been handled at the factory.

There's no **flow control dial** on the head. If you have high water pressure or a small yard, you'll overshoot your target zone. You'll need to add an inline valve at the hose bib. Minor, but worth knowing.

The adjustment mechanism for the arc is a bit stiff at first. It loosens up after a few uses, but don't expect precision micro-adjustments out of the box.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have tall plants, shrubs, or a large lawn section that low sprinklers can't reach over. You want a heavy-duty unit that won't be a throwaway purchase in 12 months. You prefer impact sprinklers over the flimsy plastic oscillators that clog and break.

**Skip it if:** You only water a small, flat, low-cut lawn — a simple stationary sprinkler does the same job for half the price. Or if you have extremely low water pressure (under 30 PSI), the brass head won't cycle properly and you'll get a weak, sputtering stream.

**Q: Can I leave it outside in winter?**
A: You can, but you shouldn't. Drain it completely and store it indoors. The brass head survives freezing, but water trapped in the frame can crack the plastic components.

**Q: How far does it actually throw water?**
A: Advertised at 80 feet. Realistically, expect 50–65 feet of effective coverage before the spray thins out. On a typical suburban lot, one position covers most of the lawn.
