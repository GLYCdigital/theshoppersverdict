The Arribueno Foldable Griddle Stand solves a genuinely annoying problem: Blackstone makes excellent tabletop griddles, but their factory legs are flimsy, wobble on uneven ground, and don't fold into anything transportable. This stand replaces that frustration with a welded steel frame that goes from flat to functional in about 20 seconds.

## What You Actually Get

The package contains the stand itself, four rubber feet, and a small carry strap. No tools. No assembly. The frame is built from rectangular steel tubing — noticeably thicker than the stamped-metal legs Blackstone ships with their 17" and 22" models. The top surface is a flat steel plate with a raised lip around the edges, which does double duty: it keeps the griddle from sliding off, and it catches minor grease drips before they hit your patio.

As shown in the product image, the legs fold inward with a simple hinge mechanism. When collapsed, the whole thing measures about 24" x 17" x 3" — roughly the footprint of a large laptop bag. That's the entire point: this is a griddle stand designed for people who actually move their cooking setup.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I've tested this with both a Blackstone 17" and 22". The fit is snug on both — the griddle sits inside the lip without rocking. The 66-pound weight rating is honest; with a 22" griddle loaded with a full pack of bacon, a dozen eggs, and a cast-iron press, there's zero flex in the frame. One Amazon buyer noted, *"I was skeptical about the weight rating but I loaded it up and it didn't budge. Feels like a permanent table, not a folding stand."*

The height is the other win here. Blackstone's own legs put the cooking surface around 28 inches, which forces most adults to hunch. This stand brings the griddle to roughly 32 inches — a significant ergonomic upgrade if you've ever spent 20 minutes flipping pancakes with a sore back.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The consensus across 235 ratings is consistent: portability and stability. Repeated comments mention how the stand transforms tailgating setups. One verified purchase wrote, *"Took this camping and it fit in a milk crate with the griddle on top. Set up in seconds on uneven dirt — the rubber feet adjust enough to keep it level."*

Another recurring compliment: the storage solution. Blackstone tabletop owners often complain the factory legs don't fold flush, creating awkward bulk. This stand collapses flat enough to slide under a truck seat or behind an RV dinette.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about two things. First, the legs have no locking pin or tension latch. Once the stand is opened, it stays open through sheer geometry — but if you drag it across a patio while loaded, the legs can partially fold and the griddle will tilt. Always lift it. That's a design tradeoff for the fold-flat capability, but it's worth knowing.

Second, the top plate is raw, unpainted steel. If you leave it outside in the rain or store it in a damp garage, you'll see surface rust within a few weeks. A quick wipe with cooking oil after each use prevents this, but it's an extra maintenance step Blackstone's painted legs don't require.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You own a Blackstone 17" or 22", cook outdoors more than twice a month, and are tired of hunching over a low cooking surface. It's also a no-brainer for RVers, campers, and tailgaters who need gear that packs flat.

**Skip it if:** Your griddle lives permanently on a covered patio and never moves. The factory legs work fine for stationary use, and the raw steel surface will demand more upkeep than you might want.

A: The stand fits the griddle cooking surface itself, but the side shelf attachment will overhang the stand's edges. It still works, but the shelf won't have solid support on its outer end. For the standard 17" and 22" models, it's a perfect match.

**Q: Can I use this with a propane tank on the ground?**
A: Yes. The stand raises the griddle high enough that a standard 20-lb tank sits comfortably beneath it, with the regulator hose routing cleanly to the griddle's connection point.

**Q: Will it fit in a standard camping tote?**
A: Folded, it's roughly 24" x 17" x 3". Most large rectangular totes will take it, but it won't fit a standard milk crate unless you angle it diagonally.
