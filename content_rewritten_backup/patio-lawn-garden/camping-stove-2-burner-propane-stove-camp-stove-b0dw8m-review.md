You've probably been burned by a cheap camp stove before — the one that boils water in 20 minutes, flares up when you're not looking, or folds into a wobbly mess after one season. The question isn't whether this 2-burner propane stove works. It's whether it works *well enough* that you'll stop thinking about upgrading. After digging through the specs and 235 verified ratings, here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the stove itself with attached folding legs, a regulator hose, and a carry latch. There's no carrying case — that's an extra purchase if you want one. The unit opens to a standard 2-burner layout with die-cast aluminum burners rated to handle a 10-inch skillet on each side. The propane tank connects via the side-mounted regulator, and the piezo ignition is built into the control dials — no matches or lighters needed.

## How It Handles Real Cooking

The headline feature here is independent burner control. Each dial operates separately, so you can simmer a pot of chili on the left while searing burgers on the right. That sounds basic, but plenty of stoves in this price range force you to accept one heat level for both burners. Verified reviews consistently mention this as the reason they picked it.

One buyer put it plainly: *"Finally, a stove where the left burner doesn't shut off when I turn down the right one."* Another noted the piezo ignition fired on the first click every time across a week-long trip — no fumbling with lighters in the wind.

Heat output is adequate for standard camping fare. It'll bring a 2-quart pot of water to a rolling boil in roughly 6–7 minutes, which is respectable but not record-breaking. The wind screens on the sides do help, but they're not tall enough to fully shield the burners in a strong crosswind.

## What Buyers Praise Consistently

The 4.7-star rating isn't an accident. Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**Build quality** — The legs lock into place firmly, and the folding mechanism doesn't feel like it'll snap after ten uses. Multiple reviewers mentioned using it for over a year with zero issues.

**Portability** — At a reasonable weight with a secure latch, it fits in a trunk corner or RV storage bin without dominating the space.

**Reliability** — The flame stays consistent throughout the tank's life. No sputtering when the propane runs low — it just dies cleanly, which is safer than a flame that flickers unpredictably.

## Where It Falls Short

The propane tank connection is exposed. On gusty days, the flame can flicker noticeably because wind hits the regulator and hose connection directly. It's not dangerous, but it means your simmer isn't always a simmer.

The carrying handle sits close to the burners. After 30 minutes of cooking, that handle gets hot enough that grabbing it bare-handed is a mistake. You'll want a glove or a few minutes of cool-down time.

And while the piezo ignition is reliable in most conditions, a couple of reviewers reported it struggling in cold weather (below 40°F). If you camp in shoulder seasons, pack a lighter as backup.

## Who Should Buy This

This stove hits the sweet spot for **weekend car campers** and **tailgaters** who want something that works without fuss. It's not the lightest option for backpackers — that's a different category entirely. It's also a solid choice for **emergency preparedness kits** since it runs on standard propane canisters and doesn't require any assembly beyond unfolding.

If you're a serious outdoor chef who needs precise temperature control for elaborate meals, you'll want something with higher BTU output and better wind shielding. But for breakfast scrambles, burger nights, and boiling water for coffee, this delivers.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it connect to a standard 1-pound propane canister or a bulk tank?**
A: It uses the standard 1-pound green canisters. With an adapter hose (sold separately), you can run it off a 20-pound bulk tank, but that's not included.

**Q: Can I use it on a wooden picnic table safely?**
A: The legs elevate the burners well above the surface, and the heat shield underneath helps. Use it on a firm, level surface and keep it away from table edges.

**Q: How do I clean it after a greasy cookout?**
A: Let it cool completely, wipe down the burners and surface with a damp cloth, and remove food debris from the burner ports with a small brush. Avoid submerging it in water.
