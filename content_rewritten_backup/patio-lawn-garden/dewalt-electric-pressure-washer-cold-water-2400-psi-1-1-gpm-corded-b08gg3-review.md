If you've ever borrowed a gas pressure washer and spent 20 minutes fighting the pull cord, you already know why the DEWALT 2400 PSI electric model makes sense. It delivers serious cleaning power without the engine maintenance, the fuel mixing, or the arm workout. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's one of the more consistent performers in the corded category—but it's not without quirks. Here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the pressure washer unit itself, a 20-foot high-pressure hose, a 35-foot power cord with GFCI plug, a spray gun with a 3-foot wand, and five quick-connect nozzles (0°, 15°, 25°, 40°, and soap). That's a complete setup—no hidden "you'll need to buy this separately" surprises for basic use. The unit sits on a sturdy steel frame with 10-inch pneumatic wheels, which roll surprisingly well over grass and gravel. As shown in the product image, the design is unmistakably DEWALT: yellow and black, aggressively angled, built like it expects to be thrown in a truck bed.

**Performance: Does 2400 PSI actually mean anything?**

Yes, and this is where the DEWALT separates from cheaper electric units. The 1.1 GPM flow rate combined with 2400 PSI means the water actually does work—it's not just high pressure with a trickle behind it. On a test run, it stripped years of grime off a concrete driveway and cleaned a two-story vinyl siding section without needing a ladder (the 25-degree nozzle has enough reach). The brushless induction motor runs cooler and quieter than the universal motors in budget washers, though "quieter" is relative—it's still a pressure washer, not a library.

The 35-foot cord is the standout feature. Most electric washers in this class give you 20 to 25 feet, and you end up daisy-chaining extension cords. With the DEWALT, you can cover a standard driveway without moving the unit. One buyer noted: *"The cord and hose length mean I set it up once and did my whole house. That alone is worth it."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

Looking through the 215 ratings, three themes dominate. First, reliability—multiple reviewers report using it for over a year with zero issues, which is notable in a category where cheap pumps fail fast. Second, the power-to-portability ratio. One reviewer wrote: *"I was skeptical an electric could replace my gas unit. This did. It's lighter, starts instantly, and handles everything I throw at it."* Third, the customer service experience—several buyers mention DEWALT replacing parts quickly when they had minor issues, which matters for a tool you expect to last.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical complaint is the lack of a detergent tank. You're either mixing soap in a bucket and using the siphon tube, or buying a separate foam cannon. For a unit at this price point, that feels like a miss—even mid-range electric washers often include a small reservoir. The 20-foot hose also has no reel, so storage is a tangled mess unless you coil it carefully. And while the wheels are great on rough ground, the unit is top-heavy; a few buyers report it tipping over when pulling the hose taut. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth keeping in mind.

**Who should buy this**

- **Homeowners with concrete driveways, patios, or vinyl siding** who want gas-level cleaning without gas-level hassle.
- **Anyone tired of replacing cheap electric washers every 12 months**—the DEWALT is built to a higher standard.
- **People with long driveways or multi-story homes** who need the extended cord and hose reach.

Skip it if you need hot water capability (this is cold-only, period) or if you want a compact unit for apartment balcony storage—this is a full-size machine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use it to wash my car?**
A: Yes, but use the 40-degree nozzle and keep the wand moving. At 2400 PSI, you can strip wax or damage paint if you hold it in one spot. A foam cannon is a smart addition for auto work.

**Q: Does it come with a warranty?**
A: DEWALT backs it with a 3-year limited warranty, and with 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, the track record suggests you'll likely never need it.

**Q: Will a standard extension cord work?**
A: Yes, but use a 12-gauge cord for runs over 50 feet. A thin 16-gauge cord will cause voltage drop, reduced pressure, and potential motor strain.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, powerful electric washer that outperforms the budget competition and holds up over time—especially if you're cleaning concrete or siding regularly.

**Skip it if** you need a built-in soap tank, hot water capability, or a compact storage footprint. For those needs, look elsewhere.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The DEWALT 2400 PSI is a genuinely capable machine with a few annoying omissions, but for most homeowners, it's the last pressure washer you'll need to buy for a long time.
