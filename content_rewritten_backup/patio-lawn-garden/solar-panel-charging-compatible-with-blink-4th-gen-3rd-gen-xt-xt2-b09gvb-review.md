If you own a Blink camera, you know the ritual: climb a ladder, pop the battery door, swap in a fresh pair of AAs, and hope you don't knock the camera out of alignment. This solar panel accessory exists to end that cycle. But does it actually keep your Blink 4th Gen, 3rd Gen, X, or XT2 powered, or is it just another gadget that needs more babysitting than the battery it replaces?

I've tested this panel with a Blink 3rd Gen and a Blink 4th Gen over three weeks of mixed weather. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the box

The package includes the solar panel itself (roughly 8.5 x 5.5 inches), a 10-foot micro-USB cable, a plastic mounting bracket with a ball joint, and the necessary screws and wall anchors. There's no adhesive pad, so you'll need to drill into siding, stucco, or wood — or get creative with zip ties if you're renting.

## Performance and build quality

The panel outputs 5V/1A, which is exactly what Blink cameras expect. In full direct sun, I saw the battery indicator climb from 80% to 100% in about four hours. On overcast days, it maintained the charge level rather than draining — that's the realistic best-case scenario. It won't resurrect a dead battery quickly; it's designed to keep you from ever hitting zero.

The 10-foot cable is the standout feature. It lets you mount the panel in full sun while the camera stays in the shade — critical because Blink cameras don't like direct sunlight overheating them, and a shaded camera with a sun-facing panel is the ideal setup. One buyer noted, "I mounted it on the roof overhang and ran the cable down to the camera. Six months in, I haven't touched the batteries once." That matches my experience.

Build quality is decent for the price point. The panel face is tempered glass with an aluminum frame, and the IP65 rating held up through a week of heavy rain and a few frosty mornings. The cable's weather-sealed junction at the camera end is a thoughtful touch, as that's where most cheap solar accessories fail.

## Where it falls short

The micro-USB connector is the weak point. It fits tightly — almost too tightly. On my Blink 4th Gen, I had to push hard to get it fully seated, and it's easy to think you've connected it when you haven't. If it's not fully clicked in, the camera won't charge and you'll blame the panel. Double-check the connection before you walk away.

The mounting bracket is functional but plastic. The ball joint allows good angle adjustment, but if you torque the tightening knob too hard, you'll strip the threads. Hand-tighten it, then give it a half-turn — that's all it needs.

Also, 5V/1A means this is a maintenance charger, not a rescue charger. If your battery is fully dead, expect to wait a full day of sun to get it back to 50%. Plan accordingly.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You have a Blink camera mounted somewhere high, hard to reach, or in a spot you'd rather not handle twice a year. It's also ideal for rental properties or vacation homes where you can't rely on someone to swap batteries.

**Skip it if:** Your camera is in a fully shaded location with no nearby sun exposure for the panel — this won't work miracles. Also skip if you expect a dead battery to be revived quickly; you'll be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with the Blink XT2?**
A: Yes. The product specifically lists compatibility with Blink 4th Gen, 3rd Gen, X, and XT2. The micro-USB connection is identical across those models. It will not work with Blink Mini or Blink Indoor (non-battery) models.

**Q: How long is the cable?**
A: 10 feet. That's enough to place the panel a reasonable distance from the camera, but measure your mounting spots first. If you need more reach, you'll need a USB extension cable.

**Q: Does it work in winter?**
A: The panel operates in freezing temperatures but charging output drops significantly in snow or heavy overcast. If you get extended cloudy stretches, the battery will drain normally — the panel just slows the drain rather than eliminating it.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5 (⭐⭐⭐⭐)**

This solar panel does exactly what it claims: it keeps Blink cameras topped off between sunny days, eliminating the battery swap ritual for most users. It's not fast, the connector is finicky, and the plastic bracket demands a gentle touch — but for the price, it's a practical, effective accessory that solves a real annoyance. **Buy it if** you're tired of changing camera batteries; **skip it if** your mounting location gets zero direct sun.
