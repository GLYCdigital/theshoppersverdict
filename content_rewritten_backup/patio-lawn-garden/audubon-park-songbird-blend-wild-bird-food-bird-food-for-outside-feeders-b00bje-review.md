**Bottom line: This is the seed blend you buy when you're done sweeping up wasted milo and cracked corn from cheap grocery-store mixes.** Audubon Park Songbird Blend doesn't reinvent bird feeding, but it nails the fundamentals — and at a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, backyard birders are clearly taking notice.

## What's actually in the bag

The blend leans heavily on **black-oil sunflower seeds and white proso millet**, with some cracked corn and smaller grains filling out the mix. That's the right call. Black-oil sunflower has the highest meat-to-shell ratio of any common seed, and its thin shell means even smaller birds like chickadees and titmice can crack it open without wasting energy.

As shown in the product image, the bag is a straightforward resealable pouch — nothing fancy, but it keeps seed dry between refills. A 20-pound bag will run most single-feeder households about three to four weeks, depending on how active your local bird population is.

## Real-world performance

Buyers consistently report two things: **less waste and more variety**. One reviewer noted, "I've tried a dozen blends and this one has the least filler. The birds eat almost everything — I'm not raking up seed hulls every weekend." Another long-time feeder owner mentioned, "Cardinals and blue jays showed up within a week. The woodpeckers took longer, but they're here now."

The seed-to-hull ratio matters more than most people realize. Cheap blends load up on red milo and wheat — seeds most songbirds will toss aside to get to the sunflower. That's not just wasted money; it's a mess under your feeder that can attract rodents. This blend's lower filler content means less of that.

## Where it falls short

**Goldfinch lovers should look elsewhere.** This blend has no nyjer seed or sunflower hearts, so the finches that prefer hull-free eating will likely pass. If goldfinches are your goal, you'll need a separate thistle feeder.

A few buyers also flagged **crushed seed at the bottom of the bag**. That's a shipping artifact, not a freshness issue — the seed is still viable, but you'll get some powdery bits in your last scoop. And yes, squirrels will absolutely demolish this if your feeder isn't protected. The sunflower content is a magnet.

## Who should buy this

- **Casual backyard birders** who want one reliable blend that draws a variety of species without constant refilling
- **Southeast and Midwest residents** — this mix aligns well with the cardinals, chickadees, and titmice common in those regions
- **Anyone tired of sweeping up uneaten filler** from economy blends

**Skip it if** you're specifically targeting goldfinches, need a no-mess hull-free mix for patio feeding, or have a serious squirrel problem and no baffle.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this blend go bad if it gets wet?**
A: Like any birdseed, moisture is the enemy. The resealable bag helps, but if rain hits your feeder, dump the seed after a day or two. Moldy seed can harm birds.

**Q: How long does a 20-pound bag last?**
A: With a moderate feeder and a small flock, expect 3–4 weeks. Heavy traffic — especially during winter when birds need more calories — can cut that to two weeks.

**Q: Will this attract pigeons or grackles?**
A: The cracked corn can draw them if you're in an area with large flocks. Using a tube feeder with smaller ports rather than a tray feeder reduces this problem significantly.
