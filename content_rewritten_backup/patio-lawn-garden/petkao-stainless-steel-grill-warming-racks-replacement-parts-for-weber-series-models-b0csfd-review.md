**Where It Falls Short**

Here's the honest part. The 430-grade steel is magnetic and more prone to surface rust than 304, especially in coastal climates or if you leave the grill uncovered year-round. Several reviewers mention light oxidation appearing after 6–8 months. One wrote: *"Worked fine for a season, but I see small rust spots starting near the welds. Not a big deal, but my OEM lasted three years."*

Also, the rack sits marginally higher than the original—about a quarter-inch. That doesn't affect cooking, but if you use a rotisserie kit, you may need to verify clearance. Two reviews noted the rear edge doesn't sit perfectly flush with the grill's back wall, leaving a small gap where smaller items could theoretically slip through. Minor, but worth flagging.

**Q: Can I use this on a two-burner grill?**

**Q: Is it really stainless steel, or painted?**
A: It's genuine 430 stainless—magnetic, but solid metal throughout. No paint to chip or flake. Surface rust is possible over time with heavy moisture exposure, but it's not plated or coated.
