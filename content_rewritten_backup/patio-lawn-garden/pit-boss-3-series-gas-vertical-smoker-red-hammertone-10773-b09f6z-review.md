## The Gas Smoker That Actually Respects Your Weekend

Most vertical smokers under $400 are charcoal-fueled attention hogs. You feed them every 45 minutes, fight temperature swings, and still end up with dry brisket. The Pit Boss 3-Series Gas Vertical Smoker (model 1077) takes a different path: propane heat, wood chips for smoke, and 1,048 square inches of cooking space that lets you load up and walk away. As shown in the product image, the red hammertone finish isn't just cosmetic — it's a textured powder coat that hides grease smudges better than stainless.

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the assembled smoker body (no tools needed for setup beyond attaching the legs and side shelf), four chrome cooking racks, a wood chip loading tray, a water pan, and a 5-foot propane hose with regulator. No propane tank included — standard for the category. The side shelf folds down when not in use, which helps if you're working with a small deck. Total footprint is 19" x 20" base, standing 53" tall — it's tall and narrow, so it tucks into a corner without dominating the space.

## Real-World Performance

The 180°F–400°F range covers cold-smoking cheese up through hot-grilling chicken. In practice, most owners hover between 225°F and 275°F for low-and-slow cooks. The gas burner brings the chamber to temp in about 10 minutes — dramatically faster than charcoal. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had charcoal smokers for 15 years. This thing holds 250°F for 6 hours without me touching the dial. The flavor isn't identical to stick-burning, but it's 90% there and 100% less work."*

The wood chip tray sits directly above the burner. You load chips through a small door on the side — no opening the main door and losing heat. A standard handful of chips produces visible smoke for about 30–45 minutes. You'll reload every hour or so for heavy smoke flavor.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Temperature stability is the #1 recurring compliment. The gas regulator does the heavy lifting, and the vertical design creates even heat distribution across all four racks. Owners also mention the capacity: *"I fit two 8-pound pork butts on the top rack and a full packer brisket on the second — still had room for a pan of beans."* The chrome racks are easier to clean than porcelain-coated grates — a quick soak in warm soapy water handles most residue.

## Where It Falls Short

The water pan is the weak link. It's only about 1.5 inches deep, and on an 8-hour brisket cook, you'll refill it 3–4 times. Opening the door each time drops chamber temp by 20–30°F for a few minutes. Not a dealbreaker, but plan for it.

There's also no built-in meat probe. At this price point, some competitors include one. You'll need a $20 digital thermometer to avoid guessing. And the propane hose is short — if your tank sits more than 3 feet from the smoker, grab an extension.

## FAQ

**Q: How much wood do I use per cook?**  
A: For a 4-hour smoke, you'll use about 2–3 cups of chips. Start with a handful, add more every 45 minutes. Soak chips for 30 minutes first for longer-lasting smoke.

**Q: Can I grill on this?**  
A: Technically yes at 400°F, but it's not a grill. The vertical design means no direct heat — you're roasting, not searing. Keep burgers and steaks on a separate grill.

**Q: Does the gas flame affect the smoke flavor?**  
A: Minimal. The wood chips sit above the burner, so they smolder rather than burn with the gas. Most users report clean, mild smoke — not bitter.
