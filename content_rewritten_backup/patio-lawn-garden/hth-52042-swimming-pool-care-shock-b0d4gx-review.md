If you've stared at a green pool and wondered whether you'd need to drain the whole thing, the HTH 52042 Swimming Pool Care Shock is the product that talks you off that ledge. This is a calcium hypochlorite granular shock — the old-school, reliable workhorse of pool care — and it does exactly what the label promises: kills algae, clears cloudy water, and resets your chlorine levels fast.

**What you actually get**

The container holds 12 pounds of white, granular calcium hypochlorite (68% available chlorine). HTH packages it in a resealable jug with a child-resistant cap — minor detail, but if you've wrestled with flimsy pool chemical packaging before, you'll appreciate it. The granules are fine and uniform, which matters because that's what allows them to dissolve quickly.

The instructions on the label are your friend here: broadcast the granules directly across the pool surface while walking the perimeter. No dissolving in a bucket, no pouring down the skimmer. For a standard 10,000-gallon pool, you're looking at 1 pound per shock treatment. Double that if you're fighting algae or after a heavy rain.

**Real-world performance**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't a fluke. The most consistent praise centers on how fast this stuff works. One buyer noted, "Added it to my cloudy pool at 7 PM, woke up to crystal-clear water at 7 AM." That tracks with the chemistry — calcium hypochlorite releases free chlorine almost immediately upon contact with water. You don't get the slow-release patience game you'd deal with stabilized chlorine options.

It's also genuinely effective against the stubborn stuff. Multiple reviewers report using it to knock out green algae outbreaks that resisted other treatments. The 68% concentration means you're getting a dense, potent shock that doesn't require hauling half the store home.

**Where it falls short — be honest with yourself**

Here's the tradeoff you need to weigh: this is a calcium-based shock. If you live in an area with hard water, or if you're shocking weekly all season, you will slowly raise your pool's calcium hardness levels. That can eventually contribute to scale on pool walls and cloudy water that's actually a calcium issue, not a chlorine issue. The fix is monitoring your water chemistry and occasionally using a calcium reducer — an extra step nobody loves.

Second, the fast-dissolve formula means you can't just dump it and walk away. It wants to be spread evenly. If you pour it all in one spot, you risk bleaching your pool liner in that area. It's not a flaw in the product — it's a flaw in user technique — but it's worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- The weekend pool owner who wants one product that handles routine shocking and emergency algae treatment without maintaining a chemical arsenal.
- Anyone with a vinyl liner pool — this works fine, just follow the spreading instructions.
- People who want visible results overnight, not a multi-day treatment plan.

**Skip it if** you're on a saltwater system that requires minimal calcium addition, or if you prefer the stabilized, cyanuric-acid-free chlorine options because you're already fighting high calcium hardness.

**FAQ**

**How often should I shock with HTH 52042?**
Weekly during swim season is the standard. If you're running heavy bather loads, heat waves, or after storms, add an extra treatment. Test your free chlorine levels before each shock — you want to be adding to a baseline, not stacking on top of already-high levels.

**Can I swim right after shocking?**
Wait until the free chlorine level drops to 1–4 ppm, which typically takes 8–12 hours. If you over-shocked, it can take 24–48 hours. Test, don't guess.

**Will this work in a small above-ground pool?**
Yes. Halve the dose for 5,000-gallon pools. The granules dissolve fast enough that you won't get residue settling on the liner, but you still need to spread it evenly.
