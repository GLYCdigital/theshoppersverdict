**A 20-foot flag pole that doesn't require a crane — but there's a catch.**

Most flag poles in this price range are either flimsy 12-footers that bend in a stiff breeze or permanent installations that require a weekend of digging. The Yeshom Telescopic Aluminum Flag Pole Kit splits the difference with a clever five-section design that extends to a genuine 20 feet while collapsing down to roughly 5.5 feet for storage.

But is the convenience worth it? After digging through 210 ratings and the specific complaints that keep surfacing, here's what you need to know before buying.

## What's Actually in the Box

The kit ships with the five-section telescoping pole (anodized aluminum, 1.25-inch base diameter tapering to 0.75-inch at the top), two 3'x5' flags — a standard US flag and a Florida state flag in this configuration — plus a gold anodized ball finial and a ball-bearing swivel ring. The pole weighs about 8 pounds total, which is light enough for one person to handle during setup.

Missing from the box: a ground sleeve, mounting base, or any anchor system. The pole has a hollow bottom section that fits over a 1-inch stake or into a ground sleeve, but you'll need to source that separately. Factor that into your total cost.

## Real-World Performance

The anodized aluminum finish is genuinely corrosion-resistant — several reviewers mention leaving it up through coastal salt air without pitting. The swivel ring does its job; flags rotate freely rather than wrapping around the pole even in sustained 15-20 mph winds.

Setup is straightforward but has a learning curve. Each section twists and locks into place, and you need to align the small groove markers before seating each section fully. One reviewer noted: *"Took me about 20 minutes the first time. After that, it's a 5-minute job. Just make sure the sections are fully seated or they'll wobble."* That wobble complaint shows up a handful of times — if you don't lock each joint completely, the upper sections will flex noticeably.

The included flags are better than typical bundle-filler. They're 210-denier polyester with double-stitched hems and a reinforced canvas header. They'll hold up for a season of daily use, but don't expect 5-year durability. Plan to replace them annually if you fly them 24/7.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The height-to-value ratio is the standout. At full extension, this pole commands real presence in a yard. Multiple reviewers mention neighbors asking where they got it. The anodized finish draws consistent compliments — it looks more expensive than the price suggests.

The dual-flag setup is another winner for Florida homeowners specifically. *"Love that I can show both flags without buying a second pole,"* one verified buyer wrote. *"The state flag is actually good quality, not a cheap afterthought."*

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a mounting solution is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. You're either digging a hole and pouring concrete around a 1-inch stake, or buying a separate ground sleeve. Neither is hard, but it's an unexpected extra step and expense.

The telescoping mechanism can be finicky. In cold weather (below 40°F), the aluminum sections contract slightly and can bind during extension. Gentle persuasion works, but if you force it, you risk scratching the anodized coating. Also, this is not a storm-friendly setup — while the swivel prevents tangling, the pole will flex significantly in 30+ mph winds. Don't leave it at full height during hurricane season. Most owners lower it to about 12 feet during severe weather.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a tall, attractive flag display without a permanent installation, you're reasonably handy, and you don't mind buying a ground sleeve separately. It's ideal for renters, seasonal displays, or anyone who wants the option to collapse the pole when not in use.

**Skip it if:** You want a set-and-forget installation, live in an area with frequent severe storms, or expect the included flags to last multiple seasons. Also skip if you're not comfortable with a 20-foot pole — at full extension, it commands attention, which isn't for every neighborhood.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Yeshom delivers genuine height and solid build quality at a fair price, with the caveat that you'll need to supply your own ground anchor. The telescoping design is a real convenience, not a gimmick, and the included flags are better than typical bundle items. It's not perfect — the setup learning curve and cold-weather binding are real annoyances — but for the price, it's a legitimate value. Buy it if you want an impressive display without committing to a permanent pole. Skip it if you want a zero-maintenance installation that can shrug off severe weather.
