Let’s cut through the marketing. The Coleman Kompact is not a mountaineering expedition bag, and Coleman doesn’t pretend it is. What you’re getting is a solid, compressible cool-weather bag that does exactly what the name says—compacts down for easy hauling—without breaking your budget. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it’s clearly hitting a nerve with casual campers. The question is whether it hits yours.

## What’s Actually in the Bag

The Kompact ships as a complete system: the sleeping bag itself, a compression stuff sack, and a separate storage sack. The compression sack is the headline feature here—you can crank the straps down and shrink the bag to about half its loose volume. That’s a genuine advantage if you’re stuffing a trunk with gear for a family trip or trying to squeeze it into a duffel for a cabin weekend. The bag measures 75 inches long and 33 inches wide at the shoulder, which fits most adults up to around 6 feet tall comfortably. The shell is a polyester taffeta with a quilted construction, and the liner is cotton flannel—a nice touch at this price point that most synthetic bags skip.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the 20°F version on a 40-degree night and stayed warm with just a base layer and a thin fleece. That’s roughly consistent with what most owners report: the ratings skew optimistic. Coleman’s temperature numbers assume you’re wearing thermal layers, using a pad underneath, and sleeping in a tent. If you’re a cold sleeper, add 10 degrees to whatever rating you’re considering. The 30°F and 40°F versions are best reserved for summer nights or mild shoulder-season trips.

The zipper is a two-way YKK-style that doesn’t snag the fabric—a common failure point on budget bags—and the Thermolock draft tube runs the full length of the zipper to block cold air at the seam. That’s a feature you typically see on bags costing twice as much. The mummy-style hood cinches down with a drawcord, and the footbox is slightly articulated so your feet don’t compress the insulation.

## What Owners Keep Praising

The compression sack is the most consistent rave in the reviews. One verified buyer wrote, “Fits in my hiking pack’s bottom compartment with room to spare—didn’t think I’d get a 20-degree bag that small.” Another noted the flannel liner: “No more sliding around on slick polyester. Stays put and feels like a real bed.” Multiple reviewers also mention the bag’s durability through repeated washings—the quilting doesn’t shift and the zipper holds up.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about tradeoffs. The compression sack is great, but it’s not a dry sack—if you’re packing it in a canoe or leaving it exposed to rain, you’ll want a waterproof liner. The lack of a pillow strap is a real annoyance; a standard camp pillow migrates to the far corner of the bag by 3 a.m. And the cotton flannel liner, while comfortable, adds weight and bulk compared to pure synthetics—the 20°F version weighs in around 4.5 pounds, which is heavy for backpacking. This is a car-camping bag, not a thru-hike bag. Know that going in.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You’re a car camper, festival-goer, or scout parent who needs a dependable, packable bag that won’t wreck your budget. The 20°F version is a solid three-season bag for mild climates. The compression sack makes it a great spare bag to keep in the trunk for impromptu overnighters.

**Skip it if:** You’re a cold sleeper who runs cold at 30°F, you’re backpacking more than a mile from the trailhead, or you expect the temperature ratings to be literal.

## FAQ

**Is the 20°F bag actually warm at 20°F?**
Not for most people. Plan on comfort down to about 30°F with a pad and base layers. Below that, you’ll want a liner or an extra blanket.

**Can two bags zip together?**
No. The Kompact uses a left-side zipper with no matching right-side version, so you can’t create a double bag.

**How does it wash?**
Machine wash cold on gentle, then air dry or tumble on low. Owners report the quilting holds up fine across multiple washes—just don’t use fabric softener, which degrades insulation.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The Coleman Kompact is a smart buy for the weekend camper who values packability and comfort over ultralight specs. The compression sack genuinely works, the flannel liner is a comfort upgrade, and the draft tube shows Coleman didn’t cut corners where it matters. It’s not a technical piece of gear, and the temperature ratings demand a grain of salt. But for the money, it’s one of the better cool-weather values on Amazon right now.

*Buy it if you want a dependable, compressible bag for car camping and mild shoulder-season nights. Skip it if you need true winter performance or ultralight weight.*
