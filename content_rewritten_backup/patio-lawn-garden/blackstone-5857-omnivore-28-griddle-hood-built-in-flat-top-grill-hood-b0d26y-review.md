If you own a 28-inch Blackstone griddle, you already know the deal: the cooking surface is phenomenal, but cooking outdoors without a hood means slow heat-ups on windy days, splatter on your patio, and zero ability to melt cheese or trap steam for smash burgers. The BLACKSTONE 5857 Omnivore 28" Griddle Hood addresses exactly those pain points. After spending a season with it, here's the honest breakdown.

**What's in the Box**

You get the hood itself, two stainless steel hinge pins, and the necessary mounting hardware. Assembly takes about ten minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver — no drilling, no modifications to your existing griddle. The hood is a single-piece welded unit, which matters more than you'd think. Cheaper aftermarket hoods arrive in multiple panels that rattle and let heat escape through seams. This one doesn't.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The hood is constructed from powder-coated cold-rolled steel, and the hinge pins are stainless steel — a detail that prevents the rust-on-rust binding you'll see on knockoffs within a season. The powder coat is thick and even; no thin spots around the edges that would flash-rust after a few rain exposures.

Functionally, this hood changes how you use the griddle. With the hood down, the surface temperature stabilizes noticeably faster. On a 45°F day with a 15 mph breeze, my griddle held 375°F with the hood closed versus dipping to around 335°F without it. That's the difference between a decent sear and a frustrating cook. Trapping steam also means bacon cooks more evenly, and melting cheese over smash burgers goes from awkward to effortless.

The wind guard along the front edge is a thoughtful touch. It deflects gusts that would otherwise cool the cooking surface and blow grease toward you. It's not a full enclosure, but it's a meaningful upgrade over an open flat top.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.8-star rating across 240 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. One reviewer noted, "This hood fits perfectly on my older 28" model — I was worried it wouldn't match, but the hinge alignment was spot on." Another mentioned, "The difference in heat retention is night and day. My pancakes actually cook all the way through now instead of burning on the bottom." The consensus: it's a simple accessory that solves a real problem without overcomplicating things.

**Where It Falls Short**

No product is perfect, and this hood has a few tradeoffs. Most notably, there's no built-in temperature gauge. For a brand that equips its griddles with integrated thermometers, omitting one on the hood is a miss — you'll want a surface thermometer for consistent results.

The handle also conducts heat. After 20 minutes at high heat, it's uncomfortable to touch bare-handed. Use a grill glove or a folded towel when lifting the hood mid-cook.

Finally, compatibility is limited. This hood fits 28" models only — both the newer Omnivore and most older 28" versions. If you own a 22" or 36" Blackstone, this isn't your part. Double-check your model number before ordering.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Weekend breakfast cooks** who want even heat for pancakes, eggs, and bacon without flare-ups.
- **Smash burger enthusiasts** who need trapped steam to melt cheese properly.
- **Anyone cooking in exposed areas** where wind ruins temperature control.
- **Owners of older 28" Blackstone griddles** looking to extend functionality without buying a new unit.

Skip it if you only cook in a fully sheltered outdoor kitchen with zero breeze, or if you never cook foods that benefit from trapped heat and steam.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit my older 28" Blackstone griddle?**
A: Most likely yes. It's designed for the 28" Omnivore, but the hinge spacing matches previous 28" models. Measure the distance between your griddle's hinge mounts (they're about 28 inches apart) and compare with the hood's mounting brackets. Several reviewers confirmed a perfect fit on older models.

**Q: Can I leave the hood on during storage?**
A: Yes, and you probably should. It protects the cooking surface from rain and debris. Just make sure the griddle is dry and seasoned before covering, and the powder coat will hold up fine outdoors.

**Q: Does it help with grease splatter?**
A: Noticeably. The hood catches most of the grease that would otherwise aerosolize onto your patio or deck. The front wind guard also directs splatter back toward the griddle surface, keeping your surroundings cleaner.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own a 28" Blackstone and cook outdoors in anything less than perfect conditions. The heat retention alone justifies the price, and the build quality is a genuine upgrade over generic aftermarket hoods.

**Skip it if** you have a different griddle size, or if you only cook with the griddle fully sheltered and never need trapped steam.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-built, functional accessory with one notable omission (no thermometer) and a handle that gets hot. For most 28" owners, it's a worthwhile investment that improves cooking consistency and protects your griddle.
