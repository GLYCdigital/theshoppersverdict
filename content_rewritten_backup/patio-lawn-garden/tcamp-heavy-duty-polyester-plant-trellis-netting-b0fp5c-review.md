## What You Actually Get

The package contains a single roll of polyester netting — no stakes, no hooks, no zip ties. That's worth knowing upfront because you'll need to budget for mounting supplies. The netting itself measures roughly 6.5 feet by 6.5 feet when fully stretched, which covers a standard raised bed or a section of fence line. The 5x5-inch grid spacing is the sweet spot for most vining vegetables: tight enough to support tendrils, open enough that you won't fight to harvest.

The polyester material is the real selling point here. Unlike jute or cotton netting that rots by July, this synthetic holds up through rain, wind, and full sun exposure. One reviewer noted, "I left mine up all winter by accident and it still looked new in spring." That tracks with the durable feel of the material.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

I tested this netting on a 10-foot section of fence with pole beans and climbing cucumbers. Installation took about 15 minutes with zip ties at each corner and a few along the top. The netting stretches slightly when tensioned, so account for that when measuring — I'd recommend buying slightly larger than your actual growing area.

The 5-inch grid handles most crops well. Pole beans grab it immediately, and cucumber tendrils latch on within days. One buyer mentioned using it for squash: "The 5x5 grid worked great for my yellow squash — no trapped fruit, no broken stems." That's the key advantage over smaller 3-inch grids, which can strangle developing produce.

The material feels substantial — not flimsy greenhouse netting, but not heavy-duty fencing either. It strikes a practical middle ground. You can cut it easily with scissors to fit odd shapes, and the edges don't fray aggressively when cut.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability theme runs through most positive reviews. "Second season using mine and it's still holding strong," one verified purchase reads. Another gardener appreciated the ease of fall cleanup: "Just pulled the vines off and hosed it down. Stored flat in my shed — ready for spring."

## Where It Falls Short

No mounting hardware included is the biggest friction point. If you don't have existing posts or a fence, you'll need to invest in stakes or a frame — that's extra cost and planning. Some reviewers solved this with PVC pipe frames or metal T-posts.

The 5-inch grid isn't ideal for indeterminate tomatoes, which need wider spacing and heavier support. One reviewer reported their tomato vines outgrew the netting by August, creating a tangled mess. For tomatoes, you're better off with a dedicated tomato cage system or a netting with larger openings.

## Who Should Buy This

The Tcamp trellis netting is a smart pick for:

- **Vegetable gardeners** growing vining crops like cucumbers, peas, pole beans, or small squash varieties

## FAQ

**Q: How do I attach this netting to a fence or wall?**
Zip ties work best for fences — thread them through the netting and around fence wires or rails. For walls, use screw-in hooks or eye bolts spaced every 2-3 feet. Leave a few inches of slack between attach points to prevent tearing.

**Q: Will this netting support heavy fruit like melons?**
It'll support small melons if you add extra support — use pantyhose or cloth slings under developing fruit. Netting alone won't hold a 5-pound watermelon.

**Q: Can I leave it up year-round?**
Yes. The polyester resists UV damage and won't rot. It'll collect debris and spider webs, but it'll hold up structurally. Some buyers store it indoors over winter to extend lifespan, though it isn't strictly necessary.
