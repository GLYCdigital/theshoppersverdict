The 9-foot kayak market is crowded with flimsy, tippy bathtubs that make you regret every dollar spent. The Pelican Argo 100XR isn't one of them — but it's not without compromises. After digging through 229 ratings and the spec sheet, here's the real story for anyone eyeing this sit-in.

## What You Actually Get

The box delivers the assembled 9'6" kayak, a two-piece aluminum paddle, and a basic seat cushion. That's it — no PFD, no dry bag, no roof rack straps. The kayak itself uses Pelican's UNIPAK hull (one-piece rotational molding) with twin-arched multi-chine sides. At 39.5 pounds, it's roughly 10–15 pounds lighter than comparable sit-ins from Perception or Old Town in this length class. That weight difference is the entire reason to pick this over competitors.

## Real-World Performance

On flat water, the Argo 100XR tracks better than its 9'6" length suggests. The multi-chine hull provides respectable initial stability — you won't feel like you're in a rocking chair when you sit down. One reviewer summed it up: *"First time out, my wife and I both took turns. It's stable enough for a beginner but not so sluggish that an intermediate gets bored."*

The adjustable seatback is the standout feature. It clicks through multiple positions, and combined with the molded thigh pads, it keeps you locked in during longer paddles. Several buyers specifically praised it: *"The seat is way more comfortable than my buddy's $700 kayak. Did 4 hours without back pain."*

Where it struggles: wind. The twin-arched chine design and flat deck create noticeable weathercocking on breezy days. You'll be corrective-paddling constantly in anything over 10 mph gusts. It's manageable, but if you primarily paddle large open lakes or coastal areas, factor that in.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star average holds up across the board. Recurring themes in positive reviews:

- **Weight**: "I'm a 62-year-old woman and I load this on my SUV alone. That's why I bought it."
- **Storage**: The stern tank well plus bungee deck rigging fits a cooler and dry bag for day trips.
- **Durability**: Multiple owners report dragging it over gravel and rocks without damage — the UNIPAK hull takes abuse.

## Where It Falls Short

The included paddle is the weakest link. It's heavy, flexes under load, and the plastic blades slap water. Several reviewers noted this: *"The paddle is garbage. Buy a $60 fiberglass one and this kayak becomes a different animal."* Budget for that upgrade immediately.

The footrest adjustment system is another gripe. You have to reach under the deck through a small opening to slide the pegs, which is awkward if you need to adjust mid-paddle. Set it before you launch.

Storage capacity is modest — 30 pounds in the rear tank well — so this isn't a camping or fishing platform. It's a day-tripper, and that's fine.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Solo paddlers over 50** who value a manageable carry weight above all else
- **First-time kayak owners** on calm lakes, slow rivers, or protected bays
- **Smaller paddlers** (up to ~220 lbs) who want a responsive, maneuverable boat for exercise

**Skip it if** you paddle rough, windy water regularly, plan multi-day expeditions, or weigh over 250 pounds — the 275-pound capacity gets tight with gear.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the Pelican Argo 100XR stable enough for a nervous beginner?**
Yes. The wide beam (29.5") and multi-chine hull provide forgiving primary stability. You'll feel wobbles but not rolls. That said, practice in shallow, calm water first.

**Q: Can one person realistically car-top this without help?**
Yes — that's its main selling point. At 39.5 lbs, most adults can lift it overhead. Use a quality J-rack or foam blocks to protect your vehicle's roof.

**Q: Does it come with a paddle and seat?**
It includes a basic aluminum/plastic paddle and a padded seatback. Both work fine for casual use, but the paddle is the first thing you'll want to upgrade.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely lightweight sit-in kayak that won't break your back or your budget, and you paddle mostly calm inland waters.

**Skip it if** you need expedition storage, paddle in wind-exposed areas regularly, or refuse to spend extra on a better paddle.

For the price point, the Argo 100XR delivers exactly what it promises: an accessible, stable, and — critically — *portable* entry into sit-in kayaking. The minor flaws are acceptable tradeoffs for the weight savings. **4.6/5 stars.**
