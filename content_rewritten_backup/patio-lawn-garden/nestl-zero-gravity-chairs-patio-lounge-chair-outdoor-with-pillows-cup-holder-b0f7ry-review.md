**The short version:** If you've tried a zero gravity chair before and found the mesh straps digging into your back, the Nestl version solves that with actual padding. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's not a perfect chair — but for the price, it punches well above its weight class for backyard lounging.

## What you actually get

The box contains the chair frame, two removable pillows (headrest and lumbar), and a side-mounted cup holder that clips onto the armrest. Assembly takes about five minutes — you unfold the frame, snap the crossbars into place, and you're done. No tools required. The whole thing weighs roughly 25 pounds, so dragging it around the yard or onto the deck isn't a workout.

The frame is powder-coated aluminum tubing. That's worth calling out because a lot of budget zero-gravity chairs use steel that rusts after a season. Aluminum keeps this one light and corrosion-resistant, though it does mean a lower weight ceiling than steel frames.

## How it actually performs

The headline feature here is the cushioning. Standard zero gravity chairs use a taut mesh or fabric sling that creates pressure points at the shoulders and tailbone. The Nestl chair has roughly 2 inches of foam padding across both the seat and the backrest, which changes the comfort profile dramatically.

The recline mechanism is a simple locking system — you pull a lever under the armrest, lean back, and it catches at any angle. It locks into place firmly, so you won't get that annoying "slowly sinking backward" effect that cheap chairs develop after a few weeks. The full zero-gravity position (legs elevated above heart level) provides genuine lower-back relief. Buyers consistently mention this in their feedback. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I have a bad back and this is the only chair I can sit in for more than 30 minutes without pain."*

The pillows are a nice touch, but the star is the lumbar support. It's strapped in place, so it doesn't wander down to your lower back mid-nap like some competitors' Velcro-attached versions. The headrest pillow is softer and works well for reading or dozing.

## Where it falls short

The fabric is the biggest tradeoff. It's a soft, suede-like polyester that feels great against skin — but it retains heat. In direct afternoon sun, the seat gets noticeably warm within about 20 minutes. Multiple buyers flagged this. One wrote: *"Love the chair, but the material gets hot. I put a towel down on sunny days."* If you're a shade lounger, this won't bother you. Full-sun worshippers should plan accordingly.

The 300-pound weight capacity is another consideration. That's standard for aluminum-frame chairs, but if you're over 250 or plan to use it heavily, the steel-frame competition (often 350+ pound capacity) is the safer bet. The chair also doesn't fold completely flat for storage — it folds to about the size of a lawn chair, which is fine for a garage or shed, but not ideal if you're tight on space.

The cup holder is functional but basic. It's a hard plastic ring that holds standard 12-ounce cans and bottles securely. Wide-base tumblers or insulated mugs wobble and can tip. For a chair that's clearly designed for long relaxation sessions, the drink holder feels like an afterthought.

## Who should buy this

- **Back-pain sufferers** who want zero-gravity relief without paying $200+ for a branded ergonomic chair
- **Shade loungers** who prioritize cushioning over breathability
- **Anyone who wants a chair that assembles in minutes** and doesn't need tools or muscle

**Skip it if** you're over 275 pounds, plan to bake in direct sun for hours, or expect a premium cup holder experience.

## FAQ

**Is the Nestl chair actually zero gravity?**
Yes. It achieves the same leg-above-heart position as more expensive models. The locking mechanism holds the full recline angle without drifting.

**Can the pillows be removed for washing?**
Both pillows are removable. The covers zip off for machine washing (cold, gentle cycle). The chair's main fabric is spot-clean only.

**How does it handle wind?**
At 25 pounds, it's stable in moderate breezes but will blow over in strong gusts. The included cup holder doesn't have a lid, so expect your drink to go airborne in heavy wind.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want genuine zero-gravity comfort with real padding, and you're okay with a fabric that runs warm in direct sun.

**Skip it if** you're a heavier user, lounge exclusively in full sun, or need a chair that packs down to flat storage.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Nestl chair delivers 90% of the experience of premium zero-gravity chairs at a fraction of the cost. The heat retention and 300-pound limit are real drawbacks, but for most shoppers, this is the rare patio product that overdelivers on its price point.
