**The Fish Mate P7000 is the automatic feeder that pond owners recommend when they're tired of cheap units that jam, tip over, or dump the entire hopper into the water after one rainstorm.** It's not flashy, and it won't win design awards, but it does the one thing that matters: feed your fish reliably on schedule. With a 4.1-star average across 204 ratings, it's the workhorse of the category — and for good reason, with some caveats.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the feeder unit, a mounting flange with locking ring, a 9.5-inch drop tube, and the instruction manual. **You'll need to supply 4 D-cell batteries** — they're not included, which catches some buyers off guard. The hopper holds roughly 2.2 pounds of floating or sinking pellets, which translates to 4-6 weeks of feeding for a typical backyard pond with 10-15 fish.

The headline feature is the **dual 24-hour cycles**. Each cycle lets you program up to 4 feeding times, and you can run both cycles simultaneously. That means you can set one schedule for weekday mornings and another for weekends, or feed small amounts four times daily to mimic natural grazing patterns. Each feeding dispenses a 1-10 second burst, giving you portion control down to roughly a teaspoon per cycle.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The housing is UV-stabilized ABS plastic that holds up to direct sun without becoming brittle — a common failure point on cheaper feeders. The feeding mechanism uses a rotating drum rather than an auger, which handles most pellet sizes without jamming. One reviewer noted, *"I've had it for two seasons and it's never clogged, even with the cheap feed from the big box store."*

Battery life is genuinely impressive. The motor only runs for those few seconds during feeding, so the drain is minimal. **Most owners report 4-6 months on one set of D-cells**, even with the maximum 8 feedings per day. There's a low-battery indicator on the LCD, though it tends to appear about a week before the unit actually dies.

The mounting system deserves special mention. The flange bolts to any flat surface, and the feeder twists onto it with a locking ring. It's secure enough that I haven't found complaints about units blowing off in storms — which is rare for this product category.

## Where It Falls Short

The **programming interface is straight out of 2008**. You're working with a small LCD, two dials, and a set button. Setting up more than two feedings requires navigating through nested menus that aren't intuitive. Plan on 20-30 minutes with the manual the first time. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's the most common complaint in the reviews.

The 9.5-inch drop tube is another limitation. If your pond edge is steep or you're mounting it on a fence, the pellets may land on the bank instead of the water. Several reviewers solved this by building a small platform, but it's extra work you shouldn't have to do at this price point.

Finally, the **LCD screen is tiny and hard to read in direct sunlight**. You'll often find yourself shading it with your hand to check the settings.

## Who Should Buy This

**The Fish Mate P7000 is for you if:** You have a medium-to-large pond (500+ gallons), you're going on vacation for more than a weekend, or you have fish that need multiple small feedings daily (koi, goldfish, or trout). It's also ideal for anyone who's been burned by cheap feeders that jam or tip over.

**Skip it if:** You have a small decorative pond with just a few fish — the capacity and programmability are overkill. It's also not the right choice if you want smartphone control or app integration; this is a purely mechanical timer unit.

## FAQ

**Q: Can it handle floating pellets and sinking sticks?**
A: Yes, the rotating drum handles both. Floating pellets work best, but sinking varieties dispense fine as long as they're not oversized (over 1/4 inch).

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: The hopper and drum assembly lifts out for washing. Use warm soapy water and dry thoroughly before reassembly. Do it monthly to prevent pellet dust from gumming up the mechanism.

**Q: Will it survive winter?**
A: The unit itself will, but the batteries and LCD don't like freezing temps. Most owners bring it inside during hard freezes unless their pond stays ice-free.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, no-fuss feeder that will outlast the season and actually hold up to weather. **Skip it if** you need app control, a longer drop tube, or you're unwilling to wrestle with the dated programming interface.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not perfect, but it's the feeder that serious pond owners keep recommending for a reason. The reliability and build quality justify the price, even if the interface feels like a relic.
