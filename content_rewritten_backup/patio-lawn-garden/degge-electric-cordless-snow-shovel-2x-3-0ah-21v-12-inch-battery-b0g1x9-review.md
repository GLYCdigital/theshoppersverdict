**The DEGGE 21V cordless snow shovel is the Goldilocks option for light snow — not too weak, not overkill. Here's who should buy it (and who absolutely shouldn't).**

If you live in a region where winter means 2-5 inches of fluffy powder rather than lake-effect dumps, a full-size gas snowblower is like using a chainsaw to cut butter. The DEGGE Electric Cordless Snow Shovel fills that specific gap: a 12.5-pound, battery-powered tool that clears walkways, decks, and small driveways without the fuel, cord, or back strain.

**What you actually get**

The box includes the shovel unit, two 3.0Ah 21V batteries, a dual-port charger, and an assembly that takes under two minutes — no tools required. The 12-inch clearing width is paired with a 6-inch intake depth, and the chute rotates manually. As shown in the product image, the design is compact enough to hang on a garage wall hook when not in use.

**Real-world performance**

The dual-battery setup is the smartest feature here. While one 3.0Ah pack runs the motor, the second charges — giving you roughly 40-50 minutes of combined runtime. That's enough for a 30-foot driveway with a few passes, or a week's worth of sidewalk clearing on a single charge cycle.

The 21V motor throws light, powdery snow a respectable 20-25 feet. On dry snow up to 4 inches, it moves efficiently without stalling. One buyer noted, "Cleared my 40-foot driveway in about 15 minutes. The batteries lasted through two snowfalls before needing a recharge." That matches typical expectations.

**Where it falls short**

Honest tradeoffs matter. The plastic auger housing is the weak point — it'll handle snow fine but won't survive gravel driveway abuse. One reviewer reported, "The auger grabbed a few small stones and I can see stress marks on the housing already." Keep it on paved surfaces.

Wet, heavy snow is this tool's kryptonite. Anything above 6 inches of slushy accumulation will clog the chute and strain the motor. This is not a snowblower replacement; it's a snow *shovel* replacement. Also, there's no headlight — a surprising omission for a winter tool, since most clearing happens in low-light hours.

**Who should buy this**

- **Seniors or anyone with back issues**: The 12.5-pound weight and ergonomic handle make it far less physically demanding than a manual shovel.
- **Townhome or condo dwellers**: Perfect for a 10-20 foot walkway and a small patio.
- **First-time snow tool buyers**: It's cheaper than a gas blower, requires no maintenance beyond battery care, and takes up minimal storage space.

**Skip it if** you face regular snowfalls over 6 inches, have a large driveway, or deal with heavy, wet coastal snow. You'll need a more powerful unit.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do the batteries actually last?**
A: Expect 20-25 minutes per 3.0Ah battery under normal load — about 40-50 minutes total with both. Cold weather reduces battery efficiency by 15-20%, so plan accordingly.

**Q: Can this handle a gravel driveway?**
A: Not recommended. The plastic auger housing can crack or stress if it grabs stones. Stick to paved or compacted surfaces.

**Q: Does the chute adjust?**
A: Yes, manually. You rotate it by hand to direct snow left, right, or straight ahead. It's not as convenient as a joystick control on gas models, but it works fine.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a lightweight, battery-powered solution for light snow on paved surfaces — especially if you're physically limited or tired of shoveling. **Skip it if** you face heavy accumulations or have a large driveway.
