## Real-World Performance

The active ingredient, 2,4-D, is one of the most studied herbicides in existence — it's been used since the 1940s and remains a staple for broadleaf control. What Southern Ag delivers is a clean, no-frills version of that chemistry at a concentration that actually works.

In practice, here's what you can expect:

- **Mixing ratio:** The label calls for 3–4 fluid ounces per gallon of water for most lawn applications. That's roughly 2.5 tablespoons per gallon. For spot treatment, you can go slightly stronger, but don't exceed label rates — more isn't better here, and you risk turf injury.
- **Speed of action:** You'll see weeds twisting and curling within 48–72 hours. Full kill typically takes 7–14 days, depending on temperature and weed species. Cooler weather slows things down significantly.
- **Selectivity:** This is the key selling point. Applied correctly, it spares common lawn grasses — Bermuda, fescue, zoysia, St. Augustine (at reduced rates) — while targeting broadleaf weeds. It will not kill crabgrass, nutsedge, or other grassy weeds. That's not a flaw; it's a limitation you need to know.

One practical note: 2,4-D works best when weeds are actively growing — spring and fall, not during summer heat stress. Spraying in 90°F weather can cause turf injury and reduces efficacy.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the downsides before you buy:

- **You must mix it yourself.** There's no sprayer attached, no measuring done for you. If you're not comfortable reading a label and measuring accurately, this isn't for you. Mis-mixing wastes product and can damage your lawn.

- **Potential for turf injury** if you overspray or apply during heat stress. This is operator error, but it happens. Read the label's turf-type restrictions carefully — St. Augustine and centipede grass require reduced rates.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Q: How long after spraying can I walk on the lawn?**
Wait until the spray has dried completely — typically 1–2 hours in normal conditions. For pets and kids, the label recommends waiting until the product has dried, and ideally 24 hours before allowing prolonged contact. Be conservative here.

**Q: Can I use this on St. Augustine grass?**
Yes, but at a reduced rate — 1.5–2 ounces per gallon instead of 3–4. And avoid spraying when temperatures exceed 85°F. St. Augustine is more sensitive to 2,4-D than Bermuda or fescue.

**Q: Will this kill dandelions?**
Yes, effectively. Dandelions are one of the most susceptible weeds to 2,4-D. They'll show twisting and deformation within days and die off within two weeks. For established dandelions, apply in early spring when they're actively growing.
