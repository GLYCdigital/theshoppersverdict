**Forget Me Not Seeds Review: 500 Seeds of Delicate Blue — But Will They Deliver?**

You’re looking at a 3.8-star average from 190 Amazon ratings. That’s a solid “cautiously optimistic” score — not a runaway hit, not a dud. Here’s the real story on this 500-seed packet of perennial Forget Me Nots, and whether it deserves a spot in your garden.

## What You Actually Get

Open the packet and you’ll find roughly 500 tiny, dust-like seeds — these are genuinely small, so don’t expect easy handling. No fancy packaging, no grow kit. Just seeds, a basic label, and a promise. For the price, that’s about what you should expect.

The variety is *Myosotis sylvatica*, the classic woodland Forget Me Not. It’s hardy in zones 3–8, prefers partial shade and moist soil, and tops out around 6–12 inches tall. The image on the listing shows those iconic clusters of sky-blue flowers with yellow centers — that’s accurate, assuming germination goes well.

## Performance in the Real World

Here’s where things get interesting. Buyers report wildly different experiences, and it comes down to one factor: **stratification**. Forget Me Not seeds need a cold period to break dormancy. Some growers sow them directly in fall or early spring and get great results. Others plant in summer, skip the cold treatment, and wonder why nothing happens.

One verified buyer put it plainly: *“I scattered these in March in zone 6 and had little seedlings within two weeks. By June I had a carpet of blue.”* Another countered: *“Sowed in May, followed the packet exactly, got maybe a dozen plants total.”*

The packet’s instructions are minimal — that’s a real weakness. If you know to refrigerate seeds for 3–4 weeks before sowing (or just plant in fall), you’ll likely succeed. If you don’t, you’re gambling.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Self-seeding reliability**: Once established, these come back year after year. Many reviewers note volunteers popping up in unexpected spots — a plus if you want naturalized areas.
- **Color payoff**: The blue is unmistakable and photographs beautifully. Several buyers mention using them as a spring border under tulips.
- **Cold hardiness**: Even in northern zones, established plants shrug off winter without fuss.

## Where It Falls Short

The flip side of self-seeding is **aggression**. In mild, damp climates (think Pacific Northwest or UK-style conditions), Forget Me Nots can spread like gossip. One reviewer warned: *“Lovely for two years, then they took over my entire shade bed. Pulling them is a chore.”*

Also, **first-year blooms are underwhelming**. Direct-sown seeds typically produce foliage only in year one; the full floral show arrives in year two. If you want instant color, buy nursery starts instead.

Finally, the **seed count is inflated in practice**. Forget Me Not seeds have naturally low germination rates (often 50–60% even under ideal conditions). Of those 500 seeds, expect 250–300 viable plants — still plenty for a modest bed, but not the “carpet” the listing implies in a single season.

## Who Should Buy These

- **Woodland gardeners** with moist, shaded spots who want naturalized drifts
- **Patient growers** willing to sow in fall or stratify for spring planting

## Who Should Skip Them

- **Impatient gardeners** wanting same-season blooms
- **Tidy gardeners** who hate volunteer seedlings popping up in lawn cracks
- **Warm-climate growers** (zones 9+) where these struggle and often behave as annuals

## FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Can I sow these directly outdoors?**
Yes, but timing matters. Sow in fall (September–November) or very early spring when soil is still cool. Summer sowing without stratification will likely fail.

**Q: How long until I see flowers?**
Fall-sown seeds typically bloom the following spring. Spring-sown (stratified) seeds may produce a few flowers by late summer, but full effect comes in year two.

**Q: Will these really come back every year?**
Yes, as true perennials in zones 3–8. Even better, they self-seed, so you’ll get new plants each season — sometimes more than you want.
