Let's be clear about what you're actually buying here. This is a live **Ruta graveolens** — common rue, "herb of grace" — shipped in a standard 4-inch nursery pot. It's not a decorative houseplant, and it's not parsley. It's a Mediterranean perennial with a distinct, sharp, almost medicinal aroma that tells you instantly it's the real thing.

## What's in the pot

You get one established plant, not a cutting or seed packet. The 4-inch pot is standard nursery stock — roots should be visibly circling the bottom if you pop it out, which is a good sign of a mature root system. The plant arrives with soil intact, usually wrapped to minimize mess. Some shipments include a simple care card; most don't, so know your rue basics before it lands.

## Real-world performance

Rue is a tough plant. It thrives in poor, well-draining soil, full sun, and neglect. That's its superpower. If you're the type who kills mint, this is the herb for you. It's drought-tolerant once established and handles heat well.

The catch? Shipping stress is real. Several buyers note the plant arrives looking a bit rough — droopy, yellowed lower leaves, or leggy stems. One reviewer said, "It looked sad for the first week but bounced back after I gave it some sun and didn't overwater." That's the theme here: this plant recovers, but it's not a showpiece on day one.

Grow it in a pot with drainage holes, place it in full sun (at least 6 hours), and water only when the top inch of soil is dry. Indoors, it'll survive but won't thrive — it wants light. If you're in a cold climate, bring it inside over winter or treat it as an annual.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

First, **skin sensitivity is a real issue**. Rue contains furanocoumarins that cause phytophotodermatitis — meaning if you touch the leaves and then expose your skin to sunlight, you can get a painful, blistering rash. This isn't a niche concern; it's well-documented. Wear gloves when handling, and keep it away from kids and pets. Some buyers were caught off guard by this.

Second, **shipping damage is inconsistent**. While some plants arrive pristine, others look rough. The 4.0 average rating reflects this variability. If you're buying as a gift, factor in that it may need a recovery week.

Third, **it's not a culinary herb**. Rue is bitter and potentially toxic in large amounts. This is a medicinal, aromatic, or ornamental plant — not something you're tossing into pasta sauce. Some buyers didn't realize this and were disappointed.

## FAQ

**Q: Is rue safe to handle?**
No. Wear gloves. Rue sap plus sunlight causes a skin reaction called phytophotodermatitis — painful blisters and burns. Handle with care, especially when pruning.

**Q: Will this survive indoors?**
It can survive but won't thrive. Rue needs full sun — 6+ hours of direct light. Without it, the plant becomes leggy and weak. A south-facing window can work; a grow light is better.

**Q: How do I know it's alive after shipping?**
Look for green, firm stems and healthy roots. Yellowing lower leaves are normal stress — trim them. If the stem is mushy or black, it's dead on arrival. Most sellers offer a replacement window, but check your order's policy.
