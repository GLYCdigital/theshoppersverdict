# Pool Mate Alkalinity Increaser Review: A Simple Fix for Cloudy, Corrosive Pool Water

If your pool water tests low for total alkalinity (below 80 ppm), you're fighting a losing battle against pH swings, eye irritation, and etching on plaster surfaces. The Pool Mate Alkalinity Increaser is a straightforward, no-frills solution to that problem—and at roughly six bucks, it's the cheapest insurance policy your pool will get this season.

## What You Actually Get

The product ships as a 3-pound bucket of white, granular sodium bicarbonate—essentially high-purity baking soda formulated for pools. That's it. No tablets, no liquid, no fancy applicator. As shown in the product image, it's a simple white bucket with clear dosing instructions on the label. For reference, 3 pounds will raise total alkalinity by approximately 10 ppm in a 10,000-gallon pool (check the label for exact ratios based on your pool volume).

## Performance and Real-World Use

Here's where this product earns its keep: it dissolves fast. Unlike some alkalinity increasers that clump on the surface and take days to break down, Pool Mate granules disperse quickly when broadcast across the deep end or pre-dissolved in a bucket of water. That speed matters because you want the chemical working, not floating.

The math works in your favor too. Sodium bicarbonate is sodium bicarbonate—there's no proprietary magic blend here. Pool Mate uses food-grade quality ingredients at a fraction of what pool stores charge for the same compound in fancy packaging. ## Where It Falls Short

The 3-pound size is the honest weak point. If you own a large in-ground pool (20,000+ gallons) or need to raise alkalinity significantly (more than 30 ppm), you'll need multiple buckets. At $6.07 each, that adds up fast—though it's still cheaper per pound than most competitors. Also, there's no scoop included. You'll need a kitchen scale or a measuring cup to dose accurately. Eyeballing it is how you end up overshooting and dealing with cloudy water.

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for above-ground pool owners or small in-ground pools (under 15,000 gallons) who need routine alkalinity bumps of 10-30 ppm. It's also perfect for budget-conscious shoppers who already know their pool chemistry and just need the raw chemical at a fair price. If you're a first-time pool owner who's confused about alkalinity vs. pH, buy this along with a proper test kit—don't guess.

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I add per 10,000 gallons?**
A: To raise alkalinity by 10 ppm, add about 1.5 pounds (roughly 3 cups if using standard measuring cups). Always retest after 24 hours before adding more.

**Q: Can I swim immediately after adding it?**
A: Yes, though it's best to wait 20-30 minutes with the pump running so it fully dissolves. It's non-toxic and won't irritate skin at proper doses.

**Q: Will this lower my pH?**
A: No—it raises alkalinity, which helps *stabilize* pH. If your pH is high, you need a separate pH decreaser (muriatic acid or sodium bisulfate).
