Some hedge trimmers are toys. This one isn't. The Husqvarna 122HD60 is a serious piece of gas-powered equipment for anyone with substantial shrubbery, and at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's earned its reputation. But here's what the product page won't tell you: it's powerful enough to be genuinely dangerous if you're not careful, and heavy enough to make you regret skipping arm day. Let's break down whether this 21.7cc beast belongs in your shed.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the trimmer itself, a blade cover, and the standard Husqvarna owner's manual. No fuel mix, no oil — you supply that. The 23.7-inch dual-action blade is the headline feature here, and it's not just marketing speak. Dual-action means both blades move, which doubles cutting speed and significantly reduces vibration compared to single-blade units. The blade gap is 30mm, which the specs say handles branches up to about 1 inch thick. That's accurate in real use.

## Performance and Build Quality

Fire this thing up and you'll notice two things immediately: the X-Torq engine runs clean and idles smoothly, and the vibration is genuinely low. Husqvarna's LowVib system isn't gimmicky — it's actually engineered into the handle mounts. After 45 minutes trimming overgrown boxwoods, my hands weren't buzzing like with cheaper trimmers.

The air injection system deserves real credit. It uses centrifugal force to fling larger debris away from the air filter before it gets sucked in. If you've ever killed a cheap trimmer by clogging its filter with leaf dust, you'll appreciate this. It keeps the engine breathing clean, which translates to longer life.

Cutting performance is where this thing shines. The dual-action blades slice through new growth like butter, and they handle semi-woody branches up to 3/4 inch without bogging down. I tested it on an overgrown privet hedge with some 1-inch dead branches — it chewed through them, though it did slow noticeably. For anything thicker, you'll want loppers first.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.5-star rating isn't accidental. Buyers repeatedly mention the cutting power and the vibration reduction. One reviewer noted, "I've owned three other gas trimmers and this is the first one my hands don't go numb using." Another said, "The dual-action blades make trimming a 30-foot hedge feel like a 10-minute job." The consensus: this thing is fast and smooth.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. At 12.1 pounds, this trimmer is heavy. It's balanced reasonably well, but after 20 minutes of overhead work on tall hedges, your shoulders will feel it. The front grip is adjustable, which helps, but it's still a workout.

Gas operation is also a real consideration. You're mixing 50:1 fuel, dealing with starting procedure (choke, prime, pull — it usually starts in 3-5 pulls cold), and storing fuel. If you have a small yard with a few shrubs, a battery trimmer makes more sense. This is for people with serious hedges, not weekend touch-ups.

## Who Should Buy This

This trimmer is for the homeowner with substantial landscaping — think 50+ linear feet of hedges, or property with overgrown shrubs that have been neglected. It's also ideal for anyone who already owns gas equipment and doesn't want another battery ecosystem. If you're a casual gardener with a few small bushes, skip it. You'll be paying for power you don't need and weight you don't want.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it start easily when cold?**
A: Generally yes, but it requires the full procedure: prime the bulb 5-6 times, set the choke, pull until it sputters (usually 2-3 pulls), then release choke and pull again. Expect 3-5 pulls total. Warm starts are much easier — usually one pull.

**Q: Can it handle thick branches, or do I need loppers?**
A: It handles up to about 1 inch comfortably. Anything thicker will stall or take multiple passes. Keep loppers handy for larger cuts — this is a trimmer, not a chainsaw.

**Q: Is the vibration really that low?**
A: Yes, noticeably lower than comparable trimmers. The dual-action blade design cancels out much of the back-and-forth force. Your hands will appreciate it on longer jobs, but it's not vibration-free.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have serious hedge-trimming work, you prefer gas power for reliability and unlimited runtime, and you're willing to handle fuel mixing and a bit of weight for professional-grade cut quality.

**Skip it if:** Your hedge trimming is occasional, you want grab-and-go convenience, or you're physically limited — the 12-pound weight and pull-start are significant considerations.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Husqvarna 122HD60 delivers exactly what serious gardeners need: raw cutting power, low vibration, and engine longevity features. It's not for everyone, but for its intended audience, it's a top-tier choice that will last years with proper maintenance.
