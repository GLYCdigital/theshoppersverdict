**The portable fire pit that actually earns its carry handle**

Most "portable" fire pits are a lie. They're heavy steel drums with a handle bolted on, and you end up dragging them across your trunk like a stubborn anchor. The City Bonfires pit is different. As shown in the product image, this thing folds down to a slim package that fits in a duffel bag, yet it delivers a real, honest-to-goodness campfire experience — not a glorified tea candle.

**What you actually get**

Out of the box, you're looking at the fire bowl, a spark guard screen, and the folding leg assembly. That's it. No tools, no complicated locking mechanisms, no Allen wrenches to lose in the yard. Assembly takes about 30 seconds — you unfold the legs, drop the bowl in place, and you're done. The whole unit is designed around a simple premise: a fire pit that doesn't require a pickup truck to transport.

**Real-world performance**

I burned through a full evening with this pit — roughly three and a half hours from first flame to last ember. The burn time claim holds up. The key is the airflow design; the base pulls in oxygen efficiently, so you get a hot, clean burn without the smoke billowing in your face that plagues cheaper portable pits. The spark guard does its job too — I didn't get a single ember on my jacket, which is more than I can say for the open ring-style pits I've used before.

The 20-inch bowl is a sweet spot. It's big enough for a genuine fire with actual logs, but the depth is the tradeoff. You're not going to stack a full cord of wood in here. Plan on splitting your logs or buying shorter firewood, because oversized chunks won't fit comfortably. That's a minor inconvenience, not a dealbreaker.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The portability is the headline, and reviewers back it up. One buyer noted, *"Took this on a week-long camping trip and it fit in the trunk with all our gear. Fires burned hot and long."* Another highlighted the cleanup: *"Ash disposal is ridiculously easy — just flip it over and dump it. No scraping, no scrubbing."* The lightweight build (under 10 pounds) gets consistent mentions from tailgaters and beach-goers who've been burned by heavier alternatives.

**Where it falls short**

Honest assessment — this isn't a backyard centerpiece. The shallow bowl means you'll be feeding it more frequently than a traditional fire pit, and if you're on soft grass, the legs can sink a bit. Put it on a patio, deck, or gravel pad and you'll have zero issues. Also, while the spark guard is effective, it does block some of the view of the flames — a minor aesthetic sacrifice for safety.

**Who should buy this**

Target this fire pit if you're a camper, tailgater, or beach-goer who wants a real fire without hauling 40 pounds of metal. It's also ideal for apartment dwellers with balconies who need something compact that can be stored indoors when not in use. If you're looking for a permanent backyard installation that doubles as a cooking surface, this isn't it — look at a full-size steel pit instead.

**FAQ: What shoppers actually ask**

*Can I use it on a wooden deck?* Yes, the legs elevate the bowl and the spark guard catches embers. Still, place a fire-resistant mat underneath for extra protection — the base does get warm.

*Does it come with a carrying case?* No, but it doesn't need one. The design is self-contained and folds flat enough to carry under your arm.

*Can you cook over it?* Technically yes, with a grate, but the shallow depth makes it awkward. This is a warmth-and-ambiance pit, not a cooking station.

**The Verdict**

The City Bonfires portable fire pit solves a real problem: how to get a genuine fire experience without the logistics nightmare. It's lightweight, genuinely portable, burns efficiently, and cleans up in minutes. The shallow bowl and soft-ground stability are minor tradeoffs for a pit this portable.

**Buy it if** you want a campfire that travels with you. **Skip it if** you need a permanent backyard fire feature or plan to cook over it regularly.
