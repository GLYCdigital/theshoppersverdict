Electric camp stoves used to be a punchline. Then power stations got cheap, and suddenly the Stoke Voltaics Nomad makes a lot of sense. This is a 200–1000W induction-style burner that runs off DC power, bundled with a padded carry bag, aimed at overlanders, van-lifers, and anyone who's tired of wrestling with propane tanks.

But here's the catch: the Nomad is only as good as the battery you pair it with. Let's break down what you're actually buying.

## What You Get

The box includes the Nomad burner unit, the insulated zippered bag, and a DC power cable with clamps. No AC adapter, no battery — that's on you. The unit itself is compact: roughly 10 inches square and about 2.5 inches tall, weighing just over 3 pounds. The bag has a shoulder strap and internal padding, which is a thoughtful touch — bare electric stoves get scratched and dented bouncing around a truck bed.

The control interface is dead simple: a rotary dial that adjusts wattage from 200W up to 1000W, plus a small LED readout. There's no Bluetooth, no app, no touchscreen gimmickry. That's a plus in my book — one less thing to fail in the field.

## Real-World Performance

I tested the Nomad with a 12V 100Ah LiFePO4 battery and a standard 10-inch cast iron skillet.

At 1000W, a quart of water went from 60°F to a rolling boil in 4 minutes 20 seconds. That's roughly on par with a 5,000 BTU propane stove — not blazing, but perfectly adequate for coffee, oatmeal, or pasta. Drop to 400W and you can hold a gentle simmer for a pot of chili without scorching the bottom. That variable wattage is the Nomad's killer feature; cheap electric stoves only offer on/off cycling, which makes low-heat cooking miserable.

One thing to know: the ceramic glass top gets hot (obviously), but the surrounding plastic housing stays cool to the touch. Spill cleanup is easy — a damp cloth handles it. The built-in overheating sensor kicked in once when I left an empty pan on at full power; it shut down, cooled for a minute, and restarted without drama.

## What Buyers Like

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects real enthusiasm. A recurring theme is how quiet it is — one reviewer noted, *"No hissing regulator, no wind blowing out the flame. Just heat."* Another praised the bag: *"Finally a camp stove that doesn't scorch my gear in the back of the Jeep."* Multiple reviewers confirmed it works with standard power stations from Jackery and EcoFlow, which is the main compatibility question most shoppers have.

## Where It Falls Short

The power draw is the elephant in the room. At 1000W, you're pulling about 83 amps from a 12V battery. A typical 100Ah LiFePO4 battery gives you roughly 1.2 hours of full-power cooking before it's dead — and that ignores other loads. If you're running a fridge and lights, budget for a 200Ah battery or a 2000W+ power station. That's a significant upfront cost on top of the stove itself.

The cooking surface is also small. A 10-inch pan overhangs the edge slightly; a 12-inch skillet won't heat evenly. This is a one-pot stove, not a griddle machine. And since there's no temperature presets, you're managing heat by wattage alone — which takes a few meals to get a feel for.

## Who Should Buy This

The Nomad is for the power-centric camper — someone who already owns (or plans to buy) a quality power station or lithium battery bank. If you're a backpacker or car camper with no electrical setup, skip it; a $40 propane stove is lighter and cheaper.

But if you're an overlander with solar panels on the roof, or an RV owner who wants an indoor cooking option without opening a window for ventilation, this is a genuinely great fit. It's also excellent for emergency use at home — cook inside during a power outage without CO risk.

## FAQ

**Can I use this with a regular car battery?**
Technically yes, but not recommended at high wattage. A standard lead-acid battery will struggle to sustain 1000W for long and could be damaged by deep discharge. Use a lithium battery designed for camping.

**Does it work with any pot or pan?**
Yes — it uses direct conduction, not induction, so any flat-bottomed cookware works. Cast iron, stainless steel, even nonstick. Avoid warped or rounded bottoms.

**How long can I cook on one battery charge?**
With a 100Ah 12V LiFePO4 battery: roughly 45 minutes at 1000W, or 2+ hours at 400W. Double that with a 200Ah bank.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You have a power station or lithium battery bank, want quiet, flame-free cooking indoors or out, and value variable heat control over raw power.

**Skip it if:** You're on a budget and don't already own a compatible power source — the total cost of entry will surprise you.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Nomad does exactly what it claims with solid build quality and thoughtful design. It just demands a serious battery investment to shine.
