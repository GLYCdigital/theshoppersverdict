**The no-phosphorus lawn food that actually earns its "Superior" name — most of the time.**

Most lawn fertilizers on Amazon are either high-phosphorus blends that feed your lawn but harm local water systems, or cheap nitrogen-only formulas that green up grass for two weeks then leave it starving. The Superior Nitrogen & Potash 15-0-15 NPK sits in a smarter middle ground: a zero-phosphorus liquid concentrate designed for lawns that already have adequate phosphorus in the soil.

This is a fertilizer for people who understand their soil. If you're new to lawn care, you might not need it yet. If you've been fighting yellowing grass despite regular feeding, this could be exactly what's missing.

## What you actually get

The product arrives as a concentrated liquid — typically in a 1-gallon jug, though smaller sizes are available. You're getting a 15-0-15 NPK ratio: 15% nitrogen for green growth, 0% phosphorus, and 15% potash (potassium) for root development and drought tolerance. The "Quality Li" in the name refers to the liquid formulation, which mixes readily with water in a sprayer or watering can.

As shown in the product image, the packaging is straightforward — a plain jug with a clear label. Don't expect fancy measuring tools or a sprayer included. You'll need your own.

## Real-world performance

This is where the 15-0-15 earns its keep. Nitrogen drives the green, but potassium is what builds strong cell walls, disease resistance, and root depth. Most lawn foods skimp on potassium — this one delivers it in equal measure to nitrogen.

Users report visible greening within 5-7 days of application, with results lasting 4-6 weeks depending on rainfall and mowing frequency. One reviewer noted, *"My fescue lawn went from patchy yellow to deep green in under a week. I've used Scott's and Milorganite — this outperformed both for the price."*

The zero-phosphorus formulation is a genuine differentiator. If you live near a lake, river, or stream, phosphorus runoff is a real environmental concern. This fertilizer lets you feed your lawn without contributing to algae blooms.

## What buyers consistently praise

- **Versatility**: Works on lawns, garden beds, potted plants, and even as a compost accelerator. One user reported, *"I use it on my vegetable garden and lawn with the same bottle — it's become my only fertilizer."*
- **Cost efficiency**: Diluted properly, the concentrate goes far. A single gallon mixed at the recommended rate covers roughly 3,200 square feet.
- **No burn risk when measured correctly**: Unlike synthetic granular fertilizers, the liquid formula allows precise application, reducing the chance of fertilizer burn.

## Where it falls short

The 15-0-15 ratio assumes your soil already has adequate phosphorus. If you're starting a new lawn from seed or establishing sod, you'll likely need a starter fertilizer with phosphorus. This product won't help with that.

The lack of a measuring cap is a legitimate annoyance. You'll need a separate measuring cup or kitchen scale to dose accurately. Get this wrong and you risk over-fertilizing, which can scorch grass — especially in summer heat. Several reviewers mentioned this exact issue, with one writing, *"Great product but I wish the bottle had markings. I accidentally used double the recommended amount and burned patches of my lawn."*

Also, while the potassium content is solid, some cool-season grass owners in northern climates report needing an additional potassium application in late fall for winter hardiness.

## Who should buy this

- **Established lawn owners** who have been feeding for a year or more and want a balanced N-P-K without phosphorus buildup
- **Eco-conscious gardeners** near waterways who want to minimize runoff
- **Gardeners with multiple needs** — one bottle for lawn, containers, and vegetable beds
- **Homeowners with clay or alkaline soils** that already test high in phosphorus

Skip it if you're seeding a new lawn, have sandy soil that leaches nutrients quickly, or prefer set-and-forget granular applications.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this on a new lawn?**
A: No. New lawns need phosphorus for root establishment. Use a starter fertilizer with a higher middle number (like 10-20-10) for the first two feedings, then switch to this 15-0-15.

**Q: How often should I apply it?**
A: Every 4-6 weeks during the growing season. For cool-season grasses, that means spring through early fall. For warm-season grasses, apply from late spring through summer.

**Q: Is it safe for pets and kids after application?**
A: Yes, once it's watered in and dried — usually 2-4 hours. The liquid formulation is water-soluble and won't leave harmful residues, but keep pets off until the grass is dry.
