If your hot tub water tests low for total alkalinity (below 80 ppm), you're fighting a losing battle with pH bounce, burning eyes, and scale on the heater. The SpaChoice Alkalinity Increaser is a targeted fix that most users report resolves this in a single treatment.

**What you actually get**

The 2.5 lb bucket is straightforward: granular sodium bicarbonate, a clear label with dosing instructions, and a 1-ounce plastic scoop tucked inside. That scoop matters more than you'd think. Most alkalinity products make you guess ounces or use a kitchen spoon; here, one level scoop per 500 gallons raises alkalinity by roughly 10 ppm. For a typical 300-400 gallon spa, that's about two scoops to move from 60 to 100 ppm. The bucket itself is sturdy plastic with a screw-off lid, though it lacks a gasket — more on that below.

**How it performs in real use**

What stands out in the feedback is how quickly it dissolves. Sodium bicarbonate can sit on the water surface in clumps if you're careless. SpaChoice granules are fine enough that several buyers report dumping the full dose in and seeing it clear within minutes. One reviewer noted: *"I added two scoops, ran the jets for five minutes, and my alkalinity went from 50 to 90. No cloudiness at all."* That matches the general pattern: most people see a 20-40 ppm jump within 4-6 hours, and pH stabilizes overnight.

It also plays well with other chemicals. Since it's pure bicarbonate, it won't interact negatively with chlorine, bromine, or shock treatments. A few users mentioned using it weekly as maintenance to prevent pH crash, which is a valid strategy if your source water is soft.

The 4.7-star average across 235 ratings reflects three recurring themes. First, predictability — the dosing chart is accurate, and the scoop makes repeatability easy. The lack of a moisture barrier on the bucket is the most common complaint. If you store it in a humid basement or an outdoor shed, the powder can absorb moisture and harden into chunks. It's still usable — just crush it before adding — but it's an annoyance. Also, at roughly $12-15 per 2.5 lb bucket, it's pricier per ounce than buying plain baking soda from a grocery store. That's the honest math: you're paying for convenience and correct particle size, not magic chemistry. If you're on a tight budget and already have a kitchen scale, generic sodium bicarbonate works fine.

**Frequently asked questions**

**How often should I add alkalinity increaser?**
Check alkalinity weekly. If it drops below 80 ppm, add one scoop per 500 gallons, wait 6 hours, and retest. Most users re-dose every 2-4 weeks depending on bather load and fill water.

**Can I use this with bromine or salt systems?**
Yes. It's pH-neutral bicarbonate and doesn't interfere with any sanitizer type. Salt systems actually benefit from stable alkalinity because it reduces scale on the salt cell.

**Does it raise pH too?**
Slightly, temporarily. Alkalinity and pH move together, so if your pH is already high (above 7.6), aerate the water for a few hours after adding to let excess CO2 off-gas.
