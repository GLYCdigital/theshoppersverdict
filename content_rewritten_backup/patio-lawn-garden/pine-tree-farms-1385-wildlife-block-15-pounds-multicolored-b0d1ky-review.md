If you feed backyard birds, you know the struggle: cheap seed mixes get blown away by wind, soaked by rain, or devoured by squirrels within hours. The Pine Tree Farms 1385 Wildlife Block is a different approach — a 15-pound compressed block of seeds, grains, and nuts that’s designed to sit there and take abuse. After digging into the specs, customer feedback, and real-world performance, here’s the full picture.

## What You Actually Get

This is a single, solid block weighing 15 pounds. It’s not a bag of loose seed. The block is pressed together with a binder that holds sunflower seeds, millet, cracked corn, and other mixed grains. The “multicolored” in the name refers to the varied seed types visible on the surface — you’ll see black oil sunflower, white millet, red milo, and yellow corn pieces.

As shown in the product image, it’s roughly the size of a large brick or small cinder block. You don’t need a special feeder — just place it on a flat surface like a deck railing, a platform feeder, or even a sturdy tree stump. Some folks hang it in a mesh bag, but the block’s shape makes it easier to set flat.

## Real-World Performance

The biggest selling point is longevity. Multiple reviewers note that a single block lasts several weeks even with heavy bird traffic. One buyer said, *“I put this out in late November and it’s still going in mid-January. The birds love it, and I’ve seen woodpeckers, chickadees, nuthatches, and even a pair of cardinals.”* That’s the kind of staying power you don’t get with loose seed.

The block’s density also means wind doesn’t scatter it. Rain and snow will soften the outer layer, but the interior stays dry and crumbly — birds can peck at it easily. However, if you leave it directly on the ground, expect it to turn into a muddy mess. One reviewer warned, *“It’s great on a platform, but I made the mistake of putting it on the dirt and it got gross fast.”* Use a flat feeder or a board.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 220 ratings (4.4 stars), the recurring positives are:

- **Durability:** It doesn’t blow away, doesn’t spill, and doesn’t need refilling daily.
- **Wildlife variety:** Beyond birds, many report squirrels, chipmunks, and even deer stopping by. If you want a “nature show” block, this delivers.
- **Value per pound:** At 15 pounds, it’s competitively priced compared to premium loose mixes, and you waste less due to spillage.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest — no product is perfect. The block’s main weaknesses:

- **Squirrel warfare:** If you’re trying to *exclude* squirrels, this isn’t the product for you. It’s basically a squirrel buffet. One reviewer joked, *“The squirrels treat it like a gym and a restaurant combined.”*
- **Mess on ground:** As mentioned, ground placement leads to mud and waste. It’s a platform-feeder product, full stop.
- **Shape limitations:** It’s not designed for tube feeders or hoppers. If you only have hanging feeders, you’ll need to rig something up or crumble it (which defeats the purpose).

## Who Should Buy This

- **Backyard birders who want low maintenance:** Set it once, refill monthly. Perfect for busy people.
- **People with platform feeders or large flat surfaces:** This is the ideal setup.
- **Those who enjoy watching a variety of wildlife:** If you’re fine with squirrels and occasional raccoons, this is entertainment plus feeding.

**Skip it if:** You only have hanging tube feeders, you hate squirrels, or you need a mess-free option for a balcony where seed hulls might be an issue.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does one block actually last?**  
A: Most buyers report 2–4 weeks depending on bird traffic and how many squirrels visit. In heavy winter feeding areas, expect closer to 2 weeks.

**Q: Can I use this in a regular hopper feeder?**  
A: No — it’s too large and rigid. Use a platform feeder, a large tray, or place it on a flat stump or railing.

**Q: Does it attract deer?**  
A: In rural or suburban areas, yes. The corn and grain content is attractive to deer. If you don’t want deer, keep it closer to the house.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a set-and-forget bird feeding solution that survives weather and lasts for weeks. It’s a solid value for the price per pound, and the wildlife variety is a bonus.

**Skip it if** you need something for hanging feeders, or if squirrels are a dealbreaker.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A durable, effective block with a few placement constraints. Worth the money for the right setup.
