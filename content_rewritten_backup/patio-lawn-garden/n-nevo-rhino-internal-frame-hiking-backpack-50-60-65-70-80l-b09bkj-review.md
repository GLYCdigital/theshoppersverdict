**The $80 pack that punches above its weight — until you load it past 40 pounds.**

You've seen the Amazon listings: internal frame, 50 to 80 liters, $60–$90. They all blur together. The N NEVO RHINO stands out for one specific reason — the torso adjustment system actually works. Most budget packs give you a fixed frame and pray you're average height. This one has a velcro ladder that slides up and down, giving you a 15-to-21-inch range. That's the difference between a pack that rides your shoulders like a sack of potatoes and one that transfers weight to your hips where it belongs.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the pack, a detachable rain cover, and a sternum strap with a built-in whistle. No surprises, no useless accessories. The 65L version I tested weighs 3.9 pounds empty — heavier than a Hyperlite Mountain Gear but reasonable for the price class. The fabric is 600D polyester with a water-resistant coating; it's not seam-sealed, which is why the rain cover exists. Use it. The cover fits snugly over the main body and cinches down.

The layout is straightforward: a main top-loading compartment with a drawstring closure, a floating lid with two zippered pockets, a front horseshoe zipper that opens the main body for packing-cube access, and a bottom sleeping bag compartment with an internal divider. Two stretch-mesh side pockets, one zippered hip belt pocket per side, and a daisy chain system on the front for lashing gear.

**How it performs on trail**

I loaded it with 35 pounds for a three-day section hike — tent, sleeping bag, cook kit, food, 3 liters of water. The frame is aluminum stays with a polyethylene back panel. It's not a trampoline-style suspension, so your back will sweat. But the airflow channels do help; it's noticeably cooler than a flat foam panel.

The load transfer is where this pack shines at this price. Crank the load lifter straps (the ones that angle from the top of the shoulder straps to the frame), tighten the hip belt, and the weight settles onto your iliac crest. I did 11 miles the first day and my shoulders were fine. My hips were sore by evening — the padding is about 5mm thick, enough for civilian loads, not enough for expedition loads.

The front horseshoe zipper is genuinely useful. I could reach my rain jacket and puffy without unpacking everything. The bottom sleeping bag compartment has its own zipper, so you can grab your bag at camp without disturbing the main load. These are features you'd expect on a $200 pack.

**What 225 buyers consistently say**

The 4.5-star rating holds up in the review text. A verified buyer with 47 helpful votes wrote: *"Took this on a 6-day Philmont trek. Carried 42 lbs day one, no frame failure, no seam issues. The torso adjustment was a lifesaver for my 5'4" frame."* Another reviewer noted: *"The rain cover is the best part — it actually stays on in wind, unlike my old Osprey's."* Multiple buyers mention the size runs true to liter capacity, which is rare for budget packs.

**Where it falls short**

The hip belt padding is the weak point. At 40+ pounds, the pressure points make themselves known by mile 8. If you're planning a thru-hike or carrying winter gear, budget for a better pack or add a padded hip belt cover. The side pockets are also tight — a 32oz Nalgene fits but requires a contortionist move to retrieve while walking. And the included rain cover, while effective, adds bulk to the bottom pocket; it's not a packable afterthought.

**Who should buy this**

This is the right pack for the weekend warrior who hikes 2-4 times a month, carries 25–35 pounds, and wants a do-everything bag for camping, travel, and occasional overnights. It's also a strong pick for high school or college students on a budget who need something that fits a range of torsos (growing kids, shared gear). It's not for ultralight enthusiasts, heavy expedition loads over 40 pounds, or anyone who expects Osprey-level hip belt comfort at this price.

**FAQ**

**Is the 80L version too big for a first backpack?** Yes, unless you're carrying gear for two or doing a 10-day resupply. The 65L is the sweet spot for most — it compresses well with the side straps when you don't fill it.

**Will this fit my torso?** Measure from your C7 vertebra (the bump at the base of your neck) to your iliac crest. If that's between 15 and 21 inches, you're covered. This is the rare budget pack that accommodates both 5'2" and 6'4" hikers.

**Does the rain cover stay attached in a storm?** Yes — it clips to the pack body at four points and cinches under the lid. One reviewer reported a full day of rain on the Appalachian Trail with no water intrusion.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, adjustable internal-frame pack under $100 that handles 20–40 pound loads without drama. **Skip it if** you're planning a thru-hike with heavy gear, need plush hip belt comfort for 15+ mile days, or demand ultralight weight.

The N NEVO RHINO doesn't reinvent the wheel — it just gives you a well-balanced one at a price that leaves room for better boots. That's a fair trade.
