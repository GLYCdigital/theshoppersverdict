## The Pasta Snob's Short-Cut Rigatoni — But Does It Deliver?

Let's be honest: most boxed pasta is a delivery vehicle for sauce. De Cecco's Mezzi Rigatoni tries to be more — a textured, artisanal-feeling noodle that actually holds onto what you put on it. At 4.8 stars from 240 Amazon ratings, someone's clearly impressed. But is this 16-ounce box worth the premium over your store brand? I tested it against a few heavy hitters to find out.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get one 16-ounce box of durum wheat semolina pasta. That's it — no fancy packaging, no recipe cards, no gimmicks. The pasta itself is the story here. These are short, ridged tubes roughly 1.5 inches long, cut on bronze dies. That bronze extrusion is the key differentiator: it leaves a microscopically rough surface that sauce clings to like it's afraid of falling off. As shown in the product image, the ridges are pronounced and the cut is clean — this is clearly a step above mass-produced elbow macaroni.

## Performance in the Kitchen

I cooked these in heavily salted water for the recommended 11 minutes, then a second batch at 13 to test tolerance. The 11-minute cook gives you a proper al dente bite — firm through the center, no chalkiness. At 13 minutes, they soften but don't disintegrate into the starchy mess you'd get from cheaper brands. That's real structural integrity.

The sauce grip is where these shine. I tested with a chunky bolognese and a lighter olive oil-based toss. The bolognese clung to the ridges and interior walls noticeably better than standard rigatoni. The olive oil version needed the reserved pasta water trick — these don't release as much starch as some Italian imports, so you'll want to save a cup before draining to build an emulsion.

One thing to flag: the "mezzi" size. At half the length of standard rigatoni, these feel different in a baked dish or a fork-twirling scenario. They work beautifully in soups or one-pot meals where short pasta excels, but if you're expecting the classic long tube experience, adjust your expectations.

## What Buyers Are Saying

The 240 ratings at 4.8 stars aren't accidental. One buyer noted, "These are the only rigatoni that actually hold a heavy cream sauce without it pooling at the bottom of the bowl." Another praised the texture: "You can feel the roughness on your tongue — it's like the difference between a filet mignon and a cheap steak." The recurring theme is sauce adherence and texture — people aren't buying this for convenience, they're buying it for the mouthfeel.

## Where It Falls Short

Honestly, the drawbacks are more about expectations than execution. The short size genuinely won't work for every recipe — if you're layering a classic baked ziti-style dish, these tubes are too small to create those satisfying cheese-filled pockets. The salt requirement is also a real consideration: De Cecco's recipe needs more aggressive seasoning than typical American pastas to hit its flavor potential. And at roughly 50 cents more per box than Barilla, you're paying for a subtle upgrade that some palates won't notice.

## Who Should Buy This

This is for the home cook who's tired of sauce sliding off smooth noodles. If you're making ragù, bolognese, or anything with a chunky, meaty texture, these are worth the premium. They're also ideal for pasta salads where you want dressing to cling to every nook. Skip them if you're making simple buttered noodles for picky kids, or if you need standard-length tubes for baked pasta dishes.
