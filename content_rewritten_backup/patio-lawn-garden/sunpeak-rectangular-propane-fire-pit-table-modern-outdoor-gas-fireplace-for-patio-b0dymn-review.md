The SunPeak rectangular propane fire pit table doesn't try to be the cheapest option on Amazon. It tries to be the one you don't replace in three years. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's mostly succeeding. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What you actually get

The box contains the fire pit table body (pre-assembled), the burner pan with 5 feet of stainless steel burner, a lava rock set, a 10-foot hose with regulator, and the hardware kit. The cement board top arrives attached — that's the dark, slate-textured surface you see in the product image. You'll need a standard 20-pound propane tank, not included. Assembly takes about 30 minutes if you have a second person to help flip the unit upright.

## Performance and build quality

The headline feature is the 48,000 BTU output from the rectangular burner. That's a meaningful step up from the typical 30,000–40,000 BTU round tables. On a cool evening, you feel the difference — the rectangular shape throws heat across a longer seating area rather than in a circle. One buyer put it plainly: "This thing puts out serious heat. We sat around it in 45-degree weather with sweatshirts and were comfortable."

The burner pan is full stainless steel, not the painted mild steel you'll find on cheaper units. The piezo ignition sparks reliably — though a few reviewers noted they still use a lighter on windy nights, which is common across all fire pit tables. The flame adjustment is smooth from a low whisper flame up to a roaring 5-foot column.

The table surface is a cement board composite that handles heat well — you can rest a drink on the edge while the fire burns. The powder-coated frame is solid, and the whole unit feels planted at 68 pounds. No wobble, no flex in the legs.

## What buyers consistently praise

The aesthetics get repeated mention. Multiple reviewers called out the clean, modern look — the rectangular profile, the hidden control knob behind a magnetic door, and the way it doesn't scream "generic patio furniture." One verified buyer wrote: "We got this to replace a round table that looked like every other fire pit on the block. The rectangular shape and dark top make it look like actual furniture, not a propane appliance."

Heat output and flame visibility are the other frequent compliments. The glass wind guard — which is attached to the table, not loose — keeps the flame dancing even in a breeze while protecting it from sudden gusts.

## Where it falls short

The propane tank placement is the biggest design compromise. It sits on an open side shelf, exposed to view and weather. On a covered patio, fine. But if your setup is fully exposed, you'll want to consider a tank cover. The shelf does keep the tank off the ground and accessible — no digging behind a cabinet door — but it's not hidden.

The weight is real. At 68 pounds empty, this is not a "drag it to the shed for winter" unit. You'll want a cover and a permanent spot. Some buyers also mentioned the lava rock included is a bit sparse — you may want an extra bag to fully cover the burner.

## Who should buy this

- **The patio entertainer** who hosts longer gatherings and needs heat across a seating area, not just a small circle
- **The design-conscious buyer** who wants a fire feature that looks like furniture, not a propane tank with flames
- **The quality-first shopper** who'd rather spend a little more now than replace a rusted-out cheap unit in two years

Skip it if you need a lightweight portable unit, or if your space demands a fully enclosed tank cabinet.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the ignition work reliably every time?**
Mostly yes. The piezo spark ignites on the first or second click. A few reviewers noted occasional misses on very windy days — keep a long lighter handy as a backup.

**Q: How long does a 20-pound tank last on high?**
At full output, roughly 8–10 hours. On low, you can stretch it to 15+ hours. That's typical for this BTU range.

**Q: Can you use it under a covered patio or pergola?**
Yes, with clearance. The manual specifies at least 18 inches of clearance above the burner and proper ventilation. Don't use it in an enclosed space.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a substantial, good-looking fire pit table that actually puts out heat and will survive multiple seasons outdoors. The build quality and heat output justify the price.

**Skip it if** you need portability, a hidden tank, or you're on a tight budget — there are cheaper round tables that'll do the job, just not as well.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A strong, well-built fire pit table with minor compromises that don't overshadow its performance.
