If you've ever watched a flag wrap itself around a pole because the bracket forced it flat against the house, you know the frustration. The BONWIN 1" Flag Pole Holder Brackets solve that with a simple but effective design: two fixed positions that let you angle the pole away from your wall. It's a small product, but it does exactly what it promises — and does it well enough to earn a 4.7-star average from 235 Amazon shoppers.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains two identical flag pole holder brackets, each rated for a standard 1-inch diameter pole. The steel construction is stout — this isn't a flimsy stamped-metal accessory. The powder-coated black finish looks clean against most exteriors and holds up against UV and moisture. Each bracket has pre-drilled holes and a set screw that locks the pole in place once it's seated. The two-position design is the headline feature: you can mount the bracket so the pole sits at 45 degrees or 90 degrees relative to the wall, giving you flexibility depending on whether you're mounting to a flat wall, under an eave, or on a porch column.

**Real-World Performance**

You feel the quality the moment you handle these. The set screw bites into the pole firmly, and once tightened, there's zero play. One reviewer noted, "I put these up in October and they survived a winter of snow, ice, and 50 mph gusts. The flag never slipped and the brackets didn't budge." That tracks with the material spec — the steel is thick enough that you won't worry about flex or fatigue over time.

The 90-degree position is the clear winner for most installations. It holds the pole perpendicular to the wall, keeping your flag fully extended and visible. The 45-degree option is more of a niche use — it works well under low eaves where a straight-out mount would hit a gutter, but be aware that the flag will sit closer to the wall and may rub against siding in moderate wind. That's not a defect; it's geometry. If your mounting surface is flush, use 90 degrees.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme across the 235 ratings is the build quality. Shoppers repeatedly mention how solid these brackets feel compared to cheaper alternatives. "I've bought three different brands of flag pole holders over the years, and these are the first ones that don't look like they'll bend in a strong breeze," wrote one verified buyer. **Where It Falls Short**

The honest downsides are few but worth noting. First, no mounting hardware ships in the box. That means a trip to the hardware store for masonry anchors if you're drilling into brick or stucco, or appropriate wood screws for siding. It's a minor annoyance, but at this price point, including basic fasteners would be a welcome addition.

Second, the set screw is the only thing holding the pole vertically. If you're using a heavier flag or a pole longer than 6 feet, the screw can loosen over time from vibration. A drop of thread-locker or a check every few weeks solves it, but it's worth knowing.

Third, the 45-degree position has a practical limitation beyond the siding-rub issue: it reduces the effective clearance for the flag's bottom edge. If you're mounting low (like on a porch rail), you may find the flag dragging closer to the ground than you'd like.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the homeowner who wants a permanent, low-maintenance flag mount and doesn't want to overthink it. It's ideal if you have a standard 1-inch aluminum or fiberglass pole and a flat wall surface — brick, vinyl, wood, or stucco. It's also a strong choice if you need an angled mount under an eave, since the 45-degree option is rare at this quality level. Skip it if you're using a pole larger than 1 inch (you'll need a different bracket) or if you expect tool-free installation.

**FAQ**

**Will this fit a fiberglass flag pole?**
Yes. The 1-inch interior diameter fits standard fiberglass and aluminum poles. Just tighten the set screw firmly — fiberglass can compress slightly, so re-check the screw after the first week.

**Can I mount this horizontally on a fence post?**
Technically yes, but the design isn't optimized for it. The set screw holds the pole perpendicular to the bracket's base, so a horizontal mount would leave the flag parallel to the ground. Stick with vertical wall or post mounting.

**How do I keep the set screw from loosening?**
Apply a small amount of blue thread-locker (Loctite 242) to the screw threads before installation. That's the standard fix and lasts through a full season.
