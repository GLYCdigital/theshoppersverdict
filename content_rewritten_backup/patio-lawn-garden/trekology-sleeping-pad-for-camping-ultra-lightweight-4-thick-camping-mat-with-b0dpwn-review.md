**A 4-inch thick camping pad that doesn't weigh like one — but there's a catch.**

Most sleeping pads force you to choose between "actually comfortable" and "light enough to carry." The TREKOLOGY 4-inch pad tries to split that difference, and based on 215 Amazon ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's landing reasonably well. As shown in the product image, this is a generously thick air mattress-style pad with horizontal baffles, not the mummy-tapered minimalist mats you'd expect at this weight class.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains the pad itself, a small repair kit, and a stuff sack. That's it — no pump bag, no pillow, no inflation sack. The built-in foot pump is integrated into the pad's base, which is either clever engineering or a gimmick depending on your patience level. It's a 4-inch thick pad when fully inflated, measures roughly 76 x 25 inches (standard rectangular, not tapered), and the ripstop nylon shell feels substantial for the price point.

The foot pump is the headline feature. You step on the integrated pump chamber repeatedly to force air in — no bending over, no separate pump bag to misplace. It works, but it takes about 40-50 stomps to reach firmness. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about the foot pump but it filled the pad in about two minutes. My back didn't hurt the next morning, which is more than I can say for my old foam pad."

**How it actually performs**

The 4-inch thickness is the real differentiator here. Most pads in this weight range (under 2 pounds) top out at 2.5 to 3 inches. Going to 4 inches means side sleepers won't feel the ground through their hip or shoulder — the most common complaint with thinner pads. The horizontal baffles keep the air distributed evenly, so you don't get the "balloon effect" where your weight pushes all the air to your feet.

The face fabric is a 40-denier ripstop nylon. That's not ultralight territory, but it's more durable than the 20-denier fabrics on premium pads. On rocky or pine-needle-covered ground, this matters. Multiple reviewers mentioned using it on gravel without issues, though the included patch kit suggests TREKOLOGY knows punctures happen.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Comfort is the recurring theme in positive reviews. "Best sleep I've had camping in years," one 5-star review reads. "I'm a 200-pound side sleeper and I didn't touch the ground once." Another buyer praised the inflation system: "No more huffing and puffing into a tiny valve. The foot pump is genius."

The weight — listed at 1.9 pounds — also gets consistent mentions. For a 4-inch thick pad, that's genuinely competitive. Most comparable thickness pads run 2.5 pounds or more.

**Where it falls short**

The packed size is the biggest tradeoff. At roughly 11 x 5 inches rolled up, it's noticeably bulkier than a Therm-a-Rest NeoAir or similar ultralight options. If you're packing for multi-day backpacking with a 40-liter pack, this will eat significant space.

The R-value being unlisted is a real omission. TREKOLOGY doesn't publish a thermal resistance rating, which makes it hard to trust this pad for shoulder-season camping. Based on the materials and lack of reflective insulation, treat this as a 3-season pad. You'll want a foam layer underneath if temps drop below 40°F.

The foot pump, while clever, has a learning curve. You need to keep the pad partially inflated while stepping, and if you over-inflate, releasing air requires fumbling with the valve rather than the precise micro-adjustments a pump sack allows.

**Who should buy this**

This pad is ideal for car campers, festival-goers, and weekend backpackers who prioritize sleep quality over shaving ounces. It's also a strong choice for taller side sleepers — the rectangular shape gives more shoulder room than tapered pads. Skip it if you're a thru-hiker counting every gram or if you winter camp in genuinely cold conditions.

**FAQ**

*Does the foot pump actually work, or is it a gimmick?* It works, but it requires 40-50 firm stomps. You'll get a full inflation in about two minutes. It's not faster than a pump sack, but it's one less thing to carry.

*Is this pad good for side sleepers?* Yes — the 4-inch thickness prevents hip and shoulder contact with the ground, which is the primary complaint with thinner pads.

*Can you use this with a regular sleeping bag?* Yes, the rectangular shape works with any bag. The 25-inch width is standard.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely thick, comfortable sleeping surface without carrying a car-camping behemoth. The 4-inch loft at under 2 pounds is a solid value proposition.

**Skip it if** you're an ultralight backpacker who needs minimal packed volume or if you camp in freezing conditions and need a verified R-value.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A comfortable, well-built pad with a clever inflation system. The packed size and unlisted R-value keep it from a perfect score, but for most campers, this is a very easy recommendation.
