If you've killed more basil plants than you'd care to admit, this planter won't fix your watering habits. But it will fix the layout problem. The 5 Herb Garden Planter Indoor with Wooden Box splits your countertop growing into five dedicated compartments, so you can stop cramming rosemary, thyme, and cilantro into one overcrowded pot.

## What You Actually Get

The kit arrives as a solid wood box — no particleboard, no MDF — with five equal sections divided by removable slats. As shown in the product image, the natural wood finish looks more like a piece of kitchen decor than a gardening tool. You get the planter itself, the dividers, and a set of plastic labels. No seeds, no soil, no drainage tray. That last omission is worth noting before you click buy.

The box measures roughly 16 inches long and 5 inches deep — compact enough for a windowsill or narrow counter. Each section holds about the equivalent of one 4-inch nursery pot of soil, so you're growing starter herbs, not a full farm.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The wood is the standout feature. It's thick enough that the box doesn't flex when you lift it with soil inside. The dividers slide in and out without catching, which makes it easy to harvest roots or replace a failed plant without dumping the whole box. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "Finally a planter that doesn't look like plastic junk on my counter."

Drainage is where things get complicated. The bottom has small holes, which is good for root health, but there's no matching tray. Another reviewer noted, "I put a baking sheet under mine because water drips through every time I water." That's workable, but it's an extra step that should have been solved in the box.

The wood's interior is unsealed. If you're disciplined about watering, that's fine. If you tend to overwater — and most indoor herb growers do — moisture will sit against the raw wood and can cause staining or warping over months of use. A $5 can of clear sealant fixes this, but you shouldn't have to modify a product on day one.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The five-section design earns the most positive feedback. Being able to grow different herbs side by side without root entanglement is a genuine advantage, and owners mention it repeatedly. One wrote, "I can actually keep mint alive without it choking out everything else." Another appreciated the look: "It matches my kitchen aesthetic and my plants are thriving."

The removable dividers also get consistent mentions. They make planting, thinning, and cleaning much easier than a fixed one-piece planter.

## Where It Falls Short

The missing drainage tray is the biggest miss. It's a minor cost to include, and its absence forces buyers to improvise or risk water damage on surfaces. The unsealed interior is a related concern — this planter demands a light touch with the watering can. And despite the product photos suggesting generous space, each section is modest. If you're planning to grow a full basil bush or sprawling rosemary, you'll outgrow this box within a month or two.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you want an attractive, organized way to grow three to five culinary herbs on a counter or windowsill, and you're confident you can water moderately. It's ideal for apartment dwellers, small-kitchen cooks, or anyone who wants a giftable, non-plastic-looking planter for a friend who loves cooking.

**Skip it if** you're a heavy-watering beginner, want to grow mature plants to full size, or expect everything you need to start included in the box. You'll need to budget for soil, seeds, and a drainage tray.

## FAQ

**Does this planter come with drainage holes?**
Yes, the bottom has holes, but no tray is included. You'll need to place it over a saucer, baking sheet, or similar catch basin.

**Can I keep it outdoors?**
The wood is not weather-sealed. A covered porch is fine, but direct rain or full sun exposure will degrade the box quickly. Keep it indoors or in a sheltered spot.

**What size pots fit in each section?**
Each compartment fits approximately one 4-inch nursery pot worth of soil. That's suitable for small to medium herb starts, not large mature plants.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The 5 Herb Garden Planter Indoor earns its positive reputation through solid wood construction and a genuinely useful partitioned design. It's not a complete kit, and the missing tray is a real oversight. But for the price, you're getting a durable, attractive planter that solves the "everything in one pot" problem cleanly.

**Buy it if** you want organized, attractive countertop herbs and don't mind a little DIY setup. **Skip it if** you need a truly beginner-proof setup or full-size plant capacity. This one's for cooks who care about both their basil and their kitchen aesthetic.
