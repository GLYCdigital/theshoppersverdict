If you've ever tried starting seeds indoors without a humidity dome, you know the drill: crusty soil, uneven germination, and seedlings that stretch into pale, leggy sticks. The Burpee Indoor Greenhouse Kit solves the humidity problem with a simple, no-frills design—a black tray, a clear plastic dome, and 36 compressed peat pellets. It won't win any design awards, but it works, and at this price point, that's what matters.

**What's actually in the box**  
You get the black plastic base tray with molded cell dividers, a clear vented dome, and 36 Burpee-branded peat pellets. No seed packets, no labels, no heating mat—just the hardware. The pellets are the real standout: drop them in warm water, and they expand to about 1.5 inches tall in under 10 minutes. They fit the tray's cells perfectly, which means you won't be wrestling with loose soil or hunting for pots.

**How it performs in the real world**  
I tested this with basil, marigolds, and cherry tomatoes. The dome's seal is genuinely tight—it holds condensation on the walls, keeping the pellet tops moist without you touching them for the first week. Ventilation slits on the dome let you dial back humidity as seedlings emerge, which is a thoughtful touch. The tray itself is shallow, so roots hit the bottom fast; you'll need to transplant to larger pots within 3–4 weeks for anything beyond herbs.

The plastic is the weak link. It's thin—moldable with finger pressure—and multiple Amazon reviewers report cracks if the tray is lifted by the edges when full. One user wrote, *"The tray cracked on the second use when I moved it to a sunnier windowsill."* Treat it gently and it'll last a season or two, but don't expect heirloom durability.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The value proposition is the headline. With 215 ratings at 4.3 stars, the chorus is consistent: it's cheap, it works, and the pellets are high-quality. A verified buyer noted, *"I've tried peat pots and cell packs—these pellets are far easier and the roots grow right through the mesh."* Another praised the dome's effectiveness: *"Germination went from 50% to 90% just by keeping the dome on for the first week."* For beginners, the simplicity is a major plus—no guesswork on soil mix or watering schedules.

**Where it falls short**  
The biggest functional gap is the lack of a bottom-watering system. You must water from the top, which can dislodge fine seeds like lettuce or petunia. The included pellets also have no added fertilizer—seedlings will yellow by week three if you don't start feeding with a diluted liquid fertilizer. And the 36-cell capacity is fine for a windowsill herb garden, but if you're planning a vegetable plot, you'll need two or three of these or a larger tray system.

One more nitpick: the dome has no adjustable vent dials—just fixed slits. It works, but you can't fine-tune airflow, which matters if you're in a dry climate or a humid bathroom.

**Who should buy this**  
- **Beginner gardeners** who want a foolproof, low-cost introduction to seed starting.  
- **Herb lovers** growing basil, cilantro, or dill on a sunny kitchen counter.  
- **Teachers or parents** doing a classroom or home science project—the clear dome makes germination visible.  
- **Skip it if** you're a serious vegetable gardener starting dozens of tomatoes and peppers; you'll outgrow this in one season and need a larger, sturdier system with bottom watering.

**FAQ**  

*Can I reuse the peat pellets?*  
No—they break down after one use. But you can buy replacement pellets separately (Burpee sells them) and reuse the tray and dome.

*Do I need a heat mat?*  
Not for herbs like basil or lettuce. For tomatoes and peppers, a heat mat will speed germination, but it's not mandatory—just slower (7–14 days instead of 5–7).

*How long do seedlings stay in the tray?*  
About 2–4 weeks, until they have two sets of true leaves. Then transplant to 3-inch pots; the roots will be dense by then.

**The Verdict**  
The Burpee Greenhouse Indoor Kit is a solid 4.2 out of 5. It's not built like a tank, and it won't scale to a market garden, but for $20-ish (check current pricing), it delivers exactly what it promises: reliable humidity, decent pellets, and a forgiving learning curve.  

Buy it if you're new to seed starting or just want fresh herbs without the grocery store markup. Skip it if you need a long-term, heavy-duty system—you'll be replacing this within a year or two. For the price, it's hard to beat.
