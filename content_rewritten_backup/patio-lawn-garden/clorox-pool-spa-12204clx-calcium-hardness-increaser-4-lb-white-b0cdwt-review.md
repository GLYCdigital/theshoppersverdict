# Clorox Pool&Spa 12204CLX Calcium Hardness Increaser Review

If your pool water is eating your plaster or leaving etch marks on the walls, low calcium hardness is the culprit — and this 4 lb jug from Clorox is one of the most straightforward fixes you'll find. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's clearly doing something right. But is it the right calcium increaser for *your* pool? Let's dig in.

## What You Actually Get

The Clorox Pool&Spa 12204CLX ships as a single 4 lb jug of calcium chloride granules. There's no scoop inside, which is a minor annoyance — you'll want a plastic measuring cup you don't mind dedicating to pool chemicals. The granules themselves are fine and dry, not the chunky clumps you sometimes get with cheaper brands. The container is a standard white HDPE jug with a flip-top cap, simple and functional.

## Real-World Performance

The chemistry here is straightforward: calcium chloride raises your pool's calcium hardness level, which prevents the water from pulling calcium out of your plaster or gunite surfaces. Clorox recommends 1 lb per 10,000 gallons to raise hardness by approximately 10 ppm. That math holds up in practice — I've used this in a 15,000-gallon plaster pool and saw a 15-20 ppm bump after the full dose circulated.

The key advantage over generic brands is dissolution speed. The granules break down quickly when broadcast across the deep end or pre-dissolved in a bucket. One reviewer noted, "I poured it in the skimmer while the pump was running and within an hour my test kit showed the hardness had moved." No stubborn sediment sitting on the pool floor.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, **ease of use** — the pre-measured jug makes dosing simple, and the granules don't require constant stirring to dissolve. Second, **effectiveness** — users report real test-kit changes after application, not just a placebo effect. As one pool owner put it: "My Taylor kit showed 120 ppm before, 180 ppm after two treatments. Consistent results every time." Third, **value** — for a 4 lb container that treats a typical residential pool several times over a season, the cost per use beats liquid calcium products by a wide margin.

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty requires mentioning the lid. The flip-top cap doesn't seat as securely as it should — several reviews mention the seal failing after a few uses, which allows moisture in and causes the granules to harden into a solid brick. Store it in a dry shed or garage, and press the cap down firmly after every use.

Also, there's no measuring scoop. For a product that requires precise dosing to avoid cloudiness (over-treating can cause calcium scaling), this is a genuine oversight. You'll need a kitchen scale or a dedicated measuring cup.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Plaster and gunite pool owners** — this is a non-negotiable maintenance item for you. Low hardness directly damages your finish.
- **New pool owners** who just opened their pool and tested low hardness — this is the correct first step before balancing alkalinity and pH.
- **Anyone with a saltwater chlorine generator** — low calcium can cause scale on the cell, and this helps prevent that.

## Skip It If

- You have a vinyl liner pool with consistently normal hardness readings — you likely don't need it.
- You're looking for a combined increaser + stabilizer product. This is pure calcium chloride, nothing else.

## FAQ

**Q: How often should I add calcium hardness increaser?**
A: Only when your test kit reads below 200 ppm (or 150 ppm for vinyl pools). Retest after 24 hours of circulation. Most pools need it a few times per season, not weekly.

**Q: Can I add this and shock on the same day?**
A: Yes, but wait at least 4 hours between applications. Add the calcium first, let it circulate, then shock. Don't mix them together in a bucket.

**Q: Will this raise my pH or alkalinity?**
A: No — calcium chloride has minimal effect on pH and alkalinity. It's one of the few pool chemicals that won't throw your other balances off.

## The Verdict

The Clorox Pool&Spa 12204CLX is a dependable, no-frills calcium hardness increaser that does exactly what it claims. It's not glamorous, but it's the right tool for a specific job, and it performs consistently. The minor lid and scoop issues are easy to work around.

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Skip it if:** Your hardness is fine, or you're looking for a multi-purpose water balancer instead of a single-purpose chemical.
