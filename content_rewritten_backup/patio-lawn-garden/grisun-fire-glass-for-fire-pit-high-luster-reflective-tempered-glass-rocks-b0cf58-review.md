## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the math. A 10-pound bag looks substantial until you realize fire glass is measured by depth, not weight. For a standard 36-inch round fire pit, you need 2 inches of depth minimum — that's roughly 30-40 pounds of glass. At roughly 3-4 bags, the cost adds up quickly. One reviewer warned: "I bought one bag thinking it would do the job. It covered maybe a third of my pit. Budget for more."

Handling is another consideration. These are sharp, tempered glass chunks — not smooth river stones. Wear heavy gloves during installation or you'll be picking slivers out of your hands for a week. This is inherent to the product category, but worth flagging.

## FAQ

**Q: How much glass do I need for my fire pit?**  
A: Measure your pit's diameter and depth. Multiply length × width × desired depth (2 inches is recommended) in inches, then divide by 231 to get gallons. Each gallon requires about 13 pounds of glass. For a 36-inch round pit, that's roughly 35-40 pounds.

**Q: Can I use this with a wood-burning fire pit?**  
A: Technically yes, but practically no. Wood burns produce soot that coats the glass and dulls the reflective finish. This is designed for gas or propane burners.

**Q: Will it crack or explode?**  
A: No. Tempered glass is heat-treated specifically to withstand high temperatures and thermal shock. It's the same safety glass used in car windows. Safe up to 1,400°F.
