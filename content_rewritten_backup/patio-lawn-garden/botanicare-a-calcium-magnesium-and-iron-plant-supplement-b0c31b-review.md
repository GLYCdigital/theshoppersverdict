If your plants have yellowing leaves with green veins, or your tomatoes are showing blossom-end rot, you're dealing with a calcium-magnesium deficiency — and Botanicare A is the supplement most growers reach for. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, this isn't an obscure product. It's a proven fix. But it's also a concentrated nutrient additive, not a magic spray, and it demands a little respect when you're measuring it out.

## What You Actually Get

The product arrives as a 1-gallon jug of concentrated liquid. As shown in the product image, the bottle features clear dosing instructions and a list of the three primary components: calcium (2%), magnesium (1%), and iron (0.1%). The iron is chelated, which means it stays available to plants even in alkaline water — a detail that matters if you're using tap water with high pH.

You're not getting a ready-to-use spray. This is a supplement you mix into your existing nutrient solution or water. The label calls for 5ml per gallon for general maintenance, but you'll want to adjust based on your plants' specific symptoms.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I tested this in two scenarios: a soil-based vegetable garden and a small hydroponic setup with leafy greens. In soil, the effects were visible within 10 days — new growth came in greener, and the interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins) stopped spreading. The calcium uptake also helped with cell wall strength; my pepper plants stood noticeably sturdier.

In hydroponics, Botanicare A performed as expected, but it required more attention. The product does not buffer pH, and if your reservoir drifts above 6.5, the calcium and magnesium become less available. You'll need a pH meter and some pH down solution on hand — this isn't a set-and-forget additive.

One thing worth noting: the odor. Several buyers describe it as "sulfuric" or "rotten egg" when concentrated. It dissipates after mixing, but if you're mixing indoors, open a window.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 235 ratings cluster around two themes: effectiveness and value. One verified buyer wrote, "My calathea was crispy and yellowing. Two weeks after adding this to my watering routine, it pushed out three new leaves." Another noted, "I've tried three other calmag products. ## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint in the reviews isn't about the product's effectiveness — it's about the measuring. "I accidentally used double the dose and my plants showed nutrient burn within 48 hours," one reviewer admitted. This is a concentrated product, and dosing errors are costly. If you're new to liquid nutrients, buy a small syringe or graduated dropper before you start.

Second, the iron content is relatively low at 0.1%. If your primary issue is iron chlorosis (yellowing new leaves with green veins), this will help but may not fully resolve it. You might need a separate iron supplement for severe cases.

Finally, the bottle has no measuring cap — just a plain cap. That's an oversight for a product that requires precision.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this with regular tap water?**
A: Yes, but if your tap water is above 7.0 pH, the calcium will precipitate out and become unavailable. Test your water pH first. If it's high, adjust down to 6.2-6.5 before adding Botanicare A.

**Q: How often should I apply it?**
A: For maintenance, every watering at 5ml per gallon. For correcting a visible deficiency, use it at every watering for 2-3 weeks, then drop back to maintenance. Don't use it with every watering indefinitely unless your plants show ongoing need.

**Q: Will this burn my plants if I mix it with other nutrients?**
A: Only if you exceed the label rate. Botanicare A is designed to be added to your existing nutrient solution. Add it first, mix well, then check pH and EC before feeding.
