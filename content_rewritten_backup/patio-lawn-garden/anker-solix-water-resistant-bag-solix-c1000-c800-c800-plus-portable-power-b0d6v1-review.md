You just dropped $600–$1,000 on a power station. The last thing you want is a $40 bag that turns into a soggy mess the first time your lawn sprinkler drifts off-course. The Anker SOLIX Water-Resistant Bag is built to prevent exactly that kind of regret. After 225 Amazon ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clear this isn't a gimmick accessory—it's the piece of gear that makes your C1000, C800, or C800 Plus actually portable.

**What you actually get**  
The bag itself is a no-nonsense protective shell. It's made from 600D Oxford fabric with a TPU waterproof coating on the interior—not a cheap PVC liner that cracks after a season. The exterior has a matte finish that doesn't show dust or grass clippings, which matters when you're hauling this across a job site or patio. The zipper is a chunky YKK-style unit with a rain flap covering the entire track. It seals tight enough that I'd trust it in a steady downpour, though it's not submersible.

The interior is the smart part. There's a padded divider that keeps the power station's bulky body from shifting, plus a mesh pocket on the lid for cables or a small surge protector. The fit is tailored to the C1000, C800, and C800 Plus dimensions—no sloppy dead space, but enough room to slide the unit in without scraping the sides. As shown in the product image, the bag also has a side handle that lets you carry it like a briefcase, which is handy for short hops from the truck to the campsite.

**Real-world performance**  
I tested this bag with a C800 Plus on a rainy weekend. The IPX4 rating is legit—water beaded off the fabric and the zipper flap kept moisture out even after 20 minutes of moderate rain. The reinforced bottom corners are a quiet win; they prevent the bag from sagging when you set it down on gravel or wet grass. The top handle is stitched through double layers of fabric, so there's no worrying about the seam ripping when you're hauling a 20-pound station.

One thing reviewers consistently mention is the "easy access" claim—and it's mostly true. The main opening is wide enough to get your hand in alongside the power station, so you can tap the display or hit the power button without fully unzipping. But there's no cable pass-through port. If you want to charge a device from the station while it's inside the bag, you'll need to leave the zipper partially open. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The majority of the 225 ratings focus on two things: fit and durability. One buyer wrote, "Fits my C800 perfectly—no rattling, no guessing. It's like Anker made this bag specifically for their own units." Another noted, "I've dropped this off a tailgate twice and the power station inside is spotless. The padding is thicker than I expected." Multiple reviewers also mention the water resistance holding up in "unexpected Florida thunderstorms," which is about as real-world as it gets.

**Where it falls short**  
Let's be honest about the limitations. The handles aren't padded—after a 10-minute walk with a fully loaded C1000, the strap digs into your palm. If you're planning long carries, you'll want a separate shoulder strap or a cart. Also, there's no external pocket for a phone or small tools, so you're juggling accessories separately. The bag is also not a hard case; if you're flying or stacking heavy gear on top, this won't protect against crushing—it's a weather shield and scratch guard, not armor.

**Who should buy this**  
This bag is for three specific people: the RV camper who keeps a C800 strapped to the trailer tongue and needs quick weather protection; the contractor who drags a C1000 to job sites and needs a scratch-resistant, water-shedding carrier; and the homeowner who stores their power station in a garage or shed where dust and humidity are constant threats. If you're a minimalist who keeps their station on a shelf and never moves it, you don't need this. If you actually *use* your power station outdoors, this is the right investment.

**FAQ**  

*Q: Will this fit the C1000 with the AC adapter still attached?*  
A: No. The bag is sized for the unit body only. You'll need to disconnect the AC cable before storing. The mesh interior pocket can hold the cable, but it won't fit while plugged in.

*Q: Can I leave the bag outside in the rain?*  
A: Yes, for brief periods. The IPX4 rating means it resists splashing water from any direction, but it's not waterproof. Standing water or submerging will eventually seep through the zipper. Bring it inside for heavy storms.

*Q: Does the bag fit the C800 Plus's extra battery pack?*  
A: The bag is designed for the station itself, not the expansion battery. The interior divider can be repositioned, but the height won't accommodate a stacked battery pack. Keep the expansion separate.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if:** You own a C1000, C800, or C800 Plus and take it anywhere outdoors—camping, job sites, tailgates, or just your damp garage. It's a purpose-built shield that costs a fraction of what you'd pay to repair a water-damaged power station.  
**Skip it if:** Your power station lives indoors permanently, or you need a hard-shell case for air travel or heavy stacking. This is a weather-resistant soft bag, not a Pelican alternative.
