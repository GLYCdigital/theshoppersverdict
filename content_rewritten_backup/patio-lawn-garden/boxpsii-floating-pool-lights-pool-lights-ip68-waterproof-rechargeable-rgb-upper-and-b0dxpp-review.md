So you've got a pool, and you've realized the built-in lighting is either nonexistent or about as exciting as a parking lot. The BOXPSII Floating Pool Lights are the kind of product that promises to turn your backyard into a resort with zero hardwiring. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, they're not perfect — but they're close enough that most buyers are genuinely impressed.

## What you actually get

The set includes four spherical floating lights, each roughly the size of a softball. They're made from a durable ABS plastic shell with a clear polycarbonate lens — not glass, which matters when kids are throwing things at them. Each light houses an internal rechargeable battery, and the kit ships with a charging cable and a small remote control. That's the whole package. No mounting hardware, no base station, no solar panel to babysit.

## Real-world performance

Here's where these earn their keep. The IP68 rating isn't marketing fluff — these things are genuinely sealed. I've seen reviewers submerge them for hours, leave them out in rainstorms, and run them in chlorinated pools all summer. One buyer noted, *"Left mine in the pool for three weeks straight. Still working flawlessly."* That's the kind of feedback that matters.

Battery life lands in the 8–10 hour range on a full charge. That covers a typical evening swim session, but here's the catch: recharging takes 4–5 hours. If you're hosting back-to-back weekend parties, you'll need to rotate sets or accept that the second night starts with a partial charge. Plan accordingly.

The remote gives you 16 solid colors plus several flashing and fading modes. The color rendering is genuinely vibrant — the RGB LEDs punch through murky pool water better than I expected. The "color changing" mode cycles smoothly rather than snapping between shades, which looks premium in person.

## What buyers consistently praise

Looking through the verified reviews, a few themes pop up repeatedly:

- **Build quality exceeds the price point.** Multiple reviewers commented on how solid these feel versus cheaper alternatives that crack or leak within weeks.
- **The remote actually works.** Many budget pool lights require you to manually cycle through colors by twisting the casing. Having a real remote is a genuine convenience upgrade.
- **They're genuinely waterproof.** This shouldn't be remarkable, but at this price point, it is. Reviewers report zero leakage even after extended submersion.

## Where they fall short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The remote range drops to roughly 10–15 feet once the lights are in the water, so you'll want to set your colors before placing them in the pool. That's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

There's no auto-on/off sensor. You're manually switching each light on before tossing it in the water. For four lights, that's four separate switches. Not a big deal, but worth knowing before you buy.

And while the battery life is respectable, it's not industry-leading. Some competitors push 12+ hours, though they typically cost more or lack the IP68 rating. You're trading some runtime for durability here, and I think that's a fair swap.

## Who should buy these

- **Backyard entertainers** who want instant ambience without electrician costs
- **Parents** looking for safe, submersible pool lighting for kids' swim nights
- **Renters** who can't modify permanent pool lighting but want the upgrade anyway

Skip them if you need lights that run all night without attention, or if you refuse to manage charging cycles.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these stay in the pool permanently?**
A: Technically yes — the IP68 seal handles continuous submersion. But battery life means they'll eventually die and need recharging. Remove them every few days to recharge and clean the lens.

**Q: Do they work in saltwater pools?**
A: Yes. Multiple reviewers confirm they hold up fine in saltwater systems, though you should rinse them with fresh water after prolonged exposure to extend the casing lifespan.

**Q: How do the colors look during the day?**
A: They're noticeably dimmer in direct sunlight. These are evening products — they really shine after dusk when the LED output has darkness to work against.
