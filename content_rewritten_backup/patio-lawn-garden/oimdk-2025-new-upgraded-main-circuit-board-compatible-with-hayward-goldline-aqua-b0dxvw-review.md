If your pool pump controller just died, you're looking at a $300–$600 service call or a $120–$180 part you can swap yourself. The **OIMDK 2025 Upgraded Main Circuit Board** sits in that second category, promising a drop-in fix for Hayward and Goldline AquaLogic/Pro Logic systems. With 180 ratings averaging 3.6 stars, it's clearly working for some owners — and frustrating others. Here's the real breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the bare circuit board — no mounting hardware, no standoffs, no thermal compound. The board itself is a fiberglass PCB with clearly labeled terminal strips (that silk-screening is genuinely better than OEM boards, making wire identification much easier). It's designed to replace the main logic board in Hayward Goldline AquaLogic and Pro Logic controllers, including the PS-8, PS-16, and AQL-*-* series.

## Performance and Build Quality

The key upgrade here is the **relay rating**. OEM Hayward boards often fail because the relays overheat and crack their solder joints after a few seasons. The OIMDK board uses relays rated for higher continuous current, which addresses that known failure point. In practice, buyers who got a good board report it fixed their salt cell display, pump control, and heater interface without issue.

But here's where the 3.6-star rating gets earned. A **meaningful minority of buyers received dead-on-arrival boards**. The most common complaints: relay pins with cold solder joints, boards that power up but won't trigger the pump relay, and one instance where a buyer had to reflow solder on three relay connections to get it working. That's not acceptable for a part you're trusting to run a pool pump.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The DIY crowd loves this board when it works. One verified buyer put it plainly: *"Saved me $450 in service fees. My pool guy quoted me a new controller — this board fixed it in 20 minutes with a screwdriver."* Another noted: *"The terminal labels are much clearer than the original — I didn't need the manual at all."* Fitment is also repeatedly confirmed as direct for most AquaLogic models, with no harness modification needed.

## Where It Falls Short

**Quality control is the elephant in the pool.** The DOA rate appears higher than you'd want from a new part. Amazon's return policy covers you, but a failed pool controller in summer means your pump is down while you wait for a replacement. Also, the **compatibility chart is genuinely confusing** — it lists multiple Hayward and Goldline models, but buyers have reported ordering for their specific unit and receiving a board that didn't match. Triple-check your controller's model number (it's on the sticker inside the door) against the listing photos before purchasing.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY pool owners** comfortable with basic electrical work and a multimeter — you'll need to verify continuity on arrival and potentially reflow a solder joint.
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who can tolerate a 10–15% chance of needing a return or a quick solder touch-up.
- **Anyone with steady hands and a soldering iron** — if you can fix a cold joint in five minutes, this board is a steal.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable troubleshooting electronics, or if your controller is still under warranty — go OEM in that case.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my Hayward AquaLogic PS-8?**
A: It's designed for the PS-8 and PS-16 models, but verify the board layout against your unit's photos. If your model has a different relay count, it won't fit.

**Q: Do I need to reprogram anything after installing?**
A: Yes — the board resets to factory defaults. You'll need to re-enter your salt level, pump speed settings, and freeze protection settings. Budget 15 minutes with the manual.

**Q: Is it really plug-and-play?**
A: Electrically yes — the harness connectors match. But physically, you'll need to transfer over your old mounting standoffs and apply fresh thermal paste to the heat sink.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're a hands-on pool owner who wants to save $300+ on a service call and can verify the board on arrival. It's a solid value at roughly half the OEM price — when you get a good unit.

**Skip it if** you want zero-risk reliability or can't troubleshoot a potentially faulty board. For those buyers, the OEM Hayward board is worth the premium.
