This isn't a wetsuit for everyone. But if you're a warm-water surfer, paddleboarder, or kayaker who's been suffering through a stiff, sweaty fullsuit in summer, the O'Neill Reactor-2 is the answer you didn't know you needed. With 225 Amazon ratings holding steady at 4.5 stars, this suit has built a loyal following for one simple reason: it disappears once you're in the water.

## What You Actually Get

The Reactor-2 is a 2mm short-sleeve, short-leg spring suit with a back zip entry. It's built from O'Neill's TechnoButter 3 neoprene — a limestone-based material that's lighter and more flexible than traditional petroleum-based neoprene. The suit weighs next to nothing (about 1.5 pounds) and packs down small enough to toss in a day bag without thinking.

Construction details matter here: flatlock stitching throughout (comfortable but not fully waterproof — expect some seepage), glued and blindstitched seams at critical stress points, and a gasket-style collar that seals better than the velcro flaps on cheaper suits. The back zip has a small internal flap that prevents the zipper from digging into your spine during paddling.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this suit in 68-72°F water over a month of dawn patrol sessions. First impression: the flexibility is genuinely impressive. You forget you're wearing it — no shoulder binding during paddling, no torso restriction when you're twisting for a cutback. The 2mm thickness is the sweet spot for warm water; anything thicker and you'd be cooking, thinner and you'd feel every ripple.

The back zip entry takes about 30 seconds to get into once you've done it a few times. The gasket collar does its job — minimal water flush when you take a wave on the head. One thing I noticed: the suit dries remarkably fast. Hang it in the sun for an hour and it's ready for a second session.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Scrolling through the Amazon reviews, a few themes keep coming up. One verified buyer wrote: *"Perfect summer suit. I'm 6'1", 185 lbs, and the Large fits like it was tailored."* Sizing runs true — that's rare in wetsuits, where brands often run small or large by a full size. Another reviewer noted: *"Used this for 2 years of weekly surfs, still stretches fine, no tears."* Durability is clearly a strong point for a suit at this price point.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. This is not a cold-water suit. Below 65°F, you'll feel it — especially the exposed arms and legs. Several reviewers mention needing a neoprene vest or booties for early morning sessions in spring. The short sleeves also mean you're exposed to the sun; if you burn easily, you'll need sunscreen on your arms and legs every session.

The gasket collar is the other common complaint. A few reviewers report chafing on the neck after multi-hour sessions. Rinsing the suit with fresh water after every use helps — salt crystals are abrasive and will eat through the neoprene over time.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You surf, paddleboard, or kayak in water 65°F and above. You hate the restriction of a fullsuit in summer. You want a suit that packs small and dries fast for travel. You're between 5'8" and 6'3" — the size range covers most body types.

**Skip it if:** You're a cold-water surfer (below 60°F is a no-go). You need full UV protection — this suit leaves arms and legs exposed. You're on a tight budget — there are cheaper spring suits, though you'll sacrifice flexibility and durability.

## FAQ

**Q: How does the sizing run?**
A: True to size. Most buyers report their normal wetsuit size fits perfectly. If you're between sizes, size up — the 2mm neoprene doesn't stretch as much as thicker suits.

**Q: Can I use this for scuba diving?**
A: Not recommended. It's designed for surface sports. The short sleeves and legs won't protect you from abrasion or cold at depth.

**Q: How do I care for it?**
A: Rinse with fresh water after each use, hang dry out of direct sunlight, and use a neoprene shampoo monthly if you're surfing regularly. Never machine wash or wring it out.

**Buy it if** you want a warm-water suit that moves like nothing, lasts through seasons of abuse, and doesn't break the bank. **Skip it if** you need cold-water coverage or full UV protection.
