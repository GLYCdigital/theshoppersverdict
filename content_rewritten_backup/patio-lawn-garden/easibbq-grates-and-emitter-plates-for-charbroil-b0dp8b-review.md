**EasiBBQ Grates and Emitter Plates for Charbroil Review: The Upgrade Your Grill Actually Needs**

If you own a Charbroil gas grill, you already know the two weak links in the system: the stock grates that flake and rust after two seasons, and the emitter plates (those angled metal tents over the burners) that corrode into Swiss cheese. The EasiBBQ replacement set tackles both simultaneously, and with 215 ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it’s clearly solving a widespread pain point.

Here’s the honest breakdown after digging through the specs and real user feedback.

**What’s in the Box**

This is a complete replacement kit, not a partial upgrade. You get:
- Two stainless steel cooking grates (roughly 17 x 13 inches each, depending on your specific Charbroil model)
- Two or three emitter plates (quantity varies by kit configuration)
- All necessary mounting hardware — the bolts and clips that inevitably get lost or corrode on your original grill

The grates feature a flat-bar design with narrow spacing (about 3/8 inch), which is a meaningful difference from the round-tube design on many stock grates. That flat surface means fewer flare-ups and better sear marks, but it also means smaller food items won’t slip through.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The key spec here is the material: stainless steel, not the cheap porcelain-coated steel that comes standard on most Charbroil grills. That matters because porcelain coating chips, and once it chips, the underlying steel rusts out fast. Stainless doesn’t have that failure mode.

The emitter plates are thicker than OEM replacements — roughly 22-gauge steel based on user reports — and they’re angled properly to direct grease away from the burners. That angle is critical: if the plates are too flat, grease pools and causes flare-ups; if too steep, heat distribution suffers. Multiple buyers confirmed the fit is flush and the plates sit correctly in the burner channels.

One buyer noted: *“The grates are heavier than the originals, and you can feel the difference when you pick them up. They hold heat better and the sear marks are actually visible now.”*

Another said: *“I was skeptical about aftermarket parts, but these fit my Charbroil 4-burner perfectly. No modifications needed.”*

**Where It Falls Short**

The most common complaint in the reviews is rust on the grates — specifically, surface rust that appears after a few months. That sounds alarming, but here’s the nuance: this is a known issue with *all* stainless steel grates, not just EasiBBQ’s. Stainless steel contains chromium, which resists corrosion, but it’s not rust-proof. If you don’t season the grates with oil before first use and after each cleaning, surface rust will develop.

The second issue is fitment. While most buyers report a perfect fit, a handful of users with older Charbroil models (pre-2018) noted the plates were slightly narrower than the originals, leaving a small gap at the edges. It doesn’t affect cooking performance, but it does allow some grease to bypass the plate and drip directly onto the burners.

Finally, the grates are not dishwasher-safe — you’ll be hand-scrubbing them with a grill brush after each heavy use. That’s standard for quality grates, but worth noting if you’re used to the low-maintenance of disposable foil liners.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kit is ideal for three specific buyers:
- **The 3-to-5-year Charbroil owner** whose original grates are rusting and warping. This is a direct, cost-effective replacement (typically $40–$60, far less than a new grill).
- **The frequent griller** who cooks multiple times a week and needs grates that hold up to constant heat cycling.
- **The upgrade-seeker** who wants better heat retention and sear marks without spending $200+ on a full grill replacement.

Skip it if you have a very old Charbroil with a non-standard burner layout, or if you’re expecting a true “never rust” product — no stainless grate delivers that.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Will these fit my specific Charbroil model?**
Check the product listing’s fitment chart carefully. EasiBBQ lists compatible models (usually 300/400/500-series and common models like the 463340315). Measure your current grates before ordering — width and depth matter more than brand compatibility claims.

**Q: Do I need to season the grates before first use?**
Yes, absolutely. Wash them with warm soapy water, dry thoroughly, then coat lightly with high-smoke-point oil (canola or grapeseed) and heat the grill on high for 15 minutes. This creates the protective polymer layer that prevents rust. Skipping this step is the #1 cause of the rust complaints in the reviews.

**Q: How long do these actually last?**
With proper seasoning and regular cleaning, buyers report 3–5 years of heavy use. The emitter plates typically outlast the grates since they don’t take direct heat from food contact. That’s 2–3 times longer than the stock Charbroil parts.
