**Review Body**

Most cordless sprayers under $100 are toys. They leak, they dribble, or they die halfway through a fence line. The Fanttik NB8 Nano isn't that—but it's also not the professional-grade unit the marketing photos suggest. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 204 ratings (4.1 stars) and user feedback.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the sprayer body with a 2.1-gallon tank, a retractable wand that extends to 33.5 inches, a shoulder strap, a charging cable, and a spare nozzle. Assembly takes about 90 seconds—screw the wand into the hose, clip the tank on, charge it up. Nothing complicated.

The tank itself is translucent polypropylene, so you can see liquid levels without opening it. The pump is a 12V diaphragm unit rated for 2.1 gallons per minute. That's respectable for this class.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where the NB8 shines and stumbles. The spray pattern is consistent and the motor doesn't surge or stutter like cheaper units. One reviewer noted, *"It sprays evenly until the last drop—no sputtering when the tank is low."* That matches what I've seen: the diaphragm pump holds pressure well.

The retractable wand is genuinely useful. Fully extended, it reaches about 4.5 feet above your head, which means you can treat tall tomato cages or hanging baskets without dragging a step stool around. The wand locks firmly at three positions—no collapsing mid-spray.

Battery life is the selling point. The 4.0Ah lithium pack runs 45–60 minutes of continuous spraying. For a typical homeowner—treating a lawn, spraying weed killer along a driveway, or applying foliar feed to a vegetable garden—that's usually enough to finish the job. But here's the catch: recharging takes 4–5 hours. If you have a big property and need a second session, you're waiting. Plan accordingly.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated positive feedback centers on two things: ease of use and reach. Users with mobility issues specifically mention that the trigger requires minimal effort and the weight distribution is balanced when worn cross-body. One verified buyer wrote, *"I can spray my entire rose bed without my hand cramping—my old pump sprayer was a nightmare by comparison."*

The tank capacity also gets consistent love. At 2.1 gallons, you're not refilling every 10 minutes like with the 1-gallon backpack units. For a 1/4-acre lot, most users report one fill handles the whole yard.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be direct about the flaws.

First, there's no pressure adjustment. The fixed output is strong—too strong for delicate applications like seedling misting or applying insecticidal soap to tender new growth. If you need a fine mist, this isn't your tool. You'll blast leaves off young plants if you're not careful.

Second, the shoulder strap is a weak point. With 2.1 gallons of liquid, the unit weighs roughly 18 pounds when full. The thin nylon strap digs into your shoulder after 15 minutes. Several reviewers mention this specifically. A padded strap would fix it, but Fanttik didn't include one.

Third, the wand connection can loosen over time. A few long-term owners report the threaded joint needs periodic tightening to prevent drips. It's not a dealbreaker, but check it before each use.

**Who Should Buy This**

The NB8 Nano is ideal for homeowners with 1/4 to 1/2 acre who need to apply weed killer, fertilizer, or pest control without the hassle of a manual pump sprayer. It's also a strong option for older gardeners or anyone with arthritis—the electric trigger removes the hand fatigue that comes with traditional sprayers.

Skip it if you're a professional landscaper needing adjustable pressure for varied chemicals, or if you have delicate plants that require a fine mist. Also skip it if you have a large property (1+ acres) and need multiple consecutive hours of spray time—the 4–5 hour recharge will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use it with bleach or acidic chemicals?**
A: Yes, the tank and wand are made of chemical-resistant polypropylene and nylon. Rinse thoroughly with clean water after each use. Avoid leaving chemicals in the tank overnight—it will degrade the seals over time.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last in real use?**
A: Expect 45–60 minutes of continuous spraying, depending on trigger pull frequency. Intermittent use (spray, rest, spray) stretches it closer to 75 minutes. The battery indicator shows three lights, but it drops from two lights to one fairly quickly near the end.

**Q: Does it come with extra nozzles?**
A: It includes a standard cone nozzle and a flat fan nozzle. No adjustable mist nozzle. If you need finer spray patterns, buy a universal adapter—but test the fit first.

**The Verdict**

**Rating: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you want a cordless sprayer that handles typical homeowner tasks without hand fatigue, and you value tank capacity and reach over fine spray control.

**Skip it if** you need adjustable pressure for delicate work or you're covering acreage that requires hours of continuous spray time.

The Fanttik NB8 Nano is genuinely good at what it does—it's just not a one-size-fits-all tool. For the majority of suburban and small-farm users, it'll pay for itself in saved time and spared hand cramps within a season. Just budget for a padded strap and mind the recharge time.
