If you've ever pulled up an above-ground pool liner to find a constellation of pinhole leaks from a stray pebble, you know the value of a good underlayment. The Poolergetic Pool Liner Pad aims to be more than a glorified tarp — it's a closed-cell foam mat that actually cushions the pool floor. At 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it's performing well, but let's talk about whether it's the right foundation for your specific setup.

**What You Actually Get**

The Poolergetic arrives as a pre-cut round pad sized to match standard above-ground pool diameters (available in common sizes from 12' up to 28'). It's made from closed-cell polyethylene foam at roughly 10mm thickness — think of a thick yoga mat, but denser. The material won't absorb water, which matters because a waterlogged underlayment becomes a breeding ground for mold and algae between the liner and the ground.

The pad unrolls flat with minimal creasing. You get the pad itself, nothing else — no tape, no adhesive, no repair patches. For the price point, that's acceptable, but first-time pool builders should note they'll need their own seam tape if they're piecing together multiple pads for an oval pool.

**Real-World Performance**

Closed-cell foam is the right call here. Unlike thin polyethylene tarps that shred when a rock shifts underneath, this material compresses and distributes weight. Buyers consistently report the pad surviving multiple seasons. One reviewer noted, *"I had a sharp root I missed during ground prep, and the foam absorbed it. No liner puncture after a full summer."* That's the core job, and it does it well.

The pad also provides mild thermal insulation, which means the water at the bottom of the pool stays slightly warmer in early spring and late fall — a subtle benefit, but one that extends your swim season by a week or two in cooler climates.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest frustration across reviews is the lack of pre-cut openings for the skimmer and return fittings. You're going to need a sharp utility knife and a willingness to measure carefully. Get it wrong, and you've got a misaligned hole that leaks or a gap that defeats the purpose of the pad. This is a 15-minute job, but it's an annoyance that cheaper underlayments don't have because they're designed to just be trimmed around fittings.

The surface is also slick. When the liner is set on top, it can shift if there's any breeze during assembly. A few reviewers recommend weighting the edges with bricks or sandbags until you fill the pool — a workaround, not a design feature. If you're assembling solo, budget extra time for this.

**Who Should Buy This**

The Poolergetic pad is ideal for first-time pool owners who want a no-fuss, durable foundation without laying down multiple layers of sand. It's also great for sandy or uneven ground, where a flexible tarp would just conform to every imperfection. If you're replacing an aging liner on existing ground, this pad gives you a fresh, smooth surface to work with.

Skip it if you're working with a perfectly level, pre-smoothed concrete pad — you're paying for cushioning you don't need. Also skip it if you're on a strict budget; a double-layer of cheap tarp will offer marginal protection for a fraction of the cost, though you'll sacrifice durability and insulation.

**Three Questions Buyers Ask**

*Will this prevent liner wrinkles?* No pad can guarantee a wrinkle-free install — that's about how carefully you pull and seat the liner. But the closed-cell foam does reduce ground-induced ripples that appear over time as the earth shifts.

*Can I walk on it during installation?* Yes, but wear soft-soled shoes. The foam compresses underfoot and recovers, but sharp objects on your shoes can score the surface.

*How long does it last?* Most reviewers report 3-5 seasons of use before needing replacement. The foam doesn't degrade in water, but prolonged UV exposure during disassembly can make it brittle.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely protective underlayment that handles rough ground, roots, and small stones without the hassle of layering sand and tarp. The insulation bonus and pre-cut round shape justify the modest premium over basic tarps.

**Skip it if** you're a budget builder who doesn't mind re-leveling ground thoroughly and using a $15 tarp instead. The Poolergetic is a convenience and durability upgrade, not a necessity for perfectly prepared ground.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Solid protection with a couple of installation quirks. For most above-ground pool owners, this is the last underlayment you'll need for several seasons.
