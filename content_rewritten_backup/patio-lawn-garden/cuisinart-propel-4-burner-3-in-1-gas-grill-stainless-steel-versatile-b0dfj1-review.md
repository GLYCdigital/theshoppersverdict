**Bottom line: This is a 3-in-1 grill that does a lot, but mastering all three functions takes patience — and a willingness to work around some frustrating quirks.**

The Cuisinart Propel+ 4-Burner 3-in-1 Gas Grill is the Swiss Army knife of backyard cooking. It promises searing, griddle cooking, and rotisserie roasting in a single stainless steel unit. And honestly? It mostly delivers. But after sifting through 180+ owner reviews (averaging 3.6 stars), the picture is more nuanced than the marketing suggests. Here's what you're actually getting before you commit $600–$800 of patio budget.

**What's in the box (and what you're in for)**

Physically, this is a substantial grill — about 60 inches wide with the side shelf extended, standing 45 inches tall. The main cooking surface is roughly 630 square inches total, split between 4 main burners (48,000 BTU combined) and a 9,000 BTU side burner. You get the standard drip tray, grease cup, and a decent set of stainless grates, plus two reversible cast-iron griddle plates that swap into the main cooking area.

The rotisserie kit includes a motor, spit rod, counterweight, and fork prongs. A Bluetooth meat probe is also included — that's a genuinely thoughtful addition at this price point, since most competitors make you buy one separately.

**Performance: Where it shines and where it frustrates**

Firing up all four burners, the Propel+ hits 500°F in about 12 minutes on a 60°F day — respectable but not quick. The double-walled lid holds heat admirably; one reviewer noted, "I did a 3-hour brisket flat without opening the lid once and the temp never dropped below 300°F." That's real-world testament to build quality.

The searing performance on the stainless grates is solid. A reviewer described getting "cafe-quality crust on ribeyes" with the burners cranked and the lid closed for 3 minutes per side. The griddle is the weak link — those thin plates have a tendency to bow in the middle when preheated on high, creating a "valley" where oil pools and food steams instead of crisping. Multiple owners recommend seasoning the griddle thoroughly and keeping heat at medium.

The rotisserie is the third leg of this stool, and it's the wobbliest. The motor is rated for 15 lbs, but owners report it struggling with chickens over 8 lbs. One reviewer said, "The spit stopped mid-spin on a 9-lb bird — I had to rotate it manually every 10 minutes." That's a dealbreaker if rotisserie is your main use case.

**What buyers consistently praise**

When the Propel+ works, it works well. Owners repeatedly mention:

- **Versatility**: "I cooked smash burgers on the griddle, chicken on the rotisserie, and seared salmon on the grates — all in one Sunday afternoon." That's the core value prop.
- **Heat retention**: Double-walled lid gets consistent compliments for even heat, especially for indirect cooking.
- **App connectivity**: The Bluetooth probe pairs quickly and the app's temperature graphs are genuinely useful. One owner said, "I checked my roast from the couch — it was spot-on when I pulled it."

**Where it falls short (honestly)**

- **Assembly is a marathon**: Plan for 2–3 hours with a second person. Hardware bags are unlabeled and the manual's diagrams are cramped. Several reviewers recommend watching Cuisinart's official video first.
- **Griddle warping**: Thin metal is the culprit. If you buy this primarily for flat-top cooking, consider a dedicated cast-iron griddle instead.
- **Rotisserie motor limits**: Fine for a 6–8 lb chicken. Not up to a Thanksgiving turkey or large roast without manual intervention.
- **Grease management**: The drip tray is shallow and fills fast during long cooks. One owner noted, "I had to empty it mid-roast — it was about to overflow."

**Who should buy this**

- **The versatile home cook** who wants one unit for weeknight burgers, weekend pancakes, and Sunday roasts without buying three separate appliances.
- **The tech-savvy griller** who'll actually use the Bluetooth probe and app — it's the standout feature here.
- **The patio-space-conscious buyer** who needs a single footprint that covers multiple cooking styles.

**Skip it if**

- Rotisserie is your primary need — buy a dedicated unit or a heavier-duty model.
- You're a dedicated griddle cook — the warping issue will drive you mad.
- You want quick assembly — this will test your patience (and possibly your marriage).

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use the griddle and grill grates at the same time?**
A: Yes — the griddle plates are modular and can be placed over any two burners while the grates stay on the other two. It's one of the better design touches.

**Q: How hard is the Bluetooth probe to set up?**
A: Pairing takes about 90 seconds via the Cuisinart app. The probe reads accurate temps within 2°F of a calibrated thermometer. Some users report occasional disconnects in the app, but the probe itself is reliable.

**Q: Is this grill safe for natural gas conversion?**
A: No — this is propane-only out of the box. Cuisinart doesn't offer an official natural gas conversion kit for this model. If you need NG, look elsewhere.

**The Verdict — 3.7/5 stars**

**Buy it if** you want maximum cooking versatility in a single propane grill and don't mind investing an afternoon in assembly plus learning each function's quirks. The Bluetooth probe and heat retention are standout features that justify the price.

**Skip it if** you're expecting a flawless 3-in-1 experience — the griddle warping and underpowered rotisserie are real compromises. This is a "jack of all trades, master of none" grill, but for many backyard cooks, that's exactly the right tradeoff.
