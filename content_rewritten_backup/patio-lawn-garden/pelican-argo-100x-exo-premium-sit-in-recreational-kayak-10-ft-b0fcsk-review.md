**The Pelican Argo 100X EXO isn't just another entry-level kayak. It's the rare budget sit-in that actually solves the problems that make beginners hate sit-ins: water sloshing over the cockpit rim, cramped legroom, and seats that leave you numb after an hour.**

I've paddled enough plastic boats to know that most sub-$500 kayaks cut corners where you feel it — the seat, the tracking, the hull flex. The Argo 100X EXO doesn't. Pelican's EXO cockpit rim is the headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff. That raised, contoured lip keeps splashes out far better than flat-rimmed sit-ins, which means you stay drier on choppy water. It also makes getting in and out less awkward — you're not doing that clumsy leg-over-the-gunwale dance.

**What you actually get:** The kayak itself (10 feet long, 28.5 inches wide), a cushioned seat with an adjustable backrest, molded-in carry handles fore and aft, and a basic aluminum paddle. That paddle is serviceable but heavy — plan to spend $60-$80 on a fiberglass shaft model if you paddle regularly. There's no dry storage hatch, so your phone, keys, and snacks go in a dry bag strapped to the deck lines. The molded-in footrests adjust fore and aft with a simple slide mechanism — no tools needed.

**On the water, it surprises.** The twin-arched multi-chine hull is Pelican's answer to the "am I going to tip over?" anxiety that plagues new paddlers. It tracks straight — I didn't find myself zig-zagging after 20 minutes like I do on some rental-grade kayaks. At 275 pounds capacity, it handled my 200-pound frame plus gear with plenty of freeboard. The seat is genuinely good: cushioned, supportive, and the backrest actually holds you upright without digging into your shoulder blades. One reviewer put it simply: *"I've owned three kayaks and this is the most comfortable seat I've sat in for under $500."*

The EXO cockpit rim deserves more praise. Traditional sit-ins let water pour over the rim every time a wake hits. This one sheds water — I stayed dry through a windy afternoon with whitecaps on the lake. Another buyer noted: *"The EXO cockpit is a game-changer. My old kayak left me soaked; this one kept me dry the entire trip."*

**Where it falls short:** At 39 pounds, it's not a featherweight. Loading it on a roof rack solo is doable but awkward — you'll want to practice the lift-and-slide technique before your first trip. The lack of any storage hatch is the biggest functional gap. If you want to carry a lunch, a change of clothes, or fishing gear, you're limited to what fits behind the seat or in a dry bag. And the stock paddle? It's fine for a casual afternoon, but it flexes noticeably under power strokes. Upgrade it if you plan to paddle more than a handful of times per season.

**Who should buy this:** The first-time kayak owner who wants a sit-in that's forgiving, stable, and doesn't require a truck bed to transport. The casual paddler who does weekend lake trips, slow rivers, and calm coastal bays. The taller paddler (up to about 6'2") who needs legroom — the Argo 100X EXO has more foot space than most boats in this class. It's also a solid choice for smaller paddlers who want a boat they can control without wrestling.

**Skip it if:** You need serious storage for multi-day trips, you're over 275 pounds, or you want a boat light enough to shoulder-carry for long portages. Also skip if you're strictly a whitewater paddler — this is a flatwater boat through and through.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Is the Pelican Argo 100X EXO stable enough for a beginner?**
Yes. The wide 28.5-inch beam and multi-chine hull design provide excellent primary stability. You won't feel like you're about to tip with every paddle stroke. It's one of the most forgiving sit-ins I've tested in this price range.

**Q: How does the EXO cockpit rim differ from a standard sit-in kayak?**
Standard sit-ins have a flat rim that lets water pour in over the edge. The EXO rim is raised and contoured, creating a partial barrier that deflects splashes and waves. It also provides a more secure fit around your hips, which improves control.

**Q: Can I install a rudder or skeg on this kayak?**
The Argo 100X EXO doesn't have pre-drilled rudder mounts, but the hull tracks well enough that you likely won't need one. If you're paddling in consistently windy conditions, consider a kayak with a factory-installed rudder instead.

**The Verdict:** The Pelican Argo 100X EXO earns its 4.7-star rating. It's not perfect — the missing storage hatch and basic paddle are real compromises — but for the price, it delivers the best combination of stability, comfort, and dry-ride performance in the recreational sit-in category. It's a boat you'll actually enjoy paddling, not just a toy you use twice a year.

**Buy it if** you want a comfortable, stable sit-in for lakes and slow rivers that won't leave you soaked or sore. **Skip it if** you need expedition-level storage or a sub-30-pound boat for car-topping every weekend.
