If you're managing more than a couple acres, dragging a garden hose around stops being an option. The NorthStar ATV Broadcast and Spot Sprayer exists to solve that specific problem — and for the most part, it does. With 200 ratings averaging 4.0 stars, this is a sprayer that's been through real seasons of real use. Here's what that actually looks like.

**What you're getting**

The box contains the 25-gallon polyethylene tank, a 12-volt diaphragm pump, 20 feet of hose, the 2-nozzle boom (60-inch swath), a hand wand for spot treatments, and all the mounting hardware to bolt it to your ATV or UTV racks. The tank has a wide mouth for easy filling and a built-in sump that keeps fluid flowing to the pump even when you're bouncing across uneven ground. The pump draws 15 amps, so make sure your vehicle's electrical system can handle it — most stock ATV batteries will, but if yours is weak, budget for an upgrade.

**Real-world performance**

The pump is the heart of any sprayer, and NorthStar got this one right. It moves 2.2 gallons per minute and self-primes quickly, which matters more than you'd think. Several buyers noted the convenience of flipping between broadcast mode and the spot wand without stopping. One reviewer put it plainly: "I sprayed 3 acres of pasture in about 40 minutes. The wand is great for fence lines and around trees." That's the workflow this unit is built for — cover the open ground fast, then switch to targeted treatment without climbing off the machine.

The boom's 60-inch width means you'll make fewer passes than with a single-nozzle unit. The nozzles are adjustable, which lets you dial in droplet size — finer for herbicides, coarser for fertilizer. Most owners found the sweet spot after a couple of uses. The hose is 20 feet, which is enough to treat areas you can't drive into without disconnecting anything.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- The tank's sump design. Multiple reviewers running on hilly terrain mentioned they didn't lose suction on inclines.
- The quick-switch between boom and wand. As one owner said: "No more dragging a 300-foot hose. I do everything from the seat."
- Durability of the poly tank itself. It's UV-stabilized, so it won't turn brittle after a summer in the sun.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the weak points. The plastic fittings at the hose connections are the first thing to fail. Multiple reviewers reported cracking them by over-tightening — and once they crack, you're replacing the whole fitting. Buy a set of brass replacements upfront; it's a $15 fix that saves you a mid-season headache. The lack of a pressure gauge is another genuine annoyance. You're adjusting flow by how the spray pattern looks, which means on a breezy day you'll overshoot or undershoot your coverage. It's workable, but it takes practice. A few owners also mentioned the 15-amp draw can tax smaller ATV batteries, especially if you're running lights or a winch at the same time.

**Who should buy this**

- **Hobby farmers and acreage owners** managing 2-10 acres who need to treat pasture, fence lines, and orchards without hiring out the work.
- **Landscapers with ATVs** who want a mounted unit that doesn't take up truck bed space.
- **Not for you** if you're treating a half-acre suburban lot — a backpack sprayer is cheaper and easier to store.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I use this with Roundup or other herbicides?*  
Yes. The pump and fittings handle most standard herbicides, pesticides, and liquid fertilizers. Rinse the system thoroughly after each use — especially with herbicides — to prevent clogging and corrosion.

*Q: How long will a full 25 gallons last?*  
At typical broadcast rates, expect 30-45 minutes of continuous spraying, depending on flow setting. For spot work, a tank can last an entire day.

*Q: Does it come with everything needed to mount it?*  
Yes, including bolts and brackets. You'll need basic hand tools and about 30 minutes. The wiring kit with inline fuse and battery clamps is included.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need reliable, mounted spraying for a few acres and want the flexibility of both broadcast and spot treatment without swapping equipment. The 4.2/5 rating reflects real capability — this is a workhorse, not a toy.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget and can't absorb the plastic fitting issue, or if your property is small enough that a $150 backpack sprayer gets the job done. For serious acreage, though, the NorthStar earns its place on your rack.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a solid, capable sprayer held back from greatness by cheap fittings and a missing gauge. Upgrade those and it's a 4.5+.
