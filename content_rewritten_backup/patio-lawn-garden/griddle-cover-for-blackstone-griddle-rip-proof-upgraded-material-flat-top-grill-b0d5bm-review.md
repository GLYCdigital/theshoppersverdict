## What You Actually Get

This isn’t the flimsy, silver polyester sheet that ships with many budget griddles. The Upgraded Mate uses a thick, multi-layer composite—the outer layer has a matte, almost rubberized texture designed to shed water, while the interior is a soft fleece lining meant to prevent scratching your cooking surface.

Key measurements matter here. The cover is designed for the 36-inch Blackstone models (the standard 4-burner and the Pro Series), with a drop length that reaches roughly mid-leg. It’s not a full-body bag; it’s a canopy-style cover with an elastic hem and toggle cinch cords. The seams are double-stitched and taped, which is the main upgrade over the stock covers that fail at the corners.

## Real-World Performance

**The Fit:** On a standard 36" Blackstone, this fits like a tailored suit—snug across the top and side shelves, with enough slack to accommodate the grease cup and magnetic paper towel holder without straining. If you have the air fryer lid accessory, you’ll need to measure; the cover is deep enough to clear it, but the cinch cords get tight.

**The Rip-Proof Claim:** This is where the product earns its name. Standard covers tear at the corners when you yank them off in a windstorm. The Upgraded Mate uses a woven polyester base rather than a film laminate. One reviewer noted, "I’ve had mine for six months in the Texas sun and it hasn't cracked or split like the last two did." The stitching on the stress points (the corner grommets) is reinforced with bartack stitching—the same technique used on heavy-duty backpacks.

**Weather Resistance:** The coating does a solid job beading water. In heavy rain, puddles form on top rather than soaking through. The downside? The dark color absorbs heat, so on a 90-degree day, the metal underneath gets hot enough to warp the cover’s shape slightly. It recovers, but it’s worth noting if you live in a scorching climate.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and this cover has two notable flaws.

**1. The Hook-and-Loop Straps Are Weak.** The straps that wrap around the legs to secure the cover are thin and use a small Velcro patch. After a few months of sun exposure, the Velcro loses its grip. You’ll rely on the elastic hem more than the straps. It’s a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

**2. The Color Fades.** The charcoal gray turns a chalky, purple-ish gray after a season in direct UV light. It doesn’t affect performance, but if you care about aesthetics, expect some fading by month eight.

**3. Condensation Risk.** The fleece lining is great for protecting against scratches, but it also traps moisture. If you cover a hot griddle (which you shouldn’t, but people do), you’ll get condensation under the cover, which can lead to rust on the cooktop. Always let the griddle cool completely before covering.

Skip it if you have a 17-inch or 22-inch Blackstone—those need the smaller companion model. Also skip it if you store your griddle in a garage or shed; the weatherproofing is wasted money.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my Blackstone with the side shelves folded up?**
A: Yes, the cover is designed to fit over the standard 36" model with the shelves in the flat position. If you fold them up, it will still fit, but the fit will be tighter and you’ll need to use the cinch cords to get a full seal.

**Q: Can I use this in winter with snow and ice?**
A: Yes, the material won’t crack in freezing temps. However, if snow accumulates, you’ll need to brush it off periodically. The weight of wet, heavy snow can stretch the elastic hem prematurely.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
A: Rinse with a hose and wipe down with a mild soap solution. Do not machine wash or put it in the dryer. The coating will degrade if you use harsh chemicals.
