**What you actually get**

The Organo Republic Flower Seeds Pack arrives as a set of sealed, clearly labeled packets. Each one lists the variety, planting depth, spacing, and sun requirements right on the front — a small touch that saves you from juggling a phone while your hands are covered in dirt. You're getting roughly 1 gram per packet, which is about 50-100 seeds depending on the variety. That's enough for a decent border or a few containers, but not a full meadow.

The lineup mixes workhorses with showstoppers. You'll find black-eyed Susan and lavender for perennial structure, plus cosmos, zinnia, and marigold for quick annual color. In the product image, you can see the full assortment laid out — it's a well-thought mix, not just a random grab bag.

**Real-world germination and performance**

The manufacturer reports 85-90% germination from their 2025 trials. That's credible for fresh heirloom stock, and reviewers generally back it up. One buyer noted: "Planted the zinnias and cosmos in early May — had blooms by late July. The lavender took longer to establish but is still going strong in September." That matches the typical pattern: annuals give you payoff in the first season, perennials take a year to settle in.

Cold stratification is the one thing that trips up new gardeners. The perennial varieties — especially lavender and black-eyed Susan — often need 4-6 weeks in the fridge before planting. It's not mentioned prominently in the listing, and several reviewers dinged the pack for it. If you skip that step, expect spotty germination on the perennials. The annuals, though, are forgiving: direct sow after the last frost and they'll take off.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The packets are also small — about a gram each — so if you're trying to fill a 4x8 raised bed, you'll need two or three packs. And there's no printed growing guide in the box. The packet labels cover basics, but if you're new to gardening, you'll want to research timing and stratification on your own.

**Who should buy this**

This pack is ideal for three types of gardeners: beginners who want a low-stakes variety pack to learn with, container gardeners looking to fill several pots with mixed color, and anyone building a pollinator garden who wants to skip the planning phase. Skip it if you need large quantities of a single species, or if you're planting late in the season — these need a full growing cycle to shine.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these really perennial?**
A: About half the pack is perennial (lavender, black-eyed Susan, coneflower). The rest are annuals that complete their life cycle in one season. The listing is accurate, but don't expect everything to come back next year.

**Q: Do I need to cold stratify the seeds?**
A: Only the perennial varieties. Check each packet — it's labeled. For lavender and black-eyed Susan, mix with damp sand and refrigerate for 4-6 weeks before planting.

**Q: How many flowers will I get per packet?**
A: Roughly 50-100 seeds per packet, depending on variety. Zinnia and cosmos seeds are larger, so fewer per gram. Expect a solid cluster of plants, not a field.
