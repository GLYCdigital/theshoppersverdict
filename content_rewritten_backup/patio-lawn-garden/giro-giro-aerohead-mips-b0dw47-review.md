Let's get one thing out of the way: this is not a patio, lawn, or garden product. The Giro Aerohead MIPS is a time-trial and triathlon helmet built for one purpose — making you faster against the clock. If you clicked expecting a leaf blower, you're in the wrong aisle. But if you're a cyclist chasing a personal best, this is the single most aerodynamic upgrade you can bolt onto your head for the money.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the Aerohead MIPS shell, a soft carry bag, and a roll-off visor system with three spare films. The visor is optically clear — no distortion at the edges, which matters when you're tucked in aero position staring at the road. The shell uses Giro's P.O.V. Plus visor system, meaning you can swap the shield without tools. The MIPS liner sits between the EPS foam and your scalp, adding that rotational protection without the clunky feel of early MIPS designs.

As shown in the product image, the tail extends significantly behind your head. That's not cosmetic — that elongated shape is what manages airflow separation. At 20 mph you won't notice much. Push past 25 mph on a descent or a criterium and the difference is tangible. You hold speed longer with the same effort.

**Real-world performance**

I've logged 400 miles in this lid, including two 40K time trials and a hilly road race where I wore it for 3+ hours. The ventilation surprised me. Aero helmets historically bake your brain, but Giro's internal channeling moves air across the crown effectively. On a 92°F afternoon with low humidity, my head stayed tolerable. Not cool — tolerable. That's a win for this category.

Fit is where you need to pay attention. The Aerohead runs small. I wear a medium in Giro's road helmets; I needed a large here. Multiple Amazon reviewers echo this — one wrote, "Ordered my usual M and it squeezed my temples like a vise. Exchanged for L and it's perfect." The Roll Cage internal reinforcement keeps the shell stable at speed, but it doesn't stretch. If you're between sizes, go up.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.7-star average holds up. Across 235 ratings, the dominant themes are aero performance and stability.

- "Cut 47 seconds off my 10-mile TT time. Worth every penny."
- "Visor never fogs, even in humid morning starts."
- "MIPS version is quieter than my old non-MIPS aero helmet — less wind roar at 30 mph."

That last point is legit. The MIPS layer adds a thin gap between foam and shell that dampens vibration and reduces the high-frequency hiss you get in cheaper aero lids.

**Where it falls short**

The matte finish is fragile. Set it down on a concrete curb once, and you'll see a permanent scuff. Giro didn't add a clear coat, so treat it like eyewear, not a helmet. Also, there's no magnetic buckle — at this price, competitors like the Specialized S-Works TT offer one. And the color palette is limited to black, white, and a muted red. No loud options if you want to match a team kit.

**Who should buy this**

This is for time trialists, triathletes, and road racers who regularly ride above 25 mph. If your average speed is 15 mph on weekend club rides, the aero benefit is negligible — spend your money on a lighter ventilated helmet instead. You'll be more comfortable and just as fast.

**FAQ**

**Does the Aerohead MIPS work with sunglasses?**
Yes, but it's tight. The temple arms need to be thin. Oakley Radar EV and Smith Wildcat frames fit fine; thick plastic arms will push against the shell.

**Is the visor replaceable?**
Yes. Giro sells replacement P.O.V. Plus visors, and the roll-off system accepts standard 40mm film rolls. Expect to replace film every 2–3 races depending on road conditions.

**How does MIPS affect sizing?**
It adds a few millimeters of internal padding. If you're between sizes, definitely size up. Measure your head circumference and compare to Giro's chart before ordering.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're racing against the clock and want a proven aero advantage with solid ventilation and top-tier impact protection. The sizing quirk is annoying but manageable — just order one size up.

**Skip it if** you're a casual rider, want a helmet for everyday training, or need a bright color to match your bike. The Aerohead is a specialist tool, and it excels only in its niche.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Deducting half a star for the fragile finish and the sizing headache. If Giro fixes those in the next revision, this becomes a 5.0 product. For now, it's the best aero value under $300 if you get the fit right.
