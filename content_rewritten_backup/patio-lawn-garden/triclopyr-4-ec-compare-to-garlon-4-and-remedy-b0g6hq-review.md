## What's Actually in the Jug

You're getting a 1-gallon jug of concentrated herbicide concentrate. The label reads "Triclopyr 4 EC" and lists the identical active ingredient concentration as Garlon 4 Ultra and Remedy Ultra. That's not marketing speak — it's the same chemical, same formulation type (emulsifiable concentrate), same EPA registration category. The difference is the sticker price.

The label covers the full spectrum: pasture brush, fence rows, right-of-ways, industrial sites, and non-crop areas. I counted the weed list — it's comprehensive. Multiflora rose, poison ivy, oak, sumac, blackberry, kudzu, and over 200 other woody species are on there. This is serious brush control, not a cosmetic lawn product.

## Real-World Performance

The chemistry does what it promises. Triclopyr is a systemic herbicide that translocates to the root system, which is why it kills the whole plant rather than just browning the leaves. One reviewer put it plainly: "Killed my multiflora rose patch that I'd fought with a mower and Roundup for three years. This stuff actually got the roots."

Mixing is straightforward: 1 to 3 quarts per acre for foliar applications, or a 20-30% solution for cut-stump treatment. For basal bark work, you'll mix it with diesel or crop oil at a 1:4 ratio. I recommend a dedicated sprayer — this stuff is oil-based and will linger in equipment.

## Where It Falls Short

The odor is the first thing you'll notice. It's a strong, solvent-heavy smell that hangs around. One reviewer summed it up: "The smell will clear a room. Mix it outside, wear gloves, and don't get it on your skin." A respirator with organic vapor cartridges is worth the money here.

Second, this is not a lawn product. It's non-selective and will kill grass, ornamentals, and anything else green it touches. If you have a small yard with scattered brush, spot-treat carefully or you'll be reseeding bare patches.

Third, the concentrate is thick. If you don't shake the jug vigorously before measuring, you'll get inconsistent concentrations. A few reviewers missed this and wondered why results varied between batches.

## FAQ

**Is this exactly the same as Garlon 4?**
Same active ingredient (triclopyr BEE), same concentration (4 lb/gal), same use rates. The inert ingredients may differ slightly, but field results are indistinguishable. It's a generic equivalent, like store-brand ibuprofen.

**Will it hurt my lawn?**
Yes, if it touches it. This kills broadleaf plants including grass. Use a shielded sprayer or apply only to targeted brush. It's not labeled for lawn use at all.

**How long until I see results?**
Initial wilting in 7-14 days, complete kill in 3-6 weeks depending on the species and season. Woody plants treated in late summer or fall show slower top-kill but better root translocation.
