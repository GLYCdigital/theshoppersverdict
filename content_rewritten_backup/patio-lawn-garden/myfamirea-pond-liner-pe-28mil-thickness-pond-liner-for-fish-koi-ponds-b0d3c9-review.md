If you've priced out a proper EPDM pond liner lately, you know the sticker shock. That's exactly why the MYFAMIREA 28Mil PE liner keeps showing up in carts — it offers fish-safe containment at a fraction of the rubber liner cost. But is saving money worth the tradeoff in flexibility and longevity? I dug through 200 ratings, installation photos, and the spec sheet to give you the straight story.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the folded liner sheet itself — nothing else. No seam tape, no patch kit, no underlayment. You're buying a single continuous sheet of 28-mil polyethylene, which is thicker than the typical 20-mil pond liners in this price range. The material arrives folded (not rolled), so expect crease lines that will take patience and warm weather to relax. For a 10x15-foot koi pond, this thickness translates to roughly 150 pounds of material — get a helper.

**Performance and Real-World Durability**

Here's where this liner surprises. Multiple buyers report successful installations over compacted soil with a layer of sand or old carpet as underlayment. One reviewer noted, "I was skeptical at 28 mil, but it handled the rocks I couldn't fully clear from the bottom without puncturing." The UV stabilizers seem legitimate — several users report two full seasons of direct sunlight exposure with no visible embrittlement or cracking.

That said, PE is not EPDM. It's stiffer and has less give. On a simple rectangular or oval pond, it lays down fine. On anything with tight curves, steep walls, or a freeform shape, you'll fight wrinkles that refuse to smooth out. The material also has memory — those shipping creases can become permanent channels if you don't stretch and weight the liner during installation.

**What Buyers Praise**

The fish-safety factor is the most consistent compliment. The PE formulation contains no plasticizers or heavy-metal stabilizers, meaning it won't leach into the water column. As one koi keeper put it, "My fish were swimming within 24 hours and showed zero stress. Water tests came back clean." The thickness also earns repeated praise for puncture resistance — several buyers specifically mention it outperformed cheaper 20-mil liners they'd used previously.

**Where It Falls Short**

The biggest complaint across reviews is the creasing issue. The fold lines from shipping can be stubborn, and in colder weather (below 60°F), the material gets noticeably stiffer and harder to work with. You'll want to install on a warm day or use a heat gun carefully — though PE melts fast, so go slow.

Second, the slick surface is a genuine hazard during installation. Once the liner gets damp, it slides on itself and on the underlayment. One reviewer described "a wrestling match" trying to keep the liner positioned while adding water. Weight the edges immediately.

Third — and this is critical — you cannot rely on the manufacturer for seam support. There's no included tape, no welding instructions, and customer support is minimal. If you need to join two pieces for a larger pond, budget extra for professional-grade PE seam tape and watch several tutorial videos first.

**Who Should Buy This**

Get the MYFAMIREA if you're building a simple, geometric-shaped pond (rectangle, square, oval) and want a fish-safe liner that won't break the bank. It's also a solid choice for raised ponds, bog filters, or as a second liner over an existing one that's failing.

Skip it if you're building a large freeform pond with complex contours, if you need to seam multiple pieces together, or if you live in a climate with harsh winters — PE gets brittle below freezing, and while it usually survives, EPDM handles cold flex far better.

**FAQ**

*Q: Is this liner truly safe for koi and goldfish?*
Yes. The PE formulation contains no phthalates or heavy-metal stabilizers. Rinse it with clean water before install, and you're good.

*Q: Can I use this over dirt or do I need underlayment?*
Use underlayment. While the 28 mil resists punctures, a single sharp rock under water pressure can still breach it. Sand, old carpet, or commercial underlayment costs little and adds years of life.

*Q: How long will it last compared to EPDM?*
Realistic expectation is 5-8 years with proper installation and underlayment. EPDM typically lasts 20+. You're paying half the price for about half the lifespan.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you're building a straightforward pond on a budget. Skip it if you need maximum flexibility or seam reliability.**

For the DIY pond builder with a simple design, the MYFAMIREA 28Mil delivers exactly what it promises: thick, fish-safe containment at a price that leaves room in the budget for a decent pump and filter. Just plan your installation day around warm weather, recruit a friend, and accept that this is a 5-to-8-year solution — not a lifetime one.
