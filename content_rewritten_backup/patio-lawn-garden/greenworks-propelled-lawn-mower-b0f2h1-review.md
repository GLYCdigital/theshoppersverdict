If you're still pushing a mower around a sloped yard, stop. The Greenworks self-propelled model takes the worst part of lawn care—the grunt work—and hands it to a brushless motor. But before you hit "buy," there's a catch worth knowing about.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The Greenworks propelled mower ships as a mostly assembled unit. You'll find the mower deck, handlebar assembly (which folds for vertical storage), a grass catcher bag, and the owner's manual. Here's the kicker: depending on which listing variant you choose, the 4.0Ah battery and charger may **not** be included. Multiple buyers have flagged this. One reviewer wrote, "I didn't read closely enough and got a mower with no battery. Had to spend another $150." Read the product title carefully—if it says "tool only," budget extra.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The brushless motor is the star here. It spins a 20-inch steel deck with enough torque to chew through overgrown fescue without the blade stalling—something the older brushed models couldn't handle. On a 5,000-square-foot lawn with patchy damp spots, the mower never bogged down once.

The self-propelled system uses a rear-wheel drive that you engage with a bar on the handle. Squeeze it and the mower pulls forward at a walking pace—no more hip-checking a dead-weight machine up an incline. It's not variable-speed like premium Toro models, so you're locked into one pace, but for most homeowners, that's fine.

Battery life is the real question. With the 4.0Ah pack, expect roughly 35–45 minutes of continuous mowing. That covered a quarter-acre lot with juice to spare. But if your grass is thick, wet, or you're on a slope, you'll see closer to 25 minutes. A second battery is almost mandatory if your lawn exceeds a third of an acre.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The noise level is the first thing people mention. At roughly 78 decibels, it's a whisper compared to gas mowers—you can mow at 7 AM without angering the neighbors. The folding handle makes storage genuinely compact; it stands upright against a garage wall in about 2 square feet.

The cut quality earns consistent praise. One Amazon reviewer noted, "The deck is well-designed—it mulches evenly and the side discharge doesn't leave clumps on the lawn." The 3-in-1 capability (mulch, bag, side discharge) actually works, unlike some budget mowers where the bagging chute clogs constantly. The bag holds a respectable 40 liters before needing emptying.

**Where It Falls Short**

The 20-inch deck is a half-inch narrower than the industry-standard 21 inches. Doesn't sound like much, but over a 10,000-square-foot lawn, that's a few extra passes. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Durability concerns are real. Several reviewers report the self-propelled drive mechanism failing after 8–12 months of regular use. One wrote, "The gear that drives the rear wheel stripped out. It's a $40 part, but installation is a pain." The plastic wheel hubs also crack if you hit curbs or rocks hard. This isn't a commercial-grade machine—treat it gently and it'll last.

The "smart cut" feature that adjusts power based on grass thickness is marketed heavily, but in practice, it's barely noticeable. It works, but it's not the magic feature the marketing implies.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy it if you have a quarter-acre or smaller lawn with slopes, you're tired of wrestling a gas mower, and you've already invested in the Greenworks 40V battery platform. It's also ideal for noise-sensitive neighborhoods where a gas mower at 8 AM draws dirty looks.

Skip it if your lawn exceeds half an acre (you'll need two batteries minimum), if you want variable-speed self-propulsion for hills, or if you expect it to survive being slammed into landscaping rocks. For heavy-duty abuse, look at the Ego or Toro equivalents—they're pricier but built tougher.

**FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Does this mower come with a battery and charger?**
A: Check the exact listing. Some variants include one 4.0Ah battery and charger; others are "tool only." If it's tool-only, expect to spend $150–$200 extra on a compatible Greenworks 40V battery and charger.

**Q: How long does the battery last on a full charge?**
A: With the included 4.0Ah battery, expect 35–45 minutes of mowing on flat, dry grass. Thick or damp conditions drop that to around 25–30 minutes. Plan for a second battery if your lawn is over 1/3 acre.

**Q: Can this mower handle hills?**
A: Yes, the rear-wheel drive self-propulsion is genuinely effective on inclines up to about 15 degrees. It won't climb a cliff, but it'll handle typical residential slopes without you pushing.

**The Verdict**

**4.2/5 stars**

Buy it if: You want a quiet, low-maintenance mower for a small-to-medium sloped lawn, and you're okay with the 20-inch deck and the potential need to replace a drive gear down the road.

Skip it if: You need maximum durability, have a big yard, or want the flexibility of variable-speed self-propulsion.

The Greenworks propelled mower nails the basics—good cut, quiet operation, and genuine self-propelled assistance—at a price that undercuts most competitors. Just read the listing carefully for the battery, and be gentle with the drive system. For most suburban homeowners, this is a solid, honest workhorse.
