If you're tired of shocking your pool every other day just to keep algae at bay, the C3 1-inch Stabilized Chlorine Tablets deserve a hard look. This isn't a flashy product — it's a bucket of white discs that do one job well. But that job matters: keeping your water clear without babysitting it.

## What's actually in the bucket

You're getting a standard 5-pound bucket of 1-inch tablets. Each tablet packs 95% available chlorine — that's on the higher end, compared to the 90% you'll see from many generic brands. The difference matters because it translates to fewer tablets per week and less sediment at the bottom of your feeder.

These are stabilized with cyanuric acid (CYA), typically around 50-55%. That's the ingredient that keeps the sun from destroying your chlorine before it can do its job. For above-ground pools and smaller in-ground setups, this is the right call.

## Real-world performance

The dissolving rate is where C3 earns its keep. Multiple buyers mention these lasting 5-7 days in a floater, even in peak summer heat. One reviewer noted, "I put two in my floater and didn't touch the pool for a week. Water stayed crystal clear."

That consistency comes from the compression process — the tablets are dense, not crumbly. You won't find half-dissolved chunks clogging your skimmer basket. The slow dissolve also means you can run a floater without obsessively checking levels every day.

In hot climates, this matters. Unstabilized chlorine burns off in hours of direct sun. The CYA buffer keeps your free chlorine working for days, not hours. That's the difference between a maintenance routine and a part-time job.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the limits. The 1-inch size is a deliberate choice for smaller feeders and floaters — if you've got a standard 3-inch chlorinator, these won't fit. That's not a flaw in the product, but it's a compatibility check you need to make before ordering.

The bigger issue: CYA buildup. Stabilized chlorine is great short-term, but over a season, cyanuric acid accumulates. Once you're above 70-80 ppm CYA, chlorine becomes less effective, and you'll need to partially drain and refill. If you're using these weekly all summer, plan a mid-season water change. It's not a reason to skip the product — it's a reason to test your water monthly.

Also, the bucket doesn't come with a measuring scoop. It's minor, but for the price, a small scoop would've been thoughtful.

## FAQ

**Q: How many tablets do I need per week?**
A: For a typical 5,000-gallon above-ground pool, one tablet per 5,000 gallons daily is the general rule — so roughly 1-2 tablets per week in a floater. Test your chlorine levels after day two to dial it in.

**Q: Can I use these in a saltwater pool?**
A: Yes, but monitor CYA carefully. Salt systems generate their own chlorine, and adding stabilized tabs can push cyanuric acid too high. Use them sparingly, or switch to unstabilized tabs if your CYA creeps above 50 ppm.

**Q: Do these work in cold water?**
A: They dissolve slower below 60°F, which is fine — you need less chlorine in winter anyway. Just expect tablets to last longer, and don't over-shock.
