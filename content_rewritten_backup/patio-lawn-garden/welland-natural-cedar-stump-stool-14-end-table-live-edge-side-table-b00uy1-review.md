If you've been scrolling past plastic resin tables and powder-coated metal stands, this WELLAND cedar stump stool is the antidote. It's a solid, 14-inch slab of real cedar with a live edge, and it does exactly what the listing promises — no more, no less. At 4.7 stars across 235 ratings, it's one of the better-rated natural wood pieces on Amazon, and after digging through the buyer feedback, here's the honest story.

## What You Actually Get

This is a single piece of kiln-dried cedar, cut into a stump shape roughly 14 inches tall. The top is flat and sanded — enough to set a drink on — while the sides retain the original bark and live edge. It ships bare, meaning no varnish, no stain, no protective coating. You're getting raw wood, which is both the charm and the catch.

The footprint is smaller than you might expect from the photos. It's a side table, not a coffee table. It fits a standard Adirondack chair perfectly — one iced tea, one book, one phone. That's its lane.

## Real-World Performance

As a stool, it's surprisingly sturdy. The flat base sits level on concrete, deck boards, and packed grass. Several buyers use it as a plant stand, a porch side table, and even a rustic nightstand. One reviewer noted: *"It's heavier than I expected, which is good — my dog bumps into it constantly and it doesn't tip."* That weight (anywhere from 15 to 25 pounds depending on the unit) keeps it planted in gusts that would send hollow resin tables tumbling.

Cedar is the right wood choice here. It resists moisture, rot, and insects naturally, so leaving it outside in rain or snow won't destroy it. But — and this is important — it won't stay looking like the listing photos forever. The bark is the weak point.

## What Buyers Praise

The aesthetic is the headline. Over and over, buyers mention how the natural grain and bark texture elevate a basic patio setup. One five-star review reads: *"I bought two for our cabin porch. They look like they were cut from our own property. Guests always comment on them."* Another mentions using it as a rustic wedding guest book table — the live edge gives it that handcrafted feel you can't fake with molded plastic.

It's also genuinely functional as a small stool. At 14 inches, it's the right height for a low chair or a kid's step-up. The flat top holds a 12-ounce glass without wobble.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: the bark will shed. Multiple reviewers report pieces of bark flaking off within the first month, especially if the stool sits in direct sun or freezing temps. One buyer wrote: *"I love the look, but I'm sweeping bark chips off the deck every week. I finally stripped the bark off and sanded it down."* That's a manageable fix — a few hours with a paint scraper and some outdoor polyurethane — but it's work you should budget for.

Also, there's no finish on the top surface. Some units arrive with a slightly rough texture that can catch on bare legs or snag a towel. A quick sanding with 220-grit solves it, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Finally, the "live edge" means every piece is unique. That's a pro for charm, but a con for consistency — one buyer's stool may be darker, another's lighter, another's more tapered. If you're buying two expecting a matched pair, order them together and be prepared to pick your favorites.

## FAQ

**Will it survive winter outdoors?**
Yes, but the bark will suffer. Cedar itself handles freezing and thawing fine. The bark is what peels. If you want it to last several winters, strip the bark and apply a coat of exterior polyurethane or linseed oil before the first hard freeze.

**Is the top smooth enough for a drink?**
Barely. It's sanded, not polished. A sweaty glass won't leave rings, but the surface is slightly textured. If it bothers you, a light sanding and a coat of food-safe mineral oil or matte poly will make it glass-smooth.

**How much weight can it hold?**
It's solid cedar, so it's strong. Buyers report using it as a step stool, a plant stand for heavy pots, and a seating option for adults up to about 200 pounds. It's not built for jumping on, but it won't collapse under normal use.
