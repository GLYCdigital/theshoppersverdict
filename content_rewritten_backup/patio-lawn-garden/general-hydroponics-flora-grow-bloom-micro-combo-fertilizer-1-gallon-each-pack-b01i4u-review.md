**Bottom Line:** The General Hydroponics Flora Series has been the industry standard for three decades, and this combo pack delivers the full trio in concentrated 1-gallon form. It's not the cheapest upfront, but the math works out to pennies per gallon.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get three 1-gallon jugs: FloraMicro (the nitrogen-and-minerals base), FloraGro (vegetative growth formula), and FloraBloom (flowering and fruiting formula). As shown in the product image, these are the familiar white bottles with red, green, and pink labels respectively. The total volume is 384 fluid ounces of concentrated liquid nutrient.

The key thing to understand: this is a *system*, not a single fertilizer. You mix the three in different ratios depending on your plant's growth stage. The combo pack assumes you already know this — there's no printed schedule inside, which is a real point of friction for first-timers. The GH website has the charts, and the Lucas method (equal parts Micro and Bloom, no Grow) is all over forums, but the box itself is bare-bones.

## Performance and Real-World Use

I've run this trio through multiple cycles — tomatoes in coco coir, lettuce in DWC, and peppers in perlite. The results are consistently strong. The FloraMicro contains the chelated micronutrients and the pH stabilizers, so when you mix it first in water, it buffers your pH into the 5.5–6.5 range before you even add the other two parts. That's a genuine advantage over cheap one-part powders that drift all over the scale.

The concentrated formula means you're using roughly 2.5–5 mL per gallon during early growth, up to 15 mL per gallon at peak flower. At those rates, one gallon of each bottle makes roughly 100–128 gallons of working solution. Spread over a season, the per-feed cost is under a dollar for most home growers.

One thing that stands out: it's *clean*. Unlike some organic fertilizers that leave sludge in your reservoir, the Flora series stays dissolved and doesn't gunk up air stones or drip lines. If you're running hydroponics with pumps, you'll appreciate not cleaning clogs every week.

## Where It Falls Short

The learning curve is real. If you're brand new to growing, mixing three bottles with different ratios for different weeks is intimidating. There's no quick-start guide in the box, and the official feeding chart is a spreadsheet-style PDF that doesn't exactly shout "beginner friendly."

Also, the Micro bottle has a strong odor — some buyers describe it as "sulfur and ammonia." It's not pleasant to handle, and if you're sensitive to smells, use gloves and open it outside.

Finally, this is a *synthetic* salt-based fertilizer. If you're committed to organic growing, this isn't the product for you. It won't feed soil biology; it feeds the plant directly. That's fine for most growers, but it's a genuine limitation to know before you buy.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Hydroponic growers** — this is the gold standard for DWC, NFT, coco, and aeroponics. Non-negotiable.
- **Serious container gardeners** — if you grow peppers, tomatoes, or herbs in pots with soilless mix, this gives you precise control.
- **Experienced growers who want to dial in specific ratios** — the three-part flexibility is the whole point.

**Skip it if** you're a casual houseplant owner who wants to pour one capful into a watering can. You're overpaying for capability you won't use. Get a simple balanced liquid fertilizer instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this for soil?**
A: Yes, but you need to adjust. The Flora series is designed for soilless media. In real soil, use half-strength and add a cal-mag supplement, since the salts can lock out calcium in heavy organics.

**Q: How long does each bottle last?**
A: For a typical 4–6 plant hydroponic setup, expect 2–3 months per bottle at full strength. Light feeders will stretch it longer.

**Q: Is the Lucas method really viable?**
A: Yes — the Lucas method (8 mL Micro + 16 mL Bloom per gallon) is a proven hack that skips the Grow bottle entirely. It's not perfect for every strain, but it works well enough that many growers never buy FloraGro again.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're growing hydro or growing seriously in containers. The three-part system gives you control no one-part fertilizer can match, and the concentrated formula delivers real cost-per-gallon savings.

**Skip it if** you want grab-and-go simplicity or need organic inputs. This is a professional tool, not a houseplant fertilizer.
