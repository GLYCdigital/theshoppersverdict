Let me skip the pleasantries: if you're paying name-brand prices for glyphosate concentrate, you're burning money. The Albaugh Gly Star Plus 2.5-gallon jug delivers the exact same active ingredient — 41% glyphosate, isopropylamine salt — as the big orange bottle, at roughly half the cost per ounce. That's the headline. Here's whether it actually holds up.

**What's in the jug**

You get one 2.5-gallon (320 fl oz) container of amber liquid concentrate. That's it — no measuring cup, no surfactant, no sprayer. At the standard 2.5 oz per gallon of water mix rate for general weed control, this makes about 128 gallons of finished spray. For spot treatment at 1.5 oz/gallon, you're looking at over 200 gallons. If you're managing a one-acre property or a long fence line, this is a season's worth of weed control in a single jug.

**Real-world performance**

Gly Star Plus is a non-selective systemic herbicide — it kills anything green it touches, roots included. The 41% concentration is the key spec here; it matches Roundup Original's formulation exactly. On established weeds like dandelion, thistle, and bindweed, you'll see yellowing within 5–7 days and full die-back in 2–3 weeks. For annual grasses, it's faster — often visibly wilting within 48 hours.

One thing I've learned from buyer feedback: this stuff works best when you follow the label's mixing instructions precisely. Several reviewers noted that skimping on the ratio to "save product" just means partial kills and regrowth. One buyer put it plainly: "Mixed at 2.5 oz per gallon as directed, it wiped out my entire overgrown back lot in one pass. I tried diluting it more for the second round and regretted it."

**The surfactant catch**

Here's the honest tradeoff. Glyphosate needs a surfactant to penetrate waxy leaf surfaces on weeds like poison ivy, English ivy, and mature broadleafs. Most name-brand concentrates have a surfactant already blended in. Gly Star Plus does not — you'll need to add a non-ionic surfactant (about $10–15 for a bottle that lasts years) for toughest weeds. For soft-stemmed annuals and general grass control, you won't notice the difference. For woody or waxy perennials, skip the surfactant and you'll get partial control.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 240 ratings at 4.8 stars, three themes dominate. First, value: "This is the same chemical as the $80-per-gallon stuff, just without the marketing budget." Second, effectiveness on tough weeds: multiple reviewers mention it killing poison ivy and blackberries that survived other herbicides. Third, shelf life — the concentrate stores well for 2+ years if kept from freezing, so bulk buyers aren't wasting money.

**Where it falls short**

Beyond the missing surfactant, the packaging is functional but basic. The 2.5-gallon jug has a plain screw cap — no measuring marks, no pour spout. You'll need a dedicated measuring cup and ideally a transfer pump, because pouring from a 22-pound jug without spilling is a two-person job. Small complaints, but worth knowing before it arrives.

Also note: this is a professional-grade concentrate. If you only need to treat a small backyard or occasional driveway cracks, a 32-ounce ready-to-spray bottle makes more sense. You won't use 2.5 gallons before it degrades.

**Who should buy this**

- **Rural property owners** managing acreage, fence lines, or pasture edges
- **Landscapers and lawn pros** who burn through herbicide weekly
- **Anyone with established perennial weed infestations** (poison ivy, bindweed, Canadian thistle) that need root-kill
- **Cost-conscious buyers** who don't mind mixing their own spray

Skip it if you only need occasional spot treatment, or if you're not comfortable measuring and mixing chemicals.

**Q: Is this the same as Roundup?**
A: Chemically, the active ingredient is identical — 41% glyphosate as the isopropylamine salt. It's a generic equivalent. The difference is no added surfactant and no fancy packaging.

**Q: How much area does 2.5 gallons cover?**
A: At the standard 2.5 oz per gallon mix rate, you get 128 gallons of spray. A backpack sprayer at 20 gallons per acre covers roughly 6 acres of spot treatment, or 1–2 acres of broadcast application.

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Visible wilting in 3–5 days on annuals, 7–14 days on perennials. Full root kill takes 2–3 weeks. Don't mow or till treated areas for at least a week after application.
