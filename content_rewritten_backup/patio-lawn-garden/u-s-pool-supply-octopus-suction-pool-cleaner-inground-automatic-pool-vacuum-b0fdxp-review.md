The U.S. Pool Supply Octopus is the budget-friendly contender in the suction-side automatic cleaner arena. At roughly half the price of a Polaris or Hayward, it promises similar results. But does it deliver, or is this a case of "you get what you pay for"? After digging through 185 ratings averaging 3.7 stars, here's the real picture.

**What's in the Box**

You get the Octopus unit itself, a 32-foot feeder hose, a 5-foot leader hose, a flow control valve, and a swivel adapter. Notably absent: any kind of debris bag. This is a filter-fed cleaner, meaning everything it picks up goes straight to your pool's pump basket or filter. That's not inherently bad, but it changes how you maintain your system.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The Octopus's party trick is its scrubbing action. The unit uses a venturi-style suction design with two spinning brush heads that genuinely work to scrape algae and fine silt off pool floors. Buyers consistently report it handles green pools and heavy debris better than expected for the price. One reviewer noted, "It picked up stuff my old Polaris left behind for years." The weighted design helps it climb walls and navigate corners reasonably well, though it won't navigate around obstacles with the intelligence of pricier robotic units.

The build quality is where the 3.7 rating starts to make sense. The plastic body feels serviceable but not premium. The hose fittings on some units ship loose — a buyer reported, "The hose connections leaked air until I tightened them with pliers." Not a dealbreaker, but a quality-control annoyance.

**The Big Catch: Flow Requirements**

Here's the honest truth: this cleaner is only as good as your pump. It needs serious suction — ideally a dedicated vacuum line or a skimmer with strong flow. If you have a variable-speed pump running at low RPM for energy savings, the Octopus will crawl or stall. Several reviewers with older, weaker pumps reported it just "sat at the bottom, vibrating." Check your pump's flow rate before buying. If you're running anything under 1 HP, expect frustration.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the flow sensitivity, the lack of a debris bag means your filter becomes the collection point. If you have a sand filter, expect to backwash more often. Cartridge filter owners will be cleaning cartridges weekly. That's not a flaw per se, but it's a hidden maintenance cost many buyers don't anticipate.

The 32-foot hose is also shorter than some competitors' offerings. If your pool is larger than 20 feet across, you may need to purchase an extension hose separately — an added expense that chips away at the budget appeal.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is a solid pick for **first-time pool owners** who want an affordable automatic cleaner without committing to a $700+ robotic unit. It's also a good fit for **seasonal pool users** with adequate pump flow who need a workhorse for spring opening and fall closing. If you have a modestly sized inground pool and a decent pump, the Octopus will handle routine maintenance admirably.

**Skip It If**

Skip this if you have a **large pool (over 25 feet)** , a **weak pump**, or if you're the type who wants set-and-forget reliability. Also skip if you hate cleaning filters more frequently — this unit will make you work a bit harder on filtration maintenance.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with saltwater pools?**
A: Yes. The materials are saltwater-resistant, and buyers with salt systems report no premature corrosion issues.

**Q: Will it climb walls?**
A: It climbs, but not as aggressively as pricier models. It handles gentle slopes and straight walls well, but struggles with steep, curved surfaces or steps.

**Q: How often does it get stuck?**
A: It gets stuck on drains and ladders more often than robotic cleaners. Most buyers report freeing it once or twice a week, which is manageable for the price.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a standard inground pool, a pump with real suction power, and you're willing to trade a bit of convenience for significant savings.

**Skip it if** you want hands-off operation, have a weak pump, or own a large pool requiring longer reach.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A genuinely useful budget cleaner with real scrubbing muscle, held back by flow sensitivity and quality-control quirks. It's not a Polaris, but it costs a third as much. For many shoppers, that tradeoff is worth it.
