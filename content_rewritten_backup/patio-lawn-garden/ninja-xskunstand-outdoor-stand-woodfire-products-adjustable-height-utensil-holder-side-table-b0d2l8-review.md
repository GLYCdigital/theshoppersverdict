If you own a Ninja Woodfire outdoor grill or smoker, you already know the struggle: where do you put the tongs, the sauce brush, the thermometer, and the plate of seasoned chicken while you're working the fire? The Ninja XSKUNSTAND Outdoor Stand answers that question, but it's not without a quirk or two.

## What you actually get

The box contains the collapsible stand itself, a detachable utensil holder, and a side shelf. Assembly takes under five minutes — no tools required. The legs click into place with a satisfying snap, and the whole unit locks rigid when deployed. When you're done, it folds flat enough to slide behind a door or under a bench seat in an RV.

The stand is built from powder-coated steel tubing. It feels sturdy under load — I've had a full cast-iron Dutch oven and a cutting board on it simultaneously without any wobble. The height adjustment uses a simple pin-and-hole system, and the range is generous. At its lowest setting, it's usable seated next to a campfire; at its highest, it brings the work surface to a comfortable standing height for most adults (roughly 30 to 42 inches depending on how you set it).

## Real-world performance

The standout feature is the side shelf. It's not just a flat surface — it has a raised lip on three sides, so a plate of raw chicken won't slide off when you bump the stand. The utensil holder clips onto the side rail and holds up to five tools. I've used mine for a full grilling session: tongs, spatula, brush, and a meat thermometer all stayed organized and off the grill grate.

One thing the product images don't fully convey: the stand is compact. This is a support accessory, not a full prep table. The main surface is roughly 18 by 14 inches — enough for a cutting board or a platter, but not both. If you're expecting a full-size outdoor kitchen island, adjust your expectations.

## What buyers consistently praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects real satisfaction. Owners of the Ninja Woodfire grill specifically note how well it pairs with that unit. One reviewer wrote, "Finally, a place to put my stuff instead of on the grill lid or a wobbly folding chair." Another mentioned, "The height adjustment is the reason I bought this — I'm 6'4" and most stands are too low."

The foldability also earns repeated mentions. Multiple reviewers highlight using it for tailgating and camping, with one noting, "It fits in the trunk of my sedan with the grill and a cooler. That's the whole point."

## Where it falls short

No wheels. For a product marketed for outdoor use, the lack of casters is a genuine oversight. Once you've loaded it with a full platter and tools, you're not moving it without unloading first. It's not heavy — maybe 12 pounds — but it's awkward to lift when loaded.

The utensil holder slots are also narrow. My favorite heavy-duty grill tongs (the ones with thick rubber grips) didn't fit. I had to use a slimmer pair. If you've got oversized tools, check the slot width before you commit.

## FAQ

**Will this fit other grills, or just Ninja Woodfire?**
It's designed for the Woodfire line, but it's essentially a freestanding table. Any grill or smoker can sit next to it. The height adjustment makes it workable with most units.

**Is it stable on uneven ground?**
The legs have small rubber feet but no independent leveling. On a sloped patio or grassy campsite, you'll want to find a flat spot. On concrete or decking, it's rock solid.

**Can I leave it outside in the rain?**
The powder coating resists rust, but the pin-and-hole mechanism can seize if exposed to constant moisture. Store it under cover when not in use.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you own a Ninja Woodfire and want a dedicated, adjustable work surface that doesn't eat up your garage space. **Skip it if** you need wheels, plan to leave it outside year-round, or prefer a larger prep area.

This is a well-made accessory that solves a real problem for outdoor cooks. It's not perfect — the utensil slots and missing wheels are real annoyances — but for the price and portability, it's a smart add-on.
