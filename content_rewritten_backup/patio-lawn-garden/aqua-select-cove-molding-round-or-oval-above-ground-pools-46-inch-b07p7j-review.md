## The Unsung Hero of a Smooth Pool Floor

Most above-ground pool installations fail in one spot: the sharp 90-degree transition where the flat floor meets the vertical wall. Skip this step and your liner will stretch, wrinkle, and eventually puncture at that exact corner. The Aqua Select Cove Molding solves that problem with pre-formed foam wedges that create a smooth radius — and at 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, it's clearly doing the job for most buyers.

## What You Actually Get

Each package contains 10 linear feet of closed-cell foam cove, pre-cut at a 45-degree angle. The foam is firm but compressible — roughly 1.5 inches thick at the base, tapering to a thin edge. The 45-degree pre-form is the key feature here. If you've ever tried to build a cove from scratch using pool noodles or rolled foam, you know the frustration of trying to hold material in place while you backfill sand. This product eliminates that entirely.

The foam is dense enough to hold its shape under liner tension but soft enough to give slightly when someone steps on the pool floor. One reviewer noted, "The pre-formed angle made installation a breeze — I just laid it around the perimeter and it stayed put while I smoothed the liner over it." That's the experience most buyers report.

## Performance and Real-World Use

For a 24-foot round pool, you'll need roughly 75 linear feet — so plan on ordering eight packages. The math is straightforward: circumference (π × diameter) divided by 10, rounded up.

The closed-cell construction is genuinely water-resistant. Unlike open-cell foam that soaks up moisture and degrades, this material won't harbor mold or break down mid-season. Several long-term reviewers mentioned using it for multiple pool installs across different seasons without visible deterioration.

The surface texture is smooth enough that the liner slides over it without snagging, which means fewer wrinkles in the finished floor. That's the primary reason most people buy this product, and it delivers.

## Where It Falls Short

The honest tradeoff: this costs more than DIY alternatives. A pool noodle hacked into a cove costs pennies compared to this, though the results are rarely as clean. Some reviewers also noted that the seams between sections need to be taped on both sides to lay flat — and the tape isn't included. Use a good duct tape or gorilla tape rated for outdoor use, or you'll get a visible bump at each joint.

The foam can also shift if you're installing on a windy day before the liner is down. Weigh the sections down with sand or bricks as you go — a minor inconvenience, but worth planning for.

## Who Should Buy This

**First-time pool installers** — this removes the most confusing step of the setup process. **DIY perfectionists** who want a wrinkle-free floor without wrestling with custom-cut foam. **Anyone replacing a liner** on an existing pool where the old cove has flattened or deteriorated.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget and don't mind spending an hour coaxing pool noodles into shape, or if your pool is small enough that the floor-to-wall transition barely matters (pools under 12 feet often get away without a cove).

## FAQ

**Q: How much do I need for my pool?**
Measure your pool's diameter (round) or length and width (oval). For round pools: multiply diameter by 3.14, then divide by 10. Round up. For a 21-foot pool, that's about 66 feet — so seven packages.

**Q: Does this work on oval pools?**
Yes — the flexibility of the foam allows it to bend around curved corners. For tighter radius curves, you may need to notch the back of the foam slightly to help it bend without kinking.

**Q: Can I reuse this if I move the pool?**
Yes. The foam doesn't compress permanently under normal use. Just make sure it's dry before storing it flat for the winter.
