Let me cut through the marketing noise: the EF ECOFLOW DELTA Pro Ultra is not a camping gadget. This is a serious piece of home infrastructure disguised as a portable power station. At 115 pounds with the battery installed, it's the kind of unit you wheel into your garage, wire into a transfer switch, and forget about — until the grid drops.

**What you actually get**

The base unit ships with the DPU inverter (the brains), one 4kWh LFP battery pack, and an AC charging cable. You're looking at roughly the size of a mini-fridge — 26.5" tall, 15" deep, and about 18" wide. The front panel gives you a 4.3-inch touchscreen, six AC outlets (including a 240V NEMA 14-50 for EV charging), two USB-C PD ports at 100W each, and two USB-A ports. There's also an Anderson-style port for third-party solar, but you'll want EcoFlow's own panels for the smart tracking to work.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this unit with a 1,500W space heater, a 1,200W microwave, and a refrigerator simultaneously — it didn't break a sweat. The 7,200W continuous output is the headline feature, and it's legit. You can run a 240V well pump, an EV charger, or a full kitchen load without tripping the inverter. The 15ms transfer time means my desktop PC never blinked during a simulated outage test.

Battery chemistry is LiFePO4 — 4,000 cycles to 80% capacity. At daily use, that's over a decade before meaningful degradation. The 4kWh base capacity runs a typical fridge for about 36 hours. Add an extra battery (sold separately) and you're at 8kWh, which covers most homes for a full day of essentials.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews reflects real user satisfaction. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've owned the Delta Pro and the original Delta — this is the first one that actually replaces my generator. Ran my whole house for 11 hours during a storm and still had 40% battery left."* Another noted, *"The 240V output is the differentiator. I charge my Model 3 at 5.5kW overnight using solar input during the day."*

The expandability gets consistent love. Multiple reviewers mention starting with one battery and adding a second after their first outage — a smart buying strategy if you're budget-conscious.

**Where it falls short**

The weight is the obvious one. At 115 pounds, this isn't moving around your patio casually. EcoFlow sells a $99 dolly that should honestly be in the box. Several reviewers mention this: *"Great unit but plan its permanent location before you unpack it."*

Solar input is capped at 2,400W, which sounds generous until you realize that requires 4-6 panels (400W each) wired in series. That's another $1,200-$2,000 depending on panel choice. The unit works fine without solar — it charges from a wall outlet in about 2.5 hours — but if you want true off-grid capability, budget accordingly.

The app, while functional, occasionally drops Bluetooth connection and requires a firmware update out of the box. Nothing deal-breaking, but worth knowing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Homeowners with frequent outages** — this replaces a noisy generator for everything except multi-day events with no sun
- **EV owners** — the 240V output gives you emergency charging capability most competitors can't match
- **Off-grid cabin or workshop** — if you already have solar panels, this is your battery bank
- **Not for campers or tailgaters** — you'll want the smaller Delta 2 or Delta Pro for portability

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this power my whole house?**
With the 30A twist-lock outlet and a transfer switch (sold separately), yes — up to 7,200W of continuous load. That covers a fridge, furnace blower, lights, and a window AC simultaneously. You won't run a central AC or electric water heater.

**Q: How long does it take to charge?**
From a wall outlet: about 2.5 hours for the base 4kWh. With 2,400W of solar: roughly 3-4 hours in full sun. Fast charging is a standout feature versus competitors.

**Q: Is it weatherproof for outdoor use?**
The unit has an IP65-rated enclosure, meaning it handles rain and dust. But the AC outlets aren't gasketed, so keep it under an eave or in a shed when in use.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need serious, expandable backup power with 240V capability and plan to integrate solar. This is the best-in-class for whole-home backup in the portable category.

**Skip it if:** You need something you can carry, or you're only powering a CPAP and a phone overnight. The Delta 2 at a third the price handles that.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducted half a star for the missing wheels and the solar ecosystem cost. Everything else exceeds expectations.
