Let's be blunt about the cordless leaf blower market: most of them are toys. They push dry grass around a driveway and call it a day. The Toro F700 is not that. This is the first cordless blower I've used that genuinely makes me question why anyone buys a gas backpack unit for a suburban lot.

## What You're Actually Getting

The F700 is Toro's flagship 60V handheld. The box includes the blower, a 7.5 Ah battery, and a charger. That battery is massive — it's the same one Toro sells for their 60V mowers, which tells you something about the power draw here.

The specs are the headline: **700 CFM (cubic feet per minute) and 135 MPH** at the nozzle. For context, most cordless blowers from Ryobi or DeWalt sit in the 400-550 CFM range. Toro jumped a full tier ahead with this one. The design features a metal impeller — not plastic — which is the reason it can push that much air without shredding itself.

## How It Performs in the Real World

I tested this on a quarter-acre lot with mature oaks. The first thing you notice is that this thing *moves* leaves — wet ones, matted ones, the kind that stick to the lawn like they've been glued down. At full throttle, it clears a 10-foot path in one pass. You're not making three or four passes over the same strip.

The variable-speed trigger is a welcome relief. Squeeze lightly for a gentle sweep around flower beds, then mash it for the heavy stuff. The cruise-control lock is positioned so you can flip it with your thumb without shifting your grip — small detail, but it matters when you're out there for 45 minutes.

The battery situation is the honest tradeoff. Toro claims 30-45 minutes of runtime on the 7.5 Ah battery. In my testing, that's accurate if you're feathering the trigger. If you run it flat-out the whole time, expect closer to 20 minutes. It charges in about 2.5 hours, which means you'll want a second battery if you have a large property.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The Amazon reviews (229 ratings, 4.6 stars) echo my experience. One buyer put it well:

> "I was skeptical about cordless after my last one died in a year. This thing has power comparable to my old gas Echo. It just works."

Another frequent theme is the build quality:

> "The metal fan makes a difference. I've run over gravel and it doesn't phase it."

The most common complaint? Weight. At nearly 13 pounds with the battery, it wears on your arm. Several reviewers mention switching hands frequently during big jobs.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Weight:** This is a heavy handheld. If you have a large yard (half-acre or more), your forearm will burn well before the battery dies. Toro sells a shoulder strap accessory — get it.

**Noise:** It's loud. 71 dB at full throttle is similar to a gas blower. You absolutely need ear protection. If you have close neighbors, use the trigger lock at lower speeds for morning work.

**Battery ecosystem:** The 7.5 Ah battery uses Toro's newer 60V platform. If you already own older Toro 40V tools, those batteries won't work here. You're starting fresh.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a quarter-acre to half-acre lot with trees, you're tired of pulling a cord or mixing gas, and you want cordless power that actually competes with gas.

**Skip it if:** You have a tiny yard (under 5,000 square feet) — you're paying for capability you won't use. Also skip it if you have a full acre or more; at that point, get a backpack blower.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
A: Realistically 20-45 minutes depending on trigger use. The 7.5 Ah battery is the biggest Toro offers, but full-throttle work drains it fast. Plan for 30 minutes of mixed use.

**Q: Does it work for cleaning gutters or patios?**
A: Yes, but it's overkill. The variable trigger lets you dial it down for patios and decks. For gutters, you'll want the extension tube accessory — the nozzle is too wide to fit standard gutters.

**Q: Is this compatible with Ryobi or EGO batteries?**
A: No. It uses Toro's 60V battery system exclusively. If you're invested in another platform, stick with that brand.

## The Verdict

The Toro F700 is the best cordless handheld blower I've used, period. It matches gas performance without the maintenance headaches. The weight and price (it's not cheap) are the only real reasons to look elsewhere.

**Buy it if** you want gas-level power without gas-level hassle. **Skip it if** your yard is small or you're already deep into another battery platform.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — deducting only for the weight and average battery life. This is the blower to beat in 2026.
