**The Panovue Fire Pit Is the Right Kind of Heavy**

Most fire pits under $100 are basically tin cans with legs. They rust out in a season, tip over if a dog bumps them, and the spark screen bends like aluminum foil. The Panovue heavy-duty wood burning fire pit is not that. At 4.3 stars across 215 ratings, it's earning its keep in backyards where people actually burn wood — not just toast marshmallows once in June.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the fire bowl, three legs, a spark screen with a lift handle, a log grate, a poker, and the hardware to bolt it together. The bowl measures 32 inches across and roughly 13 inches deep — big enough for a real fire, not a decorative one. The steel is thick enough that you can feel the difference when you knock on it. Assembly is genuinely simple: flip the bowl, bolt on the legs, drop in the grate. Fifteen minutes, no swearing required.

**How It Performs in Real Use**

Here's where the Panovue separates itself from the cheap stuff. The depth of the bowl means logs sit below the rim, which keeps embers from shooting across your deck. The spark screen actually fits snugly — no gaps where sparks sneak through. The poker is long enough that you're not leaning into smoke to poke at logs.

One thing that surprised me in customer feedback: multiple reviewers noted how hot the fire gets. That sounds obvious, but shallow pits radiate heat outward and waste it. The deep bowl directs heat up and out to people sitting around it. One reviewer put it plainly: *"This thing throws serious heat. We sat around it in 40-degree weather and were comfortable."*

The log grate deserves a mention. It's not the flimsy wire grate you see on budget pits. It keeps wood elevated for airflow, which means you get a proper burn instead of a smoldering mess. Ash cleanup is straightforward — lift the grate, shovel out the bowl.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The durability theme runs through the reviews. *"Had mine for two seasons now and it still looks brand new,"* one buyer noted. Another said, *"Built like a tank. My neighbor's cheap pit rusted out in one winter; this one doesn't even have surface rust."* The stability gets called out too — the legs are welded solidly, and the wide stance means no wobble on uneven ground.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, there's no lid. If you leave it outside, rain pools in the bowl, and standing water accelerates rust. Plan to buy a cover or flip it upside down when not in use.

Second, the factory paint on the inside burns off during the first few fires. It's normal for powder-coated pits, but it looks like something's wrong — gray flakes and a smell that lingers for the first burn or two. Don't panic; it's cosmetic.

Third, at roughly 40 pounds, this is not a portable pit. You're not dragging it to the beach or moving it from patio to yard on a whim. It lives where you put it.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The backyard entertainer** who wants a reliable centerpiece for weekend gatherings — this handles a full fire ring of chairs.
- **The wood-burning purist** who'd rather split logs than deal with propane tanks and hissing burners.
- **The value shopper** who's tired of replacing cheap pits every other year.

Skip it if you need something portable, want a gas hookup, or plan to leave it uncovered in a rainy climate.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the paint really burn off on the first use?**
Yes. It's baked-on powder coat, and it will flake inside the bowl during the first burn. It's cosmetic, not a defect. Burn a small fire the first time to get it over with.

**Q: Can you cook on this fire pit?**
Technically yes, but it's not designed as a grill. The grate sits low, so you'd be cooking close to the flames. Better to use a cast iron grate accessory or stick to roasting sticks.

**Q: How long does the steel last if left outside?**
With a cover, several years. Without one, expect surface rust within a season, especially in humid climates. A $15 cover is cheap insurance.

**Skip it if** you need portability, prefer gas convenience, or won't bother covering it between uses.
