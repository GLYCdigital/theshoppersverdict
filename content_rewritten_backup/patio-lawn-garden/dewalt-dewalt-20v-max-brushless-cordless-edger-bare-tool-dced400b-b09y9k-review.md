If you're already holding DEWALT 20V MAX batteries, this edger is the missing piece in your lawn toolkit. If you're not, the DCED400B has a steeper entry price than you might expect — and that's the first thing to understand.

## What You Actually Get

This is a **bare tool**. No battery, no charger, no manual in the box beyond the standard paperwork. What you do get is the edger itself, a metal blade guard, and the tool-free depth adjustment mechanism. It's designed to pair with any DEWALT 20V MAX battery you already own — the 5Ah and 6Ah packs work best for reasonable runtimes.

The head pivots for curb edging, which sounds gimmicky but genuinely works. You can angle it to run along sidewalk edges without cranking your wrist into an unnatural position. As shown in the product image, the blade is a standard 7.5-inch metal edging blade that cuts a clean trench.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The brushless motor is the star here. It spins the blade fast enough to chew through thick bermuda or fescue without bogging down, and it does it with a smooth, consistent torque that feels closer to a gas edger than most cordless rivals. On a 5Ah battery, you'll get roughly 25-30 minutes of actual edging — enough for a typical suburban front and back yard.

The blade depth adjustment is a thumb wheel on the side. It's genuinely tool-free, which matters more than you'd think when you're mid-job and need to cut deeper for a clean line. The guard does a decent job of deflecting debris, though it's not perfect — more on that below.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

The 4.3-star average across 215 ratings reflects real satisfaction. Multiple owners highlight the power-to-weight tradeoff. One reviewer put it plainly: "I was skeptical a battery edger could handle my St. Augustine grass, but this thing cuts through it like butter." Another noted the pivot head was the deciding factor: "Being able to angle it for curbs saved my back."

Battery compatibility also shows up repeatedly. Owners already in the 20V MAX system appreciate not buying another proprietary battery platform.

## Where It Falls Short

The weight is the most common complaint. At around 12 pounds with a 5Ah battery, it's not heavy by gas edger standards, but the weight sits forward. On longer runs — say, edging a 100-foot driveway — your arms will feel it. The front-heavy balance is the tradeoff for the powerful motor.

The dust and debris control is imperfect. The guard catches most of the discharge, but you'll still get grass clippings on your pants and shoes. Wear appropriate footwear.

A few owners reported the blade guard arrived slightly misaligned, causing the blade to rub. It's a 10-minute fix with a wrench, but it's annoying on a premium-priced tool.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You already own DEWALT 20V MAX batteries, you have a modest-sized lawn, and you want cordless convenience without sacrificing cut quality. It's ideal for homeowners who edge weekly and want to skip the gas can and pull cord entirely.

**Skip it if:** You're starting from scratch with no DEWALT batteries — the bare tool plus a battery and charger pushes the real cost over $250. Also skip if you have a sprawling property with hundreds of feet of edging; you'll want a gas model or a second battery.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my older 20V MAX batteries?**
A: Yes, it's compatible with all DEWALT 20V MAX batteries, including the older 2Ah and 3Ah packs. Just know that smaller batteries drain fast — expect 10-15 minutes max on a 2Ah.

**Q: Can it edge along brick pavers without chipping them?**
A: The metal blade will mark soft brick if you're careless. Adjust the depth shallow and angle the head slightly. It works fine on concrete and asphalt curbs.

**Q: Does the blade need sharpening or replacing?**
A: It's a standard edger blade that can be sharpened with a file or angle grinder. Replacement blades are widely available and easy to swap with the included wrench.
