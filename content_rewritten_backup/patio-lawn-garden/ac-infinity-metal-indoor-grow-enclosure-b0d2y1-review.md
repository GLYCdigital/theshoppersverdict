You've seen the fabric tents. They work, but they wobble, sag, and eventually the zippers give out. AC Infinity built this metal enclosure to answer those complaints — and for the most part, it delivers. But at 4.0 stars from 200 ratings, it's not a slam dunk. Here's the real breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the assembled metal frame structure, reflective interior lining, zippered front door with viewing window, and all hardware. No floor tray, no light traps, no ducting. The metal panels lock together with a more rigid connection than any fabric tent I've tested. As shown in the product image, the front door opens fully for unobstructed access — a genuine improvement over the roll-up flaps on cheaper tents.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The metal construction is the headline feature, and it earns the praise. The frame doesn't flex when you hang a 25-pound LED fixture or attach oscillating fans to the interior poles. One buyer put it plainly: "I've had fabric tents for years. This thing doesn't budge when I bump into it. Feels like furniture, not a tent."

Light-proofing is excellent when everything's zipped. The metal-to-metal seams don't leak like fabric stitching often does. For anyone running photoperiod plants that need absolute darkness during flower, this matters more than any other spec.

But there's a catch: the ventilation ports. They're smaller than the 6-inch and 8-inch openings you're used to on comparable fabric tents. If you already own an AC Infinity inline fan and ducting, you'll likely need an adapter. Several reviewers flagged this, and it's a legitimate friction point if you're upgrading from a tent setup.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The reflective interior is genuinely effective. Multiple reviewers noted more uniform growth and better lower-branch development compared to their old tents. One grower summed it up: "My canopy is way more even. The light bounces around in there instead of getting absorbed."

Assembly quality also gets consistent credit. Panels align properly, screws thread cleanly, and the included instructions are actually readable — rare in this category.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the ventilation port issue, the biggest complaint is the missing floor tray. At this price point, that's frustrating. You'll be wiping runoff off your floor unless you buy a separate tray that fits the footprint.

Assembly is also more involved than a pop-up tent. Budget 45 minutes to an hour, not the 15 minutes advertised. The metal panels are heavier and less forgiving if you misalign a corner.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Serious indoor growers** who want a permanent, sturdy home for their plants — not a temporary pop-up
- **Photoperiod growers** who need guaranteed light-proofing during dark cycles
- **Anyone sick of replacing zippers** on fabric tents every year or two

Skip it if you're a beginner on a budget, if you're moving frequently and need portability, or if you already own standard 6-inch ducting without adapters.

## FAQ

**Q: Does it come with a floor tray?**
A: No. Buy a boot tray or plastic liner sized to the enclosure footprint before your first watering session.

**Q: Will my existing AC Infinity inline fan fit the ports?**
A: Not directly. The ports are smaller than standard tent openings. Check the product dimensions and grab an adapter kit — the brand sells them separately.

**Q: Is it truly light-proof?**
A: Yes, when fully zipped. This is one of the best-sealed enclosures at this price point. No pinprick leaks along seams.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you're setting up a permanent grow space and want something that won't wobble, sag, or leak light. The metal frame is a genuine upgrade over fabric.

**Skip it if** you need portability, hate adapter hunting, or don't want to buy a separate floor tray. 

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, sturdy enclosure that cuts corners in exactly the wrong places (ventilation, floor tray) but nails the core job: holding plants and keeping light in.
