The EarthWay 2600A-PLUS sits in an awkward spot: it's priced like a serious homeowner tool but built with some compromises that'll frustrate perfectionists. At 3.7 stars across 185 ratings, the consensus is "good bones, finicky delivery." Here's what that means for your lawn.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the spreader fully assembled except for the handle — about five minutes of work with a screwdriver. The 40-pound hopper is the headline feature; it's genuinely useful for anyone with a third of an acre or more. The all-terrain wheels (10-inch diameter) are the other standout, with aggressive tread that tracks straight over lumpy ground. You also get an EdgeGuard lever that stops broadcast from the right side, letting you run along beds and driveways without overspray. The spreader uses a standard broadcast pattern — no drop-spread option here.

**How it performs in the real world**

The 40-pound capacity is the reason most people buy this. On a typical suburban lot, you'll cover the whole lawn on one fill if you're spreading at recommended rates. The wheels genuinely do roll well over ruts and small bumps — this is the one area where the build feels above its price class.

The calibration is where things get dicey. The dial has numbered settings, but they're not tied to any manufacturer chart in the manual; you're expected to figure out the right setting for your specific fertilizer through trial and error. Several buyers note the flow rate drifts as the hopper empties — one reviewer put it plainly: *"I set it for a moderate rate and by the end of the bag it was dumping heavier in the turns."* The impeller plate does fling material in a consistent arc, so the spread pattern itself is fine — it's the volume control that's inconsistent.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The EdgeGuard feature gets near-universal love. It's mechanical, reliable, and saves you from the "yellow stripe of shame" along your flower beds. Owners also repeatedly mention the all-terrain wheels as a genuine upgrade over cheaper spreaders. As one five-star reviewer wrote: *"Rolls through my bumpy backyard without lurching — my old Scotts bounced all over the place."* The 40-pound hopper is also frequently cited as the reason they chose this over competitors.

**Where it falls short**

The plastic gearbox is the weak point. It's not sealed, so moisture and fertilizer dust can get in and gum up the works over a season or two. A handful of reviewers report the handle flexing noticeably when pushing heavy loads up a slope — it's not going to snap, but it doesn't inspire confidence. The biggest functional gripe is clumping: with fine granule fertilizers like lime or pelletized gypsum, the rate dial can clog mid-spread, requiring a shake to clear. That's frustrating mid-job. Assembly is simple but the bolts are small and easy to strip if you overtighten.

**Who should buy this**

This spreader fits two people well: homeowners with uneven, bumpy terrain who want the all-terrain wheels, and anyone with a large enough lawn that the 40-pound hopper saves them three trips across the yard. It's also a decent choice if you want the EdgeGuard feature without paying $150+ for a tow-behind unit.

Skip it if you're on flat, even ground with a small lawn — a cheaper drop spreader will give you more precise control for less money. Also skip it if you're planning to spread fine powders regularly; the clumping issue will drive you mad.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this spreader handle lime or pelletized gypsum?**
A: Coarse pellets work fine. Fine powders clump at the dial mechanism — you'll need to stop and shake it periodically. Plan accordingly.

**Q: How do I calibrate the spreader for my fertilizer?**
A: The manual doesn't give a chart. Start with the dial at 3 and test on a small area, then adjust. Expect to waste a little product finding the right setting.

**Q: Is assembly really that simple?**
A: Yes, the main body comes pre-assembled. You attach the handle with four bolts and you're done — about 10 minutes total.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have uneven terrain and want the 40-pound capacity to cut refill trips. The wheels and EdgeGuard justify the price for those specific needs.

**Skip it if** you're on flat ground or want precise, consistent spreading of fine products. You'll be fighting the flow rate all season.

This is a solid 3.7/5 — a capable spreader with real strengths for specific use cases, but not the universal tool its price suggests. If the features match your lawn, you'll be happy. If not, look elsewhere.
