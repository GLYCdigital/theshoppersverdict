**Coleman Skydome Tent Review: 4.2/5 Stars**

---

**The Short Version**

The Coleman Skydome with the Full-Fly Weather Vestibule is the rare tent that actually delivers on its headline promise. The 10-foot-wide dome design gives you near-vertical walls and a genuinely usable 6-foot interior height—meaning you can stand up, change clothes, and move around without the claustrophobic crouch most dome tents force. With 215 Amazon ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in backyards and campsites. But the "Full-Fly" marketing name hides a real limitation, and you need to know exactly what you're buying before you pull the trigger.

---

**What’s in the Box**

You get the tent body, the full-coverage rainfly (this is the key upgrade over the standard Skydome), a pole set with the signature hinged door frame, stakes, guylines, and a carry bag that's actually large enough to repack without rage. The vestibule is integrated into the rainfly design, not a separate add-on—so setup is still a one-person job at roughly 10 minutes once you've done it once. The included stakes are adequate for fair weather but you'll want to upgrade if you camp in wind or hard ground.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The 68D polyester body with a 1,200mm-coated fly is decent for the price point. The welded floor corners are a genuine improvement over stitched seams—water stays out where the ground meets the tent. The full fly, when properly guyed out, creates a true vestibule that's big enough for a couple pairs of boots and a small cooler, keeping your sleeping area dry and clean.

But here's the honest catch: the full fly does not extend to the ground on all sides. It covers the top and sides with a bathtub floor underneath, but there's a gap between the fly's bottom edge and the ground on the vestibule side. In heavy rain with wind, you'll get some splash-back under the fly. It's not a dealbreaker—the vestibule is still significantly better than the half-fly on the standard Skydome—but don't expect a four-season expedition shelter.

The 10-foot width is the real differentiator. At 4.2 feet of interior floor space per person in the 6-person model, it's genuinely roomier than the 6-person average of 3.5-4 feet. The near-vertical walls mean you can actually use that space, not just lie in it.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The standing height is the runaway favorite. As one verified buyer put it: *"I'm 6'1" and I can stand up straight. That alone sold me."* Another reviewer highlighted the vestibule's practicality: *"The fly kept everything dry through a full night of rain—boots, gear, everything. My old tent would have been soaked."*

Setup speed gets consistent mentions. The pre-attached poles and the hinged door frame mean you're not fumbling with separate pole sleeves. Most buyers report 8-12 minutes solo.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

The vestibule gap I mentioned is the biggest complaint. One reviewer noted: *"In a real downpour with wind, water pooled at the base of the fly and seeped under the edge. Not a flood, but damp gear."* The included stakes are flimsy—plan on replacing them if you camp in anything but soft soil.

Ventilation is adequate but not stellar. The two ground vents help, but on humid nights without a breeze, you'll get condensation on the fly interior. The carry bag, while larger than competitors', is still a tight squeeze when the tent is dry and properly folded.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Weekend car campers** who want a roomy, easy-up shelter with decent weather protection
- **Families with kids** — the interior height and 10-foot width make it feel like a small cabin, not a coffin
- **Tall campers** (5'10" and up) who are tired of hunched-over tent living
- **Budget-conscious buyers** who want full-fly performance without paying $400+ for premium brands

**Skip it if** you're a serious backpacker (it's 22 pounds, not pack-friendly), or you camp exclusively in exposed, high-wind areas where you need a ground-to-fly seal.

---

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the vestibule keep gear dry in heavy rain?**  
A: Yes, in moderate rain it's fully effective. In sustained downpours with wind, you may get minor splash-back at the base. Keep gear off the floor of the vestibule and you'll be fine.

**Q: How long does setup actually take?**  
A: First time, 15-20 minutes. After that, 8-12 minutes with one person. The hinged door frame and pre-attached poles make it one of the faster full-size tents to pitch.

**Q: Is the 4-person version big enough for two adults plus gear?**  
A: Yes, comfortably. The 4-person is 8 feet wide with the same 6-foot height. Two adults plus cots or a queen air mattress fit with room to spare.

---

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a roomy, tall, easy-pitching tent for car camping and you're willing to accept that "full fly" means "full coverage from above" rather than "sealed to the ground." At the typical street price, it's one of the best values in its category.

**Skip it if** you camp in sustained high winds, need a true four-season shelter, or expect the vestibule to be a mudroom in all conditions.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Skydome with Full-Fly delivers on the features that matter most (space, height, setup speed) and only stumbles on the edge cases. For the 90% of campers who want a comfortable weekend shelter, it's a solid, honest purchase.
