You've wrestled a standard rake for years — the one that forces you to bend at the waist until your lower back files a formal complaint. The Serqd Adjustable Garden Rake attacks that exact problem with a telescoping shaft that stretches from 36 to 73 inches. Is it a gimmick or a genuine upgrade? I tested it against a season's worth of oak leaves, pine needles, and lawn debris to find out.

**What's in the Box**

You get the fully assembled rake — no tools required. The handle is aluminum with a textured grip at the top and a secondary grip zone about 18 inches down for two-handed raking. The head is 18 inches wide with 22 spring-tempered steel tines, each about 2.5 inches long and spaced tightly enough to catch small debris without clogging. The whole unit weighs 3.1 pounds, which is heavier than a typical $15 fiberglass rake but reasonable given the telescoping mechanism.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The telescoping lock is the make-or-break feature here, and Serqd got it right. A push-button collar releases the inner shaft, and when you click it back, the extension holds firm — no twisting, no slipping, even when I leaned into heavy piles of wet leaves. At full extension, I'm 6'1" and didn't bend my spine once during a 45-minute session. At the shortest setting, it tucks into tight spaces like between shrubs or under low-hanging branches.

The tines are the real standout. They're stiff enough to pull matted leaves but flexible enough to spring back without bending permanently. I accidentally raked over a buried garden hose and the tines flexed, then returned to shape — no kinks, no damage. The 18-inch width is a genuine time-saver; I cleared my 40x50 foot backyard in about 25% fewer passes than my old 14-inch rake.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews isn't random. Owners repeatedly mention the same wins:

- "I'm 5'2\" and my husband is 6'4\" — we both use this without adjusting our posture. It's the first rake that works for both of us."
- "The extension actually locks. I've had cheap telescoping tools that collapse mid-swipe. This one doesn't."

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The added weight is real — 3.1 pounds feels fine for 20 minutes, but an hour of continuous raking will fatigue your forearms more than a lightweight fiberglass rake. Also, the head angle is fixed at 90 degrees. If you need to aggressively dethatch a lawn or rake under low decks, you'll want a rake with adjustable tine pitch. Finally, the telescoping collar collects dirt over time; a quick rinse and occasional silicone spray keeps it smooth, but it's maintenance a one-piece rake doesn't need.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Couples with different heights**: The 37-inch adjustment range means one rake fits everyone in the household.
- **Anyone with back or knee issues**: Standing upright while raking changes the ergonomics completely.
- **Small-yard owners with storage constraints**: It collapses to 36 inches, so it hangs easily in a garage or even a closet.
- **Skip it if**: You're a professional landscaper logging 8-hour days — the weight and fixed head angle become limiting. Buy a dedicated dethatching rake instead.

**FAQ**

*Q: Will the handle hold up if I lean my weight on it?*
A: The aluminum shaft is sturdy, but the locking collar is the weak point. It holds under normal raking force, but don't use it as a walking stick or pry bar. One reviewer bent the collar doing exactly that.

*Q: Does it work on wet leaves?*
A: Yes, but wet leaves are heavier and will strain the tines more. Work in smaller piles and let the leaves dry if possible.

*Q: Can I replace the tines if one breaks?*
A: No. The tines are riveted into the head. The good news is that spring-tempered steel rarely breaks — I've seen no reports of tine failure in the reviews.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're tired of hunching over a short rake, you share raking duties with someone of a different height, or you want one tool that handles both lawn and gutter cleanup. **Skip it if** you need a heavy-duty dethatcher or you're a stickler for minimal weight.

The Serqd Adjustable Garden Rake earns a solid **4.6 out of 5 stars**. It solves a real ergonomic problem without sacrificing raking performance. The tradeoffs — extra weight and a fixed head angle — are acceptable for the vast majority of homeowners. For under $40, it's one of the smartest garden tool upgrades you'll make this season.
