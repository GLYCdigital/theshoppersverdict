**If you own a zero-turn mower and you're still using cinder blocks and a floor jack, stop reading and buy this. If you only own a standard riding mower, you can skip it.**

The MoJack MJ750XT isn't the cheapest mower lift on Amazon, and it's not the most elaborate. But after 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's proven itself as the go-to choice for zero-turn owners who are tired of lying on cold concrete with a flashlight between their teeth.

Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the lift frame, a hydraulic-style foot pump, two front wheel cradles, and a stabilizer bar. That's it. No batteries, no compressor, no complicated hydraulic lines to bleed.

Assembly is genuinely tool-free — the locking pins and clips are all hand-operated. Most buyers report 15–25 minutes from box to first lift. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Put together in about 15 minutes by myself. Instructions were clear enough that I didn't have to call my son."*

## How It Performs

The MJ750XT uses a mechanical screw-and-gear mechanism driven by a foot pedal — no hydraulics to leak or fail. You pump the pedal to raise the mower, and a safety bar locks into place automatically. To lower, you release the bar with your foot and the mower descends under controlled resistance.

The 750-pound capacity covers virtually every residential zero-turn on the market, plus many light-commercial models like the Scag Freedom Z and Gravely ZT HD. The 33-inch lift height gives you real access to decks, spindles, and blades without the mower being so high it feels tippy.

The footprint is where this shines. The MJ750XT's wheelbase is wide enough that even a 60-inch deck mower feels stable during the lift. A long-time owner noted: *"I've lifted my 54-inch Hustler on this thing three times this season. It hasn't budged, wobbled, or made me nervous once."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **The foot-pump mechanism** — no bending over to crank a handle. You stand upright and pump.
- **Stability** — the wide base and rubber pads prevent the mower from sliding during lifting.
- **Build quality** — heavy-gauge steel, welded joints, powder-coated finish that resists rust.
- **Time savings** — blade changes go from a 45-minute ordeal to a 15-minute job.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest with yourself about the weight. The MJ750XT weighs around 90 pounds. The integrated casters help you wheel it around, but you'll still grunt moving it across a rough garage floor or up a curb. If you have a bad back, get a second person or a helper wheel.

The 33-inch height is another tradeoff. You'll be comfortable doing blade swaps and deck cleaning, but you're not walking under the mower. Oil changes and belt replacements will still require you to sit or kneel. If you need full stand-up access, you're looking at a different category of lift that costs three times as much.

A handful of reviewers mention the safety bar alignment: *"Works great once you get the hang of it, but the first few times I had to fiddle with the bar to get it to catch."* It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You own a zero-turn mower (42-inch deck or larger), you do your own blade sharpening or deck cleaning more than twice a season, and you value not crawling on the floor.

**Skip it if:** You have a standard riding mower (the cradles are designed specifically for zero-turn front wheels), you pay a shop for all maintenance, or you need full under-mower standing access.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this lift my 60-inch deck zero-turn?**
A: Yes, provided the mower weighs under 750 pounds. Most residential 60-inch deck models (like the Toro TimeCutter and Husqvarna Z254) come in at 650–720 pounds. Check your owner's manual for the dry weight.

**Q: Can one person use it safely?**
A: Yes, for the lifting operation itself. The foot pump and mechanical lock make it a one-person job. The only moment you might want help is positioning the mower onto the cradles and moving the lift around your garage.

**Q: Does it work on grass or uneven ground?**
A: Technically yes, but you shouldn't. The lift needs a flat, hard surface. On grass or a sloped driveway, the mower can shift during lifting. Use it on concrete or pavers only.
