A porch swing that jerks to a stop every time someone sits down isn't relaxing — it's annoying, and it slowly wrecks your chains. That's the problem these springs solve. The 2Pack Premium Porch Swing Springs are a simple, low-cost fix that smooths out the ride, and with a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, they're clearly doing something right. But are they right for *your* swing? Let's dig in.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two complete spring assemblies. Each spring is made from galvanized steel — that's important because it means they won't rust into orange streaks on your porch within a year. You get the coil spring itself, plus mounting hooks and S-hooks already attached. As shown in the product image, the hardware is all there to connect the spring between your swing chain and the ceiling mount.

One thing that isn't included: lag bolts for the ceiling side. If you're mounting to a wooden beam, you'll need 1/4-inch or 3/8-inch lag bolts rated for your swing's weight. Most porch swing kits include those, but if you're replacing old springs, check your existing hardware before you start.

## How They Perform

Installation takes about 10 minutes per spring. You attach one end to the ceiling mount, then connect your swing chain to the other end using the S-hook. The key is to install both springs at equal tension — if one is tighter than the other, your swing will hang crooked and twist.

Once installed, the difference is immediate. The springs absorb the initial weight when you sit down, so the swing glides smoothly instead of snapping. They also reduce the bounce when someone hops off. For heavier adults (200+ pounds), the springs compress noticeably and give a softer, more cushioned ride. For lighter users, the motion feels subtler — the springs are stiff enough that a 100-pound person might barely feel them engage.

Customers consistently mention the smooth motion. One reviewer noted, "My wife has knee issues and getting on the swing used to be a jarring experience. These springs fixed that completely." Another said, "Took the strain off the chains — I'm not worried about them snapping anymore." That's the real value: protecting your chains and your joints.

## Where They Fall Short

The most common complaint is that these springs are on the firmer side. If you have a lightweight resin or small two-person swing, the springs may not compress much at all — you'll get a subtle dampening effect, not a dramatic bouncy ride. For those setups, a lighter-gauge spring is a better match.

Also, the galvanized finish — while rust-resistant — isn't exactly pretty. It's utilitarian gray metal, not decorative. If your porch swing is a showpiece, you might want to paint the springs or hide them with covers, because they're visible when you look up.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You have a wooden or metal porch swing with chains, you weigh over 150 pounds, or you're tired of the jarring stop every time you sit down. They're also ideal if your chains are showing wear — the springs take the strain off the links.

**Skip them if:** You have a lightweight decorative swing, you're under 130 pounds, or you're looking for a dramatic bouncy motion. These are dampeners, not amusement park springs.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my swing?**
A: They fit any chain-style swing with standard 1/4-inch or 3/8-inch hooks. Measure your chain link opening — if it's wider than 1/2 inch, you'll need to swap the S-hooks for larger ones.

**Q: Do I need both springs?**
A: Yes, use both — one on each side. A single spring will cause the swing to list to one side and eventually twist the frame. That's why this is a 2-pack.

**Q: How much weight can they handle?**
A: Reviews indicate they work well for 300+ pound combined loads. The galvanized steel is rated for the strain, but your ceiling mount and chains are the actual limiting factors — make sure those are sound.
