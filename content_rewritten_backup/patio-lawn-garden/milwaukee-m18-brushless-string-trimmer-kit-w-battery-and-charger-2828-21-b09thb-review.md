The Milwaukee M18 Brushless String Trimmer Kit (model 2828-21) is the cordless trimmer that contractors and lawn-obsessed homeowners keep coming back to. With 229 Amazon ratings averaging 4.6 stars, it's clear this tool earns its reputation. But at this price point, you deserve to know exactly what you're getting — and where it cuts corners.

## What's Actually in the Box

This kit includes the trimmer head, a Milwaukee M18 5.0Ah High Output battery, and the standard M18 charger. No shoulder strap, no extra spool, no edger attachment. The bare tool weighs 8.4 pounds, and with the 5.0Ah battery attached, you're looking at about 11 pounds total. That's heavier than some competitors, but the weight distribution is surprisingly well-balanced — the battery sits low and forward, which takes strain off your arms during extended trimming sessions.

The shaft is 62 inches long, which suits most users between 5'6" and 6'2" without requiring a bend. The handle rotates 180 degrees, and the auxiliary handle adjusts for grip position.

## Performance: Where It Shines

The brushless motor delivers a fixed 15-inch cutting swath with 0.080-inch line. There's no variable speed trigger — it's either on or off at full power. That's a deliberate tradeoff: Milwaukee prioritized torque over finesse. The result is a trimmer that chews through overgrown fescue, crabgrass, and even saplings up to a quarter-inch thick without stalling.

The line feed system is a "bump feed" design with a twist — it uses a gravel guard that actually works. Most trimmers' bump feeds jam when you tap them on pavement, but this one feeds cleanly on hard surfaces. That's a small detail that makes a big difference in real-world use.

Battery life with the included 5.0Ah pack runs about 25-30 minutes of continuous heavy trimming, or roughly 45 minutes of typical start-stop yard work. For a quarter-acre lot, you'll get the job done on a single charge. Half an acre? Plan on buying a second battery.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The power-to-weight ratio gets mentioned constantly. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical about an electric trimmer, but this one has more balls than my old gas Stihl."* Another noted the build quality: *"The metal gear case and rubberized grip feel like a pro tool, not a homeowner toy."*

The line retention is also a recurring theme. The dual-line system feeds 0.080-inch line that doesn't shatter on fences or concrete curbs. Multiple reviewers reported going weeks without reloading, which is rare for any trimmer.

## Where It Falls Short

The single-speed motor is the biggest limitation. Around flower beds or delicate landscaping, you can't feather the throttle — you're either full send or off. That's a genuine inconvenience for meticulous gardeners.

The 5.0Ah battery is decent, but not the High Output 8.0Ah or 12.0Ah packs Milwaukee offers. For $199-$249 (depending on the day) you can grab a second 5.0Ah, but that adds up. Also, the trimmer head doesn't pivot for edging — you have to rotate your wrists awkwardly to cut vertical edges.

One more note: the charger is the standard M18 charger, not the rapid charger. A full charge takes about 60-70 minutes, which is fine overnight but annoying if you're doing back-to-back jobs.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The M18 ecosystem owner**: If you already have Milwaukee batteries, this is a no-brainer. The bare tool version is cheaper, and you'll likely have compatible packs.
- **The gas-to-electric convert**: If you're tired of pull cords, fuel mixing, and carburetor maintenance, this trimmer delivers comparable power with zero hassle.
- **The small-to-midsize lot owner**: Quarter-acre and under, this is perfect. Half-acre and up, budget for a second battery.

Skip it if you need variable speed control for precise landscaping, or if you're on a tight budget — there are competent corded trimmers for under $100, and lighter battery options from other brands for less.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the head pivot for edging?**
No. The head is fixed, so edging requires manual rotation of the tool. It works, but it's not as comfortable as dedicated edging trimmers.

**Q: Can you use thicker line, like 0.095-inch?**
The trimmer is designed for 0.080-inch line. Thicker line may not fit the spool properly and could cause feed issues. Stick with what's recommended.

**Q: How long does the battery actually last?**
Real-world testing shows 25-35 minutes of continuous medium-to-heavy trimming. Light touch-up work can stretch that to 45 minutes.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want gas-level power without gas-level maintenance, you're already in the M18 ecosystem, or you value build quality that survives years of abuse.

**Skip it if** you need variable speed for precision work, or you're trimming more than half an acre without a second battery.

This is a professional-grade trimmer hiding in a homeowner-friendly package. It's not the cheapest option, but it's one of the few cordless trimmers that genuinely replaces a gas unit without compromise. The fixed-speed limitation is real, but for 90% of trimming tasks, you won't miss it.
