**The $60 question: does a 3-in-1 cordless trimmer actually replace three separate tools?** The DURATECH 3-in-1 tries to answer that with a hedge trimmer, grass trimmer, and shrubber head all sharing one 20V battery. At 3.8 stars from 190 ratings, buyers are split — some call it a "game changer for small yards," while others report it dying mid-job. Here's the real breakdown.

**What's in the box:** The trimmer body, three interchangeable heads (hedge blade, grass trimmer line head, and shrubber), one 2.0Ah battery, a charger, and a basic manual. No carrying case, no spare line spool, no gloves. The heads click on via a spring-loaded release — it genuinely takes less than 10 seconds to swap, and the mechanism feels solid, not flimsy. The hedge blade measures 8 inches; the grass head spins a single .065-inch line. Extension pole? Not included, so you're working at ground level.

**Performance and build quality:** The 20V motor delivers enough torque for soft new growth, but this is not a brush-cutter. On hedges with established woody stems over 3/8-inch, the blade stalls — one reviewer put it plainly: "It chews through new boxwood shoots like butter but chokes on anything with real wood in it." The grass trimmer head is adequate for edges and under fences, but the single line snaps frequently if you hit a rock or hard edging. You'll burn through the included spool fast.

The 2.0Ah battery is the weak link. Advertised run time is around 60 minutes, but real-world cutting — not idling — gets you 25–35 minutes. A second battery is nearly mandatory for any yard bigger than a postage stamp, and DURATECH sells them separately. There's no charge indicator on the tool; the battery pack has a small LED, but you can't see it while attached.

**What buyers consistently praise:** The ergonomics. At 4.5 pounds, it's noticeably lighter than corded alternatives, and the rubberized grip reduces vibration. Multiple reviewers mention using it for "overhead trimming without arm fatigue." The tool-free head swap is also a genuine win — one buyer noted, "I switched from hedge to grass in the middle of a job without putting anything down." For small urban yards or tight spaces, the compact form factor is a real plus.

**Where it falls short:** Beyond the battery and blade limitations, there's a durability concern. Several long-term reviews report the trigger mechanism becoming sticky after a few months, and the grass head's plastic housing cracking if dropped. The blades are not self-sharpening, so plan to sharpen them after a season of heavy use. There's also no shoulder strap or auxiliary handle — for a tool this light, it's manageable, but you'll feel the lack of a second grip on extended jobs.

**Who should buy this:**
- **Small-yard homeowners** (under 1/4 acre) with light trimming needs — this handles it fine
- **Renters or condo owners** who need one tool for multiple tasks and lack storage space
- **Budget buyers** who want cordless convenience without dropping $150+ on a premium brand

**Skip it if:** You have large overgrown hedges, need all-day run time, or expect professional-grade durability. This is a light-duty tool.

**FAQ:**

**Q: Does it come with a second battery?** A: No, just one 2.0Ah pack. Budget for a spare — it's roughly $25-30 extra and doubles your usable time.

**Q: Can the hedge trimmer handle thick branches?** A: Not really. Stick to new growth and soft greenery. Anything woody over 3/8-inch will stall or jam the blade.

**Q: Is the grass trimmer line replaceable?** A: Yes, it uses standard .065-inch line, but you'll need to manually cut and thread it — there's no auto-feed bump head.

**The Verdict: 3.7/5**

**Buy it if** you need a lightweight, versatile trimmer for small-scale maintenance and value the tool-free head swaps. **Skip it if** you expect heavy-duty performance or long battery life — you'll be frustrated within a month. It's a fine starter tool, not a workhorse. For the price, it earns its place in a small shed, but keep your expectations in check. Order a spare battery with it.
