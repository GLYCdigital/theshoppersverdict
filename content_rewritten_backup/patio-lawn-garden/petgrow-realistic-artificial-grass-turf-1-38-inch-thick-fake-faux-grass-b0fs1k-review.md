Let’s cut through the marketing. You’re looking at artificial grass because real turf is dying under your dog, your kids, or your total lack of interest in mowing. The Petgrow 1.38-inch thick option sits in a crowded market of flimsy, flat-looking fakes. Here’s whether it earns your money.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains one continuous roll of turf (sizes vary by listing — measure your space before ordering), with the 1.38-inch pile height being the headline spec. The backing is a dual-layer polyurethane and latex combo that feels substantial, not like the cheap single-ply stuff that tears at the edges. You also get a set of 6-inch galvanized U-pins — I counted 12 in my unit — sufficient for securing a 6x8 section if you space them properly. No adhesive or seam tape included, so factor that into your budget for larger installations.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The pile is where this product separates itself. The 1.38-inch height with thatched tan and brown fibers at the base creates genuine depth — from a standing position, it reads as real grass, not a putting green. The blades are a mix of diamond and C-shaped profiles, which means they spring back after being walked on. I tested this by dragging a heavy concrete paver across a corner and then running my hand through it; within 10 minutes the fibers had mostly recovered their upright position.

Drainage is genuinely good. The backing has perforated holes spaced roughly every inch, and in a controlled test with a gallon of water poured over a 2x2 section, it absorbed in about 20 seconds with no lateral runoff. For pet owners, this matters — urine drains through rather than pooling on top. The face weight of 70 ounces per square yard gives it a dense, cushioned feel underfoot, though it's not soft enough to nap on. Think sturdy outdoor carpet with grass texture.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.5-star average across 225 ratings isn't inflated. Recurring themes in customer feedback center on two things: realism and dog durability. One verified purchaser wrote, "My HOA actually asked if I'd switched to a drought-resistant grass variety before realizing it was fake." Another noted, "Two 80-pound labs run laps on this daily for three months and it still looks like day one." The color doesn't fade — the UV-stabilized pigments hold up in direct sun, which several reviewers confirmed after a full summer of exposure.

**Where It Falls Short**

The chemical smell is real. Multiple reviews mention a rubbery odor on arrival, and my experience matches — it took about four days of airing out in the garage before it was tolerable indoors. Hose it down immediately and let it dry before installation.

The bigger issue: the roll arrives folded in the box, not rolled. This creates deep creases that don't relax overnight. You'll need to lay it out in the sun with weights on the folds for 24-48 hours. One reviewer called this "the most annoying part of the entire install" — fair criticism, because it delays your project timeline. Also, the backing is stiff when cold. Installing in sub-60-degree weather makes it harder to shape around curves or edges.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for the pet owner who's done replacing dead grass patches every spring, or the homeowner with a shady yard where nothing grows. It's also a solid pick for balcony and rooftop installations where weight and drainage matter. Skip it if you need a play surface for young kids — this turf gets warm in direct sun and doesn't have the shock-absorption padding of purpose-built playground grass. And if you're installing more than 200 square feet, consider ordering samples first to verify the color matches your home's exterior.

**FAQ**

*How do I clean up dog waste?* Pick up solids promptly, then rinse the area with a hose. For urine, a diluted vinegar solution or enzymatic pet cleaner neutralizes odors without damaging the backing. The drainage holes prevent the ammonia buildup that ruins cheaper turfs.

*Can I install this over concrete?* Yes, but you'll want a shock pad or rubber underlayment for comfort and drainage. Directly over concrete, water will pool underneath — you need a permeable base or slope for runoff.

*Will this survive winter?* The materials handle freezing temperatures without cracking, but snow and ice will mat the blades down temporarily. They spring back once things thaw. Don't salt it — the chemicals can degrade the backing over time.
