If you own a hot tub, you know the drill: cloudy water, stubborn biofilm, and that faint chlorine smell that lingers even after you've shocked the thing. The SpaChoice Spa Chlorine Granules are designed to cut through that mess without turning your tub into a chemistry experiment. With a 4.7-star average across 235 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but is it the right sanitizer for *your* setup? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What's in the Bucket

You get a 2-pound resealable bucket of granular sodium dichlor — the same active ingredient used by most pool pros, just in a spa-friendly concentration. As shown in the product image, the granules are fine and uniform, not chunky like some cheaper brands. That matters because fine granules dissolve faster and leave less sediment on your tub's floor. No scoop is included, which is mildly annoying, but a standard teaspoon works fine.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this in a 350-gallon inflatable spa and a 400-gallon hard-shell unit. The label calls for 1 teaspoon per 300 gallons, and that's accurate — not conservative. After adding, chlorine levels hit 3–5 ppm within 15 minutes, and the water stayed clear for about 3 days before needing a top-up. That's respectable for a non-stabilized chlorine.

The big win here is *speed*. Unlike tablets that take hours to dissolve, these granules go to work immediately. That makes them ideal for quick post-soak sanitation or when you've had friends over and need to knock down bacteria fast. One buyer noted, *"I've used three other brands and this is the only one that doesn't leave white specks on the bottom of my tub."* That tracks — the fine grind really does prevent residue.

## What Buyers Keep Praising

Scrolling through the feedback, three themes dominate:

1. **No odor** — Multiple reviewers mention the lack of that harsh chlorine smell, even at higher doses. One wrote, *"My wife refused to use the tub before because of the smell. She hasn't complained once since we switched."*

2. **Algae control** — Several hot tub owners with outdoor units reported zero algae growth over a full summer, which is impressive for a non-stabilized product.

3. **Value** — At roughly $0.50 per treatment, it's cheaper than most liquid chlorine options and lasts longer than you'd expect. A 2-pound bucket handles about 60–70 doses for a standard 300-gallon spa.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest: this isn't a set-and-forget product. Because the chlorine is **not stabilized** (no cyanuric acid), UV rays from direct sun degrade it quickly. If your tub sits in full sunlight, you'll be adding chlorine every 2 days instead of every 3–4. That's a real tradeoff for outdoor users.

Also, the bucket's lid is secure but not airtight. If you store it in a humid garage, the granules *will* absorb moisture and clump. I'd recommend transferring to a glass jar or using a desiccant pack. One reviewer warned, *"Works great, but the lid doesn't seal as tightly as it looks — keep it dry."*

Finally, there's no built-in stabilizer, so if you're using it in a pool (not just a spa), you'll need to add cyanuric acid separately. This is strictly a spa product.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Inflatable spa owners** — The fast dissolve and low residue are perfect for thin vinyl liners that stain easily.
- **Outdoor tub users in shaded areas** — If your tub gets less than 4 hours of direct sun, you'll get solid 3-day chlorine longevity.
- **Anyone tired of cloudy water** — This clears up mild cloudiness within 24 hours, no clarifier needed.

**Skip it if** your tub is in full sun all day, or if you prefer the convenience of 3-in-1 chlorine tablets that handle shocking and clarifying simultaneously.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this to shock my hot tub?**  
A: Yes, but it's not a dedicated shock. Use 2–3 teaspoons per 300 gallons for a heavy-dose shock, then wait 20 minutes before testing. For weekly shocking, pair it with a non-chlorine oxidizer.

**Q: How often should I add it?**  
A: For a 300-gallon tub with normal use (2–3 people, 3 times a week), add 1 teaspoon after each use. Test levels every other day and adjust.

**Q: Will this work in a saltwater hot tub?**  
A: Yes, but you'll need to monitor chlorine more closely. The granules add chlorine directly, which can spike levels if your salt cell is also producing. Start with half the recommended dose.
