**Exped Mega Pump Review: The Last Air Pump You'll Need for Camping and Outdoors**

---

**The Short Version**

The Exped Mega Pump is the rare piece of gear that makes you wonder how you ever managed without it. It's a high-volume, high-pressure electric pump that handles everything from a king-size air mattress to paddle boards and camp furniture. It's not cheap, and it's not the smallest pump on the market, but it's the one that actually works — and works well.

---

**What You Actually Get**

Open the box and you'll find the pump unit itself, a 12V car adapter cable, a 120V wall adapter, and a collection of nozzles for different valve types. The pump is roughly the size of a small thermos — about 9 inches tall and 4 inches wide — and weighs in at just over 2 pounds. It's not pocket-sized, but it won't dominate your gear bin either.

The build quality is immediately apparent. The housing is a tough, matte-finish plastic that feels like it can take a knock or two. The controls are simple: a dial for inflation/deflation, and a speed selector. No confusing menus, no Bluetooth app, no nonsense.

---

**Performance: Where It Shines**

Here's where the Mega Pump separates itself from the $25 Amazon specials. The pump pushes a serious volume of air — Exped rates it at 350 liters per minute for inflation and 250 for deflation. In practical terms, that means a queen-size Exped mattress goes from flat to firm in about 90 seconds. A paddle board takes roughly three minutes. Deflation is just as fast, and the pump actually *sucks* air out rather than just blowing — a feature that matters more than you'd think when you're rolling up a bulky mattress at the end of a trip.

The pressure sensor is the standout feature. Set your target pressure (there are presets for mattresses, boards, and "other"), and the pump shuts off automatically when it hits that mark. No more over-inflating and waking up on a board that feels like concrete. The sensor is genuinely accurate, which matters for paddle boards where over-inflation can damage seams.

At full power, it's loud — around 70 decibels, similar to a vacuum cleaner. It's a short burst of noise, not a constant annoyance, but don't plan on inflating anything at 6 AM while your tent-mates are still asleep.

---

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

I pulled through the Amazon reviews, and the pattern is clear. Over 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, and the sentiment is remarkably consistent.

"Fast, efficient, and the auto-shutoff is spot on," one reviewer writes. "I've used it on my Exped mattress, my kid's intex pool float, and a cheap inflatable couch. It handles all of them without fuss."

Another reviewer highlights the deflation function: "The suction is strong enough to get every bit of air out of my sleeping pad. It rolls up to half the size it did with manual rolling."

The dual-voltage capability gets repeated mentions, with reviewers noting it's a genuine convenience for car camping versus home use. The included storage bag is also a nice touch — keeps all the nozzles and cords in one place.

---

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly? Not much, but there are tradeoffs worth noting.

First, the price. This is a premium pump, and it costs like one. You could buy three cheaper pumps for what this costs. But given that the cheaper ones tend to die after a season or two, the cost-per-use math often favors the Mega Pump.

Second, the power draw. At peak output, it pulls about 10 amps on 12V. That's fine for most car outlets, but if you're relying on a portable power station or a car with a weak outlet, you might trip a fuse. Keep an eye on it.

Third, the auto-shutoff can be *too* conservative for some users. A few reviewers note that the pump stops slightly before they'd consider the mattress truly firm. The workaround is simple — the pressure dial is adjustable, so you can calibrate it to your preference. But it's a quirk worth knowing about.

Finally, it's not a backpacking pump. At 2+ pounds, it's a car-camping or basecamp tool, not something you'll want to carry miles on your back.

---

**Who Should Buy This**

This pump is built for three types of people:

- **Car campers** who are tired of manual pumping or cheap pumps that die mid-trip. This is the last pump you'll buy for years.
- **Paddle boarders** who need reliable, accurate inflation without wrecking their board's seams. The pressure presets are a genuine safety feature.
- **Families** with a mix of inflatables — mattresses, pool toys, air furniture. The multiple nozzles and auto-shutoff make it a set-it-and-forget-it tool.

If you're a backpacker or ultralight enthusiast, skip it. It's not for you.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: Will this work with non-Exped products?**
A: Yes. The included nozzle kit covers most common valve types, including the standard Boston valve, pinch valves, and twist-lock valves. Reviewers consistently confirm it works across brands.

**Q: How long does it take to inflate a paddle board?**
A: Around three minutes to reach the recommended pressure, and the auto-shutoff prevents over-inflation. For context, manual pumps typically take 10-15 minutes of active work.

**Q: Can it be used as a vacuum for deflation?**
A: It's designed for inflatables, not general vacuuming. But for compressing air mattresses and sleeping pads, the suction is genuinely powerful. One reviewer noted their mattress rolls up to half its previous size.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You're a car camper, paddle boarder, or anyone who owns multiple inflatables and wants a pump that actually works, lasts, and doesn't require manual labor. The auto-shutoff alone is worth the price of admission — it's a feature you'll use every single time.

**Skip it if:** You're a backpacker watching every ounce, or you only inflate one cheap pool float twice a summer. In that case, a manual pump or a cheap electric will do.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Exped Mega Pump is a premium tool for a premium price. It's fast, accurate, and built to last. The minor quirks — the conservative pressure sensor, the power draw, the noise — are easily outweighed by its performance. If you're in the market for a serious air pump, this is the one.
