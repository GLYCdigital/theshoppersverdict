Let me be straight with you: most magnet fishing kits on Amazon are overpriced toys with inflated pull ratings and rope that snaps on the second throw. The Logui Projects 1200 LB kit is not that. It's a legitimate two-magnet setup that's earned its 4.5-star average across 225 ratings — but it has quirks you need to know about before you drop your cash.

## What's in the box

You get two neodymium magnets (each rated at 600 lb pull), 65 feet of double-braided polypropylene rope, a hard-shell carrying case with pre-cut foam inserts, and carabiner connectors. The case is the standout here — it's not the flimsy zippered pouch most kits ship with. It's a rigid, latch-sealed case that keeps everything organized and protects the magnets from chipping each other during transport. As one buyer put it, "The case alone is worth the price — everything has its place and nothing rattles around."

## Real-world performance

The 1,200 lb combined rating is technically accurate but requires context. Each magnet pulls 600 lb individually, and when you attach both in tandem, you get the full 1,200. This matters because you'll often use just one magnet for lighter work — and 600 lb is still plenty for pulling up bike frames, tools, and scrap metal from typical riverbeds and lakes.

The rope deserves specific credit. It's double-braided polypropylene, which resists water absorption and holds up against rough concrete edges and submerged rocks. The carabiners are solid steel with a threaded locking mechanism — no cheap spring clips that fail under load. Multiple reviewers confirmed the rope "held up after dozens of throws without fraying."

The magnets themselves are coated in a nickel-copper-nickel triple layer. I'd still recommend rinsing them with fresh water after saltwater use — a step some buyers skip and then complain about surface rust.

## Where it falls short

Three honest downsides:

1. **No gloves or grappling hook.** For ~$70, I'd expect at least a pair of work gloves. You'll need both for safe handling — magnets this strong can pinch skin badly, and a grappling hook is essential for snagging targets on rough bottoms.

2. **Eye bolt loosening.** The threaded eye bolts can work loose under heavy side-loading, especially when you're dragging a heavy catch over obstacles. Check and retighten after each session. Some reviewers reported losing magnets because they skipped this step.

3. **The 1,200 lb rating can mislead.** If you're expecting a single magnet that pulls 1,200 lb, you'll be disappointed. It's two 600 lb magnets. Read the fine print.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You're starting magnet fishing seriously and want a complete kit that won't fall apart. The hard case makes it ideal for car storage, and the dual-magnet setup gives you flexibility for different water conditions.

**Skip it if:** You're a weekend dabbler who wants the absolute cheapest entry point — basic single-magnet kits run half the price. Also skip if you're doing deep-water salvage; 65 feet of rope won't reach the bottom of many rivers and canals.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use this in saltwater?**
Yes, but rinse everything with fresh water afterward. The nickel coating resists corrosion, but salt accelerates wear on the eye bolts and carabiners.

**Q: How do I store the magnets without them snapping together?**
The foam inserts in the case keep them separated. Never store loose magnets near each other or metal tools — they'll slam together and can shatter.

**Q: Is 600 lb per magnet enough for real finds?**
Absolutely. Most magnet fishers pull up bikes, safes, tools, and scrap metal. The 600 lb rating gives you a wide safety margin for typical finds.
