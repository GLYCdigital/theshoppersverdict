## Real-World Performance

Twin-wall polycarbonate works because it traps air between two layers of plastic. That air gap gives you insulation — roughly R-value of 1.5 to 2.0 for 4mm panels, better for 6mm — while still transmitting around 80-85% of available light. For most hobby greenhouse applications, that's the sweet spot: enough light for seedlings and winter greens, enough insulation to keep frost off your plants.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and these panels have real tradeoffs:

- **Haze over time** — Even with UV protection, polycarbonate will eventually cloud slightly. Expect 5-8 years of clarity before you notice a reduction in light transmission. It's not a failure — it's the chemistry of the material.
- **Expansion and contraction** — Polycarbonate moves more with temperature changes than glass does. If you fasten these panels too tightly, they'll buckle or crack at the screw holes. You need to use oversized holes and rubber washers — the instructions should cover this, but it's easy to miss if you're in a hurry.
- **Scratch sensitivity** — The surface is softer than glass. Cleaning with anything abrasive — or dragging a hose nozzle across the surface — will leave permanent marks. Use a soft cloth and mild soap.

## FAQ

**Can I cut these panels to custom sizes?**
Yes. Use a fine-tooth circular saw blade for straight cuts, or score and snap thinner panels (4mm). Always cut with the UV-protected side facing up to avoid chipping the coating. Leave 1/8" gap on all sides for thermal expansion.

**How do I clean them without scratching?**
Use lukewarm water and a mild dish soap. Apply with a soft sponge or microfiber cloth, rinse with clean water, and let it air dry. Never use abrasive pads, ammonia-based cleaners, or pressure washers.

**What's the difference between 4mm and 6mm panels?**
Thickness affects insulation and strength. 4mm is lighter, cheaper, and easier to cut, but offers less insulation. 6mm provides roughly 30% better R-value and handles snow loads better — worth the extra cost for northern climates.
