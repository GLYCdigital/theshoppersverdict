# VNLA Junior Stealth Jam Skates: The Budget Skate That Punches Way Above Its Weight

Most sub-$100 skates are toys. The VNLA Junior Stealth is not. Designed as a unisex jam/rhythm skate, this boot has quietly become a favorite among rink regulars and derby newbies alike — and at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, the numbers back up the hype. But is it the right skate for *you*? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find the skates fully assembled — a genuine suede boot mounted on an **aluminum alloy plate** with an adjustable toe stop. That plate is the headline feature here. At this price point, most competitors use nylon or plastic plates that flex under pressure. The alloy plate gives you a stiff, responsive platform that transfers every push directly to the wheels. Also included: indoor wheels, bearings, and a hex tool for adjustments. No assembly required — you can lace up and roll within five minutes.

## Build Quality and Performance

The suede upper is the real deal. It's thick enough to protect your ankles during deep edge work, and the padded collar hugs your foot without cutting off circulation. The boot has a slight heel raise (standard for jam skates) which puts you in a proper skating posture — knees bent, weight centered.

On the rink, the **aluminum plate** makes a noticeable difference. Edges bite cleanly, spins feel stable, and the adjustable toe stop lets you dial in your toe-work preference. One buyer put it simply: *"I've had $200 skates that didn't feel this solid."* Another noted, *"The plate is the selling point — it's the same hardware you'd find on skates twice the price."* That's the consensus: the boot and plate punch above their weight class.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Sizing is the biggest issue.** The VNLA Junior line runs small and narrow. Multiple reviewers warn to size up a full size, and if you have wide feet, this probably isn't your skate. One reviewer wrote, *"Ordered my normal size, couldn't even get my foot in. Exchanged for a size up and they fit perfectly."* That exchange process is a hassle — measure your feet against the size chart before ordering.

**The stock wheels are mediocre.** They're hard (about 95A), which makes them slick on dusty or polished floors. For casual rink skating, they're fine. For serious jam skating or frequent use, budget $30–40 for softer wheels (82A–85A). The bearings are also entry-level, so don't expect buttery-glide performance out of the box.

**The laces are thin.** They stretch within a few sessions, leading to heel slip. Swap them for waxed laces early — it's a $5 fix that makes a real difference.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You're a beginner-to-intermediate skater who wants a real upgrade from rental skates without dropping $150+. You're a parent buying for a growing teen (the Junior sizing fits youth 3–6 and women's 5–8). You want a skate you can grow into — the alloy plate will handle advanced skills down the road.

**Skip them if:** You have wide feet or high insteps. You're a competitive derby skater who needs a low-cut, highly customizable boot. You want a skate for outdoor use — these are indoor-only wheels, and the suede sole isn't meant for asphalt.

## FAQ

**Q: Do these fit true to size?**
A: No. Size up a full size from your street shoe. They run narrow, especially across the toe box. Measure your foot length in centimeters and match it to the VNLA size chart — don't rely on your usual shoe size.

**Q: Are these good for beginners?**
A: Yes, with a caveat. The alloy plate is forgiving and stable, making it easier to learn edges and stops. But the hard stock wheels can be slippery on dusty floors — consider softer wheels if you're just starting and want more grip.

**Q: Can I use these outdoors?**
A: Not recommended. The wheels are indoor-specific, and the suede boot will wear quickly on concrete. If you want outdoor skating, budget for a separate wheel set (78A hardness) and be prepared to swap them regularly.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

The VNLA Junior Stealth Jam Skates deliver exceptional value for indoor skaters. The aluminum plate and suede boot are features you'd expect on skates costing twice as much, and the overwhelmingly positive buyer feedback confirms this isn't a fluke. The sizing quirks and mediocre stock wheels are real drawbacks, but both are solvable with a little research and a small upgrade budget.

**Buy it if** you want a durable, performance-oriented indoor skate without breaking the bank. **Skip it if** you have wide feet, need outdoor capability, or demand premium wheels out of the box. For everyone else — this is the smart money play in the sub-$100 skate market.
