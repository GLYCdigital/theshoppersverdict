## What's Actually in the Box

You get one 50-foot length of 2-inch reinforced PVC hose with factory-installed NPT pin lug couplings on both ends. The fittings are aluminum-bodied with male NPT threads — a step up from the plastic-threaded junk that strips if you look at it wrong. The hose wall is spiral-reinforced, which matters more than the marketing photos suggest. As shown in the product image, the couplings are pre-attached, but the barbs are bare metal. You'll need two 2-inch stainless hose clamps (not included) to secure it to your pump outlet.

## Real-World Performance

The headline spec is the full 2-inch inner diameter. That's the difference between this and the 1.5-inch hoses that choke your pump's output. On a standard 2-inch trash pump pushing 150 GPM, this hose flows without backpressure. The spiral reinforcement keeps it round under suction and pressure — no ovaling, no collapse, no embarrassing kink at the bend.

One buyer noted, *"I've used this on my 2-inch Honda pump weekly for six months. The couplings haven't leaked a drop, and the hose takes abuse from dragging across gravel."* That tracks with the build. The PVC wall is thicker than the cheap clear stuff — it resists punctures from debris and doesn't turn brittle after a few weeks of UV exposure.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest tradeoff: this hose is **stiff**. In summer heat it's manageable, but below 50°F, it coils like rebar. If you're a northern user planning to winterize, this is not the hose you want to wrestle with at 6 AM. It needs to warm up before you can roll it out flat.

The missing hose clamps are a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. But it's a common complaint in the reviews — one user wrote, *"Great hose, but I didn't realize I'd need to buy clamps separately. Not a big deal, just annoying."* Factor that into your total cost.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit a standard sump pump discharge?**
A: Only if your pump has a 2-inch NPT male outlet. Most residential sump pumps are 1.5-inch, so you'll need a reducer bushing. For 2-inch trash pumps and transfer pumps, it's a direct thread-on.

**Q: How much pressure can it handle?**
A: This is discharge hose, not pressure hose. It's rated for working pressures around 100-150 PSI, which covers any trash pump you'll buy. Don't use it on a pressure washer or boiler drain — that's not what it's for.

**Q: Can I leave it outside in winter?**
A: You can, but it'll get stiff and may crack if water freezes inside it. Drain it completely and store it indoors if you want it to last more than a season.
