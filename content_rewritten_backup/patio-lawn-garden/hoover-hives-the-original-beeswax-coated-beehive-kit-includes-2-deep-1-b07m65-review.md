Let me be blunt: if you're looking for a "beekeeping in a box" shortcut, this isn't it. But if you want a solid, durable hive that'll outlast cheaper pine kits, the Hoover Hives beeswax-coated setup earns its reputation. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this is one of the most consistently praised hives in its price bracket — and for good reason.

Here's the real story.

## What's actually in the box

You get two 10-frame deep boxes, one medium super, 20 plastic frames with pre-coated beeswax foundation, a telescoping lid, bottom board, and an inner cover. The wood is Western cedar — not the pine you'll find on budget kits. That matters because cedar resists moisture and rot naturally, which means this hive should last 5–7 years longer than a pine equivalent.

The frames are the standout feature. They're plastic with beeswax already applied, so you skip the messy, time-consuming job of wiring frames and dipping foundation. As shown in the product image, the frames snap together — no glue, no specialized tools.

## Build quality and assembly reality

The pre-coated foundation is a genuine time-saver, but don't believe any listing that says "simple assembly." You're looking at 200+ nails and roughly 3–4 hours of work to assemble everything. One buyer put it this way: *"Took me an entire Saturday, but the fit was precise. Every joint lined up perfectly — no gaps, no wobble."* That precision is the payoff. The cedar is pre-drilled, and the pieces fit tightly, which means fewer drafts for your bees during winter.

The beeswax coating on the exterior isn't just for looks — it seals the wood against moisture and gives the hive a warm amber finish that weathers gracefully. It's a small detail that most kits skip.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 225 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Frame quality** — The pre-waxed plastic frames get consistent shout-outs. One reviewer noted, *"The bees accepted the foundation immediately — no coaxing, no drawn comb delay."* That's a huge advantage over bare plastic frames, which can take weeks for bees to accept.

2. **Cedar durability** — Multiple long-term reviews (1+ season) mention zero warping or cracking, even in humid climates. That's the cedar advantage showing up in real-world conditions.

3. **Customer service** — Hoover Hives has a reputation for replacing damaged parts quickly. Several reviewers mentioned receiving a replacement bottom board within days of reporting a shipping crack.

## Where it falls short

Be honest about the gaps. This kit does **not** include a queen excluder, entrance feeder, or smoker. You'll need to budget an extra $60–100 for those essentials. That's not a dealbreaker, but it's a hidden cost that surprises first-time beekeepers.

The assembly time is the other friction point. If you're not handy with a hammer, this will be a slog. A cordless drill with a nail driver bit is almost mandatory — doing it by hand will test your patience. One frustrated reviewer wrote, *"My wife and I bickered through the whole build. Wish they'd offered a pre-assembled option."*

Also note: the medium super is included for honey storage, but you'll eventually want a second one — most established hives fill the first super within a season.

## Who should buy this

- **First-time beekeepers** who want a durable hive without paying premium prices for pre-assembled kits. The beeswax-coated frames eliminate the messiest step.
- **Hobbyists upgrading from cheap pine hives** that warped or rotted after a season or two.
- **Anyone who enjoys precision assembly** and doesn't mind an afternoon of methodical work.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a plug-and-play kit, don't want to buy separate accessories, or prefer all-wood frames over plastic.

## FAQ

**Q: Do the bees actually accept the plastic frames with wax coating?**
A: Yes — that's the whole point. The beeswax coating signals to the bees that the foundation is ready to use. Most buyers report comb drawn within 1–2 weeks, compared to 3–4 weeks with unwaxed plastic.

**Q: How many boxes do I need for a full season?**
A: This kit covers a new colony's first year: two deeps for brood, one super for honey. In strong nectar flows, you'll likely add a second super by mid-summer.

**Q: Can I leave this hive outside in winter?**
A: Yes, the cedar handles it. But you'll need to add an entrance reducer and mouse guard (not included) and consider a moisture quilt box for climates with harsh winters.
