The DSTANA Chicken Feeder Port solves one of the most annoying problems backyard chicken keepers face: feed that gets pecked, scratched, pooped on, and soaked. At $20.99, this galvanized steel port turns a standard 5-gallon bucket into a gravity feeder that keeps your flock fed without the daily cleanup routine. But is it worth adding to your coop setup? Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes the galvanized steel feeder port itself, a marking template, stainless steel mounting screws, and a rubber gasket for a tight seal. The port measures roughly 8 inches across the opening, designed to fit standard 5-gallon buckets, though it also works with most 3.5-gallon pails. The metal is a solid 22-gauge galvanized steel — noticeably heavier than the flimsy plastic ports you'll find for half the price.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

This thing is built to last. The galvanized coating resists rust even in humid coop environments, and the steel is thick enough that even aggressive pecking won't bend or warp it. Installation takes about 10 minutes: trace the template, cut the bucket opening, mount the port with the included screws, and you're done. The rubber gasket creates a snug fit that keeps moisture and pests from sneaking in around the edges.

The design works exactly as intended. As shown in the product image, chickens step up to the port and eat through the opening while the bucket above holds the feed. The angled tray at the bottom lets feed flow naturally as birds eat, so you don't have to shake or refill constantly. One 5-gallon bucket holds roughly 25 pounds of feed, which means you're filling up about once a week for a small flock.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects a pattern of satisfied customers. The most common praise centers on feed savings. One reviewer noted, "I was going through a 50-pound bag every two weeks with a standard feeder. Now it lasts almost a month." ## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The metal edges on the bucket cutout are sharp — you'll want to wear work gloves during installation and consider filing down any rough spots. A few reviewers reported minor rust forming on the screws after several months, though the port itself held up fine.

The bigger limitation is the port depth. If you keep bantam breeds or young chicks, they may struggle to reach feed at the bottom of the tray. Some buyers solved this by placing a brick or block under the bucket to elevate it. Also, the port is open when not in use — it doesn't come with a cover or door, so dust and debris can still get in if your coop gets particularly messy.

## Who Should Buy This

This feeder port is ideal for backyard chicken keepers with 3-8 birds who want a no-fuss feeding solution. It's also a great upgrade if you're tired of refilling flimsy plastic feeders or losing feed to pests and weather. If you're raising bantams or show birds, look for a smaller port with a shallower tray instead.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my bucket?**
It fits standard 5-gallon buckets and most 3.5-gallon pails. Measure the bucket's diameter at the top — if it's between 11 and 12 inches, it'll work.

**Can I mount this on a metal container?**
Yes, but you'll need a metal-cutting blade on your drill or jigsaw. The included screws are designed for plastic buckets, so pick up self-tapping screws for metal.

**How do I clean it?**
Remove the port from the bucket, wash with warm soapy water, and dry thoroughly. The galvanized steel can be hosed down, but avoid abrasive scrubbers that might scratch the coating.
