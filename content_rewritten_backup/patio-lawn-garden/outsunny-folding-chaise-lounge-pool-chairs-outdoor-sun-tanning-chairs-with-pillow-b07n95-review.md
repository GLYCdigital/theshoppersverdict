**The Review**

You don't buy a $60 chaise lounge expecting heirloom quality. You buy it because you want to lay flat in the sun without spending $300 on a designer lounger. The Outsunny Folding Chaise Lounge sits right in that budget sweet spot—but the 3.5-star average across 175 ratings tells you there's nuance here. Let me break down what you're actually getting.

**What's in the Box**

Outsunny ships this disassembled but mostly tool-ready. You get the steel frame (pre-welded into two halves), the Textilene mesh seat, the backrest, a small foam pillow, and the hardware bag. Assembly takes about 20 minutes with a Phillips head screwdriver—no specialty tools needed. The instruction sheet is mostly diagrams, and they're clear enough. As shown in the product image, the final form is a classic sling-style lounger with a visible steel skeleton and mesh fabric stretched across.

The dimensions matter here: 74.8 inches long, 24.4 inches wide, and 13 inches off the ground at seat height. That's a full-length lounger, not a compact chair. When folded flat, it drops to about 4 inches thick—this is the single biggest selling point.

**Real-World Performance**

The 5-position backrest is genuinely useful. You can ratchet from flat to roughly 60 degrees, and the locking mechanism uses a sliding rail system that feels secure when engaged. I've seen cheaper loungers where the adjuster slips under weight—that's not the case here. The steel frame (1.5mm square tube) supports up to 265 pounds, and the cross-bracing underneath prevents the lateral wobble you get on flimsier competitors.

The Textilene mesh is where things get complicated. It's a PVC-coated polyester weave that dries in minutes and doesn't hold heat like solid fabric. In direct sun, your back won't sweat against it. But multiple buyers report sagging within a season. One reviewer put it plainly: *"After about 10 uses, the mesh started to stretch and my hips sank lower than my feet. It's not terrible but you can feel the give."* That's consistent with what I've seen—the mesh is 1,000-denier, which is adequate but not heavy-duty.

The included pillow is the weakest component. It's a thin foam pad with no strap or tether, so it slides around when you recline. You'll end up repositioning it constantly. It's removable, but the velcro strip underneath doesn't hold well on the mesh texture.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The fold-flat design gets near-universal love. Owners repeatedly mention storing these against a garage wall or under a bed. One long-term reviewer noted: *"I bought two of these for a tiny apartment balcony. They fold flat enough to slide behind the couch. That's the reason I keep them."* The weight (about 14 pounds) also makes them genuinely portable—easy to carry from yard to pool deck without dragging.

The price-to-performance value is the other recurring theme. At this price point, buyers aren't expecting premium materials, and most feel they got their money's worth.

**Where It Falls Short**

The sagging issue is real. If you're heavier or plan to use this daily, expect the mesh to stretch within 60-90 days. It won't rip, but the support profile changes—your lower back will feel the difference.

No armrests is a design miss. Getting up from a flat position requires rolling to your side and pushing off the ground. For older users or anyone with mobility issues, this is a genuine problem.

The frame finish is powder-coated, but it's thin. Buyers report scratching after a season of moving the chair around. Rust can form on those scratches if you leave it outside uncovered.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers and small-space owners** who need fold-flat storage
- **Casual sunbathers** using it a few times a month, not daily
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who accept tradeoffs for the price
- **Pool owners** who want spares for guests without investing in premium furniture

**Skip it if** you want armrests, need heavy-duty daily durability, or expect the mesh to stay taut for multiple seasons.

**FAQ**

*Does the backrest lock securely at all positions?*
Yes, all 5 positions lock via a sliding rail mechanism. It holds firm under body weight, though you need to disengage the lock fully before adjusting—forcing it can bend the release tab.

*Can this be left outside in rain?*
The frame resists rust initially, but the powder coat is thin. Cover it or store it folded if you live in a humid climate. The mesh itself drains and dries quickly.

*Is assembly difficult?*
No. Two people make it easier, but one person can handle it. About 20 minutes, straightforward bolt-and-screw assembly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a foldable, portable lounger for occasional use and value storage space over luxury comfort. **Skip it if** you're a daily sunbather who needs armrests and long-term structural integrity.

The Outsunny Folding Chaise Lounge earns a **3.7/5**. It's honest for the price, useful for the right buyer, and falls short of premium in ways you'll notice over time. If you understand that going in, you'll be satisfied.
