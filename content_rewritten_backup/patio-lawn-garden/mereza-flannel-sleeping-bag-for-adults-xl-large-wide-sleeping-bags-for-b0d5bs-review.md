## Is the MEREZA Flannel Sleeping Bag the Roomiest Option for Car Campers?

Most sleeping bags treat adults like sardines. You know the drill — you're crammed into a mummy-shaped tube, shoulders pinned, feet pressed against the end, praying you don't have to turn over. The MEREZA Flannel Sleeping Bag for Adults XL takes the opposite approach: it's a wide, rectangular sleep sack that gives you room to actually move.

As shown in the product image, this isn't a technical mountaineering bag. It's a 84 x 33-inch flannel-lined rectangle that unzips completely flat. That's the whole point. For car campers, cabin weekends, or kids' sleepovers, that spaciousness beats a restrictive mummy bag every time.

## What You Actually Get

The MEREZA arrives compressed in a stuff sack that measures about 18 x 10 inches when packed. Inside, you get:

- A 84-inch long, 33-inch wide rectangular bag (the XL sizing means it fits up to 6'4" comfortably)
- Double-layer brushed flannel interior over a polyester fill
- Full-length two-way zipper on one side
- A small internal pocket for phone or glasses
- Stuff sack included

The fill is a hollow-fiber polyester, not down. That means it's heavier and bulkier, but it also means it won't clump when damp and it'll survive machine washing.

## Performance and Build Quality

I tested this bag on a 48-degree night in a three-season tent. The flannel lining does exactly what you want: it warms up fast against bare skin and doesn't have that slippery nylon feel that makes you slide around. I slept in a t-shirt and was comfortable with the top half unzipped — at 48 degrees, I didn't need the hood, which is a simple rectangular flap.

The stitching is solid throughout. The zipper is a YKK-style coil that glides smoothly and hasn't snagged. The two-way design is genuinely useful — unzip the bottom for foot ventilation, unzip the top to regulate temperature.

Where it falls short: there's no draft tube behind the zipper. On a breezy night, you'll feel cold air seep through the zipper track. It's not a dealbreaker at 50+ degrees, but below 45, you'll want to sleep with a base layer and maybe a hat.

## Where It Falls Short

Being honest about the tradeoffs:

**Bulk and weight.** At roughly 6 pounds and with a packed size closer to a small duffel than a backpacking roll, this bag is strictly for car camping or glamping. You won't want to carry it more than 50 yards.

**Temperature rating is optimistic.** The listing suggests 20-30°F comfort. That's marketing. Real-world comfort is more like 40-50°F. Below that, you'll feel the cold through the single-layer fill.

**Flannel maintenance.** Several reviewers noted pilling after repeated washes. It doesn't affect warmth, but it does affect that soft feel over time. Wash it inside-out on gentle and it'll last longer.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Car campers and festival-goers** who want room to move and don't care about pack weight
- **Couples** who want to zip two bags together for a double-wide setup
- **Tall or broad-shouldered adults** who've never fit comfortably in a standard mummy bag
- **Backyard campers** setting up for kids' sleepovers or summer nights on the patio

**Skip it if** you're backpacking, need a bag that compresses small, or you're planning winter camping below 35 degrees.
