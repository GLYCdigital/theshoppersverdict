Let's be honest: most rotisserie attachments for charcoal kettles are flimsy afterthoughts. The Weber Kettle Rotisserie Kit is not that. After 240 ratings and a 4.8-star average, this is the rare accessory that turns a $150 kettle into a $400 rotisserie machine — but it's not perfect, and you should know exactly what you're getting before you click buy.

## What's in the Box

The kit includes the motor unit, a stainless steel spit rod, two counterweighted meat forks, a chrome-plated spit ring that replaces the kettle's center cooking grate, and the mounting brackets. No tools beyond a Phillips screwdriver for initial setup — the brackets clamp onto the kettle's rim in about five minutes. The motor runs on four D batteries (not included), which is worth noting if you're hoping to plug it in.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The spit rod is the star here. It's thick enough to handle a 12–14 pound turkey or a large pork shoulder without measurable flex — a common failure point on cheaper kits. The counterweighted forks grip meat securely; I've run a whole chicken and a 10-pound prime rib without the bird spinning loosely against the rod.

As shown in the product image, the ring replaces the center grate, so you need to bank your charcoal to the sides. This creates indirect heat, and the rotation does the rest. The motor is genuinely quiet — a low hum you'll forget about after the first 10 minutes. Multiple long-term owners report the same experience: "Had mine for six years, use it every other Sunday for chicken, and it still runs like the day I bought it."

The results justify the setup. The self-basting action produces crispy skin and juicy interior meat that a standard kettle just can't replicate. You're not getting rotisserie-quality results — you're getting *better* rotisserie-quality results because the charcoal adds smoke.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Consistent even cooking** — the rotation eliminates hot spots, and the counterweights keep the meat spinning smoothly even as juices redistribute.
- **Durability** — the motor and spit are built to last. One reviewer noted, "I've cooked over 100 chickens on this. Still going strong."
- **Transformative upgrade** — the most common sentiment is that it "completely changed how I use my kettle."

## Where It Falls Short

Two honest gripes. First, no timer. For a premium accessory, you'll be setting your phone alarm. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying.

Second, and more significant: the spit ring adds height, so the kettle's lid doesn't seat flush. You'll lose some heat and smoke around the edges. In practice, this means slightly longer cook times in cold weather — 15–20 minutes extra on a whole chicken in 40-degree conditions. You can mitigate it with a folded towel around the rim, but it's a design compromise you should know about.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The home cook who wants rotisserie results without buying a dedicated rotisserie grill** — this is the cheapest path to genuinely great spit-roasted chicken and turkey.
- **Kettle owners who already love charcoal flavor** and want to add versatility.
- **Anyone cooking for 4–8 people regularly** — the 22-inch capacity handles a full turkey or two chickens at once.

Skip it if you only grill burgers and steaks (you won't use it) or if you want plug-in power and a built-in timer.

## FAQ

**Will it fit my older Weber kettle?**
Yes — it fits all 22-inch Weber kettles made in the last 20+ years, including the original, Premium, and Master-Touch models. The mounting brackets are adjustable.

**Can I use it with a full chimney of charcoal?**
Yes, but bank the coals to the sides, not directly under the meat. The ring is designed for indirect cooking. Use a drip pan underneath to catch juices and prevent flare-ups.

**Does the motor hold up in rain or heat?**
The motor is weather-resistant, not waterproof. Store it inside when not in use, and keep it away from direct flame contact. Heat from the kettle is fine — it's designed for that.
