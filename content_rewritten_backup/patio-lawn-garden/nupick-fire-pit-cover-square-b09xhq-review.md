# NUPICK Fire Pit Cover Square Review: A No-Nonsense Shield for Your Patio Investment

You spent real money on that square fire pit. You’ve gathered around it, roasted marshmallows, and watched flames dance into the night. But when the season ends, most people toss a flimsy tarp over it and call it a day. Six months later, they’re staring at rust spots, faded metal, and a pit that looks a decade older than it is. The NUPICK Fire Pit Cover Square exists to stop that cycle. After digging through 240 ratings (averaging 4.8 stars), here’s the honest breakdown of whether this cover earns your cash.

## What You Actually Get

The NUPICK is a square cover built from 600D heavy-duty Oxford cloth with a waterproof PVC backing. That’s a meaningful spec — 600D is the sweet spot for outdoor gear. It’s tough enough to resist punctures from falling branches or curious pets, yet flexible enough to fold flat for off-season storage. The cover comes with a drawstring hem around the base and two side straps that cinch the fit snugly around your pit legs. You also get built-in air vents on the top, which sound minor but matter more than you’d think.

## Real-World Performance

Let’s talk about what this cover actually does well. First, the waterproofing is legitimate. The PVC backing creates a true moisture barrier — rain beads up and rolls off rather than soaking through. One buyer noted, *“Survived two Nor’easters and a foot of snow without a single drop getting to the fire pit.”* That tracks with the material specs. Second, the wind resistance is better than most covers in this price range. The combination of drawstring and straps means it won’t balloon up and fly off during a storm. A customer in a windy mountain town wrote, *“Neighbors keep finding their covers in the yard. Mine stays put.”* Third, the air vents deserve credit. They prevent condensation from building up underneath, which is the silent killer of fire pits — trapped moisture causes rust and corrosion long before rain ever touches the metal.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the NUPICK has two honest flaws. The fabric is notably stiff when it first arrives. It arrives folded, and the creases take a few days in warm weather to relax. Until then, the cover looks a bit rumpled and won’t drape as elegantly as you might want. It’s cosmetic, not functional, but worth knowing. Second, while the material is UV-treated, it’s not invincible. In high-sun southern climates, the black color may fade to a dull gray after two or three seasons. The waterproofing and structural integrity hold up — it’s purely an aesthetic tradeoff.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You own a square fire pit between 40–48 inches in diameter and live anywhere with actual weather — rain, snow, wind, or all three. This is also a strong pick if you’ve already ruined one fire pit by leaving it uncovered and want to protect your next investment.

**Skip it if:** Your fire pit lives under a covered patio or pergola where it never sees direct rain or snow. In that case, you’re paying for protection you don’t need. Also skip it if you have a non-standard oversized square pit larger than 48 inches — measure first, because the fit is tailored, not one-size-fits-all.

## Three Questions Buyers Ask

**Q: Will this fit my specific fire pit brand?**  
Measure the top width of your pit first. The cover is designed for square pits in the 40–48 inch range. If yours falls in that window, it will fit with room to cinch down. If it’s larger, look elsewhere.

**Q: Does it actually stay on in high winds?**  
Yes, thanks to the drawstring plus the two side straps. One reviewer in Kansas noted, *“We get 40 mph gusts regularly. This cover has never moved.”* The straps wrap around the pit legs and hold firm.

**Q: How do I clean it?**  
A hose and mild soap is all you need. Let it air dry completely before storing it folded. Do not machine wash or dry — the PVC backing will crack.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The NUPICK Fire Pit Cover Square is a well-built, genuinely protective cover that solves the two biggest problems with fire pit maintenance: water damage and wind blow-off. The stiff fabric out of the box and minor fading over time are acceptable tradeoffs for the level of protection you get.
