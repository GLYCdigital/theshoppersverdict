**Real-world performance**

Zinnias are about the easiest annual you'll grow from seed, and these behave accordingly. Direct-sow after the last frost when soil temps hit 60°F or warmer. In customer feedback, germination reports are consistently positive: sprouts in 7 to 14 days, with strong early growth. One buyer noted, "Planted a whole row in late May and had blooms by the Fourth of July — can't beat that for cut flowers."

Mature height runs 24 to 36 inches depending on the variety in the mix. The plants are sturdy enough to hold up in the garden without staking, though heavy rain can flop the taller dahlia types. Deadheading keeps them blooming from midsummer through the first hard frost — plan on a weekly pass if you want continuous color.

**FAQ**

**Q: When should I plant these outdoors?**
A: After the last frost date in your area, when soil temperature is consistently above 60°F. In most zones, that's late May to early June. Zinnias are frost-tender — planting too early will stunt or kill them.

**Q: How far apart should I space the seeds?**
A: Thin seedlings to 6–12 inches apart, depending on the variety. For the larger dahlia types, give them a foot. Crowded zinnias get powdery mildew fast because air can't circulate.

**Q: Do these come back every year?**
A: No — zinnias are annuals. They'll bloom until frost, then die. But if you leave a few spent flower heads on the plant, they'll self-seed and often return the following spring with volunteers.
