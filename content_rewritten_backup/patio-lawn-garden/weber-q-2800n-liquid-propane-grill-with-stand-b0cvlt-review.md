Let's cut through the marketing noise. The Weber Q 2800N+ is the grill that tries to be everything — tailgate companion, balcony workhorse, and backyard backup — and mostly pulls it off. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, shoppers are clearly finding something to love here. But is it the right grill for *you*? Here's what you need to know before handing over your cash.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the grill head, the folding cart/stand, and the essential accessories: a 21,700 BTU liquid propane burner, two stainless steel burners underneath porcelain-enameled cast-iron cooking grates, and Weber's signature dome lid with a built-in thermometer. The cooking surface spans 393 square inches — enough for about 12 burgers or a whole spatchcocked chicken. The cart's two side tables flip down for storage, and the whole assembly rolls on two wheels. Assembly takes roughly 45 minutes with basic tools, and the instructions are Weber-clear, which means you won't be staring at a pile of mystery hardware.

**How it actually performs**

The cast-iron grates are the standout feature here. They retain heat aggressively, which translates to proper searing on steaks and those coveted crosshatch marks on vegetables. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *"The grill marks are restaurant-quality, and cleanup is just a quick scrape."* The built-in thermometer reads the dome temperature accurately enough that you can trust it for indirect cooking — just set up a two-zone fire and you're golden.

The 21,700 BTU output is honest but not spectacular. You're looking at a 10-12 minute preheat to hit 500°F, which is slower than Weber's larger Spirit models. But once it's hot, it holds temperature admirably steady, even on breezy patios. The lid seals well, and the grease management system routes drippings away from the burner tubes — a design choice that shows Weber's engineering maturity.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Beyond the searing capability, reviewers repeatedly mention three things. First, the even heat distribution — no cold spots to fight with. Second, the portability factor. One buyer noted, *"I've taken this camping three weekends in a row. The stand folds flat and it fits in my trunk with room to spare."* Third, the build quality. Weber isn't cutting corners on materials here — the cart's steel frame feels solid, and the lid's porcelain finish resists chipping.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The lack of rotisserie compatibility is a genuine miss for a grill at this price point. If you want to slow-roast a whole chicken, you're doing it with indirect heat — which works, but it's not the same result. The side tables are also smaller than they appear in the product images; don't plan on staging a full mise en place out there. And the wheels are modestly sized — fine for rolling on flat concrete, but they'll catch on gravel or uneven grass.

One more thing: the propane tank sits exposed on the cart's lower shelf. It's stable, but there's no built-in tank guard, so if you're hauling this in a truck bed, you'll want to secure the tank separately.

**Who should buy this**

This is the right grill for three specific buyers: apartment dwellers with balcony space who want something that performs above its footprint; tailgaters and campers who want a full-size cooking experience without the full-size cargo space; and anyone who wants a reliable second grill for when the main event is occupied. It's not the right buy for serious backyard pitmasters who need 60,000 BTU output or a rotisserie option.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Does it use standard 20-pound propane tanks?*
Yes, the standard green tank connects directly to the regulator. No adapters needed.

*Can you grill with the lid closed for smoking?*
You can do low-and-slow cooks at 250-300°F, but don't expect true smoke penetration. Add a smoker box for wood chips and you'll get decent results.

*How hard is it to clean the cast-iron grates?*
Scrape them hot, then wipe with oil. The porcelain coating prevents rust, but the grates are heavy — about 8 pounds each.

**The Verdict**

Rating: 4.6/5 — and it's earned.

**Buy it if** you want a portable grill that sears like a full-size unit, you value even heat distribution, and you're okay with a slower preheat.

**Skip it if** you need rotisserie capability, you're cooking for crowds larger than six regularly, or you demand instant high-heat output.

The Weber Q 2800N+ nails the portable-but-capable formula. It's not the most powerful grill on the market, and it won't replace a dedicated backyard setup. But for its intended audience — space-conscious cooks who refuse to compromise on results — it's a smart, well-built investment that'll last years with basic care.
