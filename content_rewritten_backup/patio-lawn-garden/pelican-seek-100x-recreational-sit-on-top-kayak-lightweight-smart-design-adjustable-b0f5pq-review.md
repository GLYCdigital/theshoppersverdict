You don't buy a sit-on-top kayak for speed. You buy it for the ability to slide off into the water, swim, fish, and climb back aboard without performing a J-stroke ballet. The Pelican Seek 100X nails that core promise, but it's not without compromises. Here's what 220 Amazon reviewers (4.4 stars) actually think after paddling it.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the 9-foot, 10-inch RAM-X polyethylene hull, one adjustable EVA foam seat with padded backrest, a two-piece paddle, and the drain plug. That's it. No paddle leash, no crate, no rod holders. At 38 pounds, the Seek 100X is genuinely light — one reviewer noted, *"I'm a 62-year-old woman and I can load this on my SUV roof rack by myself."* That's the headline feature and it holds up.

**How it performs on the water**

The hull is a modified V-shape with a wide 30-inch beam. It tracks better than you'd expect for a recreational boat — straight-line paddling takes minimal correction. Primary stability is excellent; you can lean back, stretch, and cast without white-knuckling the gunwales. The 275-pound capacity rating is realistic for a solo paddler with a cooler and tackle box.

The adjustable seat is the standout feature. It clips into three positions along a molded track, letting you move the backrest forward or backward mid-paddle. One buyer called it *"the most comfortable kayak seat under $500 — I did four hours on a lake without numbness."* The recessed footwells give you three positions, though here's the catch: the tracks are spaced for longer legs. Multiple reviewers under 5'4" reported the footrests felt too far away, forcing them to paddle toes-up.

**What buyers consistently praise**

- The weight-to-durability ratio. RAM-X plastic is scuff-resistant; one paddler dragged it over a gravel launch and reported only surface scratches.
- The scupper holes. Twin channels drain water fast, and the raised seat keeps your backside dry.
- The molded side handles. They're positioned at the balance point, making solo carries less awkward than center-handle designs.

**Where it falls short**

The included paddle is the weak link. It's a heavy, flexy plastic blade that'll tire you out after 45 minutes. Factor in a replacement. The lack of any paddle keeper is also annoying — the paddle slides off the hull when you stop to take photos or swim. And while the hull is tough, it flexes noticeably under heavy paddlers; at 240 pounds, I felt the deck give slightly when bracing.

**Who should buy this**

- **Weekend lake paddlers** who want a stable, easy-to-transport boat for casual paddling and swimming.
- **Older or smaller paddlers** who need a kayak they can load and unload solo without a truck bed or trailer.
- **First-time kayak buyers** who want a forgiving, low-maintenance hull without spending $800+.

**Skip it if** you're over 6'1" (knee clearance in the footwells is tight), you plan to paddle rivers with current (the flat hull catches crosswind), or you expect to stand up and fish — this is a sit-down boat.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can two people fit on this kayak?**
Technically, the 275-pound capacity allows it, but the seat track is designed for one. A child could sit in the rear footwell, but it's not a tandem boat.

**Q: Is it stable enough for a beginner?**
Yes. The flat-bottom profile and wide beam make it very forgiving. The primary stability is high; secondary stability (leaning) is moderate — expect to feel tippy if you lean hard.

**Q: Does it come with a paddle?**
Yes, a basic two-piece plastic paddle is included. Most buyers replace it within a season.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a lightweight, comfortable sit-on-top for calm lakes and slow rivers, and you value solo-loading convenience over speed and performance features.

**Skip it if** you're tall, plan to paddle in wind, or want a kayak that's ready for fishing out of the box.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Seek 100X earns its stars on comfort and portability. The paddle and footrest quirks keep it from perfection, but for casual paddlers, it's a smart, honest buy.
