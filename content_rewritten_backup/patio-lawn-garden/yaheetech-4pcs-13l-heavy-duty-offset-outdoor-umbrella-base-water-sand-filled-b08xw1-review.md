If you own an offset umbrella, you already know the struggle: the cantilever arm creates leverage that makes lightweight bases useless. The Yaheetech 4-pack of 13L heavy-duty bases attacks that problem with sheer mass — but only if you're willing to put in the work to fill them. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through 215 ratings and the spec sheet.

**What you actually get**

The box contains four identical cross-shaped bases. Each measures about 21.5 inches across and stands roughly 3 inches tall when empty. The 13-liter capacity is the headline number — that's roughly 3.4 gallons per base, or about 28 pounds when filled with sand, 29 pounds with water. The cross design isn't just aesthetic; it spreads that weight over a wider footprint than a round base, which matters for offset umbrellas that pull from one side.

The center pole collar accepts diameters up to 1.5 inches, with a tightening knob to lock the pole in place. The plastic is heavy-duty polypropylene, not the thin blow-molded stuff you see on cheap bases. It won't crack from UV exposure in the first season, based on buyer reports.

**Real-world performance**

The critical question: does a 28-pound base hold a 10-foot offset umbrella? The math says borderline, but physics says it depends on your fill and wind conditions. Buyers who filled with sand report solid stability in moderate breezes. One reviewer noted, "I use these for two 9-foot offset umbrellas on a deck that gets afternoon gusts. Sand-filled, they haven't budged." Water-filled bases are noticeably less stable — water sloshes and shifts, reducing effective mass. If you're in a windy region, sand is non-negotiable.

The cross footprint fits under standard 48-inch round patio tables without interfering with legs. That's a thoughtful design choice. The fill cap is threaded and seals well, but pouring sand into a 13-liter opening without a funnel is a two-person job. Plan for mess.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the standout. Four bases for roughly the price of one or two premium singles is the recurring theme. One buyer wrote, "Bought these to replace two rusty steel bases. At this price for four, I have backups for the pool area." Another praised the stability: "My 10-foot cantilever umbrella finally stays put through a full afternoon of wind." The build quality also gets consistent nods — no sharp edges, no warping after a full season outdoors.

**Where it falls short**

The empty weight is essentially zero, so you're buying a container, not a ready-to-use base. That's fine if you plan ahead, but a frustrating surprise if you expect to set up immediately. Sand filling without a funnel is genuinely annoying — consider buying a sandbag or funnel with your order.

The 1.5-inch pole collar is a hard limit. If you have a commercial-grade umbrella with a 2-inch pole, this won't fit. Check your pole diameter before ordering. Also, no wheels or handles means moving a filled base is a workout. Plan to place it once and leave it.

**Who should buy this**

- **Patio owners with offset umbrellas** who need multiple bases and don't want to pay $80+ per single unit
- **DIY installers** who can plan ahead for sand filling and have a funnel handy
- **Renters or temporary setups** who want decent stability without permanently mounting anything

Skip it if you need a ready-to-use base out of the box, have a pole over 1.5 inches, or expect water-filled performance in windy conditions.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I use these for a standard straight umbrella, not just offset?**  
A: Yes. The 1.5-inch collar fits most residential straight poles too. The cross shape is actually better for straight umbrellas since it resists tipping equally in all directions.

**Q: How much sand do I need per base?**  
A: Exactly 13 liters per base. A standard 50-pound bag of play sand is about 28 liters, so plan on two bags for all four bases, with a little left over.

**Q: Do these rust or corrode outdoors?**  
A: No — the body is all polypropylene plastic. The only metal is the tightening knob, which is zinc-plated and holds up fine, per long-term buyer reports.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want four solid, sand-filled bases for a fraction of the cost of premium singles, and you're willing to spend 20 minutes filling them properly. **Skip it if** you need instant setup, have oversized poles, or live somewhere with constant high winds — you'll want a heavier single base with a larger footprint.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great value, solid build, and real stability when filled correctly. The filling hassle and pole size limit keep it from a perfect score.
