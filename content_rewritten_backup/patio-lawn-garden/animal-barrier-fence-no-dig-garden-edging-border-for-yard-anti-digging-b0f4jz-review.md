## What's in the Box

The package includes multiple galvanized steel panels with pre-drilled stakes. As shown in the product image, the fence sections connect via interlocking edges to form a continuous barrier. You're getting roughly 20 linear feet of coverage per set, with each panel standing about 11 inches tall. The steel is powder-coated, not painted — that distinction matters because powder coating adheres better and resists chipping when you're hammering stakes into hard soil.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this fence around a raised vegetable bed and along a flower border. Installation took about 20 minutes with a rubber mallet — no trenching, no digging, no concrete. The stakes drove cleanly into loamy soil, and the interlocking design held firm even when I tugged on it.

The anti-digging claim is mostly accurate. The fence's primary defense is physical — animals can't push through the panels, and the height deters casual sniffers. One buyer noted, "My dog stopped digging under the fence after I installed this. He tried once, got a nose full of metal, and gave up." That tracks with my experience.

The flexibility is a genuine selling point. I curved it around a circular herb garden without any buckling or kinking. The steel bends cleanly and holds its shape once positioned.

## Where It Falls Short

This is not Fort Knox for your garden. The 11-inch height is the obvious limitation. A determined deer can clear it easily, and large dogs might jump it if properly motivated. For those situations, you'd need something taller or with a top rail.

The stakes also have a weakness in loose soil. After a heavy rain, I found a few sections slightly tilted in sandy areas. The fix is straightforward — re-stake at a deeper angle — but it's worth knowing before you install on slopes or sandy ground.

Finally, "anti-digging" has limits. This fence is a deterrent, not a fortress. If you have persistent groundhogs, you'll want to bury the bottom edge 2-3 inches deep rather than relying solely on the stakes.

**Q: How long does this fence actually last?**
A: With the galvanized coating, expect 5-8 years of outdoor exposure. The main failure point is the stakes loosening over time, not the panels corroding.

**Q: Can I cut the panels to fit smaller spaces?**

**Q: Does this work on slopes?**
