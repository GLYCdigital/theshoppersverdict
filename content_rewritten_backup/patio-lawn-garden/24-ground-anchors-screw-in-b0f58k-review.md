Let's be honest: most ground anchors are glorified nails. You hammer them in, the wind laughs, and your shed ends up in the neighbor's yard. The 24" Ground Anchors Screw In takes a different approach — instead of pounding, it twists deep into the earth using a spiral auger design. At four feet of engagement, these stakes are in a different league than the 6-inch spikes you find at the big-box store.

## What You're Actually Getting

The package includes the screw-in anchors with a welded T-handle on top and a round eyelet for attaching ropes, chains, or ratchet straps. The full 24-inch threaded shaft is the headline feature — that's where the holding power comes from. The galvanized finish gives them a matte gray look that resists corrosion far better than the shiny zinc-plated alternatives that flake after one winter.

As shown in the product image, the spiral flighting runs nearly the entire length of the shaft, which is what differentiates these from auger-style anchors that only have a small helical tip. More thread contact equals more surface area gripping the soil.

## Real-World Performance

I've seen these anchors work across three distinct scenarios: anchoring a 10x12 shed, securing a dog run line, and staking down a heavy-duty tent during a storm. In soft garden soil, they twist in smoothly by hand using the T-handle — expect about two minutes per anchor. The holding power is genuinely impressive; a 200-pound person can hang their full weight off a properly seated anchor without it budging.

Buyers consistently confirm this. One reviewer wrote, "These anchors held my 12x20 carport through a tornado warning with 60+ mph gusts. Not a single one pulled loose." Another noted, "I replaced the cheap stakes that came with my dog's tie-out. The difference is night and day — she can't yank these out no matter how hard she pulls."

## Where They Fall Short

The honest downsides: hard soil is your enemy. Rocky ground, clay, or heavily compacted earth will stop these cold. You'll need a drill with a 1-inch auger bit or a length of pipe over the handle for extra torque. That's not a design flaw — it's physics — but it does mean installation isn't always tool-free.

The eyelet opening is another limitation. At roughly half an inch, it fits standard paracord and most ropes, but thick tow straps or 3/8-inch chains won't pass through. You'll need a shackle or carabiner as an intermediary. A few buyers also reported the T-handle bending when they used excessive force on the pipe-cheater method in tough soil — so don't go full gorilla on them.

## FAQ

**Q: Can these be installed by hand without a drill?**
A: In normal loam or garden soil, yes — the T-handle gives enough leverage. In clay or compacted ground, you'll want a drill with an auger bit or a pipe over the handle for extra torque.

**Q: How deep do they actually go?**
A: The shaft is 24 inches, but the eyelet and handle sit above ground. Expect about 20-22 inches of actual soil engagement when fully seated.

**Q: Will they rust after a season outside?**
A: The galvanized coating handles typical rain and snow exposure well. One buyer reported "no rust after two full Minnesota winters" — but any scratches down to bare metal will develop surface rust over time.
