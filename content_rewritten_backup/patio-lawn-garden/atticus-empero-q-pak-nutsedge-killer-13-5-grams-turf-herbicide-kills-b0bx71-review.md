# Atticus Empero Q-Pak Nutsedge Killer Review: The 1,000-Square-Foot Solution That Actually Works

Nutsedge is the weed that makes lawn care pros curse. It looks like grass, laughs at standard weed killers, and spreads through underground tubers that keep regenerating. If you've fought it with glyphosate or broadleaf blends and lost, the Atticus Empero Q-Pak is worth a serious look — because it targets the root system, not just the leaves you can see.

## What You Actually Get

The Q-Pak comes as a box of water-soluble packets. Each 13.5-gram packet treats exactly 1,000 square feet of turf. The active ingredient is sulfentrazone at 4 pounds per gallon — a proven nutsedge and kyllinga killer used by professional turf managers. You dissolve one packet in 1 gallon of water and spray. That's it. No measuring cups, no guessing, no leftover concentrate sitting in your garage.

The packets themselves are the standout feature here. If you've ever spilled liquid herbicide or tried to measure 0.38 fluid ounces accurately, you'll appreciate the pre-measured format. As shown in the product image, each packet is clearly labeled and dissolves fully in water — no clumping or residue in your sprayer.

## Real-World Performance

Here's what the 210 Amazon ratings at 4.2 stars tell you: this stuff works, but it demands follow-through. The sulfentrazone needs to be watered in — the label requires about 0.5 inches of irrigation within 14 days of application. That's not optional. One reviewer put it plainly: "I applied it, forgot to water, and the nutsedge came back in three weeks. Second application with proper watering knocked it out completely."

When applied correctly, control is impressive. Sulfentrazone inhibits photosynthesis in susceptible weeds, and because it moves through the plant to the tubers, it stops regrowth at the source. Most users report visible yellowing within 7-10 days and full die-off in 2-3 weeks. Multiple reviewers noted it cleared "years of nutsedge infestation" in a single season with two applications spaced 6-8 weeks apart.

Broadleaf weeds like clover, dandelion, and wild violet also get hit — a bonus if you're dealing with mixed weed pressure.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The pre-measured packets earn the most love. "No more measuring liquid concentrate and hoping I got it right. Drop a packet in the sprayer, fill with water, done." Another common theme: safety on established turf. Users with Bermuda, Zoysia, and St. Augustine reported no damage when they followed the label rates.

The value proposition also gets mentioned. Compared to paying a lawn service $75-150 per treatment, DIY application with this product costs a fraction — even with the multiple boxes large lawns require.

## Where It Falls Short

The 1,000-square-foot coverage per packet is the biggest limitation. If you have a 5,000-square-foot lawn, you're buying five boxes at once. At roughly $25-35 per box depending on the listing, that's $125-175 for a single application. For severe infestations needing two treatments, the cost approaches professional service territory.

The watering requirement is also a genuine hassle. If you're in a drought or have irrigation restrictions, meeting the 0.5-inch requirement within 14 days can be tough. And the label warns against application when rain is expected within 4 hours — so you're playing weather roulette.

Finally, this is a post-emergent product. It won't prevent nutsedge from germinating. If your lawn has a history of heavy infestations, you'll likely need annual applications.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a confirmed nutsedge or kyllinga problem on an established lawn, you're comfortable following label directions exactly, and you have reliable irrigation or rainfall.

**Skip it if:** You're looking for a one-and-done solution, you have a lawn larger than 3,000 square feet on a tight budget, or you can't guarantee watering after application.

## FAQ

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Yellowing typically appears within 7-14 days. Complete die-off takes 2-3 weeks. Don't reapply before 6 weeks — you'll just waste product.

**Q: Can I use this on a newly seeded lawn?**
A: No. Wait until new grass has been mowed at least twice before applying. Sulfentrazone will damage young seedlings.

**Q: Will it harm my pets or kids?**
A: Once the spray dries, it's safe for re-entry. Keep pets and children off the treated area until the solution dries completely, typically 1-2 hours.
