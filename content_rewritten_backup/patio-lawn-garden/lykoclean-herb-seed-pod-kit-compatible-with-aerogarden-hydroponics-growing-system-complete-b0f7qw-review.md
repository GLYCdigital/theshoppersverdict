If you own an AeroGarden, you already know the dirty secret: the brand-name pods are expensive, and the selection is thin. The LYKOCLEAN Herb Seed Pod Kit steps into that gap with six popular culinary herbs at a fraction of the cost. But does it actually work in your machine, or is it another third-party gamble? I've dug through the 200 ratings and the technical specs to give you the straight answer.

**What's actually in the box**

The kit ships with six complete grow pods — basil, dill, cilantro, parsley, mint, and chives — each pre-seeded in a biodegradable grow sponge inside a plastic basket. You also get two bottles of liquid nutrient solution sized for a full grow cycle. As shown in the product image, everything arrives ready to drop into your AeroGarden's deck without any assembly. The baskets are the critical piece here; they need to seat firmly in the 1.5-inch holes without rocking. Based on buyer reports, the fit is solid across the AeroGarden Harvest, Sprout, and Bounty models.

**Performance and germination**

The core question for any seed pod kit is whether the seeds are fresh enough to sprout. The 4.0-star average tells a mixed but mostly positive story. Buyers consistently report germination within 5-10 days, with basil and chives being the most reliable. One verified purchaser noted, *"I was skeptical about third-party pods, but all six sprouted within a week. The basil is outpacing my AeroGarden brand pods."* That sentiment repeats throughout the reviews — the seeds are viable, and the sponges hold moisture correctly without turning soggy.

However, there's a consistency issue worth flagging. Several reviewers reported uneven seed counts between pods. Some pods come with just two seeds while others have five, which means you'll get denser growth in some herbs than others. It's not a dealbreaker — you can thin seedlings — but it's sloppy quality control.

**The nutrient situation**

The included nutrient solution is a genuine value-add. It's pre-mixed and measured for a standard 6-pod grow cycle, so you won't need to buy AeroGarden's expensive brand-name plant food immediately. That said, a handful of reviewers mentioned the nutrients seemed weaker than the OEM version, with slower leaf development in the second month. If you're growing heavy-feeding herbs like basil long-term, budget for a higher-quality nutrient supplement down the line.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest practical annoyance: no labels. The pods come unmarked, and once the sponges get wet, you can't tell basil from cilantro. You'll need to create your own marking system or keep a diagram. It's a small thing that adds friction to an otherwise plug-and-play product.

Also, the cilantro and parsley have noticeably lower germination rates than the other four herbs. If you specifically need those two, expect to plant a few extra seeds or accept thinner patches.

**Who should buy this**

This kit is ideal for AeroGarden owners who want variety without the premium price tag. It's also a solid starter kit for hydroponic beginners who want to test multiple herbs at once without committing to a single variety. If you're a heavy user who grows continuously, the nutrient strength and seed count inconsistencies might push you toward OEM pods — but for casual kitchen gardeners, the savings are worth it.

**FAQ**

**Will these fit my AeroGarden model?**  
Yes, the baskets match the standard 1.5-inch hole sizing used across the Harvest, Sprout, Bounty, and Farm series. No adapters needed.

**How long until I can harvest?**  
Most herbs are ready for first trimming in 3-4 weeks. Basil and chives will be fastest; cilantro and parsley take closer to 5-6 weeks.

**Can I reuse the pods after harvest?**  
The sponges are biodegradable and break down after one grow cycle. You'll need to purchase replacement seed pods or refill the baskets yourself with fresh sponges.
