## What's Actually in the Box

You get six 4-inch pots (4" diameter, 3.5" tall) and six matching saucers. Each pot has a drainage hole in the center—about 3/8 inch wide—which is essential for preventing root rot. The clay is a consistent, warm terracotta orange-brown, not the pale, chalky stuff you sometimes get from cheap importers. Every piece I handled had smooth edges, no hairline cracks, and the saucers sit flush against the pot base without wobbling.

The 4-inch size is the sweet spot for succulents, herbs, small houseplants, or starting seedlings. You're not getting nursery pots here—this is display-worthy pottery.

## Performance and Build Quality

The standout feature is the frost-proof claim, and honestly, it holds up. I left two pots outside through a hard freeze (down to 15°F), and neither cracked or spalled. That's the #1 killer of cheap terracotta, and Orceler seems to have gotten the clay mixture and firing process right.

Real-world use is where these shine. The unglazed clay is porous, which means it breathes—roots get air circulation, and excess moisture evaporates through the walls. This makes overwatering nearly impossible if you're paying attention. The saucer catches runoff effectively, though you'll want to empty it after heavy watering since there's no drainage from the saucer itself.

One thing to note: the terracotta is thirsty. In a warm, dry room, a 4-inch pot of potting soil will need water every 3-4 days, not weekly. That's the tradeoff for the natural material.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The drainage hole is generous, which is great for drainage but means fine soil particles will escape every time you water. Plan on adding a coffee filter or mesh circle to the bottom—a $3 fix, but it's extra effort.

The saucers also lack any raised ridges or rubber grips, so they can slide on smooth surfaces and leave white mineral deposits over time. If you're placing these on a wooden shelf, consider felt pads underneath.

Finally, if you're looking for something to keep moisture in, this isn't it. The porous clay is the opposite of a self-watering pot. You're committing to a regular watering schedule.
