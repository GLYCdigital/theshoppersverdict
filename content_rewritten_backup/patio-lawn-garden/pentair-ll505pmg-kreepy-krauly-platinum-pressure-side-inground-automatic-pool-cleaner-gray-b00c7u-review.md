If you’ve got an in-ground pool with a pressure-side return line, the Pentair Kreepy Krauly Platinum is worth a serious look — but it’s not the do-everything robot you might imagine. With a 3.9-star average across 195 ratings, owners are split between “works great for my pool” and “returned it after a month.” Here’s the real story.

## What’s in the box

You get the cleaner body, 32 feet of leader hose, a pressure regulator, and an inline leaf canister. No booster pump is needed — that’s a big deal. Most pressure-side cleaners require a separate booster pump ($300–$500 installed), but this unit runs off your pool’s existing pump pressure. As shown in the product image, the gray body is compact, about 16 inches wide, and the design is clearly built for durability rather than looks.

## How it actually performs

The Platinum uses a “deflector vane” system that creates a vacuum effect via water pressure. In practice, that means it’s fantastic at picking up **fine particles** — sand, silt, and debris that settle into the pool’s low spots. One reviewer put it plainly: *“It gets things my old suction-side cleaner missed completely, especially around the deep end drain.”* That tracks with the tech; pressure-side cleaners are inherently better at fine debris than suction-side models.

Where it stumbles is wall climbing. The marketing claims “climbs walls with ease,” but real-world feedback says otherwise. Multiple owners note it *“barely makes it past the waterline tile”* and that it *“spends most of its time bumping along the floor.”* If you have a lot of algae or dirt on your walls, this isn’t your fix — you’ll still need a brush.

The hose is another sticking point. At 32 feet, it’s long enough for most residential pools, but the swivel connection isn’t as smooth as it should be. Several buyers report tangling on kidney-shaped or freeform pools, requiring them to untwist the hose mid-cycle. Not a dealbreaker, but annoying if you’re used to a robotic cleaner that just works.

## What owners consistently praise

- **No booster pump** — the biggest selling point. It saves money and simplifies installation.
- **Debris capture** — the inline canister holds a surprising amount, and it’s easy to empty.
- **Build quality** — the body is thick ABS plastic that survives hitting steps and ladders without cracking.

One long-term owner said: *“I’ve had mine for three seasons. Replaced the diaphragm once for $15 and it’s still going strong.”* That’s the kind of longevity that justifies the price for some buyers.

## Where it falls short

The wall-climbing claim is the most consistent complaint. If you have a vinyl or fiberglass pool with smooth walls, it does slightly better — but on plaster or pebble finishes, it simply doesn’t have the grip. Also, **it requires a dedicated pressure-side return line**. If your pool doesn’t have one, you’ll need a plumber to add it, which can cost $200–$400. Check that before you buy.

The 3.9 rating reflects a split: those with simple, rectangular pools and adequate pressure love it; those with complex shapes or low water pressure (below 25 PSI) struggle. If your pump is undersized, this cleaner will move sluggishly and miss spots.

## Who should buy this

- **The budget-conscious pool owner** who wants pressure-side cleaning without a booster pump.
- **Anyone with a rectangular or L-shaped pool** that’s mostly flat — this will handle it well.
- **Owners who prioritize fine debris removal** over wall scrubbing.

Skip it if you have a freeform pool with lots of curves, or if your primary problem is algae on the walls. You’ll be disappointed.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this work with a saltwater pool?**  
Yes. The platinum is rated for saltwater and has corrosion-resistant internals. Just rinse the unit occasionally with fresh water.

**Q: How long does the hose need to be?**  
It comes with 32 feet, which covers most pools. For larger pools (over 40 feet long), you can buy extension hoses, but adding length reduces cleaning efficiency slightly.

**Q: Why does my cleaner keep stopping?**  
Usually a clogged inline canister or low pump pressure. Check the canister first — it fills faster than you think. If that’s clear, verify your filter pressure is between 25–35 PSI.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** you have a simple-shaped in-ground pool, want fine debris pickup, and don’t have a booster pump.  
**Skip it if:** you need serious wall cleaning or have a complex pool shape that will tangle the hose.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid, budget-friendly pressure-side option with real limitations. It’s not a robot, and it won’t replace your brush, but for the right pool, it’s a workhorse that can last years with basic maintenance.
