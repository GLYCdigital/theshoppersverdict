## The $12 Bottle That Ends Pool Chemistry Guessing

If you're still matching colored water against a plastic card, you're flying blind. The Taylor Swimming Pool Test Kit Titrating Reagent Chlorine Bottle (2 oz) is the reagent that turns guesswork into measurable certainty. It's not a standalone tester — it's the fuel for Taylor's FAS-DPD titration method, the gold standard pool chemists and service pros have trusted for decades. And at roughly 12 cents per test, it's the cheapest accuracy you'll buy all season.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 2-ounce (59 mL) bottle of R-0871 DPD indicator reagent. It's the third step in the FAS-DPD free chlorine test: after adding the DPD powder and titrating with the R-0872 drops, you use this to flash the sample pink-to-clear. The bottle has Taylor's signature wide-mouth dropper tip that delivers consistent 1-drop increments — no squirting, no mess.

As shown in the product image, it's a compact, unassuming white bottle. Don't let that fool you. This is the exact reagent Taylor uses in their K-2005 and K-2006 kits, which are the same kits the EPA recommends for pool operators. The 2-ounce size yields roughly 100 tests for most pools — about two months of daily testing for the average homeowner.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where titration beats everything else: resolution. Color-matching tests give you a range (e.g., "1.0–2.0 ppm"). The FAS-DPD method gives you a precise number — 1.4 ppm, 2.2 ppm, whatever it actually is. That precision matters when you're balancing for a salt chlorine generator or maintaining commercial compliance.

The reagent itself is stable. Taylor packages it with a shelf-life indicator on the label. One reviewer noted, "I've had my bottle for two seasons and it's still accurate — I verified against my pool store's digital test." Another buyer who runs a 30,000-gallon gunite pool said, "This replaced the guesswork. My chlorine is dialed in to 0.1 ppm now."

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the limitations. First, this bottle is useless without the full Taylor test kit — you need the R-0870 powder and R-0872 titrant to complete the reaction. If you're buying this as a standalone, you've wasted your money.

Second, at 2 ounces, heavy users will burn through it. If you're testing multiple pools weekly or running a service route, the 16-ounce refill is the smarter buy. The per-ounce cost is actually better on the larger size, so this small bottle is really for the single-pool homeowner.

One more honest gripe: the dropper tip can clog if you let reagent dry on it. Rinse the tip after each use or you'll be fighting uneven drops.
