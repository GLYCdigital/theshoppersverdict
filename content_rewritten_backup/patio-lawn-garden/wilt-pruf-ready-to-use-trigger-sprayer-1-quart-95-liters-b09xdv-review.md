**The 30-Second Hook**

If you've ever watched a prized rhododendron turn crispy brown in February, you already know the problem: winter wind strips moisture from evergreen leaves faster than frozen roots can replace it. Wilt-Pruf isn't a fertilizer or a pesticide — it's a clear, biodegradable polymer that forms a flexible, invisible film over foliage, slowing moisture loss and protecting plants from winter burn, transplant shock, and drought stress. This ready-to-use trigger sprayer version is the easiest entry point for homeowners with a handful of precious plants.

**What's In the Bottle**

The product arrives as a 1-quart (0.95 L) opaque white bottle with a spray trigger already attached. No measuring, no mixing — you twist the nozzle to "spray," pump a few times, and apply. One quart covers roughly 100–150 square feet of foliage, which translates to about 6–10 mature evergreen shrubs or a dozen rose canes. The formula is a water-based emulsion that dries clear and won't clog sprayers.

**How It Performs in the Real World**

Timing matters. Apply Wilt-Pruf in late fall when temperatures are above 40°F, before the ground freezes. One thorough coat — leaves should glisten but not drip — protects evergreens, boxwoods, and azaleas for up to three months. Gardeners also use it year-round: spray transplants right after planting to reduce shock, or apply it to new sod and Christmas trees to slow moisture loss.

The key is coverage. You need to hit both the top and underside of leaves for full protection. The trigger sprayer produces a fine mist that handles this well on small to medium shrubs, but if you're spraying a 15-foot holly hedge, you'll be refilling constantly. That's when the 1-quart size becomes a limitation rather than a convenience.

**Where It Falls Short**

Be honest about the residue. If you over-apply or spray in direct sunlight, the film can leave a faint white sheen on dark green foliage until the next rain. It's harmless but noticeable. Second, the value math: at this quart size, you'll pay a premium per ounce compared to the 1-gallon concentrate. If you have a larger landscape, buy the concentrate and a pump sprayer — you'll save money and get better coverage. Finally, it's not a cure-all; severely stressed or already-wilted plants won't bounce back just because you sprayed them.

**Who Should Buy This**

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

*Can I use this on vegetables and fruit trees?* Yes. Wilt-Pruf is biodegradable and labeled for edible crops. Spray young fruit trees in fall to prevent winter dieback, or apply to tomato transplants during a heatwave to reduce moisture stress.

*How often do I need to reapply?* One application lasts 3–4 months. For winter protection, spray in late fall after temperatures stay below 50°F. For summer drought relief, reapply after heavy rain or new growth, roughly every 6–8 weeks.

*Will it harm beneficial insects or pollinators?* No. The film is non-toxic and doesn't affect bees, butterflies, or birds. It simply slows water evaporation through leaf pores.
