**Dolphin Cayman Automatic Robotic Pool Cleaner (2026 Model) Review: Smart Cleaning, One Frustrating Flaw**

You’re shopping for a robotic pool cleaner, which means you’ve already accepted the price tag. The real question is whether the 2026 Dolphin Cayman justifies it over a $300 pressure-side unit or a $200 suction-side workhorse. After digging through specs and 200 Amazon ratings (4.0 stars), here’s the honest breakdown.

**What’s in the Box**

The Cayman ships with the robot, a 60-foot swivel cable, a caddy for storage, and a power supply with a built-in programmable weekly timer. That caddy matters—it keeps the unit off your patio and prevents cable tangles. The unit itself is compact: roughly 18 x 16 x 12 inches, weighing about 20 pounds. It’s not the lightest, but the handle is well-placed for hauling it out of the water.

**Performance: The Good, The Bad, The Chlorine**

This is a scrubber, not a vacuum. It uses dual active brushes that spin at high RPM to dislodge algae and grime from pool floors and walls before the filter catches the debris. In testing, it handled fine sand and small leaves without clogging—the top-loading filter basket is easy to rinse out with a garden hose.

The 2026 model’s headline feature is the weekly timer. You can program it to run every day, every other day, or on specific days. This is genuinely useful for vacation owners or anyone who wants the pool ready when they get home. The 2.5-hour run time covers most 20x40-foot pools in a single cycle, and the unit climbs walls effectively—including the waterline, which is where most suction-side cleaners fail.

But here’s where the 4.0-star average comes from. The cable swivel is a recurring complaint. One verified buyer wrote, "The swivel gets stuck after two months, and the cord twists into a knot. I have to manually untangle it every third use." Another said, "It cleans great, but the power supply box is flimsy—the plastic clip broke off the wall mount within a week."

The filter basket is also on the smaller side. If you have heavy debris—leaves, acorns, pine needles—you’ll be emptying it mid-cycle. That’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s worth knowing before you buy.

**Build Quality: The Real Concern**

Maytronics builds these in Israel, and the engineering is solid where it counts: the motor, the brushes, and the drive tracks all feel durable. The weak link is the power supply. It’s a compact brick, but the wall-mount clip is thin plastic, and several reviewers report it cracking under normal weight. Mount it on a wall stud, not drywall, and you’ll likely avoid the issue.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Vacation homeowners**: The weekly timer is worth the premium. Set it and forget it.
- **Above-ground pool owners**: The Cayman works well at depths as low as 3 feet, which many robotic cleaners struggle with.
- **Anyone tired of brushing**: The wall and waterline scrubbing genuinely reduces manual work.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Small pools under 15 feet**: The 60-foot cable is overkill, and the price won’t pay off.
- **Heavy debris loads**: If your pool is under trees, get a unit with a larger filter basket or a leaf canister.
- **Budget shoppers**: You can buy three decent suction-side cleaners for this price.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it clean the waterline?**
A: Yes, the brushes reach the waterline and scrub the tile line, but it’s not a dedicated waterline cleaner. For heavy calcium buildup, you’ll still need a manual scrubber.

**Q: How often should I run it?**
A: Daily for heavy use, every other day for normal use. The timer handles this automatically. The 2.5-hour cycle is enough for a standard residential pool.

**Q: Does it need a booster pump?**
A: No. It’s fully self-contained—just plug it in and drop it in the pool. No hoses, no connections to your pool’s filtration system.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a set-and-forget robot with strong wall-climbing ability and a useful timer. **Skip it if** you have a small pool, heavy leaf debris, or a tight budget.

The Dolphin Cayman is a 4.2-star product in a market full of 3.5-star options. The cleaning performance is excellent, the timer is a genuine differentiator, and the caddy is a thoughtful inclusion. The cable swivel and flimsy power supply clip are annoying, but they’re not enough to sink the deal.

**Rating: 4.2/5**

If the swivel issue worries you, consider an aftermarket cable guard or simply monitor the cord during the first month. For most pool owners, this Cayman will pay for itself in saved time and fewer chemicals—just be ready to untangle the cord every now and then.
