If you've ever wrestled with a brittle, cracked chlorine floater that sinks after one season, you already know the pain this product is trying to solve. The U.S. Pool Supply floating collapsible tablet dispenser takes a straightforward concept—float a few chlorine tablets in your pool—and adds two genuinely useful twists: a collapsible body and a real flow-control dial. At 4.5 stars across 225 Amazon ratings, it's clearly working for most buyers. But is it the right feeder for your specific setup? Let's dig past the marketing.

**What you actually get**

The package contains the dispenser itself and nothing else—no tablets, no gimmicks. The unit measures roughly 8 inches in diameter when assembled and stands about 6 inches tall. The collapsible design is the headline feature: the top section folds down into the base, reducing the height to about 2 inches for storage. That's a genuine space-saver for anyone with a crowded garage or shed. The body is made from thick ABS plastic with a threaded lid that seals via a rubber O-ring. The base is wider than most competitors, which gives it a lower center of gravity and makes it noticeably less prone to tipping over when the pool pump kicks on.

**Real-world performance**

The flow dial is the star here. It's not a simple "open or closed" valve—it's a rotating disc with a graduated range. Turn it fully counterclockwise for maximum chlorine output (roughly a full tablet dissolving every 3-5 days in a 15,000-gallon pool), or dial it back to nearly closed for maintenance doses. In testing, the adjustment range was genuinely useful, not just decorative. One Amazon reviewer noted, "I was able to dial it down to almost zero output when my pool was already balanced, then open it up after heavy rain. That's the control I couldn't get with my old floater."

The collapsible mechanism works as advertised. Twist the top section, and it telescopes down into the base. It takes about five seconds and requires no tools. The tradeoff: the twist-lock collar that holds the collapsed position is snug when new. Several reviewers mention needing two hands to collapse it for the first few weeks. It loosens up with use, but don't expect a one-finger operation out of the box.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The three most repeated positives across the 225 ratings are the adjustable flow rate, the stable base, and the storage-friendly collapse. One verified buyer wrote: "I've bought three different floaters over the years. This is the first one that doesn't flip over when the kids are splashing." Another summed it up: "It just works. No leaks, no sinking, no drama." The O-ring seal holds up well—no complaints about water getting inside the tablet chamber, which is the most common failure point on cheap floaters.

**Where it falls short**

The stiff adjustment dial is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. It's not a dealbreaker, but it does require real effort to turn when the unit is new. If you have arthritis or limited hand strength, this could be frustrating. Second, this is not a saltwater pool product. The manufacturer doesn't claim it is, but the ABS plastic will degrade faster in salt systems. If you run a saltwater chlorine generator, skip this and buy a feeder rated for salt. Finally, the flotation ring is a flat surface that collects a green algae film if left floating for weeks without attention. A quick wipe-down every couple of weeks solves it, but it's an extra maintenance step some buyers didn't expect.

**Who should buy this**

This is the right dispenser for the average residential pool owner with a chlorine tablet routine who wants a reliable, adjustable feeder without spending $50+ on a skimmer-mounted system. It's especially good for seasonal pool owners who store their gear over winter—the collapsible design genuinely frees up shelf space. It's also a smart pick for pools with variable chlorine demand, like those under heavy tree cover or with frequent heavy use. Skip it if you have a saltwater pool, if you need a feeder that handles 1-inch tablets (this takes 3-inch only), or if you want a zero-maintenance option—no floater is that.

**FAQ**

*Does it hold multiple tablets at once?*
Yes, it holds up to three 3-inch tablets stacked in the chamber. That's about a week of coverage for a typical 15,000-gallon pool, depending on your dial setting.

*Can I use it with a solar cover on the pool?*
Yes, the low profile (about 6 inches tall) means it won't snag most solar covers. Just make sure the cover is fully floating, not taut, so the dispenser can move freely.

*How do I refill it without getting chlorine on my hands?*
Twist off the lid, lift the inner basket out, and dump old tablet fragments into the trash. Use gloves or a scoop for new tablets—direct skin contact with chlorine tablets can cause irritation.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a stable, adjustable chlorine floater that stores flat and won't tip over at the first splash. **Skip it if** you run a saltwater pool, need 1-inch tablet compatibility, or want a dial that's effortless to turn from day one.
