## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, the price — you're paying a significant premium for the Maytronics logo. Third-party 29V supplies for Dolphin cleaners run $60–$100, and some reviewers report success with those. But the failure rate is notably higher, and a bad power supply can damage the robot's control board, turning a $120 fix into a $500 problem. The OEM part is the safer bet, but it's not cheap.

Second, the 10-foot cable is limiting. If your pool's electrical outlet is on the far side of the deck, you'll either need an outdoor extension cord (a tripping hazard) or a permanent outlet relocation.

Third, there's no diagnostic LED beyond the basic power indicator. If the supply powers on but your robot still won't run, you're back to guessing whether the issue is in the cable, the robot's internals, or something else entirely.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this work with my Dolphin Nautilus CC Plus?**
A: Yes. The 99956035 is compatible with the Nautilus CC, CC Plus, Discovery, and S-series models. Check your robot's model number against Maytronics' compatibility list if unsure, but this covers the vast majority of current Dolphin cleaners.

**Q: How do I know if my power supply is actually dead?**
A: If the LED on the supply doesn't light up when plugged in, it's almost certainly dead. If the LED lights but the robot won't run, the problem is likely in the robot's cable or internal components, not the supply.

**Q: Can I use an extension cord with this?**
A: Yes, but use a heavy-duty outdoor-rated cord (14-gauge or thicker) and keep it as short as possible. Long, thin extension cords can cause voltage drop, which may prevent the robot from running properly.
