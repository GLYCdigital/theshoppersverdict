## You're One Click Away From a Box of Live Plants — Here's What Actually Shows Up

Ordering live plants online feels like a gamble. I've been burned before by crushed stems and rotting roots. So when Altman Plants — one of the largest succulent growers in the US — started selling directly on Amazon, I was cautiously interested. With 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the consensus is clear: this is one of the more reliable ways to get healthy succulents shipped to your door.

## What's Actually in the Box

You're getting live succulents in 2-inch nursery pots — not bare-root cuttings wrapped in damp paper towel like some budget sellers do. That matters. Established root systems mean the plants skip the stressful "re-rooting" phase and start growing immediately.

Depending on which listing you choose, you'll get 6 to 12 different species. The mix packs are where the value lives. You'll find Echeveria varieties with their signature rosette shapes, spiky Haworthia, and trailing Graptopetalum — the kind of diversity you won't find in the sad, sunburned selection at your local Home Depot. Each pot is labeled with the species name and basic care instructions, which is genuinely rare for online plant sellers.

## How They Actually Perform

The plants arrive in surprisingly good condition. The grower uses a thick cardboard box with each 2-inch pot secured in its own compartment — no rattling around, no crushed leaves. One buyer noted, "All six arrived perfectly, better packed than most electronics I've ordered."

The health of the plants themselves is the standout. These are greenhouse-grown, meaning they're acclimated to bright, filtered light — not the harsh full sun they'd get on a south-facing windowsill. Give them a week to adjust before moving them to their permanent spot. They're also well-rooted; you'll see white, healthy roots poking through the drainage holes within days.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across hundreds of reviews, three themes keep coming up:

**Health on arrival.** The overwhelming majority report plants that look like they came from a nursery, not a shipping warehouse. One reviewer put it simply: "Every single plant was healthy, plump, and beautiful."

**Variety.** The mix packs genuinely deliver. "I got 12 different types, all labeled, all healthy. Great variety for the price."

**Survival rate.** Even first-time succulent owners report success. These plants are forgiving, and the quality means fewer losses.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**Size can disappoint.** The photos show mature, full plants. What arrives can be smaller — sometimes significantly. One buyer warned, "The plants were much smaller than pictured. About two inches across." They'll grow into the size shown, but it takes months.

**The soil is wrong.** Altman uses a dense, peat-heavy mix that holds too much moisture for indoor growing. If you're placing these on a windowsill, repot into a gritty cactus mix within the first week. Skip this step and root rot is a real risk.

**Shipping is slow and cold-sensitive.** Expect 5-7 days in transit. If you're in a cold climate and order in winter, you're gambling — there's no heat pack option on most listings. Spring through fall is the safe window.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a curated collection of healthy, labeled succulents without hunting through big-box stores. You're a beginner who wants forgiving plants that arrive in good shape. You appreciate variety over size.

**Skip it if:** You need instant impact for a large planter — these are small starts, not showpiece plants. You're shopping in deep winter in a northern state. You don't want to repot right away.
