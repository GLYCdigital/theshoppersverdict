Let me save you some scrolling: the OnBeam Solar Bug Zapper is a solid mid-tier mosquito killer that works best when you set realistic expectations. It won't clear your entire half-acre, but for a 15-foot radius around your patio table? It pulls its weight.

**What's Actually in the Box**

The unit ships with the zapper body, a detachable solar panel on top, a 2000mAh internal battery, and plastic zip-tie style mounting straps. There's no wall adapter and no USB charging option — this is pure solar, which is either a feature or a limitation depending on your climate. As shown in the product image, the panel sits angled on top, so you'll want to mount it where it catches direct sun for most of the day.

**Build and Real-World Performance**

The housing is a sturdy ABS plastic that feels like it can take a few seasons of weather. The IP65 waterproof rating isn't marketing fluff — several buyers mention leaving it out through rainstorms and it keeps firing. The UV LED attracts mosquitoes, moths, and gnats within a 400–600 square foot radius. That's roughly a standard patio, not your back forty.

The internal battery is the real story here. A full day of direct sun gives you 8–10 hours of nighttime operation. That's enough to cover dinner through late evening. The tradeoff? Overcast days hurt. Multiple reviewers note that after two or three cloudy days, the unit runs out of juice by midnight. If you live somewhere with frequent overcast summers, you'll be manually turning it off to conserve power or dealing with a dead unit by morning.

**What Buyers Keep Saying**

The praise clusters around two things: the waterproofing and the silent operation. One reviewer put it plainly: "I've had cheaper zappers die after one rain. This one's been through three storms and still zaps every night." Another noted, "It's quiet — no loud crackling, which I actually prefer because the sound doesn't bother the neighbors."

The complaints are equally consistent. The zap is subtle, so you won't get that satisfying pop you hear from plug-in units. And the mounting straps — basic plastic ties — are the weakest link. One buyer said, "The straps work on my wooden fence, but they feel cheap. I ended up using a hose clamp instead."

**Where It Falls Short**

The 3.8-star average reflects real tradeoffs. The biggest one: solar dependence. If you're buying this for a shaded porch or covered patio, you're wasting your money — the panel needs direct sun to charge effectively. The second issue is the kill rate. It attracts and kills mosquitoes, but it's not a trap. It won't eliminate a breeding population; it just thins the ones flying through your zone. Third, the coverage claim on the box (up to 1,500 sq ft) is optimistic. Real-world testing puts it closer to half that.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Patio dwellers** who spend evenings outside and want a maintenance-free, cord-free zapper — this is your unit.
- **Campers or RV owners** who need something portable that runs without an outlet.
- **Renters** who can't hardwire anything and need a temporary solution.

**Skip It If**

- Your yard is fully shaded or you live in the Pacific Northwest. The battery won't keep up.
- You need whole-yard coverage. This is a zone tool, not a perimeter defense.
- You want visible confirmation it's working. The quiet zap won't satisfy that urge.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does it work during the day?**
A: It zaps whenever the grid is energized, but the UV light is much less effective in daylight. It's designed for dusk-to-dawn use.

**Q: How do I clean the dead bugs out?**
A: The grid pulls out with a small brush (included). Unclip the bottom guard, brush off the debris, and snap it back. Takes about 30 seconds.

**Q: Can I leave it out all winter?**
A: The housing is weatherproof, but the battery will degrade in freezing temperatures. Bring it inside if your winters drop below freezing for extended stretches.

**The Verdict: 3.7/5**

**Buy it if** you want a reliable, solar-powered zapper for a specific outdoor seating area and you have direct sun to charge it. It's waterproof, quiet, and genuinely maintenance-free.

**Skip it if** you need heavy-duty mosquito control, have a shaded yard, or expect the advertised 1,500 sq ft coverage. This is a good product with honest limitations — buy it knowing what it is, not what the box claims.
