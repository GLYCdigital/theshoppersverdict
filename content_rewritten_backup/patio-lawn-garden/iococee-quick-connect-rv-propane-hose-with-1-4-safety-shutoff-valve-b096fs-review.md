**The Short Version:** If you've ever disconnected a propane line while the tank was still open, you know that heart-stopping hiss of gas escaping. The Iococee Quick-Connect RV Propane Hose eliminates that anxiety with a simple, mechanical failsafe — and it does it without breaking the bank.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get the 4-foot hose assembly with a Type 1 ACME fitting on one end (that's the big brass nut that threads onto standard 20-pound BBQ tanks) and a 1/4-inch quick-connect plug on the other. There's also a mating socket that you'll install on your grill or fire pit. No adapters, no extra parts, no instructions needed — it's about as plug-and-play as propane accessories get.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The hose itself is wrapped in thick, industrial-grade rubber that feels substantially more durable than the thin vinyl lines that come stock with most budget grills. I've left it coiled in a truck bed through a full Northeast winter, and it's still supple — no cracking, no stiffening that would suggest internal damage.

The star of the show is the 1/4-inch safety shutoff. Here's how it works: when the quick-connect is fully seated, gas flows normally. The moment you depress the release collar, an internal spring snaps a poppet valve shut before the connection separates. In practice, that means zero gas escapes during disconnect — a feature that matters more than most shoppers realize. I tested it with a leak-detection solution after several connects/disconnects, and got no bubbles at the connection point.

One thing to note: the quick-connect requires a firm, deliberate push to seat fully. If you half-ass it, the shutoff valve won't open, and you'll get no flow. That's actually a safety feature working as intended, but it caught me off guard the first few times. A few Amazon reviewers echo this — one noted, "Takes a solid click to get it to seat, but once it does, it's rock solid."

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't inflated. The most common praise centers on the shutoff valve's reliability. As one verified buyer put it: "Finally a hose that doesn't leak when I unhook it from the grill. The valve snaps shut before the connector even separates." Another mentioned using it for a patio heater: "Perfect fit, no adapters needed, and the safety valve gives me peace of mind with kids around."

**Where It Falls Short**

The 4-foot length is genuinely limiting. If your propane tank sits more than a few feet from your grill or fire pit, you'll need to buy an extension hose — which partially defeats the convenience of a one-piece setup. Also, the brass quick-connect is heavier than you'd expect; if you're mounting this on a lightweight tabletop grill, the weight can pull on the fitting. A few reviewers also mentioned that the shutoff valve makes it slightly harder to purge air from the line when first connecting — you'll need to open the tank valve slowly and wait a moment for flow to establish.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for three types of shoppers: RV owners who want a reliable disconnect at the campsite, homeowners with permanent patio setups who leave a tank connected all season, and anyone who's ever gotten a face full of propane during a grill swap. If you're a tailgater who sets up and tears down weekly, the quick-connect will save you time and frustration.

Skip it if you need a longer hose (buy a 10-foot version instead) or if you're connecting to a non-standard appliance that uses a different thread pattern.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this work with a standard Weber or Coleman grill?**
A: Yes, if your grill has a 1/4-inch quick-connect port or a standard regulator inlet. Most modern grills, fire pits, and patio heaters use this thread pattern. Older grills with proprietary fittings will need an adapter.

**Q: Can I leave the hose connected to the tank with the valve open?**
A: Technically yes, but the safety shutoff only protects the quick-connect end. The hose itself is rated for continuous propane service, so it's safe to leave pressurized. Still, good practice is to close the tank valve when not in use.

**Q: Is this safe for indoor use with a propane heater?**
A: No. Propane appliances — including this hose — are for outdoor use only. Never use any propane product indoors or in enclosed spaces.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want an affordable, reliable way to disconnect your propane appliance without losing gas or worrying about leaks. The safety shutoff is a genuine upgrade over standard hoses, and the build quality justifies the price.

**Skip it if** your setup requires more than 4 feet of hose, or if you're looking for a lightweight, ultra-flexible option for frequent travel.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-engineered safety upgrade with a minor length limitation.
