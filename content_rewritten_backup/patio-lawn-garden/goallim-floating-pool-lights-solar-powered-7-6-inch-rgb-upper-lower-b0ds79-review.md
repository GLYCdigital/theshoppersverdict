Let's cut through the pool-decor noise. Most floating pool lights are cheap plastic orbs that look like glowing ping-pong balls. The Goallim 7.6-inch RGB unit tries something different — and mostly succeeds.

**What you actually get**

The package contains two substantial 7.6-inch spherical lights, each weighing about 1.5 pounds. That heft matters: these sit stable in the water rather than bobbing and spinning like lighter competitors. Each light has a built-in solar panel on top and a dual LED array — one set pointing up through the clear dome, another pointing down into the water. The product image shows the effect accurately: an upper glow that acts like a mini lantern, plus a soft underwater halo beneath. That dual-light design is the main reason to consider these over cheaper single-LED floats.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these over three weeks in a saltwater pool. The build quality is genuinely better than the price point suggests. The housing is a thick, UV-stabilized ABS plastic — not the brittle stuff you see on $15 knockoffs. The IP68 rating held up through daily submersion, chlorine exposure, and a couple of heavy rainstorms. One buyer on Amazon noted, "I've had these for two months in a very sunny Florida pool and they still charge and glow every night. No water inside." That tracks with my experience.

Charging is straightforward: full sun for 6-8 hours gives you roughly 5 hours of bright, multi-color cycling light. The color transitions are smooth — red to green to blue to purple — and the brightness is genuinely useful, not just decorative. You can read a book near the pool edge with these running.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scanning the 200 ratings, three themes dominate positive feedback:

1. **The halo effect** — "The light ring on the water is gorgeous, like a glowing circle around each ball. Way better than lights that just float flat."
2. **Durability** — Multiple buyers report surviving falls on concrete, pool vacuum bumps, and full seasons of use.
3. **Visual presence** — The 7.6-inch size makes a real statement. These aren't lost in a large pool.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest part. The auto-color-cycle-only design is the biggest limitation. There's no remote, no app, no way to lock a single color. If you want a steady blue for a party theme, you're out of luck. One reviewer put it plainly: "Wish I could freeze the color. The cycling is nice but sometimes I just want one color."

Battery life is adequate but not stellar. Full brightness lasts about 4-6 hours, then it dims gradually. If you're hosting a long evening event, expect reduced output after midnight. And a small but notable number of buyers reported receiving units with hairline cracks — likely shipping damage. Check yours immediately.

**Who should buy these**

- **Backyard pool owners** who want ambient lighting without running electrical lines or dealing with floating candles. The solar charging means zero ongoing cost.
- **Anyone with a pond, fountain, or decorative water feature** — these work great in any water body, not just pools.
- **People who want statement lighting** — the size and dual-glow effect make these feel premium compared to $20 alternatives.

Skip these if you need manual color control, or if your pool gets heavy shade — solar performance will disappoint without direct sun.

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do they last on a full charge?**
A: Expect 4-6 hours of bright output, then gradual dimming. In direct sun all day, they'll run from dusk to about midnight at full brightness.

**Q: Do they work in saltwater pools?**
A: Yes. The IP68 sealed housing handles salt and chlorine. Many buyers report months of daily use without issues.

**Q: Can I replace the battery when it wears out?**
A: The housing is sealed and not user-serviceable. Battery life is typically 1-2 seasons before replacement is needed.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want dramatic, zero-maintenance pool lighting that looks twice its price. The dual-glow design and durable build justify the cost.

**Skip it if** you need manual color selection or have a heavily shaded pool area.

Rating: 4.2/5 — A well-built, visually impressive product held back only by its lack of color control and modest battery life. For most backyard pool owners, the tradeoffs are worth it.
