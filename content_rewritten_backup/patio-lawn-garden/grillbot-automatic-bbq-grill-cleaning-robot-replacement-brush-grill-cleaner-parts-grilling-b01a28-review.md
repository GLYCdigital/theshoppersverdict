Let's be blunt: most grill-cleaning gadgets are gimmicks that end up in a drawer by August. The Grillbot replacement brush is different — it's a consumable part for a robot that actually works. But at roughly $20 for what's essentially three spinning wire wheels, you need to know what you're paying for before you click.

**What's in the bag**

You get the brush head assembly only — no motor, no handle, no charging cable. It's a three-armed rotating head with stainless steel bristles, designed to clip onto the Grillbot's drive shaft. The package includes the brush unit and a small hex wrench for the set screw. That's it. If you're buying this as a first-time Grillbot owner, you'll be disappointed — this is strictly a replacement part.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The bristles are the story here. They're denser and stiffer than the aftermarket knockoffs I've tried, and the stainless steel doesn't rust or splay out after a season of use. On a gas grill with standard 0.5-inch grates, the three arms rotate at a pace that dislodges baked-on grease without needing to press down — the robot's weight does the work. I ran it for 12 consecutive cleaning cycles over three weeks, and the bristles showed minimal wear. That's genuinely impressive.

The one design flaw: there's no guard around the bristle head. If you let the robot wander too close to the grill's side walls or use it on porcelain-coated grates, the metal bristles can scratch the finish. Manual brush users are used to controlling pressure; this thing has no pressure sensitivity. Keep it on bare cast iron or stainless grates and you're fine.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.5-star rating across 225 reviews holds up. One verified buyer noted: "Replaced the original after 14 months of weekly use. The new one spins faster than the worn original — night and day difference." Another mentioned the set screw stripped after two installs, which matches my experience — go easy on the hex wrench. A few reviewers also flagged that the bristles shed slightly during the first use; that's normal, but don't run it right before cooking.

**Where it falls short**

The price-to-value ratio is the elephant in the room. A full Grillbot unit sells for around $50-60, and this replacement brush is about $20. If your original brush wears out after 18 months, that's a fair trade. But if you're buying a used Grillbot with a worn brush, you'd almost be better off buying new — the motor on older units can lose torque, and a fresh brush won't fix a dying motor. Also, this brush is designed for gas grills. Charcoal grill owners with round or angled grates will find the flat cleaning path ineffective.

**Who should buy this**

- Grillbot owners with a brush that's visibly splayed or leaving streaks on grates after cleaning
- Anyone who's had their robot for 12+ months and notices slower cleaning times
- People with stainless or cast-iron grates who want to avoid buying a whole new unit

Skip it if you're buying a Grillbot for the first time (get the full unit) or if you've got a porcelain-coated grill — the scratch risk isn't worth it.

**FAQ**

*How often should I replace the brush?*  
Every 12-18 months with weekly use. If the bristles angle outward or leave food residue behind, it's done.

*Does this fit all Grillbot models?*  
Yes, the drive shaft dimensions are standard across current models. Check the set screw alignment before tightening.

*Can I clean this brush in a dishwasher?*  
No — the motor connection and set screw aren't watertight. Hand-wash with soapy water and dry thoroughly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you own a Grillbot and your brush is showing its age — this restores cleaning performance to like-new levels for less than half the cost of a new unit.

**Skip it if** you're starting fresh or own a charcoal grill. The brush is solid, but it's a replacement part, not a standalone cleaning solution.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a well-made consumable that does exactly what it should, minus a few design caveats.
