Most bird baths are either cheap plastic that tips over in a breeze or fragile ceramic that cracks after one hard frost. The Birds Choice Ohio Stoneware model sits in a different category entirely — and after digging through 225 ratings and the build specs, I can tell you exactly why it earns its 4.5-star average.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a two-piece setup: a 16-inch diameter bowl and a pedestal base that stands 24 inches tall when assembled. The whole thing weighs in around 18 pounds, which is substantial for a bird bath — that's the stoneware working in your favor. As shown in the product image, the bowl has a gently textured interior surface rather than a glossy glaze. That's deliberate: birds get traction, and the matte finish reduces glare that can spook skittish species.

What's notably absent from the box is any sort of drainage plug or rubber stopper. The bowl holds roughly three gallons of water, and you'll be dumping it by hand. Keep that in mind before you commit to a spot far from the hose.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The headline feature here is the Ohio stoneware construction. This isn't a mass-produced ceramic from overseas — it's fired clay from U.S. soil. The 0.5-inch wall thickness is what makes the difference when temperatures swing below freezing. Thin ceramics trap moisture and fracture; thick stoneware gives water room to expand without structural failure. Several reviewers confirmed leaving theirs out through full Midwest winters with zero cracking.

The weight is a double-edged sword. It won't blow over in a thunderstorm, and squirrels won't knock it down mid-drink. But you're not moving this thing around your yard casually — pick your location once and commit.

The unglazed exterior is where the product shows its one real weakness. That porous surface absorbs moisture and organic debris, which means algae and moss will build up along the seams faster than on a sealed ceramic or metal bath. A yearly coat of masonry sealer solves this, but it's maintenance you should budget for.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most repeated compliment in the reviews centers on stability. One buyer noted, "I've had robins, cardinals, and even a pair of mourning doves using it daily — the textured surface keeps them from slipping when it's wet." Another reviewer who replaced a cracked glazed bath wrote, "This is the third bath I've owned and the first that's made it through two winters without a single chip."

Bird attraction seems to be a genuine factor too. Multiple reviewers reported increased yard traffic within the first week, which tracks with the matte finish — it reads as a natural water source rather than a shiny human object.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the sealing requirement, there's the drainage issue I mentioned. Without a plug, you're either tilting an 18-pound ceramic bowl to dump it (awkward and risky — I'd recommend a small siphon) or you're letting water stagnate between cleanings. That's a mosquito breeding invitation in warmer months.

The size also deserves a note: 16 inches is on the smaller side for a pedestal bath. Larger species like blue jays or grackles will fit, but don't expect a crow or dove to do much more than perch on the rim.

**Who Should Buy This**

This bath is for the gardener who wants a permanent, attractive water feature that pulls double duty as a functional bird magnet. It's ideal for anyone in cold climates who's tired of replacing cracked baths every spring. It's also a strong pick for birders who want a stable platform that won't shift in heavy wind.

Skip it if you're looking for low-maintenance — the sealing and manual draining require commitment. And if you're on a tight budget, you can find cheaper imports that will work for a season or two, but you'll be shopping again soon.

**FAQ**

*Is the bowl removable for cleaning?*
Yes, the bowl lifts straight off the pedestal. It's just heavy — around 8 pounds on its own. Clean it with a stiff brush and water; avoid harsh detergents that can leave residue harmful to birds.

*Will this crack if I leave it out in winter?*
Reviewers report it survives freezing, but the safest practice is to empty and invert the bowl before deep freezes. The stoneware resists cracking, but empty is always safer than full.

*Does it come with a stand or just the bowl?*
The box includes both the pedestal base and the bowl. Assembly is tool-free — the bowl sits on the pedestal's lip and stays put thanks to the weight.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a durable, handsome bird bath that survives winters and won't tip over, and you're willing to do light annual maintenance.

**Skip it if:** You want set-and-forget convenience or need something you can move around the yard regularly.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The Birds Choice Ohio Stoneware bath delivers on the two things that matter most: durability and bird appeal. The sealing and drainage quirks keep it from a perfect score, but for most homeowners, this is the last bird bath you'll need to buy.
