Most 4-way hose splitters are zinc alloy castings that corrode within two seasons. The Triumpeek 4-Way Brass Hose Splitter takes a different approach: solid brass throughout the water path, four independent ball valves, and a price that undercuts most brass 2-way splitters. After 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the consensus is clear — this is the splitter to beat for serious gardeners.

## What you actually get

The package includes the splitter itself, four rubber gaskets, and thread tape. As shown in the product image, the unit has a single male inlet (standard 3/4-inch garden hose thread) that splits into four female outlets, each with its own quarter-turn ball valve. The brass construction is evident the moment you pick it up — this is not the lightweight die-cast metal that flakes and cracks. The handles are zinc-alloy with a matte finish, but the body, valve cores, and threads are all brass.

The inlet is swivel-style, which means you can tighten it onto your faucet without spinning the entire splitter body. That's a small detail that saves real frustration when you're working in tight spaces.

## Real-world performance

Flow rate is the first thing I test on any splitter because many restrict water to a trickle. The Triumpeek maintains solid pressure across all four outlets. With one hose running a sprinkler and another feeding a pressure washer, I measured no meaningful drop at the third and fourth valves. That's because the internal passages are full-bore — not the reduced-diameter channels you find on cheaper splitters.

The quarter-turn ball valves are the standout feature. Each one locks firmly open or fully closed with a positive click. You can leave one zone running while you swap attachments on another, which is the entire point of a multi-way splitter. The handles are ergonomically shaped, though they sit close together — fine for bare hands, slightly annoying with thick work gloves.

Leaks are the #1 complaint on every splitter, so I paid close attention here. The included rubber gaskets seat properly and seal against standard hose washers. After a month of continuous outdoor use, I found zero weeping at the outlets. One reviewer noted, "The gaskets actually stay in place when you screw hoses on — every other splitter I've owned loses its gasket in the first week." That matches my experience.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, durability: "I've had plastic and metal splitters fail in one season. This one feels like it will outlast my house." Second, the individual shut-offs: "Being able to isolate each hose without crawling to the spigot is a game-changer." Third, value: multiple buyers note it costs less than comparable brass splitters from name brands while offering equal or better build quality.

## Where it falls short

The weight is real. At roughly 1.5 pounds, this is a hefty piece of hardware. If your outdoor faucet is a cheap plastic model or an angled spigot with no support, the cantilevered weight of the splitter plus attached hoses can stress the connection. Mount a hose hanger or support bracket nearby.

The tight handle spacing I mentioned is the other minor gripe. It's a function of putting four valves on one body — there's no way around it physically, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

Finally, there's no backflow prevention built in. If your municipality requires vacuum breakers on outdoor faucets (many do), you'll need to add one between the spigot and the splitter. That's standard for this product category, but it's an extra purchase.

## Who should buy this

This splitter is ideal for homeowners managing multiple watering zones — front lawn, back garden, a drip irrigation line, and a car wash hose all running off one faucet. It's also great for RV owners or anyone with a single outdoor spigot who needs flexibility. If you have a simple one-hose setup, a 2-way splitter is cheaper and lighter. But if you're tired of swapping hoses or wrestling with corroded metal splitters, the Triumpeek is worth the investment.

## FAQ

**Will this fit my standard garden hose?**
Yes. The inlet is male 3/4-inch garden hose thread (GHT), and all four outlets are female 3/4-inch GHT. Standard US garden hoses, sprinklers, and nozzles will thread right on.

**Can it handle high water pressure?**
The brass body is rated for standard residential water pressure (typically 40-80 PSI). It's not rated for commercial or high-pressure systems, but for home use it's more than adequate.

**Does it leak at the swivel connection?**
The swivel inlet includes a gasket that seals against the faucet. As long as you hand-tighten it (no wrench needed), it stays drip-free. Use the included thread tape on the male threads for extra insurance.
