If you own a 46-inch MTD, Cub Cadet, Troy-Bilt, or Yard Machines mower, you already know the OEM blade game: pay dealer markup, wait for shipping, or gamble on a no-name set that doesn't fit. The MaxPower 561544B sits in the middle — an aftermarket twin-blade set that's been bought over 200 times and still holds a 4.6-star average. The question isn't whether it's cheap. It's whether it's *correct*.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two blades, and that's it. No spacers, no hardware, no washers. Each blade measures 18 inches long, 2.25 inches wide, with a 0.25-inch thickness — the standard spec for MTD 46-inch decks. The center hole and the two offset mounting holes are pre-drilled, and the cutting edges arrive sharp enough to handle a first mow without touch-up. The set weighs about 6 pounds total, which tells you these aren't stamped tin; they're solid steel.

## Fit and Installation — The Make-or-Break

The single biggest worry with aftermarket blades is whether they'll bolt up without a fight. Based on buyer feedback and my own checking, the MaxPower 561544B drops onto standard MTD/Cub Cadet spindles with zero modification. The offset holes match the OEM pattern exactly. One reviewer put it simply: "Bolted right on, no grinding, no drilling. Balanced on the first try."

That said, there's a catch. Multiple recent buyers flagged that some 2023-and-newer Cub Cadet XT series mowers use a different spindle bolt pattern. If your deck has a part number starting with "MTD" followed by 5 digits, you're likely fine. If it's a newer XT deck, pull one old blade and compare the hole spacing before you order. MaxPower's own fitment chart is decent, but it lags behind the latest model changes.

## Real-World Cutting Performance

The heat-treated steel is the headline feature here, and it isn't marketing fluff. Buyers consistently report getting a full season — 20 to 25 mows — before needing to sharpen. That's roughly 50% longer than typical OEM blades, which tend to dull noticeably by mid-summer. The cut quality is clean, especially on fescue and bluegrass; the blades produce a fine clippings texture that mulches well without leaving clumps.

Vibration is minimal. The set comes factory-balanced, and multiple reviewers noted their decks ran smoother than with the factory blades. One long-term owner said: "Two years on these, still cutting straight. My OEM blades never lasted this long." Another mentioned they'd bought a second set just to keep as spares — the highest compliment for a wear item.

## Where It Falls Short

Nothing's perfect. The black paint coating on the top surface chips off within the first few mows, leaving bare steel exposed. This is cosmetic — it doesn't affect cutting — but if you're particular about appearances, it'll bug you. More importantly, the blades aren't hardened to the same spec as premium aftermarket brands like Oregon Gator. They'll hold an edge longer than OEM, but if you're mowing over sandy soil or hitting rocks regularly, expect to sharpen more often.

There's also the fitment caveat mentioned above. MaxPower covers a huge range of MTD-family decks, but that broad coverage means they can't guarantee every new model. Double-check your deck's part number against their list before ordering.

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my 46-inch Cub Cadet XT1?**
A: Most XT1 and LT models with the standard MTD 46-inch deck will accept these. But if your deck is a 2023 or newer XT series, remove one blade and compare the bolt hole pattern first. The spacing changed on some newer spindles.

**Q: Do I need to sharpen them before the first use?**
A: No. They arrive sharp enough for a clean cut. After about 20-25 mows, plan to touch them up with a file or grinder.

**Q: Are these blades mulching or side-discharge?**
A: They're a hybrid design — a flat blade with a mild lift wing. They work well for side discharge, bagging, and light mulching. If you exclusively mulch heavy leaves, a dedicated mulching blade will do better.
