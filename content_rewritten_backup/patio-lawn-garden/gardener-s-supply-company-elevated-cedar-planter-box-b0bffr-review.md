If you've spent a single season crouched over a ground-level raised bed, you already know the pain. The Gardener's Supply Company Elevated Cedar Planter Box solves that problem with a 30-inch working height that brings the soil to you — not the other way around. It's not the cheapest option, but for gardeners with back issues or limited mobility, this box earns its keep quickly.

**What you actually get**

The box arrives as a flat-pack kit of western red cedar panels, legs, and hardware. Assembled, it measures roughly 4 feet long, 2 feet wide, and stands 30 inches tall at the soil surface. That's a real planter, not a decorative window box — it holds about 10 cubic feet of soil, which is enough for a serious tomato crop or a full herb garden. The cedar is thick, roughly 1 inch nominal, and feels substantial when you lift the pieces. You also get a plastic liner that fits inside to slow wood degradation — though Gardener's Supply sells it separately, which feels like nickel-and-diming at this price point.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The cedar is the headline here. It's naturally rot-resistant and insect-repellent, which is why it's the gold standard for outdoor furniture and planters. The box is built with mortise-and-tenon-style joinery at the corners, not just butt joints with screws — that's a meaningful structural upgrade. Once assembled, it doesn't wobble. Fill it with wet soil and it stays square, which is more than I can say for cheaper kits that rack and twist.

The elevated design does double duty: it saves your back and extends your season because the soil warms faster in spring. The bottom has drainage gaps, and the legs have adjustable feet so you can level it on uneven patio stones. That's thoughtful engineering you notice only when you need it.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews reflects a pattern. Gardeners with chronic pain or older joints repeatedly call it a game-changer. One reviewer wrote, "I can actually tend my garden without kneeling — worth every penny for my knees." Another noted, "The cedar smells amazing and looks far nicer than the plastic planters my neighbors have." Assembly complaints are the most common negative theme, though most say it's a 45-minute job with two people.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the assembly. The pre-drilled holes don't always line up perfectly, and you'll need a drill and a rubber mallet to persuade stubborn joints. It's not a 15-minute snap-together job. The cedar will also gray out within a season unless you apply a UV-protective sealer — the box ships unfinished, so plan for that maintenance if you care about aesthetics. And at this price, the fact that the liner costs extra stings.

**Who should buy this**

This is for the gardener who wants a permanent, attractive raised bed and values ergonomics over budget. It's ideal for older gardeners, people with back or knee issues, and anyone gardening on a patio or deck where ground-level beds aren't an option. If you're a renter or want something you can move easily, skip it — this box is heavy once filled and meant to stay put.

**FAQ**

**How long will the cedar last?** Untreated, expect 5-7 years before rot becomes a concern, depending on your climate. Sealing it annually can push that to 10+ years. The included liner helps, but the legs contact the ground — add a barrier if you're on wet soil.

**What can I grow in it?** The 4x2 foot footprint handles determinate tomatoes, peppers, root vegetables, or a dense herb garden. You could fit one indeterminate tomato but it'll be crowded. Depth is roughly 12 inches, which suits most vegetables except deep-rooting carrots.

**Does it need to be on a level surface?** The adjustable feet handle minor slopes, but a truly uneven patio will stress the joints. Leveling takes a few minutes and is worth doing properly.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, attractive raised planter that saves your back and looks good doing it. The cedar construction and ergonomic height justify the premium for serious gardeners.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, want a quick-assembly kit, or need something portable. The extra cost of the liner and sealer should factor into your decision.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a well-built, genuinely useful planter that loses points only on assembly fussiness and the à la carte accessories.
