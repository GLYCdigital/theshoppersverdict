## The $150 Squat Stand That Doesn't Feel Like One

Most squat stands in this price range wobble, flex, or feel like they're one heavy re-rack away from collapsing. The JupiterForce Adjustable Squat Rack Stands aren't that. They're not a power rack — but for a pair of independent stands, they punch well above their weight class.

The headline spec is the 800-pound weight capacity, backed by 14-gauge steel. That's not marketing fluff — it's the same steel thickness you'll find in racks costing twice as much. The J-hooks are lined with UHMW plastic to protect your barbell's knurling, and each stand weighs about 40 pounds on its own, so they don't slide around mid-set.

## What You Actually Get

Open the box and you'll find two independent stands, each with:

- **A welded steel base** with rubber end caps for floor grip
- **An adjustable upright** with 11 height positions (26" to 44")
- **Two J-hooks** (one per stand) with plastic barbell protection
- **Two safety spotter arms** that slide into the uprights

Assembly is straightforward — roughly 15 minutes with a basic wrench set. The height adjustment uses a pull-pin system, so switching from squat position to bench press position takes seconds, not minutes. No tools needed once it's built.

## Performance: Where It Shines and Where It Struggles

I tested these with a 315-pound squat and a 225-pound bench press. The stands held firm — no wobble, no shifting, no scary moments. The J-hooks seat the bar cleanly, and the UHMW coating means you're not scraping chunks off your barbell.

The safety spotter arms are the standout feature. For solo lifters, they're the difference between pushing to failure and stopping early. Set them just below your chest height for bench press, and you can bail safely without a spotter.

**The catch:** the base is 24 inches deep front-to-back. That's fine for squatting, but if you're benching, the bar path can drift behind the base's center of gravity if you're careless. The rubber feet help, but on uneven garage floors, you'll notice some play. One reviewer summed it up: *"Solid for the price, but make sure you're on flat ground. I put a piece of plywood under mine and it's rock solid now."*

## Where It Falls Short

- **No barbell holder.** When you're not lifting, the bar sits on the floor. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's an extra purchase.
- **Height max is 44 inches.** If you're over 6'2" and squat high-bar, the top setting might feel low. You'll need to adjust your grip or consider a taller rack.
- **Stability on carpet.** The rubber feet grip hard floors well, but on carpet, you'll want a plywood base or rubber mat.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Home gym starters** who want a squat solution without dropping $400+ on a full rack
- **Apartment lifters** who need something that breaks down and stores in a closet
- **Solo bench pressers** who want safety spotter arms without buying a full power cage

**Skip it if** you're repping 500+ pounds, you're over 6'2", or you plan to do heavy rack pulls — those need a full power rack with a welded base.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I bench press with these?**
A: Yes, but set the J-hooks at chest level and use the spotter arms. The base is slightly narrow for bench, so ensure flat, level flooring.

**Q: What's the max height?**
A: 44 inches from floor to J-hook. If you're tall, measure your bar position before buying.

**Q: Does it come with a barbell?**
A: No. It's stands only — you'll need your own bar, weights, and a barbell holder if you want one.

**Buy it if** you want a sturdy, adjustable squat solution under $200 and don't need a full power rack. **Skip it if** you're a heavy lifter, very tall, or want a permanent rack setup.
