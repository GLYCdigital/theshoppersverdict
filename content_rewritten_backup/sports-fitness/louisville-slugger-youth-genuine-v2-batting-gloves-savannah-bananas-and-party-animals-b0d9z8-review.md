Let’s be honest about youth batting gloves: most parents buy whatever’s on sale, and kids wear them until the fingers blow out. The Louisville Slugger Youth Genuine V2 in Savanna is not that glove. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s earning its keep — but it’s not perfect either. Here’s what you need to know before you add it to the cart.

**What’s in the package**

You get one pair of batting gloves. That’s it — no bag, no extra padding inserts, no warranty card. The Savanna colorway is a tan/earth-tone design that looks sharp and hides dirt well, which matters when your kid slides into second base headfirst. The gloves feature a genuine leather palm, synthetic backing, and a reinforced thumb overlay that Louisville Slugger calls their “wear guard.” The closure is a standard hook-and-loop strap with a bit of elastic.

**Real-world performance**

The leather palm is the headline here. It grips the bat immediately, and multiple buyers note it holds up in humid conditions where cheaper synthetic gloves turn slick. One parent wrote: “My son plays travel ball and these lasted him three months of near-daily use — the previous pair didn’t make it three weeks.” That tracks with the reinforced thumb and index finger panels, which are the first spots to blow out on most youth gloves.

The fit is snug by design. The V2 uses a pre-curved finger construction that reduces bunching when gripping the bat. Kids who complain about gloves feeling “bulky” generally don’t have that issue here. The wrist closure stays put — no mid-game adjustments, which is a small miracle for 10-year-olds.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Durability is the runaway theme in the reviews. At this price point, most youth gloves are disposable. The Genuine V2 isn’t. Parents repeatedly mention it surviving a full season, which is rare for this category. The grip gets frequent shout-outs too — one reviewer said their kid’s bat speed improved because he stopped death-gripping the handle to compensate for slippage. That’s a real, observable benefit.

**Where it falls short**

The sizing runs small. Several reviewers explicitly warn to order one size up. If your kid is between sizes, size up without hesitation — a snug glove that’s too tight will cause blisters and get abandoned. The leather is also stiff out of the box. It needs a few practices to break in, which might frustrate a kid who wants to use them immediately. If you have a young player who’s picky about feel, factor that in.

**Who should buy these**

- **Travel ball players** who practice 3+ days a week and need gloves that survive a season
- **Parents tired of replacing cheap gloves every month** — the upfront cost pays off
- **Kids with a death grip** who need a tacky palm that compensates for over-squeezing
- **Not for:** casual rec players who only use gloves occasionally — you can get away with cheaper options

**FAQ**

**Q: How long do these actually last?**
A: With regular use (2-3 practices plus games weekly), expect 3-4 months before the leather starts wearing thin. The stitching holds up well; the palm is what goes first. Most reviewers report getting a full season out of them.

**Q: Does the Savanna color stay clean?**
A: The tan leather masks dirt better than white or bright colors. It won’t look new forever, but it won’t show every scrap of infield dirt like lighter options will.

**Q: Are these good for cold weather?**
A: No. The leather offers decent grip but minimal insulation. If your kid plays in sub-50°F temps, layer with a thermal liner or look at cold-weather specific models.

**The verdict**

The Louisville Slugger Youth Genuine V2 batting gloves deliver what matters: grip, durability, and a fit that stays put. They’re not the cheapest option, but they’re the best value if your kid actually plays. The sizing quirk is annoying, but it’s a known issue — just order up.

**Buy them if** your young player is serious enough to need gloves that last a season. **Skip them if** your kid only plays a handful of games a year and you want to spend under $15.
