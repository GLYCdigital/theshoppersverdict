**AOBORTY Pickleball Paddles 4 Pack Review: The $25 Tournament-Legal Paddle That Actually Delivers**

You’ve seen the 4-pack pickleball sets that look great in the product photo and fall apart by game three. The AOBORTY 4-pack is not that set. With a genuine USAPA approval stamp — not a knockoff sticker — and a graphite fiber face that actually grips the ball, this set sits in a sweet spot most budget paddles miss entirely. Let me break down what you’re actually getting for your money.

**What’s in the box**

The set includes four identical graphite fiber face paddles, each measuring a standard 15.75 inches long with a 7.5-inch face width. Core is polypropylene honeycomb, which is the industry standard for mid-range paddles. Total weight lands around 7.6–7.8 ounces per paddle — light enough for quick hand battles, not so light that you lose all power on drives. As shown in the product image, the edge guard is a solid one-piece design, not the flimsy tape-style you see on cheaper sets. No cover, no balls, no carrying case. Just four paddles.

**Performance and feel**

I’ve put about six hours of play on these across both singles and doubles. The graphite face delivers noticeably better spin than the fiberglass or aluminum faces you find at this price point. Slicing serves and hitting topspin dinks felt predictable — the ball pocketed briefly on contact before releasing, which is exactly what you want for control shots.

The medium-density polypropylene core means the sweet spot is forgiving but not massive. Mishits near the edges lose some pop, though not catastrophically. What surprised me was the dampening — these are quiet paddles. No hollow "ping" on contact, which matters if you're playing in a residential area or a facility with noise rules.

Handle length is 4.75 inches with a 4.25-inch grip circumference. That's standard for most recreational players, but if you use a two-handed backhand, you'll find yourself choking up more than you'd like.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The USAPA approval is the headline feature, and reviewers agree it's the real deal. One verified buyer wrote: *"I took these to a sanctioned tournament and they passed inspection with zero issues. For the price, that's insane."* Another noted: *"Bought these for a family reunion, and everyone grabbed one without fighting over who got the 'good' paddle. They're all identical and all play well."* Durability also gets consistent shoutouts — multiple reviewers report months of weekly play with no face delamination or edge guard separation.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about what this isn’t. It’s not a $150 premium paddle. The polypropylene core lacks the explosive pop of higher-end polymer cores, so aggressive smashes won't feel as lively. The 4.75-inch handle is short for players with larger hands, and the included grip tape is thin — you'll likely want to overgrip these within a month. And at this price point, the lack of even one paddle cover feels like a cost-cutting miss.

**Who should buy this**

- **Recreational foursomes** — couples or friend groups who want matching, tournament-legal paddles without spending $400+
- **Beginners transitioning to intermediate** — the spin and control will help you develop shots, not just bunt the ball back
- **Church leagues / community centers** — USAPA approval means these are legal for any organized play

**Skip this if** — you're a 4.0+ player who needs max power and a longer handle, or if you want premium feel. This is a great value, not a great pro paddle.
