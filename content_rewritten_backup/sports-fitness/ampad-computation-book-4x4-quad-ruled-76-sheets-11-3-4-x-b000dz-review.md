If you've ever tried to draw a matrix on college-ruled paper, you know the pain. The Ampad Computation Book solves that problem with a 4x4 quad grid — four squares per inch — printed on every single page. It's the kind of tool that disappears into your workflow because it just works. But is it the right notebook for *your* work? Let's break it down.

## What You Actually Get

This is a wire-bound book measuring 11-3/4" x 9-1/4" — slightly larger than standard letter size, which gives you a touch more writing room per line. You get 76 sheets (152 pages) of 4x4 quad-ruled paper, perforated at the top for clean tearing. The cover is a flexible chipboard with a classic teal-and-black design. It's not fancy, and that's fine. This is a utility tool, not a lifestyle accessory.

## Real-World Performance

The 4x4 grid is the star here. Each square is exactly 1/4 inch, which means you can do everything from long division to circuit diagrams without needing a ruler. The grid prints in light blue — subtle enough that your work reads clearly, but visible enough for precise plotting. I tested it with a standard Bic ballpoint and a Pilot G2 gel pen. The G2 ghosted slightly on the back of the page, but nothing bled through to the next sheet. A fountain pen with wet ink? Skip it — the 20 lb paper isn't built for that.

The perforations are the quiet hero. They tear along a clean micro-perf line with no ragged edges. I've torn out dozens of pages for grading and handouts, and every single one came out clean. The wire binding lies flat when open, which is essential when you're working through a multi-step problem and need both hands on the page.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 225 ratings at 4.5 stars, the dominant theme is reliability. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've been using these for years. The grid is perfectly aligned, and the paper takes pencil and ink without issue."* Another noted the value: *"Cheaper than buying graph paper pads separately, and the perforation makes it easy to submit homework."* Engineers, math students, and even accounting professionals repeatedly cite the consistent 4x4 grid as the reason they repurchase.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The 20 lb paper is functional but not premium — if you're a heavy ink user, you'll need to flip the page carefully. The wire binding is exposed, so it can catch on backpack zippers or loose items in a bag. And the light blue grid, while easy on the eyes, can feel washed out under harsh fluorescent lighting. If you need high-contrast lines for photocopying or scanning, this isn't the best choice.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Math and engineering students** — the 4x4 grid is ideal for graphs, matrices, and problem sets. The perforated pages make homework submission painless.
- **Professionals who sketch ideas** — architects, programmers, and designers who need a quick, precise grid for diagrams and flowcharts.
- **Anyone who hates graph paper's flimsy feel** — the chipboard back and wire binding give this a sturdy, durable build that survives a semester in a backpack.

**Skip it if** you primarily use fountain pens, need heavy 32 lb paper for mixed media, or want a notebook with a covered spiral binding.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this book have a margin on the left side?**  
A: No — the grid runs edge to edge on all sides. If you need a margin for three-hole punching or binding, you'll need to draw your own or use a different product.

**Q: Can I tear out pages without losing the rest of the book?**  
A: Yes. The perforations are at the top of each page, and they tear cleanly without weakening the binding. I've removed 20+ pages and the rest of the book stayed intact.

**Q: Is the grid light enough to not distract from my work?**  
A: The lines are light blue and about 0.5 pt thickness. They're visible but unobtrusive. If you need darker lines, this isn't the product for you.
