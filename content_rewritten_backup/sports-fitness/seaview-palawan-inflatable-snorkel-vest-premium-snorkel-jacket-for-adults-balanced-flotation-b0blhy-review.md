Let’s cut through the noise. The Seaview Palawan isn’t a life jacket, and it doesn’t pretend to be. It’s a buoyancy aid designed for one thing: keeping you floating comfortably at the surface while you focus on the reef below. After 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the consensus is clear — this vest works. But it’s not for everyone, and the fine print matters.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the vest itself, a detachable CO2 inflation cartridge (16g), a mesh storage pouch, and a printed instruction card. The vest is constructed from a 3mm neoprene shell bonded to an internal TPU bladder — the same material combo used in higher-end surf vests. At 1.2 pounds, it’s light enough to forget you’re wearing it, yet the dual-chamber design provides up to 50 Newtons of lift, which is roughly the buoyancy needed to keep a 200-pound adult’s head above water.

**Real-world performance**

I took this vest out in both calm lagoon conditions and slightly choppy open water. The oral inflation valve sits high on the left shoulder, so you don’t need to crane your neck awkwardly to top it up. Here’s the honest part: full inflation takes about 10 solid breaths. It’s work, but the payoff is precise control. You can add a puff or two to adjust your float angle without overshooting into "corkscrew" territory — a common complaint with single-chamber vests.

The CO2 cartridge is a nice backup, but it’s not a replacement for proper inflation. Pull the cord and you get roughly 80% buoyancy instantly. It’s loud, a bit startling the first time, and the cartridge is single-use. Refills run about $8 each, so don’t treat it as your primary inflation method.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Scrolling through the reviews, three themes keep surfacing. First, confidence for beginners. One verified buyer wrote, *"I’m not a strong swimmer and was terrified of deep water. This vest let me relax and actually enjoy snorkeling for the first time."* Second, packability — travelers repeatedly mention it fits in a carry-on without sacrificing other gear. Third, comfort over extended sessions. The neoprene doesn’t chafe under the arms, even after two hours in salt water.

**Where it falls short**

The biggest legitimate gripe: no crotch strap. On slender builds, the vest can ride up toward the chin in choppy conditions. It’s not dangerous — you’re not going to slip out — but it’s annoying. One reviewer noted, *"I’m 5’9”, 145 lbs, and had to keep pulling it down between dives."* Also, the oral valve is stiff when new. It loosens up, but expect a few awkward minutes on your first use.

**Who should buy this**

The Palawan is ideal for three groups: casual vacation snorkelers who want extra security without a bulky life vest, photographers who need stable flotation while framing shots, and travelers prioritizing packability. It’s a poor fit for freedivers, spearfishers, or anyone needing a certified PFD for boat excursions — this is not USCG-approved.

**FAQ**

*Can I wear this with a wetsuit?* Yes, but order up if you’re between sizes and plan to layer over 3mm neoprene. The adjustable side straps have about 4 inches of give.

*How do I clean it after saltwater use?* Rinse with fresh water, partially inflate, and hang dry out of direct sunlight. Never machine wash — it’ll damage the TPU bladder.

*Is the CO2 cartridge included?* Yes, one 16g cartridge ships with the vest. Replacement 16g cartridges are widely available.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want reliable, adjustable flotation for recreational snorkeling, value packability, and don’t mind a few breaths to dial in your buoyancy.

**Skip it if:** You need a certified life jacket, have a very slender frame, or expect hands-free inflation without effort.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A well-made, thoughtfully designed buoyancy aid with one notable design miss (the missing crotch strap) that keeps it from perfection. For the price of a decent dinner out, it’s the cheapest insurance for a relaxing day on the water.
