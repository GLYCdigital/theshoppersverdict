If you've been doing landmine rows with a bare barbell end, you already know the problem: the bar digs into your palm, rolls awkwardly, and limits how much weight you can actually pull. The veimia V Bar T-Bar Row Landmine Handle solves that with a simple, brutal piece of steel. Here's the real story after digging through 225 ratings and the specs.

**What You Actually Get**

The package is straightforward: one welded steel V-handle that slides over your barbell sleeve. The collar opening is a standard 2-inch diameter, so it fits Olympic bars and most commercial landmine attachments. The V-shape gives you a neutral grip—palms facing each other—which is the whole point. It converts a standard barbell into a T-bar row station without bolting anything down.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

This thing is machined, not cast. That matters. Cast handles can have hidden voids and weak spots; machined steel from a solid billet doesn't. The welding on the V-joint is clean, and there's no lateral play once it's seated on the bar. One buyer noted, "I've loaded 4 plates on this and it doesn't budge or twist." That tracks with the design—the collar has a set screw that locks it firmly in place, so it won't rotate mid-set.

The grip diameter sits around 1.25 inches, which is comfortable for most hand sizes. The knurling is medium-aggressive—enough to hold onto under load but not so sharp that you need chalk for light warm-ups. As shown in the product image, the V-frame puts your wrists in a neutral position, which takes significant stress off the elbows compared to a straight bar.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The dominant theme in the reviews is versatility. Lifters are using this for chest-supported rows, Meadows rows, and even landmine presses. One reviewer wrote, "This replaced my $80 dedicated T-bar row handle. Same function, half the price." Another praised the grip: "The knurling is perfect—not too aggressive for high-rep work but grippy enough for heavy sets."

Fit is another recurring positive. Multiple buyers confirmed it slides onto standard Olympic sleeves with a snug, non-sloppy fit. It also works with most landmine attachments that accept a barbell, so you're not locked into one brand's ecosystem.

**Where It Falls Short**

Honestly, the flaws are minor but worth noting. The black paint finish will wear off at the contact points where the collar meets the barbell. That's cosmetic, not functional, but if you're particular about your gear looking clean, expect some scuffing. One reviewer mentioned, "The finish started flaking after a month—doesn't affect performance but looks rough."

Second, the center section of the V has no knurling. If you grip the very crook of the V—which some lifters do for close-grip rows—your hands will slip more than on the outer sections. It's a minor grip-position quirk, not a design flaw.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Home gym owners** building a landmine setup on a budget. This gives you T-bar row capability without a dedicated machine.
- **Lifters with elbow issues** who need a neutral grip to reduce joint stress on horizontal pulling.
- **Anyone who already owns a landmine attachment** and wants to expand its utility without spending $80+ on a name-brand handle.

**Skip it if** you only do strict barbell rows and never plan to use a landmine. Or if you need a handle with a rotating collar—this one is fixed, so it won't rotate during movements like a Swiss bar.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this fit a 1-inch standard barbell?**
A: No. The collar is 2 inches, designed for Olympic bars. If you have a standard 1-inch bar, you'll need an adapter or a different product.

**Q: Can I use this for landmine presses or just rows?**
A: Both. The neutral grip works well for single-arm landmine presses and rotational movements. Several reviewers use it for pressing and report no issues.

**Q: Does the set screw stay tight during heavy sets?**
A: Yes. The set screw has a flat contact point that locks against the barbell sleeve. One buyer reported doing heavy rows for months without re-tightening.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, versatile landmine handle that performs like a $60+ attachment for roughly half the cost. It's machined well, grips securely, and handles heavy loads without complaint.

**Skip it if** cosmetic wear bothers you, or you need a rotating handle for specialty movements.
