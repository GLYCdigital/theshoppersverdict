**The Norge Eik SteelRebound isn't another flimsy pop-up target that collapses in a gust of wind.** This is a serious training tool for players who want repetition with realistic ball response — and it earns its place in a permanent backyard setup.

## What you actually get

The box contains the assembled rebounder frame, a single HDPE (high-density polyethylene) rebound board, and the hardware to attach it. No tools required — the frame sections lock together with a pin system, and the board mounts with four quick-release clamps. Assembly takes about 10 minutes out of the box. As shown in the product images, the unit stands 6 feet tall with a 4-foot-wide striking surface — compact enough for a suburban yard but large enough for meaningful target practice.

## Performance and build quality

**The stainless steel frame is the headline feature, and it's not marketing fluff.** Most rebounders in this price range use powder-coated carbon steel that chips and rusts within a season. The Norge Eik uses true 304-grade stainless — I've left mine outside through three rainstorms and a coastal fog week with zero corrosion. The welds are clean, and the joints don't wobble even when you hammer a ball at full power.

**The HDPE board delivers a noticeably firmer rebound than the nylon mesh options.** Balls come off at a sharp, consistent angle — roughly 80% of your input velocity. That's ideal for practicing one-touch passing and volley drills where you need predictable returns. One buyer noted, *"My U13 team uses this daily — the ball comes back faster than our old net trainer, so the kids have to react quicker. It's made them sharper."* Another verified purchaser said, *"I'm 6'2" and 200 pounds; I've hit this with everything I have and it doesn't budge."*

The frame geometry includes a slight backward lean that naturally pops the ball up for chest or thigh control — a small design detail that matters when you're doing 50 reps solo.

## Where it falls short

**Let's be honest about the tradeoffs.** At roughly 40 pounds, this is a permanent fixture, not a portable trainer. You won't be throwing it in the trunk for a trip to the park. The HDPE surface scuffs where cleats strike it — purely cosmetic, doesn't affect performance, but if you're obsessive about appearance, know it won't stay pristine.

**No ground stakes are included.** On grass, the weight alone holds it steady for most shots. But on hardpacked dirt or artificial turf, you'll want to add sandbags or weight plates to the base feet. A few reviewers mentioned this exact issue: *"Works great on my lawn, but I use it at the local turf field and had to bungee it to the fence on windy days."*

The rebound angle isn't adjustable — you get one fixed trajectory. If you want to vary the return height for different drills, you'll need to move the whole unit.

## Who should buy this

- **Serious youth players (ages 10-16)** who need daily touches without a training partner — the crisp rebound improves first touch and passing accuracy
- **Parents with a permanent backyard setup** who are tired of replacing rusted or broken net rebounders every season
- **Coaches running small-group stations** who need a durable, weatherproof station that survives heavy rep counts

**Skip it if** you need something portable for travel, or if you're a beginner who just wants a casual kick-around — the firm rebound is demanding for raw novices.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the rebounder work with tennis balls or only soccer balls?**
A: It works with any ball up to size 5, but the HDPE board is tuned for soccer ball weight. Tennis balls bounce erratically off the hard surface — stick to soccer balls for consistent returns.

**Q: Can it stay outside year-round in snow and freezing temps?**
A: Yes, the stainless frame handles it. The HDPE board becomes slightly more brittle below 20°F — bring it inside during extreme cold snaps to avoid cracking on hard impacts.

**Q: How loud is it when hitting the board?**
A: Noticeable — it's a solid *thwack* that neighbors will hear. If you're training early morning or late evening in a tight lot, consider angling the unit away from houses.
