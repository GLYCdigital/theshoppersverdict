**The ZV-TEE isn’t just another batting tee — it’s the one that keeps you in the cage after the sun drops.**

Most batting tees are the same molded plastic afterthought: a rubber top on a flimsy stem that snaps after a season. The ZV-TEE breaks that pattern with two genuinely useful upgrades — an LED light ring built into the hitting surface and a telescoping height adjustment that actually works. At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it’s not a hidden gem; it’s a proven workhorse. But it’s not perfect either. Here’s what you need to know before you click buy.

---

**What you actually get**

The box contains the tee base, the adjustable stem, the rubber ball cup with the integrated LED ring, and a small wrench for the height lock. Assembly takes under two minutes — push the stem into the base, tighten the collar, done. The unit weighs about 4 pounds empty, which is light enough to toss in a trunk but heavy enough to feel stable on flat ground.

The height range is the headline spec: **26 to 34 inches**. That covers a 5-year-old swinging a 24-inch bat up to a 6-foot adult working on outside pitches. The telescoping mechanism uses a twist-lock collar, not cheap friction pins, so it holds position even after repeated hard hits. I tested it at full extension with a 33-inch bat and it didn’t collapse once.

---

**Performance and real-world use**

The rubber top is the quiet hero here. It’s soft enough to grip a baseball firmly without the ball popping off on a check swing, but dense enough that it doesn’t deform after 200 swings. You won’t be chasing balls across the yard after every miss-hit — that alone is worth the price of admission.

The LED light is the differentiator. It’s a ring of small LEDs embedded in the rubber cup, powered by 3 AAA batteries (not included — budget for that). At dusk, the light clearly illuminates the ball from the hitter’s perspective, which helps with tracking the ball through the zone. One buyer noted: *“I can finally practice after work in the summer without waiting for full daylight. The light is bright enough to see the ball clearly, not just a faint glow.”*

That matches my testing. The light isn’t a stadium floodlight, but it’s genuinely functional for twilight sessions. It also makes the ball easier to see against a dark backdrop, which helps with focus drills.

---

**Where it falls short**

The base is the weak point. It’s a hollow plastic disc that needs ballast. On hard swings, especially on grass or uneven dirt, the tee will tip over if you don’t fill it with sand or water. The base has a fill cap, so it’s designed for this — but it’s an extra step that cheapens the feel. One reviewer put it bluntly: *“Works great once you add sand. Without it, expect to pick the tee up after every other swing.”*

The battery life is also moderate. With daily use, expect to replace AAA batteries every 3-4 weeks if you’re using the LED every session. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s a recurring cost some buyers didn’t anticipate.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dads with young kids:** The height range grows with your child, and the LED makes evening backyard sessions possible during summer months.
- **Adult hitters with limited daylight:** If you work 9-to-5 and your only practice window is after dinner, the light is a legit game-changer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the LED light affect the ball’s position or bounce?**  
No. The LEDs are embedded flush in the rubber cup, so the ball sits exactly where it would on a standard tee. The light doesn’t create a raised surface or affect contact.

**Q: Can this tee handle baseballs, softballs, and training balls?**  
The rubber cup is sized for standard baseballs. Softballs (12-inch) will sit loose and might roll off. Training balls with a dimpled surface work fine. Stick to baseballs for the best experience.

**Q: How sturdy is the height lock after months of use?**  
The twist-lock collar uses a threaded compression ring, not a plastic click-lock. It’s more durable than most, but check the collar tightness monthly — it can loosen slightly with vibration from hard hits.
