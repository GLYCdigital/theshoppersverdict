**The Quick Hook: Two Goals, Zero Assembly Headaches**

Most kids' hockey sets give you one goal and a promise. This Franklin Sports NHL set gives you two regulation-sized junior nets (54" x 36" each) that fold flat, store thin, and survive the abuse of driveway hockey. For under $60, you're getting a complete game setup — not a toy that'll splinter by Thanksgiving. But there are quirks you should know before buying.

**What's Actually in the Box**

You get two folding goals, each with a powder-coated steel frame and black nylon netting. The crossbar and posts are 1-inch diameter steel tubing — noticeably sturdier than the plastic-frame sets at this price. The nets are pre-attached, so there's zero assembly. Unfold, lock the corner joints, and you're playing within 60 seconds. The set also includes a small mesh carry bag, though honestly, the goals fold flat enough that most parents just lean them against a garage wall.

**Real-World Performance: Where This Set Shines (and Slides)**

I tested these with a 7-year-old taking wrist shots and a 10-year-old winding up for slap shots. The frame absorbs impact well — no bending, no wobble. The 54" width is perfect for kids up to about 12; they can actually score without feeling like they're shooting into a mailbox. The folding mechanism is the standout feature: a simple hinge system locks into place with a firm push, and collapses just as easily. A 10-year-old can set these up solo.

The netting tension is decent for street hockey — pucks don't bounce out like they do on cheaper nets. But here's the honest catch: on smooth concrete or asphalt, hard shots will slide the entire goal backward. The frame has no ground stakes included, and the lightweight design that makes it portable also makes it mobile during gameplay. You'll want to add sandbags or improvise with bricks for anything beyond casual shooting practice.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.5 stars (225 ratings) cluster around three themes. First, durability — one parent wrote, *"My son is rough on everything, and these have survived two full summers of street hockey."* Second, the two-goal value: *"Finally a set where both kids can play goalie at the same time without fighting over one net."* Third, storage — multiple reviewers mention how easily these disappear into a closet or car trunk.

**Where It Falls Short — Be Honest With Yourself**

The friction-fit net attachment is the weak point. On direct shots that hit the post dead-on, the netting can pop loose from the frame channel. It's a 10-second fix, but it'll happen regularly if your kid aims for the pipes. The goals also lack any anchoring system — for windy days or aggressive play, you'll need to add weight. And while the steel frame is solid, this isn't a set that'll survive NHL-caliber slap shots from teenagers; it's designed for younger players, and that's fine.

**Who Should Buy This**

Buy this if you have kids aged 4–12 who want a real, two-goal street hockey experience without a trip to the sporting goods store. It's ideal for siblings or neighbor playdates — having two goals means actual games, not just shooting practice. It's also great for families with limited storage; these fold to nearly flat and weigh about 8 pounds each.

Skip this if you're buying for teens or adults playing hardcore street hockey — you'll need a heavier, anchored net. And if your driveway is sloped or you live in a windy area, budget extra for weights.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**Q: Do these work with street hockey balls or only pucks?**
Both, but they're best with lighter street hockey balls. A heavy puck can slide the goal on hard surfaces.

**Q: How long does assembly actually take?**
Zero assembly — unfold and lock the corner joints. First setup takes under a minute per goal.

**Q: Can these survive being left outside?**
The powder-coated steel resists rust, but the netting will degrade faster in direct sun. Bring them inside or cover them when not in use.
