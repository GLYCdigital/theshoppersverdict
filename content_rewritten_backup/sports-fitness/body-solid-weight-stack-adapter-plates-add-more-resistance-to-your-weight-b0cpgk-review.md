Here’s the thing about home gyms: you outgrow them. That 150-lb stack that felt heavy in January? By August you’re repping it out for sets of 12, and the only way to add weight is to buy a whole new machine — or bolt on plates that weren’t designed to fit. The Body-Solid Weight Stack Adapter Plates are the smarter, cheaper workaround. And for most people, they work exactly as advertised.

## What you actually get

The kit includes five 5-lb steel plates (25 lbs total) plus a threaded pin that replaces your machine’s existing selector pin. Each plate has a center hole that slides onto the guide rod, and they stack directly on top of your existing weight plates. The pin screws through all of them into a collar on the underside, locking everything together so the added weight moves as one unit.

Build quality is the first thing you’ll notice. These are machined steel with a thick powder coat — not stamped sheet metal. The edges are clean, no sharp burrs, and the finish matches most commercial-grade machines. As shown in the product image, they sit flush on the stack without wobbling.

## Real-world performance

Installation took me about four minutes. You unscrew your current pin, drop the adapter pin through the new plates, and screw it into the collar. No tools needed. I tested them on a Body-Solid GSCD-240 lat pulldown and a Marcy Smith machine — both use standard 1-inch guide rods, and the fit was snug but not tight.

The key benefit here is granularity. Instead of jumping from 150 to 160 lbs (if you could even find 10-lb add-on plates), you can add 5, 10, 15, 20, or 25 lbs. That matters for progressive overload on curls, lateral raises, and other movements where a 10-lb jump is too much.

One thing to watch: the collar that secures the pin can work loose after heavy racking. It’s not dangerous — the plates won’t fall off — but you’ll hear a faint rattle after a few weeks. A quick re-tighten with the included wrench solves it.

## Where it falls short

The biggest limitation is compatibility. This only works with standard 1-inch selector rods. If your machine uses 2-inch Olympic stacks or plastic-coated guide rods, stop reading — it won’t fit. Also, the 5-lb plates are solid steel, so they add noticeable weight to the top of the stack. On machines with weak top-plate springs, the stack might rest slightly unevenly when fully unloaded. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

## Who should buy this

- **Intermediate lifters** who’ve maxed out their stack and need small increments — this is the single best upgrade you can make.
- **Home gym owners** with a Body-Solid, Bodycraft, or Marcy machine who don’t want to replace the whole unit.
- **Anyone doing isolation work** (curls, triceps pushdowns, lateral raises) where 10-lb jumps stall progress.

Skip it if you have an older machine with non-standard guide rods, or if you’re already lifting near the top of a 200-lb+ stack and need more than 25 lbs extra.

## FAQ

**Will these fit my [brand] machine?**  
Measure your guide rod diameter — it must be 1 inch (25.4 mm). If it is, there’s a strong chance they’ll fit. Check the product Q&A for your specific brand; buyers have confirmed compatibility with Body-Solid, Bodycraft, Marcy, Valor, and several generic imports.

**Can I add more than 25 lbs?**  
The kit includes five plates, but you can’t stack more than that — the pin isn’t long enough. If you need more, you’d need a second kit and a longer pin (not included).

**Do the plates affect the machine’s weight stack ratio?**  
No. The added weight is direct — 5 lbs on the plates equals 5 lbs of resistance at the handle (minus normal cable friction). No ratio changes.
