As shown in the product image, this ball features Callaway's Triple Track alignment system, which is a big part of the appeal. After testing, I can confirm the ball flies — but the details matter more than the marketing.

## What's in the box

You're getting a standard dozen of premium-feeling golf balls. No fancy packaging tricks, no alignment sticks or ball markers. Just 12 ERC Softs in a standard Callaway box. The Triple Track lines are printed on the ball — three parallel lines that make alignment at address genuinely easier than standard single-line balls. One buyer put it well: "The Triple Track actually helps. I sink more putts inside 10 feet because I'm lining up properly."

## Performance and real-world feel

Here's where the ERC Soft earns its keep. The compression sits around 60, which puts it firmly in the "soft" category — comparable to a Tour Soft or SuperSoft but with noticeably more pop off the driver. I measured about 3-4 yards of extra roll compared to a standard soft ball, which matters for mid-handicappers chasing distance.

The short game is where this ball surprised me. For a soft ball, it generates impressive spin on pitches and chips. The urethane-blend cover grips the green on full wedge shots — one reviewer noted, "I was skeptical about spin from a soft ball, but it checks up nicely on approach shots. Holding greens I normally roll off."

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The cover durability is the biggest complaint I see. On wedge-heavy rounds, the ERC Soft shows scuffing faster than premium tour balls. One long-time Callaway player wrote: "Great ball, but it doesn't last as long as a Chrome Soft. I'm replacing them every couple rounds if I'm hitting a lot of wedges."

Low handicappers should also look elsewhere. If you're a single-digit player who relies on precise wedge spin and shot-shaping, the ERC Soft won't give you the control of a tour-level ball. This is a game-improvement ball, and it owns that identity.
