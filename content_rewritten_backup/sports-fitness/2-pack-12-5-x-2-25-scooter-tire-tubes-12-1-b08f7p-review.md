## What's Actually in the Box

You get two 12.5 x 2.25 butyl rubber inner tubes, each with a straight valve stem. The CR202L-A designation means these are designed for 12.5-inch wheels with 2.25-inch tire width — the standard sizing for Razor, Hover-1, and most entry-to-mid-range kick scooters. Butyl rubber is the key material here; it's denser than the latex tubes you'll find on cheap replacements, which translates to slower air loss and better puncture resistance. No tools, no rim strips, no valve caps beyond the basic ones pre-installed. It's bare-bones, but that's expected at this price point.

## Real-World Performance and Fit

The most common praise in the reviews centers on the tubes seating correctly the first time. One rider noted: *"I've bought tubes that claimed 12.5x2.25 but ran small — these matched the stock dimensions exactly and didn't require wrestling."* That's consistent with what I see: the butyl rubber has enough give to seat without pinching, but it's not so floppy that it folds over during installation. The straight valve stems are a double-edged sword. They work perfectly on standard Razor-style rims, but if your scooter has a deep channel or an angled valve hole, you might need a 90-degree adapter. Check your wheel before ordering.

## Where It Falls Short

The valve stem length is the biggest complaint. At roughly 1.5 inches, it's fine for most rims, but some deeper wheels (especially on cheaper scooters) leave the stem barely protruding — making inflation awkward. You'll need a slim pump head or an extension. Also, there's no rim strip in the box. If your current strip is cracked or missing, you'll need to source one separately, which adds cost and a second shipping wait. Finally, this is a generic-brand tube. Quality control is good — I found no reports of manufacturing defects — but you're not getting a branded warranty or customer support line.
