**The $150 question: Is the Wilson EZ Gear 2.0 worth it for a youth catcher, or should you spend more?**

Most catcher’s gear sets under $200 are either flimsy, bulky, or sized so poorly that your kid looks like a turtle in a too-small shell. The Wilson EZ Gear 2.0 avoids those traps — mostly. Here’s what 210 Amazon reviewers (4.2 stars) actually say after a season of use.

**What’s in the box**

You get four pieces: a hockey-style catcher’s helmet with steel cage, a two-piece chest protector, and two-piece leg guards. No throat guard included — that’s a separate $15–20 buy, which feels like an oversight. The set comes in Small/Medium only, which Wilson says fits ages 9–13, but the real-world fit is narrower than that.

**How it performs in real games**

The chest protector is the standout piece. It’s a two-piece design (separate upper and lower plates) that articulates when your kid bends or squats — something many budget sets skip. The padding is dense EVA foam, not the cheap sponge you see on $80 kits. It stops 45–55 mph rec-league fastballs without transferring much sting. One reviewer said: *“My son took a foul tip off the chest in his first game — he didn’t even flinch.”*

Leg guards run from mid-thigh to the top of the foot, with a hinged knee cap that actually tracks movement. The straps are elastic with hook-and-loop closures — easy for a 10-year-old to adjust alone. Downside: the shin length runs short for taller kids. A 5’2” 12-year-old will show ankle above the top of his cleats.

The helmet is where the kit shows its price point. The steel cage is solid and welds look clean, but the foam padding inside is only about a half-inch thick, particularly around the ears. For youth pitchers throwing 50+ mph, that’s sufficient. For travel-ball kids facing 60 mph heat, you’ll want to add a padded skull cap underneath. Several reviewers noted this exact concern.

**Where it falls short**

The buckles. All the main attachment points use plastic quick-release clips. They work fine for a season, but if your kid is hard on gear (and they all are), one or two will likely crack by mid-summer. Replacement buckles aren’t sold separately, so you’re either sewing on a strap or upgrading earlier than planned.

Sizing is the bigger issue. The Small/Medium runs true to a slim 10-year-old, not a growing 13-year-old. If your player is above the 50th percentile for height, measure their shin from knee to ankle and chest circumference before ordering. Wilson’s size chart is optimistic.

**Who should buy this**

- **Rec-league parents** who want a complete, safe set without dropping $400 on All-Star gear.
- **First-time catchers** (ages 9–11) who need something adjustable that will last two seasons.
- **Backup gear** for practices or when the primary set is being repaired.

**Who should skip it**

- **Travel-ball players** facing 60+ mph pitching — buy a helmet with thicker padding separately.
- **Bigger kids** over 5’0” or 100 lbs — the Small/Medium will be too tight by June.
- **Parents who hate replacing buckles** — budget for that possibility in year two.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Small/Medium fit a 12-year-old?**  
A: Only if they’re on the smaller side (under 5’0” and about 90 lbs). The leg guards run short, and the chest protector’s shoulder caps sit high on broader kids. Measure before you buy.

**Q: Is the helmet NOCSAE certified?**  
A: Yes, the helmet meets NOCSAE standards for youth baseball. That’s non-negotiable for game use, and this one passes.

**Q: Can you replace the helmet padding?**  
A: Yes, but it’s not easy. The foam is glued in. Some reviewers removed it and used stick-on padding from a hockey shop — doable, but it voids any warranty.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5**

**Buy it if** you need a complete, safe, reasonably protective set for a young rec-league catcher who’s still growing.  
**Skip it if** your kid is big for their age, throws heat, or you want gear that survives three+ seasons without DIY repairs.

The EZ Gear 2.0 is the best budget catcher’s set under $200 — it just isn’t the best *fit* for every player. Measure first, buy a throat guard, and you’ll get two solid seasons out of it.
