If you play more than one racket sport, you've probably got a mixed bag of balls rolling around your trunk. The Franklin Sports Pickleball, Tennis + Padel Ball Hopper Tubes tries to solve that with one collapsible tube that claims to handle all three. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's clearly doing something right — but let's dig into whether it's actually the right hopper for how you practice.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes one telescoping tube with a pickup base that doubles as a floor stand. Extended, it holds up to 15 tennis balls, 20 pickleballs, or a similar count of padel balls. Collapsed, it shrinks to roughly half its length — which is the smartest design choice here. Most hoppers are rigid cylinders that eat trunk space; this one tucks behind a seat or into a gear bag without drama.

The tube is made from a reinforced plastic that feels dense enough to survive being dropped on a hard court. The base uses the standard press-down pickup mechanism — you push the tube over a ball and it pops up inside. The vented side slots are a thoughtful touch: wet balls dry out without you having to empty the entire tube.

## How It Performs in Real Use

The pickup mechanism is where this hopper earns or loses its keep. Press down firmly on a ball and it seats into the tube cleanly. Pickleballs, being lighter and slightly larger than tennis balls, pop up with satisfying reliability. Tennis balls take a bit more pressure — you'll develop a rhythm after a few minutes. The trade-off is that the rigid base won't suffer the cracking issues that plague cheaper wire hoppers.

One thing to note: the "15" capacity is per sport, not combined. If you're mixing pickleballs and tennis balls in one tube, you'll get fewer total balls before it's full. That's not a flaw, but it's a spec that reads more flexible than it actually is.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers repeatedly mention the collapsible design as the deciding factor. One buyer said, *"Finally a hopper that doesn't take up my whole back seat. Collapses small enough to keep in my gym bag."* Another noted the versatility: *"Use it for pickleball drills on Tuesday and tennis with my daughter on Saturday. One tube does both."* The durability also gets consistent shout-outs — several reviewers report months of weekly use with no cracks or broken pickup fins.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a shoulder strap is a real oversight. A full tube of 15 tennis balls weighs around 4 pounds, which isn't heavy — but it's awkward to carry one-handed while juggling a paddle and water bottle. You'll want a second hand free or a bag to slide it into.

The press-down pickup also takes more force than some competing hoppers. If you're older or have wrist issues, this could be genuinely uncomfortable over a 20-minute pickup session. And while the plastic is tough, it's not indestructible — dropping it on concrete from waist height could crack the base rim.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You play two or more racket sports and want one hopper that handles all of them. You're short on trunk or closet space and need something collapsible. You're tired of wire hoppers that bend and snap.

**Skip it if:** You only play pickleball and need maximum capacity — a dedicated pickleball hopper will hold more. You have limited hand strength and need a no-effort pickup mechanism. You want a hopper with wheels or a strap for easy transport.

**Q: Is the pickup mechanism gentle on balls?**

**Q: Can I leave balls in the tube between sessions?**
