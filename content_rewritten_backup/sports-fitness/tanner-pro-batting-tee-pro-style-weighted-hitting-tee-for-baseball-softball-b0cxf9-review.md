The first thing you notice is the stability. As shown in the product image, the base is wide and low-profile — it's not a skinny stem poking out of a plastic disc. Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The rubber hose top is a wear item. If you're hitting 200 balls a day with a metal bat, that hose will eventually tear. It's replaceable, but it's an ongoing cost you won't have with a cheap plastic tee. Also, that 7.5-pound base is heavy — which is great for stability but a pain for a young player schlepping gear from the car to the field. This is a tee that lives in your trunk or the dugout, not one you toss in a backpack.
