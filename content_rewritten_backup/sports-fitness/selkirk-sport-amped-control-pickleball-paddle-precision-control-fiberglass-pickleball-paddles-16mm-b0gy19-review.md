You're a control player. You win points by placing the ball, not crushing it. The Selkirk Amped Control is built for exactly that — but it's not for everyone. Here's the honest breakdown after digging through specs, customer feedback, and what this paddle actually does on the court.

## What You're Getting

The box contains the paddle and a basic cover — nothing fancy, but the paddle itself is the story. It's a 16mm polymer core wrapped in a fiberglass face, measuring 15.5 inches long with a 5.25-inch handle. The face texture is subtle but present; you can feel the grit designed to grab the ball on contact. As shown in the product image, the design is clean and understated — no loud graphics, just a professional look that won't embarrass you at the courts.

## How It Performs in Real Play

The 16mm core is the defining feature here. Thicker cores mean more dwell time — the ball stays on the face longer, giving you that "catch and release" feel at the kitchen. Dinks are where this paddle shines. You can drop the ball softly over the net with confidence, and resets against hard drives are manageable because the paddle absorbs pace rather than redirecting it aggressively.

Spin is the surprise. Fiberglass faces aren't typically known for generating heavy rotation, but Selkirk's surface texture delivers. One reviewer noted, *"I'm getting more topspin on my third-shot drops than I ever did with my previous graphite paddle. The ball just grabs and rolls."* That matches the engineering — fiberglass flexes slightly on contact, creating a trampoline effect that imparts more rotation than rigid graphite.

The sweet spot is generous for a control-focused paddle. Misses toward the edges stay in play more often than you'd expect, which is a real advantage when you're stretched wide on defense.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest part. This paddle has almost no pop. If you're coming from a power paddle — something with a thinner core or a carbon face — you'll feel like you're swinging through mud on drives. Punch volleys that used to end points will now sit up for your opponent to attack. You'll compensate with more arm speed, which gets tiring.

The weight is also worth noting. At roughly 8.1 ounces, it's not heavy, but it's not light either. Players who prefer a 7.5-ounce paddle for quick hand battles at the net will feel the difference by the third game.

## FAQ

**Is this paddle good for beginners?**
Yes, with a caveat. The control and forgiveness make learning dinks and resets easier. But the lack of power means beginners who haven't developed clean technique may struggle to get depth on drives.

**How does the 16mm thickness affect play compared to a 13mm paddle?**
The 16mm core gives you more dwell time and softness at the expense of power. A 13mm paddle will feel quicker and more explosive but less forgiving on off-center hits.

**Does the fiberglass face wear out quickly?**
Fiberglass is more durable than graphite in terms of surface wear, but the grit will smooth out over 6–12 months of regular play. That's normal and won't significantly affect performance until the texture fades noticeably.
