**The $20 Pitching Rubber That Fixes Your Backyard Practice Problem**

If you've ever tried to pitch in the backyard without a mound, you know the struggle. Your front foot slips, your landing spot wanders, and your mechanics fall apart. The Franklin Sports Pitcher's Rubber solves that specific headache for about the price of a pizza. But is it just a chunk of rubber, or does it actually deliver? After digging through 235 ratings and the specs, here's the real story.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a single piece: a molded rubber slab measuring roughly 24 inches long by 6 inches wide. The headline feature is the dual-sided design. Flip it one way, and you get a 6-inch-high pitching surface for baseball. Flip it over, and it's 4 inches for softball. That's genuinely useful for families with players in both sports, or for coaches who work with different age groups.

The rubber compound is stiff but not brittle. It's got a textured top surface with molded cleat grips, which gives your push-off foot actual traction. No stakes, no anchors, no carrying bag — just the rubber. At about 2 pounds, it's portable enough to toss in a gear bag, but that lightness cuts both ways.

**How it performs in real use**

The dual-height system works exactly as advertised. Baseball players get the standard 6-inch rise, which matches the height of a regulation mound's pitching rubber. Softball players flip it over for the 4-inch version, which aligns with their 43-foot distance. That's a thoughtful touch that most competitors don't offer at this price point.

Where this rubber earns its keep is stability. The wide base and heavy-duty rubber compound grip dirt and grass surprisingly well. One reviewer noted, *"I've used it on my backyard mound for two seasons and it hasn't moved an inch."* Another mentioned, *"Perfect for bullpen sessions — my son's mechanics finally look consistent because his landing spot doesn't drift."*

The cleat-grip texture isn't just marketing. Pitchers who push off hard will appreciate the traction. The rubber doesn't compress under weight, so you get a firm, consistent push-off surface every time.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of anchoring hardware is the biggest miss. On loose, sandy soil or artificial turf, the rubber can shift mid-delivery. That's a safety concern for younger pitchers who might not notice the movement. A simple solution is to stake it down yourself, but at this price, you shouldn't have to MacGyver it.

The lightweight construction is also a double-edged sword. Leave it on a concrete patio or driveway, and a gust of wind will relocate it. Store it indoors when not in use, or accept that it's a backyard tool, not a permanent installation.

**Who should buy this**

This is squarely aimed at three groups: parents setting up a backyard practice area, coaches running bullpen sessions without a full mound, and players who want to practice pitch mechanics indoors or on flat ground. If you're a serious pitcher building a permanent mound setup, spend more on a full pitching mound. If you want a versatile, portable rubber that handles both baseball and softball, this is the one.
