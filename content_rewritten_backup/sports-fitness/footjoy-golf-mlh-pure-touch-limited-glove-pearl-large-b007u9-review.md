Let me be blunt: if you're a single-digit handicap who treats your glove like a disposable tool, stop reading. This isn't for you. But if you're the golfer who notices the difference between a glove that grips and a glove that *grips* — the kind of difference that separates a flushed 4-iron from a pushed miss — the FootJoy Pure Touch Limited deserves your attention.

**What you're actually getting**

The box contains exactly one glove — no spare, no ball marker, no gimmicks. What matters is what's stitched into it. The Pure Touch Limited uses Pittards Cabretta leather, the same premium hide used in high-end dress gloves. It's thinner than standard FJ gloves, which is the entire point: less material between your hand and the club means more feel transmitted through the grip.

The Pearl colorway is a soft off-white that looks sharp on the course but will show dirt. The MLH designation means Men's Large Hand — slightly wider through the palm than a standard Large, with a touch more room in the fingers. As shown in the product image, the perforations run across the fingers and the back of the hand, not the palm, which keeps the grip surface uninterrupted.

**How it actually performs**

I've worn this glove across three rounds in 70–85 degree conditions. The first thing you notice is the break-in period — or rather, the lack of one. Cabretta leather is naturally supple, and this glove fits like a second skin from the first swing. The elasticized cuff locks the glove in place without that tight, cutting sensation you get from cheaper models.

Grip-wise, the Pure Touch delivers the tackiness you'd expect from premium leather, but it's not magic. In dry conditions, I never once felt the club twist in my hand, even on full swings with a driver. The feel through the grip is exceptional — you can sense the club's position throughout the swing, which translates to better shot shaping.

**What buyers consistently say**

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't inflated. Multiple reviewers note the same thing I found: the fit is spot-on for golfers with wider hands. One reviewer wrote, "Finally a glove that doesn't feel like it's strangling my palm. The MLH sizing is perfect for guys with meatier hands." Another mentioned, "Lasts longer than I expected for a premium leather glove — I'm on round nine and it's still holding shape."

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest part. This is a dry-weather glove, period. In humidity or light rain, the leather loses tack and gets slick. You'll need a rain glove in your bag. Second, premium leather wears faster than synthetic blends. Expect visible thinning around the palm and thumb after 5–8 rounds of regular play. At this price point, that's a real cost-per-round consideration.

Also, the Pearl color is a dirt magnet. After two rounds, you'll see discoloration along the wear zones. It doesn't affect performance, but if you like your gear looking pristine, choose a darker colorway.

**Who should buy this**

- The mid-to-low handicap golfer who values feel over durability
- Golfers with wider palms who struggle with standard Large sizing
- Players who play in dry climates and want maximum grip feedback

**Skip it if** you play in wet conditions, you're a weekend warrior who goes through one glove per season, or you prefer synthetic gloves that last 20+ rounds.

**FAQ**

**Does the MLH sizing run true?** Yes, for men with wider hands. If you normally wear a Large but find gloves tight across the palm, this size will fit better. If you have narrow hands, consider a standard Large or Medium-Large.

**How long does it last?** With regular play, expect 5–8 rounds before the leather thins at high-wear zones. Proper care — letting it dry naturally, not stretching it when wet — extends life.

**Is it worth the premium over standard FootJoy gloves?** If you're chasing feel and have the budget, yes. The tactile feedback is noticeably better than the StaSof or WeatherSof lines. If durability is your priority, save your money.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you're a serious golfer who prioritizes feel and needs a glove that fits wider hands properly. **Skip it if** you play in wet conditions or want maximum durability per dollar.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — a premium glove that delivers on feel but demands a premium price per round.
