If you've ever taken a 90-mph fastball off the knob of the bat, you know the exact moment your hand goes numb and your at-bat is over. That's the problem the **Franklin Sports Baseball Hand and Wrist Guard (PRT LG Serie)** is built to solve. It's a simple, effective piece of protective gear that does one job well: keeping your lead hand safe when the pitcher misses his spot.

### What You Actually Get

The package contains a single hand guard designed for the **top hand on the bat** (left hand for right-handed hitters). The construction is straightforward: a **molded plastic shell** that covers the back of the hand, extends down over the wrist, and wraps slightly around the thumb side. The inside has a thin foam liner that adds a bit of comfort without adding bulk.

The **adjustable elastic strap** runs through a slot in the plastic and secures with a wide hook-and-loop closure. It's designed to be worn over a batting glove, and the fit is snug but not restrictive. Total weight is light enough that you'll forget it's there — which is exactly what you want from protective gear.

### Real-World Performance

The PRT LG Serie does what it promises. The plastic is **rigid enough to deflect a ball** rather than just absorbing the impact, which is the key difference between this and cheaper foam-only guards. When a pitch gets away and clips the handle area, the guard takes the hit and your hand keeps functioning. That's the entire job, and it does it well.

The strap system holds the guard in place through a full swing. It doesn't shift or rotate, even on check swings. The **low profile** means it doesn't interfere with your grip or your bat control — a common complaint with bulkier guards on the market. You can still feel the bat in your fingers; the guard sits on the back of your hand, not between you and the handle.

### Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. **This is not a large-size guard.** Players who wear XXL batting gloves or have broader hands have reported the strap running short and the shell feeling narrow. If you're a bigger player, measure your wrist circumference before buying — anything over 8 inches is going to be tight.

The second issue is **breathability**. The solid plastic shell doesn't have ventilation holes, so in summer heat, your hand will sweat more than usual inside your batting glove. It's a tradeoff for the protection level, but worth knowing before a July doubleheader.

Finally, this guard is **specifically for the top hand**. If you want protection for both hands (bottom hand is also vulnerable on inside pitches), you'll need to buy two — and Franklin doesn't make a mirrored version, so you'd be wearing the same guard on both hands, which feels awkward.

### Who Should Buy This

- **Adult league and recreational players** who face live pitching and want to avoid the most common hand injury in baseball
- **Cage hitters** who spend long sessions facing machine or BP pitching where wild throws happen
- **Parents of teenagers** moving up to higher velocity levels who want an affordable insurance policy

Skip it if you're a player with very large hands, or if you're looking for a guard that also covers the bottom hand.

### FAQ

**Q: Can I wear this over my batting glove?**
Yes, that's exactly how it's designed to be worn. The elastic strap secures over the glove, and the fit is snug enough to stay in place through a full swing.

**Q: Will it protect against a pitch hitting the end of the bat?**
It protects the hand and wrist area specifically. If a pitch hits the very end of the bat and drives it back into your hand, the guard will absorb some of that impact, but it won't eliminate it entirely. For handle-area hits — the most common injury spot — it's highly effective.

**Q: Is this legal in league play?**
Yes. Hand guards are permitted in essentially all amateur and adult leagues. They're not considered an alteration to the bat, and they don't provide any performance advantage — just protection.
