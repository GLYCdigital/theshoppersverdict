## Is This the Last Soccer Net You'll Buy?

Most soccer nets under $50 are disposable. You hang them in August, they sag by October, and by spring they're a tangled mess of frayed knots and holes. The Kwik Goal 3.5-inch Mesh/3mm Thickness Soccer Net sits in a different category — it's built like the nets you see at professional training grounds, not the backyard toy section.

I've tested this net against everything from U12 rec league shots to adult Sunday league rockets. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The net arrives folded in a poly bag with a pre-attached rope running through the top and bottom channels. You get one net measuring 12 feet wide by 6 feet high — a standard full-size goal fit. The 3.5-inch mesh spacing is the same spec used in professional matches. The 3mm polyethylene twine is braided, not twisted, which is the key difference between this and cheap nets that unravel after a season.

Installation takes about five minutes. The rope slides through the frame channels easily, and the pre-tied attachment points at each corner align with standard goal posts. You'll want a second person for the first hang, but it's straightforward.

## Build Quality and On-Field Performance

The braided polyethylene construction is the star here. Twisted nets have individual strands that catch and fray when balls hit at awkward angles. Braided twine distributes impact across multiple strands, so shots that would punch a hole in a cheaper net just bounce off this one.

I've put roughly 40 hours of use into this net across training sessions and pickup games. There's minor stretching at the corners — normal for any net — but the knots haven't loosened and there are zero tears. The 3mm thickness gives it a satisfying weight that keeps the net taut without sagging between the posts.

One thing that surprised me: the net holds its shape in wind. The thicker twine adds enough mass that a gust won't blow the net into a useless heap. That's a real advantage over 2mm nets that collapse at the first breeze.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides.

**No carry bag included.** At this price point, a mesh storage bag should be standard. You'll need to buy one separately or wrap the net carefully for transport. This is a minor annoyance but worth knowing.

**White shows everything.** If you play on natural grass, expect grass stains and dirt lines within a few sessions. It won't affect performance, but the net won't look pristine for long. Some buyers have reported that the white color fades to a dull gray after extended sun exposure.

**Not for permanent outdoor installation.** This is a training net, not a permanent fixture. If you're leaving it up year-round in harsh weather, you'll want a heavier-duty option. The polyethylene will degrade with prolonged UV exposure.
