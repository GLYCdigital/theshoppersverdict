**Performance and build quality**

The M8 is built around a simple premise: women's swing speeds are generally slower than men's, so the clubs need to help generate ball speed, not fight it. The graphite shafts are genuinely lightweight — noticeably lighter than comparable steel-shaft sets in this price range. The oversized clubheads on the driver and fairway wood offer a larger sweet spot, which translates to more forgiveness on off-center hits.

The 4-hybrid is the standout club here. It replaces the traditional 4-iron, which most beginners can't hit consistently anyway. The hybrid's wider sole and lower center of gravity make it dramatically easier to launch from the fairway or rough. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "I'm 52 and just started playing. The hybrid is my favorite club — I can actually get the ball in the air."

The putter is where the set shows its budget roots. It's a basic blade design with minimal alignment assistance. It works, but if you're coming from a putter with alignment lines or a mallet design, you'll notice the difference immediately.

**Where it falls short**

The stock grips are the first thing I'd change. They're functional but thin, and several reviewers with smaller hands reported they had to grip the club tighter than ideal. If you wear a petite or cadet glove, budget $40–60 for a regrip job at your local pro shop.

The driver shaft flex is also on the softer side, which is appropriate for most women but might feel whippy for stronger players swinging over 80 mph. If you're already hitting your current driver 180+ yards, this set isn't your upgrade path.
