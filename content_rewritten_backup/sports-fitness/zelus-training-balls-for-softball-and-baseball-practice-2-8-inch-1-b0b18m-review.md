Let’s cut to it: the ZELUS Training Balls are not baseballs. They’re not trying to be. At 8 inches in diameter, they match the exact size of a regulation softball, which makes them a clever visual-training tool for hitters working on swing path and timing. With a 4.8-star average across 240 ratings, these have clearly found their audience. But are they the right training aid for *you*? That depends on what you're trying to fix.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes two hollow, lightweight PVC balls in a bright optic yellow. That’s the whole kit — no tees, no nets, no extras. The 8-inch size is the headline feature here, and it’s the reason these stand out from the crowd of dimpled plastic practice balls. The larger surface area forces your eyes to track the ball differently than a standard 5-inch baseball, which is the point. If you’re struggling with pitch recognition or keeping your head down through contact, the exaggerated size builds a visual memory that translates when you step into the box against live pitching.

Out of the box, the PVC shell is smooth but not slick. The hollow core means they’re light — noticeably lighter than a real softball — which is both the product’s strength and its limitation.

## Real-World Performance

The best use case is tee work and soft toss. You can take full, violent swings without fear of launching a hard ball into a neighbor’s window or spending 15 minutes chasing foul balls into the woods. The hollow construction means they don’t carry far, which is exactly what you want when your focus is mechanics, not power. As shown in the product image, the bright yellow color also makes them easy to spot in grass or dirt, so post-session pickup is quick.

Where they shine is with younger players. Kids who are still developing hand-eye coordination benefit enormously from tracking a larger target. The forgiving weight also reduces the sting on mishits, which keeps practice enjoyable for beginners who are still building confidence.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The dominant theme in customer feedback is the realistic size. One verified buyer noted, “These are perfect for my 12U daughter — she stopped flinching at live pitching after two weeks of tee work with these.” Another reviewer highlighted the durability: “We’ve put these through a full season of batting cage abuse and they’ve only got minor scuffs.” That aligns with the PVC construction — it’s rigid enough to hold shape but flexible enough to survive repeated impacts.

## Where It Falls Short

Be honest about the tradeoffs. Because these are hollow and light, they don’t simulate the weight or feel of a real ball at contact. If you’re a high school or college hitter looking for bat-speed feedback, these will feel mushy and unresponsive. You won’t get that crisp “pop” that tells you you’ve barreled one up.

Additionally, these are not designed for pitching machines. The hollow core causes them to bounce unpredictably when fed through a machine’s wheels, leading to erratic deliveries. Stick to hand toss and tee work — that’s where they excel. A few reviewers also mentioned the balls can deform slightly if left in direct sunlight for extended periods, so store them in a bag or shade when not in use.
