If you're shopping for a first "real" set of clubs for a young golfer, you've likely hit the same wall I have: either pay $150+ for a name-brand junior set or grab a cheap toy set that breaks by hole three. The Orlimar ATS Junior Boys' Red/Black Series sits squarely in the middle — and at $35, it's worth a hard look.

**What's actually in the box**

This is a 6-piece set: a 460cc driver, 5-iron, 7-iron, 9-iron, putter, and a lightweight stand bag. As shown in the product image, the red/black color scheme is aggressive and sporty — the kind of look that makes a 10-year-old feel like he's playing in the Masters. The bag has a single shoulder strap and a couple of pockets. Nothing fancy, but it's enough to get to the course or practice range without a cart.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The clubs are sized for kids in the 4'6" to 5'0" range (roughly ages 9-12). The driver features an oversized head that's genuinely forgiving — one buyer noted, "My son was hitting the ball straighter after one range session than he ever did with his old set." That oversized face is the real selling point here. Juniors mishit constantly; this club minimizes the damage.

The irons are cavity-backed with junior flex graphite shafts. They're light enough for a smaller frame to swing fast, but here's the honest caveat: if your kid is already 5'2" or has played baseball (and therefore has real swing speed), these shafts will feel whippy. One parent mentioned their 11-year-old "hit the driver 130 yards but had trouble controlling the 5-iron because the shaft felt too soft." That's the tradeoff at this price point.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Across 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, the recurring themes are value and fit. A top review sums it up: "Perfect height for my 9-year-old. He used them all summer and they held up fine." Several parents specifically mentioned that the clubs are properly sized — not the "one size fits all" junk you find at big-box stores. The bag also gets credit for being light enough for kids to carry themselves, which means less nagging for you on the course.

**Where it falls short**

No headcovers. For a set with a graphite driver and irons, that's a real oversight — the clubs will clang against each other in the bag, and the driver face will get dinged if you're not careful. Budget $10 for a set of covers or wrap the heads in towels.

The putter is basic. It's a standard blade with no alignment aid, which is fine for a beginner but won't help a kid who's serious about improving. And as noted, the flex range is narrow. This set is ideal for a 9-11 year old of average size. If your kid is on the taller side or has significant athletic experience, he'll outgrow these clubs quickly.

**Who should buy this set**

This is perfect for the casual junior golfer — the kid who plays a few times a month, takes lessons, or just wants to hit balls with dad. It's also a fantastic backup set for a summer camp or vacation house where you don't want to risk expensive clubs. If your child is already committed to competitive junior golf, skip this and invest in a properly fitted set from a brand like US Kids or Callaway.

**FAQ**

**Q: What age is this set designed for?**
A: Ages 9-12, or roughly 4'6" to 5'0" tall. The clubs are sized for that range, not for older teens or adults.

**Q: Does it include a bag?**
A: Yes, a lightweight stand bag with a shoulder strap and small pockets. No headcovers are included.

**Q: Are the shafts steel or graphite?**
A: Graphite. They're light and flexible, which helps juniors generate clubhead speed, but they're not suited for stronger, more experienced hitters.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need a properly sized, functional set for a young beginner without spending a fortune. At $35, this is the best value in junior golf right now.

**Skip it if:** Your kid is already athletic, tall, or serious about golf. The shafts will hold him back, and you'll end up buying a second set within a year.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — Exceptional value with minor, fixable flaws (buy headcovers). For the casual junior golfer, this is the smart buy.
