## What You Actually Get

The box contains one pair of gloves (16oz as tested), nothing else—no wraps, no bag. The exterior is a PU leather that feels denser than typical entry-level synthetics. Stitching along the thumb and the webbing between fingers is double-stitched, which is where cheap gloves usually blow out first. ## Real-World Performance

The padding is the headline. It's a layered foam setup—dense on the outside, softer toward your knuckles. On the heavy bag, you get a satisfying *thud* without the jarring feedback that travels up your wrist. After 12 rounds of continuous work, the padding hadn't compressed noticeably. That's the differentiator here: most gloves in this price range go flat after a few weeks. These held their shape.

The wrist support is legit. The strap wraps wide and secures with a hook-and-loop closure that doesn't slip mid-round. I could cinch it down to a locked position without cutting circulation. For sparring, that's essential—you don't want your hand shifting inside the glove when you're blocking hooks.

The breathable palm panel works. After a 45-minute session, the interior was damp but not soaked. Compare that to fully synthetic-lined gloves that feel like you've dipped your hands in a swamp.

## Where They Fall Short

Two honest gripes. First, the finger compartment runs narrow. If you have wider hands, the 16oz size will feel snug around the knuckles. The glove does break in slightly, but it's worth ordering a size up if you're between sizes. Second, these aren't competition-legal for sanctioned bouts—they're built for training and sparring, not scoring points in the ring. That's a distinction the product page could make clearer.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need hand wraps with these?**
A: Yes. The padding protects your knuckles, but wraps support your wrist and metacarpals. These gloves have solid wrist support, but wraps are non-negotiable for any real training.

**Q: How do I choose the right size?**
A: 12oz for bag work and general fitness, 14oz for pad training, 16oz for sparring and heavier bag work. If you're between sizes on hand width, go up one.

**Q: Can I use these for MMA or kickboxing?**
A: Yes, for striking drills and bag work. For MMA sparring, you'd want grappling-specific gloves, but these work fine for stand-up training.
