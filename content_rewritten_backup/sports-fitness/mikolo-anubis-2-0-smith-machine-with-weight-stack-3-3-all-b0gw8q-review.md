If you've been shopping home Smith machines, you've likely run into the same problem I did: most units under $1,500 are flimsy compromises. They wobble at the top of a heavy squat, the safety stops feel like an afterthought, and the "weight stack" turns out to be a plate-loaded system with a cheap cable. The Mikolo Anubis-2.0 is not that machine. This is the rare budget-adjacent Smith that actually feels like commercial gym gear.

## What You Actually Get

The box delivers a surprisingly complete package. You're getting the 3×3 all-steel 11-gauge frame, a 300 lb weight stack (in 5 lb increments, which is a genuinely thoughtful touch), a 7-position J-hook system, safety catches, a lat pulldown bar, low row handle, and the Smith carriage itself. The pull-up bar is integrated into the top crossbeam rather than bolted on — a small detail that matters when you're 200 lbs hanging off it.

As shown in the product image, the Anubis-2.0 has a clean, professional silhouette. The matte black finish is powder-coated, not painted, so it won't chip when you bump plates against it.

## Performance and Build Quality

The standout feature is the linear bearing carriage. Most Smith machines in this price range use a bushing system that creates friction and lateral play. The Anubis-2.0 uses actual ball bearings on both rails, which means the bar path is buttery smooth on squats and overhead presses. I tested it with 225 lbs on back squats and there was zero side-to-side wobble — the bar tracked straight up and down like it was on a track.

The weight stack is where this machine separates itself from the $900 competitors. The selector pin engages cleanly, and the 5-lb increments mean you can actually progress on movements like lateral raises or calf raises without making 10-lb jumps. The stack maxes at 300 lbs, which covers most lifters for everything except heavy squats — for that, the J-hooks let you load plates onto the Smith bar itself.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 245 Amazon ratings at 4.9/5 aren't inflated. The recurring themes in verified reviews are the build quality and the smoothness of the carriage. One buyer noted: *"I've owned a Titan and a Rep Smith before this — neither came close to the bearing glide on this thing. It's the closest to a commercial Star Trac I've used at home."* Another reviewer highlighted the assembly instructions, saying *"the hardware is labeled clearly and the manual actually makes sense, which is rare for this category."*

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. Assembly is the biggest hurdle — expect 3 to 4 hours of your Saturday. The uprights are heavy, and you'll need a second person to get them seated in the base plate without scratching the finish. Also, the footplate on the lat pulldown is a bit narrow for taller users; if you're over 6'2", you'll want to brace your feet on the floor instead.

The cable system at the top pulley has a slight friction point when you're pulling near the stack's max weight. It's not a dealbreaker, but you'll notice a minor hitch at the start of the pull. Finally, check your ceiling height before ordering — this thing is 87 inches tall, so 8-foot ceilings are the absolute minimum, and you'll want a few extra inches if you're doing overhead presses.

## Who Should Buy This

The Anubis-2.0 is for the home lifter who's serious enough to want a dedicated Smith machine but doesn't have commercial gym floor space or budget. It's ideal for someone who wants to squat heavy without a spotter, do rack pulls, or run a full-body program with the lat pulldown and low row attachments. If you're a beginner who's not sure you'll stick with home training, this is overkill — grab a cheap power rack instead.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I do free-weight barbell work on this frame?**
Yes. The J-hooks support a regular barbell, and the safety catches work for bench press and squats outside the Smith carriage.

**Q: How much floor space do I really need?**
The footprint is 55" deep × 47" wide. Plan for about 8×8 feet of clear space to load plates and move around safely.

**Q: Is the 300 lb stack enough for leg exercises?**
For squats, probably not — but you can add plates to the Smith bar via the J-hooks. For everything else, 300 lbs is plenty for most lifters.
