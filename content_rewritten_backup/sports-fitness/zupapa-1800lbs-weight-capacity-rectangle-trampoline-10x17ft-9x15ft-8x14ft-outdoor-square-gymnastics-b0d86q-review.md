# Zupapa 1800LBS Rectangle Trampoline Review: The Gymnast's Choice, With Caveats

Most backyard trampolines are round. That's fine for casual jumping, but if your kid is serious about gymnastics — or you're an adult who wants a real workout surface — round just doesn't cut it. The Zupapa 1800LBS Rectangle Trampoline in 10x17FT is built for a different purpose: consistent, directional bounce that actually helps you improve skills rather than just burn energy.

Here's what 210 Amazon reviewers at 4.2 stars have to say about living with one.

## What You Actually Get

The 10x17FT model ships as a seriously heavy box. Inside you'll find the galvanized steel frame (split into sections), the jumping mat, 96 springs, the enclosure poles and netting, foam padding for the frame, and a ladder. The rectangular jumping surface is roughly 10 by 17 feet — noticeably larger than a typical 14-foot round trampoline's effective jumping area.

One thing shoppers consistently note: this is not a 30-minute setup. Multiple reviewers mention spending 4–6 hours assembling it. The instructions are adequate but not exceptional. You'll want a second person for the frame assembly and spring attachment — the springs alone take significant time to hook.

## Performance and Bounce Quality

The rectangle shape is the headline feature here, and it delivers. Unlike round trampolines where you're naturally funneled toward the center, a rectangle gives you a predictable, flat bounce across the entire surface. The 96 springs (versus 80–88 on comparably sized rounds) provide a firmer, more responsive deck. Gymnasts can actually practice drills with consistent takeoff and landing points.

The 1800-lb weight rating on the 10x17FT is worth emphasizing. That's not marketing fluff — the frame is built with heavier-gauge steel than typical big-box store trampolines. Two adults jumping together (or one adult spotting a kid through a new skill) won't stress the structure. One reviewer noted, "I'm 240 pounds and jump with my two kids on this regularly. The frame doesn't flex or groan at all."

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The durability feedback is overwhelmingly positive. The galvanized frame resists rust, and the UV-protected pads and netting hold their color and integrity through full summer sun. Several reviewers mention having theirs for 2+ years with no mat sag or spring fatigue.

The enclosure net design deserves credit too. The pole system arches outward, giving you more headroom near the edges — a real safety improvement over designs where poles angle inward and create collision risk.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest recurring complaint is assembly difficulty. The 10x17FT has a lot of parts, and getting the springs evenly tensioned takes patience. Budget a full afternoon.

Replacement parts are also a concern. Standard round trampolines have universally available mats and springs; rectangular sizes are less common, and Zupapa's specific dimensions mean you'll likely need to order direct from the manufacturer if something wears out.

Wind is the other issue. The large rectangular surface acts like a sail. If your yard is exposed, you need the anchor kit — and honestly, I'd recommend staking it down even if you think you're sheltered.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Gymnastics families**: If your kid is taking classes and wants home practice, the rectangle bounce is genuinely worth the premium over round alternatives.
- **Active adults**: The 1800-lb capacity makes this one of the few trampolines that comfortably handles grown adults doing cardio routines.
- **Families with multiple kids**: The larger jumping surface handles 3–4 kids without constant collisions.

Skip it if you just want a casual bounce toy for young kids — a round trampoline is cheaper, easier to assemble, and simpler to maintain.

## FAQ

**Q: Is the 1800-lb rating real, or marketing?**
A: The frame and springs genuinely support it. The 10x17FT uses thicker steel and more springs than standard models. That said, the mat itself is the limiting factor long-term — don't expect the jumping mat to last forever under heavy adult use.

**Q: Can I leave it outside year-round?**
A: The frame and pads are UV-protected and rust-resistant, but snow load and ice can damage the mat. If you live in a heavy snow region, plan to disassemble the mat and springs for winter storage.

**Q: How hard is the assembly really?**
A: Two people, 4–6 hours, basic socket wrench set. The springs are the tedious part. It's not hard, just time-consuming. Don't attempt it alone.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a real training surface, need a trampoline that handles adult weight, or have multiple kids who'll use it daily. The rectangle bounce quality and 1800-lb frame capacity justify the premium over round alternatives.

**Skip it if** you're on a budget, have a small yard, or want something you can set up in an hour.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely capable trampoline held back by assembly time and harder-to-source replacement parts. For serious jumpers, it's worth the effort.
