**The two-in-one pool toy that actually earns its keep**

Most pool games are either too flimsy to survive a summer or too boring to hold attention past July. The GoSports Splash Hoop 2-in-1 sits in a rare sweet spot: it's built like a proper piece of sports equipment, yet it converts from basketball to volleyball in minutes. At 4.5 stars from 225 buyers, it's clearly earning its place in backyard pools. But let's dig into what you're actually getting before you drop your money.

**What's in the box**

GoSports ships the full kit: two 24-inch polycarbonate backboards with breakaway-style 5-inch rims, a steel frame with PVC coating, a floating volleyball net that spans the pool width, one basketball, one volleyball, and a hand pump. The assembly is genuinely tool-free — the frame clicks together with push-button locks, and the backboards attach with quick-release clips. First-time setup runs about 15 minutes if you read the instructions, 10 if you don't.

The frame itself is the star. It's a sturdy PVC-over-steel construction with a wide A-frame base on each side. Each base has a fill cap, and the manual recommends sand. I'll be blunt: fill them. With water alone, the whole rig floats like a buoy and drifts anytime someone splashes. With sand, it sits low and planted in the water, holding position even when kids are thrashing around.

**Real-world performance**

The basketball side is where this thing shines. The twin backboards mean you can run a proper two-on-two game without waiting for rebounds. The rims are regulation-sized for mini hoops — 5 inches — which is perfect for the included mini basketball. Shots swish through cleanly, and the polycarbonate backboard gives a satisfying thunk on makes. One buyer summed it up: *"My kids are 6 and 9 and they played for three straight hours the first day. The hoop takes a beating and just keeps working."*

The volleyball conversion is clever. The net detaches from the backboard frame and reattaches across the pool using the same pole structure. It takes about two minutes and zero tools. The net has a nice tension system with side straps, so it doesn't sag into the water mid-rally. That said, the included volleyball is noticeably light — it's fine for casual tossing and bumping, but if you've got teens who actually play, you'll want to swap in a proper water volleyball.

**Where it falls short**

Nothing's perfect, and the Splash Hoop has three honest weaknesses. First, the sand requirement. The box doesn't scream "YOU NEED 40 POUNDS OF SAND" — you'll discover that in the manual. If you skip it, the entire structure wobbles and drifts. Budget for the sand and a funnel before your first use.

Second, the height. The poles extend to 7.5 feet, which is perfect for kids and shorter adults. But anyone over 5'10" will be dunking on those 5-inch rims without effort. That's fine for casual fun, but if you wanted a regulation challenge, this isn't it.

Third, the volleyball net is a single-piece design. If a seam rips — and one reviewer noted theirs tore after a summer of hard use — you can't replace just the net. You'd need the whole kit. It's a durability caveat worth knowing going in.

**Who should buy this**

This is squarely aimed at families with kids aged 5–14 and casual adult players who want something fun, not competitive. It's also a great fit for anyone with a 15-foot or wider pool — the net stretches to about 12 feet, so smaller pools will have slack. If you've got a party crowd, the two-hoop setup is a genuine social magnet. Skip it if you're a serious volleyball player or if your pool is tiny.

**FAQs from real shoppers**

**Q: Can this be used in a saltwater pool?**  
Yes. The PVC coating protects the steel frame, and the backboards are marine-grade polycarbonate. Rinse the frame with fresh water after heavy salt exposure and it'll hold up.

**Q: Is it safe for toddlers?**  
The rims are low enough for little kids to reach, but the frame is rigid. If you've got kids under 4, supervise them closely — the edges are padded but not soft-foam.

**Q: Does the volleyball net block swimming lanes?**  
It's removable, and the whole thing breaks down into sections that store in the included carry bag. Setup takes about 5 minutes, teardown about the same.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, versatile pool game that'll survive a full summer of hard play and keep multiple kids entertained. The dual-hoop basketball setup alone justifies the price, and the volleyball conversion is a legit bonus, not a gimmick.

**Skip it if** you're looking for a regulation-height volleyball experience, have a tiny plunge pool, or don't want to haul 40 pounds of sand for proper stability.

For most families, this is the rare pool toy that earns its place year after year. At 4.6/5, it's a solid investment in summer fun.
