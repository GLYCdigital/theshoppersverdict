If you've been scrolling past rowers because they're either $1,200 machines that fold into a closet or cheap ones that feel like rowing through mud, this Sunny Health & Fitness model sits in a sweet spot most brands ignore. It's a magnetic resistance rower under $300 that doesn't squeak, doesn't jerk, and doesn't feel like it'll tip over on stroke three.

**What actually arrives at your door**

The box is substantial — roughly 50 pounds — and contains the main frame, slide rail, seat, pedals with adjustable straps, a digital monitor, and the hardware kit. The steel frame has a powder-coated finish that resists scratches from sliding shoes. Assembly isn't complicated, but here's the honest truth: the rail-to-frame connection needs two people. One person has to hold the rail steady while the other threads the bolts. Plan for 45 minutes if you're methodical, 30 if you've built gym equipment before. The included tools work, but a ratchet set makes the job significantly easier.

**How it performs when you actually row**

The magnetic resistance system uses a 16-level dial mounted on the main frame. Levels 1–4 feel like warm-up strokes; levels 10–16 give you a legitimate workout that'll have you breathing hard within five minutes. The transition between levels is smooth — no clunky ratcheting like you get with air or water rowers at this price.

The 49.5-inch slide rail is the standout feature here. I'm 5'11" and had no issue with full leg extension. The seat glides on a dual-rail system with nylon rollers that feel stable, not wobbly. The foot pedals have adjustable straps that accommodate shoe sizes from women's 6 to men's 13 without pinching.

Noise-wise, this thing is quiet. The magnetic brake means no fan whoosh, no water slosh — just the soft whisper of the seat rollers and your own breathing. Multiple reviewers confirmed this, with one noting: *"I row while my husband takes calls in the same room and he can't hear it at all."*

**Where it falls short**

The monitor is the weakest link. It shows time, stroke count, and a calorie estimate that's optimistic at best. There's no heart rate strap compatibility, no Bluetooth, no app integration. If you're a data-driven athlete, you'll want to track workouts on your phone separately.

The seat cushion is firm. Some users find it uncomfortable for sessions longer than 30 minutes. A gel seat pad solves this for under $15, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

The rower folds up for storage, but at 48 pounds, it's not something you'll casually tuck away every day. It's manageable, just not effortless.

**Who should buy this**

This rower is for the person who wants a legitimate cardio and full-body strength workout at home without spending apartment-rent money. It's ideal for:

- **Beginners** who want progressive resistance to build up strength safely
- **Apartment dwellers** who need quiet equipment (this is genuinely one of the quietest rowers under $500)
- **Taller users** — the long rail accommodates heights that many budget rowers don't
- **People who want a total-body workout** — rowing engages legs, core, back, and arms in one motion

Skip it if you're a competitive rower who needs precise performance metrics, or if you plan to row for 45+ minutes daily — the seat comfort will become an issue.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Does it really fold up for storage?** Yes, there's a release lever near the front that lets the rail fold up against the frame. It takes about 10 seconds. Stored upright, it takes up roughly 2 feet by 2 feet of floor space.

**What's the maximum user weight?** The frame is rated for 300 pounds. The steel construction feels solid at that limit — no flexing or creaking during hard pulls.

**Is the resistance magnetic or air?** It's magnetic. This means no fan noise, no air drag fluctuation, and consistent resistance throughout the entire stroke. The tradeoff is that you don't get the "feel" of rowing on water that air rowers provide.

**The verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a quiet, smooth, effective rower that doesn't break the bank. It delivers 90% of what premium rowers offer at a fraction of the cost.

**Skip it if** you need advanced metrics, plan to row for extended sessions without a seat pad, or want something light enough to move between rooms effortlessly.

At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, this is one of the most consistently well-reviewed budget rowers on the market. The magnetic resistance system, sturdy frame, and long rail make it an easy recommendation for home fitness enthusiasts. It earns every bit of its 4.6/5 score — just budget for a seat cushion and a separate fitness tracker.
