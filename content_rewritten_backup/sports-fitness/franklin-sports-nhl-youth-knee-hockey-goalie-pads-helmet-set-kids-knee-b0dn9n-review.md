## Is this the goalie set your kid actually needs?

Here's the honest truth about the Franklin Sports NHL Youth Knee Hockey Goalie Pads + Helmet Set: it's a budget-friendly, kid-pleasing starter kit that does exactly what it promises — and not much more. If your child is obsessed with knee hockey and wants to feel like their favorite NHL netminder, this set delivers the look and basic protection for casual basement or driveway play. But if you're expecting real goalie-grade equipment, you'll be disappointed.

## What's actually in the box

The set includes three core pieces: a chest/body protector, two leg pads, and a helmet. The NHL branding is prominent throughout — kids love wearing their favorite team's colors, and that's a genuine selling point here. The pads use lightweight foam construction, which makes sense for knee hockey where you're sliding on floors rather than ice. The helmet is a basic shell with minimal interior padding — think Halloween costume meets sports equipment.

## Real-world performance

For indoor knee hockey, the pads do their job. The leg pads offer decent shin and knee coverage for floor slides, and the chest protector handles the occasional errant shot from a foam ball or soft puck. The lightweight design means kids can move freely without feeling weighed down — a real advantage for young players still developing coordination.

But here's where the set stumbles. The sizing is genuinely problematic. Multiple buyers report the small fits more like a toddler size than a youth small. One parent noted their 5-year-old was already on the tight side, while another said their 7-year-old couldn't get the leg pads to close around their calves. Check measurements carefully before ordering — this is not a "grow into it" purchase.

The straps are the second weak point. Several reviews mention the elastic losing tension after just a few weeks of play. Kids who play daily will likely need to re-secure straps frequently or deal with pads sliding around mid-game.

## What buyers consistently praise

The value proposition is the clear winner here. As one verified purchase put it: "For the price, you can't beat having everything in one box. My son wears this every day after school." The NHL branding is a hit with young fans, and parents appreciate not having to buy pieces separately. The lightweight design also gets consistent positive mentions for keeping kids comfortable during extended play sessions.

## Where it falls short

The helmet is the most concerning piece. It's thin plastic with minimal impact protection — fine for foam balls, but I wouldn't want a child wearing this against anything harder. Multiple reviewers describe it as "basically cosmetic." If your kids play with real pucks or hard balls, buy a proper helmet separately.

Durability is also a question mark. Beyond the strap issues, the foam can compress and lose shape with heavy use. For occasional weekend play, this will last a season or two. For daily after-school hockey, expect wear within months.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You have a 4-7 year old who's new to knee hockey and wants to look like their favorite NHL star. The price point is right for a starter set, and the NHL branding will make you a hero. It's also a solid choice for birthday gifts or holiday presents where the excitement factor matters more than long-term durability.

**Skip it if:** Your child is over 7 or on the bigger side — sizing won't work. Skip it if they play with anything harder than foam balls. And definitely skip if you're looking for protective gear that will last through a full season of regular play.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my 8-year-old?**
A: Probably not. The sizing runs small, and multiple buyers report the small fits more like a 4T/5T. Measure your child's chest and shin length before ordering. If they're over 50 pounds or 4 feet tall, look for a larger size or different brand.

**Q: Is the helmet safe for real hockey?**
A: No. This is designed for knee hockey with foam balls or soft pucks. It's not rated for ice hockey or any hard-impact play. For anything beyond casual indoor play, invest in a certified hockey helmet.

**Q: How durable is this for daily play?**
A: Expect a few months of regular use before straps stretch out and foam compresses. It's a starter set, not a long-term investment. For kids who play a few times a week, it should last a season.
