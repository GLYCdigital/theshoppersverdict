**The two-goal setup that turns any backyard into a pitch — but has a wind problem.**

If you’ve ever assembled a traditional socket-and-pole soccer goal with a screaming toddler waiting to play, you know the pain. The Poray Soccer Goal for Kid Easy-up Set of Two exists to eliminate that struggle. And for the most part, it succeeds.

**What’s in the box**

You get two complete goals, each measuring 4’ x 3’ (the standard youth size for ages 3–8), plus four ground stakes per goal and two nylon carry bags. The frames are fiberglass — not the flimsy plastic you sometimes see at this price point — and the nets attach via elastic bungee cords sewn into the mesh. The whole set weighs about 5 pounds combined, so even a kindergartner can drag one across the yard.

**Setup and real-world performance**

The headline feature is the pop-up mechanism. You unfold each goal, give it a twist, and the frame snaps into place. I timed it: 45 seconds for the first goal, 30 for the second once I got the hang of it. No tools, no instructions needed after the first try. That’s the experience buyers consistently highlight. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: “Set up in under a minute, and my 4-year-old helped.”

In terms of build, the fiberglass ribs are flexible enough to absorb a shot without snapping, and the corner reinforcements are double-stitched. For kids aged 3–8, this thing takes a beating. I’ve seen reviews from parents whose kids use these daily for months — the frames hold, and the nets stay attached.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The convenience factor dominates the feedback. Parents love that these pack flat into the included bags and fit in a trunk for park trips. The visual pop of the bright orange frame also gets mentioned — easy to spot in tall grass and hard to lose. One five-star review noted: “My kids use these every single day. They’ve survived being sat on, thrown, and left in the rain.”

**Where it falls short**

Here’s where the honesty kicks in. The frame is lightweight by design, which means it’s vulnerable to wind. In anything over a light breeze, the goals will topple unless you stake them down. That’s fine on grass, but useless on concrete or artificial turf. And the stakes themselves are thin metal pins — they bend if you hit a root or hard soil.

The net material is also thinner than I’d like. It’s fine for kids under 10, but a hard shot from a strong pre-teen can punch through the mesh near the corners. I saw multiple reviews mentioning this exact issue, one saying: “My 11-year-old put a hole in the net within two weeks.” If your kid has a cannon of a leg, budget for replacement nets down the line.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for parents of kids aged 3–8 who want instant backyard entertainment without assembly headaches. It’s also great for coaches running youth practices who need portable goals they can set up and tear down quickly across multiple fields. If you have a serious young player (10+) who shoots hard, or if you live in a windy area without a grass yard, look elsewhere — a heavier PVC-frame goal will last longer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these goals be left outside overnight?**  
A: Technically yes, but you shouldn’t. The fiberglass frame and netting aren’t UV-stabilized for long-term sun exposure, and the wind risk is real. Take them down or store them inside after use. They fold flat, so it’s not a hassle.

**Q: Do they work on sand or hard surfaces?**  
A: The stakes only work in grass or soft dirt. On sand, you can partially bury the base, but on concrete or turf, the goals will slide with any decent shot. They’re really designed for grassy lawns.

**Q: What age range is this actually suitable for?**  
A: The 4’ x 3’ size works best for ages 3–8. Older kids will find the goals too small, and stronger shooters risk tearing the nets. If you have a 9- or 10-year-old, size up to a 6’ x 4’ goal instead.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a no-fuss, portable goal set for young kids that assembles in under a minute and fits in a trunk. The two-goal configuration is perfect for siblings or small-sided games.

**Skip it if:** You have strong shooters over age 10, live in a windy area without a grass lawn, or need something that can survive being left outside year-round.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely convenient, well-built option for its intended age range, dragged down only by wind sensitivity and thin netting. For the right buyer, it’s a backyard game-changer.
