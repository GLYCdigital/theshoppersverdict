If you've watched any golf instruction on YouTube, you've heard the phrase "hold your lag" about four thousand times. The problem is that feel isn't real — what feels like a late release on the range often looks like an early cast on video. The Performance Golf Click Stick tackles this with a simple premise: make a sound when you hinge correctly, and you'll train the right movement without needing a camera setup.

## What You Actually Get

The Click Stick is a short, weighted training club with a grip that mimics a standard golf club. The key feature is an internal click mechanism paired with an adjustable resistance band near the grip. As shown in the product image, it's compact — roughly the length of a mid-iron but with a heavier head to promote feel. The package includes the stick itself and the resistance band. That's it. No carrying case, no instructional booklet, no QR code to video drills. For a $60-plus trainer, that's a minor miss.

## How It Works in Practice

The concept is solid: as you swing, the stick emits an audible click when your wrists hinge at the correct angle, then a second click when you release through impact. The band adds resistance to train wrist strength through the transition. In testing, the click mechanism is responsive and consistent — it does reward a proper wrist set. The heavier head also gives genuine feedback on tempo; rush the downswing and you'll feel it.

The build quality is acceptable for the price. The grip is comfortable and tacky, the shaft has a bit of flex, and the band attaches securely. That said, the clicking mechanism feels slightly plasticky. It's not fragile, but it doesn't inspire the confidence of a premium training aid either.

## What Buyers Say Works

The reviews trend positive on one specific point: the audible feedback genuinely helps groove a better wrist hinge. One buyer noted, "I didn't realize how early I was releasing until I heard the click happen before impact. After two weeks, my iron contact is noticeably crisper." Multiple reviewers mention it's helped their chipping too — the click trains a proper hinge in short game strokes where feel matters most.

Another consistent praise point is the portability. You can swing this in a living room without fear of breaking anything (as long as you're not near a ceiling fan). The resistance band adds a workout element, making it a decent warm-up tool before rounds.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint, echoed across multiple reviews: the click is hard to hear outdoors. On a windy range or a busy practice area, the sound gets lost. You'll need to train in a quiet space or use earbuds to hear the feedback clearly, which somewhat defeats the purpose of an auditory trainer.

The lack of instruction is another real issue. If you don't already know what a proper wrist hinge feels like, you won't know if you're clicking at the right point in your swing. The stick tells you *when* you hinge, but not *how* to hinge correctly. Beginners may find themselves chasing the sound without understanding the mechanics behind it.

Finally, the resistance band adds minimal difficulty for stronger golfers. It's adjustable, but even the highest setting feels light for players with above-average swing speed.

## Who Should Buy This

This is a tool for **golfers who already understand the basic swing mechanics** and need a reminder to stop casting early. If you've taken lessons, know what lag feels like, and want a portable way to reinforce it at home, the Click Stick delivers. It's also ideal for **practice sessions without access to a range** — you can groove the motion in your office or garage.

Skip it if you're a complete beginner. Without guidance, you'll likely just be clicking away without fixing the root cause of your inconsistency. Also skip it if you do most of your practice on a windy, crowded range — you won't hear the feedback.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this help with my slice?**
A: Indirectly. An early release often contributes to an open clubface at impact, which causes slices. If this trains a later release, you may see the ball flight straighten. But it won't fix grip, stance, or path issues.

**Q: Is it safe to swing indoors?**
A: Yes, with space. The stick is about 30 inches long, and the weighted head won't damage drywall if you maintain control. But clear the area — a full swing needs roughly a 6-foot radius.

**Q: Does the resistance band actually build strength?**
A: It builds wrist and forearm endurance more than raw strength. Think of it as a repetition tool for muscle memory, not a gym replacement.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** You're an intermediate golfer fighting an early release who wants instant, portable feedback without video analysis. It's a legitimate training aid for groove work at home.

**Skip it if:** You're new to golf, practice exclusively outdoors in noisy conditions, or expect a training aid to magically fix your swing without understanding the mechanics.

The Click Stick fills a specific niche well: simple, audible feedback for a common swing flaw. It's not revolutionary, and the lack of guidance and quiet click hold it back from greatness. But for the right golfer, it's a useful addition to the practice bag.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — a functional trainer with a clear purpose, hampered by minor execution gaps.
