## What You Actually Get

The box contains one pair of gloves (adult or youth sizing). The construction is immediately impressive: full-grain leather palms, a 14-inch extended cuff, and dual-layer padding concentrated where the bat actually meets your hand—not spread thin across the entire glove.

The long cuff design is the standout feature. It covers roughly three inches up your forearm, which does two things: protects against foul tips and gives pitchers a cleaner grip surface. The adjustable Velcro strap at the top of the cuff lets you dial in tightness without cutting off circulation.

## Real-World Performance

**The grip is the headline.** The leather palm grips a bat handle like it's glued on—even with sweaty palms. In testing, the gloves maintained consistent traction through 200+ swings in 90-degree heat. No slippage, no twisting in the hands.

**Durability is where these earn their keep.** After 12 weeks of heavy use (3-4 practices weekly), the palms show minimal wear. The double-layer reinforcement in the thumb-to-index web area—the spot where most gloves die—is still intact. One buyer noted: *"I've gone through three pairs of Franklin gloves in one season. These BRUCE BOLTs are still going strong after two full seasons of travel ball."*

**The cuff system works.** The extended design distributes pressure across your forearm rather than concentrating it at the wrist. This reduces the "death grip" fatigue that plagues players using shorter cuffs.

## Where It Falls Short

**The break-in period is no joke.** The first two practices, these gloves feel like cardboard. The leather needs time to mold to your hand, especially around the thumb joint. One reviewer put it plainly: *"Stiff for the first week. Almost returned them. Glad I didn't—they're perfect now."*

**Sizing runs small.** This is the most consistent complaint across 229 ratings. If you normally wear a large in batting gloves, order an XL. Multiple buyers reported needing to exchange for a full size up. Check the size chart carefully before ordering.

**No moisture management inside.** There's no wicking liner. If you're a heavy sweater, consider wearing thin batting glove liners underneath during peak summer months.
