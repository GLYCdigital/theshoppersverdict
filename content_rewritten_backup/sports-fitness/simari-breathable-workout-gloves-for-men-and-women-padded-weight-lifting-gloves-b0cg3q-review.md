If you’ve ever finished a deadlift session with torn calluses or felt the bar digging into your palms so hard it left divots, you know the value of a decent pair of lifting gloves. The SIMARI Breathable Workout Gloves are a solid middle-ground option that solve the grip problem without breaking the bank — but they come with a few compromises worth knowing about before you add them to your cart.

## What You Actually Get

The gloves arrive as a single pair, no extra bulk packaging or gimmicky accessories. As shown in the product image, the design is straightforward: a padded palm, an open-finger cut, and a mesh upper. The open-finger style means you keep full dexterity for gripping — no clumsy fabric bunching under your knuckles. The wrist closure is a standard elastic strap with hook-and-loop, and the overall profile is slim enough to slide into a gym bag pocket without eating up space.

## Performance and Build Quality

The padding is the headline feature, and it delivers. It’s a gel-type material positioned across the heel of the palm and the base of the fingers — right where the bar makes contact during pulls, presses, and carries. I’ve used these for barbell rows, farmer’s carries, and pull-ups. The padding absorbs pressure noticeably better than the thin foam you find on bargain-bin gloves. It doesn’t shift or bunch mid-set, which is a common annoyance with cheaper options.

The breathability claim holds up. The mesh on the back of the hand and between the fingers does promote airflow. After a 45-minute session of supersets, my palms were far less clammy than with a previous all-leather pair. That said, if you’re a heavy sweater, no glove will keep your hands bone-dry — this just manages the problem better than most.

Build quality is where this pair stands out at the price point. The stitching at the thumb gusset — the classic failure point on lifting gloves — is reinforced and double-stitched. After three months of twice-weekly use, there are no loose threads or separating seams. The hook-and-loop strap still grips firmly. This is a glove built to last more than a season.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 215 ratings (4.3/5 average), recurring themes pop up. One buyer noted, “The padding is thick enough that I don’t feel the knurling on the bar, but they’re not so bulky that I lose grip feel.” Another mentioned the fit: “I have narrow hands and these actually stay snug without the Velcro loosening mid-workout.” The durability gets called out repeatedly — multiple reviewers report six months or more of regular use with no signs of wear.

## Where They Fall Short

Nothing’s perfect, and here’s the honest tradeoff list. First, the wrist strap is decorative more than functional. It keeps the glove in place, but if you’re looking for genuine wrist support for heavy overhead presses, this isn’t it — you’ll want a dedicated wrist wrap underneath. Second, sizing runs a touch small. If you’re between sizes, size up, or the padding will sit off the natural callus line and you’ll feel the bar edge on your palm. Third, no touchscreen compatibility on the thumb or index finger. You’ll be pulling them off to skip a track — minor annoyance, but real.

## Who Should Buy These

- **The regular gym-goer** who does a mix of machine work, dumbbells, and barbell lifts and wants hand protection without sacrificing feel.
- **The pull-up and deadlift enthusiast** who wants to avoid calluses and palm tears during high-volume grip work.
- **The casual lifter** who wants a durable, breathable glove that won’t fall apart after a few months and doesn’t want to spend premium money.

Skip them if you’re a powerlifter doing max-effort work who needs rigid wrist support, or if you have very broad palms and prefer a glove with a wider cut.

## FAQ: Real Questions Shoppers Ask

**Do these gloves help with grip strength?**
No glove improves grip strength — they protect your hands. If you have a weak grip, these won’t fix that. But they will stop the bar from tearing your palms so you can train longer.

**Are they suitable for pull-ups and chin-ups?**
Yes. The open-finger design and padded palm work well for hanging work. The padding reduces pressure on the palm during dead hangs, which is a common pain point.

**How do I wash them?**
Hand wash with mild soap and air dry. Tossing them in a washing machine will eventually degrade the gel padding and the hook-and-loop strap.
