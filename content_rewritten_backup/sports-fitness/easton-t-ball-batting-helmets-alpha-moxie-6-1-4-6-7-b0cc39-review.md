## A Youth Helmet That Takes Hits Seriously (and Looks Good Doing It)

Let's be honest: most t-ball helmets are plastic bowls with foam glued inside. The Easton Alpha Moxie actually treats the job like engineering. For a youth helmet at this price point, the attention to impact protection is noticeable the moment you pick it up.

This review covers the **6 1/4" - 6 1/2" size**, which fits most kids ages 4–7 depending on head circumference. Easton built this with the same design DNA as their adult batting helmets — extended rear coverage, dual-density foam, and a fit system that doesn't require a degree in plastic engineering.

## What's Actually in the Box

You get the helmet. That's it. No chin strap (t-ball leagues typically don't require one at this age), no face guard, no carrying bag. The shell is a single-piece ABS plastic with a glossy automotive-style finish, and the interior uses two distinct foam densities — a firmer outer layer that spreads impact energy and a softer inner layer that sits against your kid's head.

The **tool-free fit dial** on the back is the standout feature. Turn it clockwise to tighten, counterclockwise to loosen. It ratchets in small increments, so you can dial in a precise fit without pinching. Parents who've dealt with the old "screwdriver and prayers" fit systems on budget helmets will appreciate this.

## Real-World Performance and Build Quality

**The protection story is legit.** The extended back shell drops down lower than most competitors — Easton claims it covers the occipital region more completely, and holding it side-by-side against a basic Rawlings or Franklin helmet, you can see the difference. The dual-density liner isn't marketing fluff; you can feel the firmer outer layer when you press on the crown versus the plush inner padding near the temples.

**The fit system works, but sizing is the catch.** Multiple parent reviews mention this helmet runs small. One reviewer noted, *"My son wears a 6 1/2 in most hats, and this fit tight even at the loosest setting. We exchanged for the next size up."* Another said, *"Ordered based on the size chart and it was snug — had to return for the larger size."* If your kid is between sizes, **size up**. It's the single most common complaint in the feedback.

**Durability is mixed.** The shell itself is solid — it'll survive being dropped on concrete, thrown in a dugout, or used as a drum. But the glossy finish is a scratch magnet. One parent wrote, *"After two games it looked like we'd dragged it behind the car. Functionally fine, but cosmetically rough."* If your kid is rough on gear, pick a matte finish or buy a helmet cover.

**Heat retention is real.** Ventilation is minimal — four small vents on top, none on the sides. During a 90-degree summer game, your kid's head will sweat. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing if your league plays in the heat.

## Where It Falls Short

- **Sizing inconsistency** — the #1 complaint across reviews. Easton's size chart says 6 1/4"–6 1/2" fits head circumferences of 20.5"–21". In practice, it runs snug. Measure your kid's head **before** ordering, then size up if they're at the top of a range.
- **Scratch-prone finish** — the glossy shell looks great day one, less great by game five.
- **No ventilation in the temple area** — sweat builds up faster than you'd expect.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Parents of 4–7 year olds** who want real impact protection, not just a league-mandated bucket
- **Kids who refuse to wear helmets** because they're "ugly" — the Moxie design genuinely gets kids excited

**Skip it if** your kid is between sizes and you can't try it on in store — the sizing gamble isn't worth it. Or if your kid sweats heavily and plays in a hot climate; a more ventilated helmet might serve better.
