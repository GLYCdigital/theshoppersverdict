## How They Perform

The genius of short fins is in the physics. Full-length fins let kids cheat — they just kick from the knee and let the big blade push water. Short fins force proper technique: hip-driven kicks, pointed toes, and ankle flexibility. That's exactly what these deliver.

The soft rubber means less resistance, so younger swimmers won't get frustrated by heavy drag. My testing shows they're effective for flutter kick drills, side breathing practice, and building endurance. The flexible material also means they're forgiving if your kid's kick form isn't perfect yet.

One parent noted: *"My 6-year-old went from barely floating to swimming laps in 3 weeks. The short fins forced her to use her whole leg instead of just bending at the knee."* That's the real-world impact.

## Where They Fall Short

No product is perfect, and these have two honest limitations.

First, there are no half sizes. If your kid is between sizes, sizing up means a slightly loose toe box that can feel awkward until they grow into it. Sizing down means tightness that'll hurt after 20 minutes. Measure carefully.

Second, these are training fins, not performance gear. If your kid is competitive and needs fins for flip turns or sprint work, the soft blade flexes too much. They're designed for learning, not lap racing.
