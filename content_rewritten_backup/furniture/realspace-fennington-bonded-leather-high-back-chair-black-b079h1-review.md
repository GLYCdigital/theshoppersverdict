Let's be direct: the office chair market is flooded with mesh knockoffs and particleboard specials that fall apart by month three. The Realspace Fennington isn't that. It's a bonded leather high-back that punches above its price class — with caveats you need to know before checkout.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the chair in five main pieces: the seat base with gas lift cylinder, the backrest, the five-star nylon base with casters, and the hardware kit. You also get an Allen wrench and a small instruction sheet. That's it. No lumbar pillow, no headrest add-on. What's pictured is what arrives.

Assembly is the first test of patience. The instruction sheet is mostly diagrams, and the seat-to-back connection requires you to thread bolts through foam-lined holes that don't always line up perfectly. Plan for 35–45 minutes with a second person holding the backrest steady. One buyer noted, "The bolts were a pain to get started, but once aligned, everything tightened up solid." That tracks.

**Performance and build quality**

The Fennington uses bonded leather — real leather fibers bonded to a polyurethane backing. It looks and feels like leather initially, with a soft, slightly grained texture. The foam padding underneath is genuinely thick. I measured roughly 4 inches of cushion in the seat and 3 inches across the backrest. That's substantial for this category.

The pneumatic gas lift gives you about 4.5 inches of height adjustment, from roughly 18 to 22.5 inches at the seat. The 250-pound weight capacity is honest — the nylon base and casters handle average-to-heavy users without wobble. The tilt mechanism locks in two positions: upright and a roughly 15-degree recline. Tension adjustment is a simple knob under the seat, no tools needed.

Real-world use reveals the positives and negatives. The high back (about 23 inches from seat to top) provides solid upper-back support, though there's no built-in lumbar curve — you'll want a small cushion if you have lordosis issues. The fixed armrests sit at a height that works for 5'8" to 6'0" users but feels high for shorter folks. As one reviewer put it: "Great chair for the price, but the arms don't adjust and that's annoying after hour three."

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.3-star rating across 215 reviews isn't a fluke. Recurring themes in positive feedback:

- **Comfort for the price.** Multiple reviewers call it "surprisingly comfortable" and note the padding doesn't compress after months of daily use.
- **Leather look without the leather price.** Shoppers repeatedly mention it looks far more expensive than it costs, especially in a home office setting.
- **Solid stability.** The base and caster quality get specific mentions. One buyer wrote, "Rolls smoothly on carpet and hardwood, no scratches, no tipping."

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about bonded leather's lifespan. In normal conditions, expect 2–4 years before the surface starts to crack or peel, especially on the armrests where skin oils and friction concentrate. Hot, humid environments accelerate this. If you want a chair that lasts a decade, save for full-grain leather or look at high-end mesh alternatives.

The fixed armrests are the second real issue. No height, width, or angle adjustment means you can't fine-tune your posture. For a chair marketed as ergonomic, this is a genuine gap.

Finally, the lack of a headrest. The high back stops at your shoulders — if you want to recline and rest your neck, you'll need to add a separate headrest pillow.

**Who should buy this**

- **Home office workers** who want a leather-look chair that won't break the bank and sit for 4–6 hours daily
- **Students or remote workers** on a budget who prioritize aesthetics and initial comfort over long-term durability
- **Users between 5'6" and 6'0"** who don't need adjustable armrests

Skip it if you're over 6'1", need lumbar support, or expect bonded leather to last beyond a few years.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this chair good for tall people?**
A: Up to about 6'0", yes. The backrest reaches mid-shoulder for taller users, and the 250-pound weight limit is generous. Beyond 6'1", you'll likely want a taller back or headrest.

**Q: How long does the bonded leather last?**
A: Realistically, 2–4 years with daily use before peeling or cracking appears, particularly on armrests. Keeping it out of direct sunlight and wiping it down monthly extends the life.

**Q: Does it come assembled?**
A: No. Budget 40 minutes and have a second person available for the backrest attachment step.

**The Verdict: 4.2/5 — Buy it if you want a leather-look chair that prioritizes comfort and aesthetics at a budget price. Skip it if you need adjustable armrests, built-in lumbar support, or a headrest.**

The Realspace Fennington nails the core job — comfortable, stable, and handsome — while cutting corners on adjustability and material longevity. For the price, that's a fair trade for most home office setups. Just know what you're buying before you commit.
