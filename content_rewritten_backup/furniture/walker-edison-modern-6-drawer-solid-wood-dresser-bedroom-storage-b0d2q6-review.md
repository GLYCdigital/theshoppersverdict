**A six-drawer dresser that actually uses solid wood — but brings a few caveats to your bedroom.**

Most dressers under $400 are engineered wood with a convincing wood-grain photograph glued on top. The Walker Edison Modern 6-Drawer is different: the frame, drawer fronts, and legs are solid pine. That's why it weighs in around 85 pounds and feels substantial when you slide a fully loaded drawer open. At 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, buyers consistently confirm it's a genuine upgrade from flat-pack particleboard — but the assembly process and a couple of design quirks keep it from being perfect.

**What you actually get**

The box arrives flat-packed, and it's a heavy one. Inside you'll find six assembled drawer boxes (real wood, dovetail joints), the main frame in two halves, six drawer fronts, tapered wooden legs, and a hardware bag with cam locks, screws, and wooden dowels. The included Allen wrench works, but you'll want a cordless drill with a Phillips bit for the 40+ screws. Total dimensions are 55 inches wide, 18 inches deep, and 30 inches tall — a footprint that fits most standard bedroom walls without dominating the room.

**Performance and build quality in real use**

The drawers glide on metal ball-bearing slides rated for 50 pounds each. Loaded with folded denim and sweaters, they still open smoothly — though you'll notice the slides aren't soft-close. They'll thunk if you push them shut quickly. The solid wood construction means no edge banding to peel and no hollow-sounding panels. The finish is a matte lacquer that resists water rings reasonably well, but it's not waterproof — wipe spills promptly.

One thing buyers consistently mention: the drawers are deep. At 18 inches, you can store items vertically like files or folded shirts on edge, which maximizes capacity. The top two drawers are shallower at 4.5 inches, suited for socks and accessories. That's a smart design, but some shoppers expected uniform depth — check the measurements before you buy.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The word "sturdy" appears in nearly every positive review. One verified purchase wrote, "I've owned $800 dressers that felt flimsier than this. The wood is legit — you can see the grain and feel the weight." Another noted, "The mid-century style looks way more expensive than it is. I get compliments from guests constantly." The tapered legs also get repeated mentions for clearing baseboards, letting the dresser sit flush against the wall.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest complaint. Multiple reviews describe the process as "tedious" and "frustrating," particularly aligning the drawer slides. The instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text, and if you misalign a slide by even a millimeter, the drawer will bind. Budget 2–3 hours if you're doing it solo. Also, there's no anti-tip wall anchor included — for a tall piece this heavy, that's a safety oversight for households with kids. You'll need to buy a furniture strap separately.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small apartment dwellers** who need substantial storage in a compact 55-inch footprint
- **Mid-century modern enthusiasts** on a budget who want real wood, not veneer
- **Anyone tired of replacing particleboard furniture** — this will outlast three cheap dressers

**Skip it if** you hate assembly, want soft-close drawers, or need a dresser under 30 inches tall for a low-profile bed setup.

**FAQ**

**Q: Is this real wood or MDF?**  
A: Solid pine for the frame, drawer fronts, and legs. Drawer bottoms are plywood, which is standard and acceptable. No particleboard anywhere.

**Q: How long does assembly actually take?**  
A: Plan for 2–3 hours with two people. The hardest part is aligning the drawer slides — take your time and use the included alignment holes.

**Q: Can I mount this to the wall for safety?**  
A: Yes, but the kit doesn't include a strap. Buy a universal anti-tip anchor for about $10 and secure it through the back panel into a stud.

**The Verdict**

The Walker Edison Modern 6-Drawer delivers genuine solid wood construction at a price that undercuts big-box alternatives. The assembly pain is real, but once it's together, this is a dresser that will serve you for a decade.  

**Buy it if** you want real wood storage without paying boutique prices.  
**Skip it if** you have zero patience for multi-hour assembly or need soft-close hardware.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting half a star for the grueling assembly and another fraction for the missing anti-tip hardware. The wood quality and storage depth earn it back.
