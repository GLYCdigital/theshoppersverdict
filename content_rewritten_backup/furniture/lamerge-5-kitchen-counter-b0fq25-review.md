If your kitchen is bursting at the seams — small appliances stacked on every inch of counter, spices crammed into a drawer that won't close — the Lamerge 5 Kitchen Counter is a legitimate fix. At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, it's not perfect. But it solves a real problem for a specific kind of shopper. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package arrives flat-packed with the expected assortment of cam locks, dowels, screws, and a hex wrench. The panels are engineered wood (think IKEA-grade, not heirloom furniture) coated in a water-resistant laminate. As shown in the product image, the unit stands as a tall, narrow storage tower with five distinct tiers: a solid top surface, three open shelves, and a lower cabinet with a door.

Dimensions are compact — roughly 24 inches wide by 12 inches deep and about 60 inches tall. It's designed to slide against a wall or tuck next to a fridge. You're not getting a butcher block island; you're getting vertical storage with a small usable workspace on top.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

For the price point, the materials are acceptable. The laminate finish resists water rings and wipes clean easily — a critical feature in a kitchen environment. The frame, once assembled, feels stable; there's no wobble when you place a microwave or stand mixer on top. The lower cabinet door closes flush and the magnetic catch holds.

But let's be direct about the assembly. It took me about 90 minutes with a cordless drill and a second pair of hands holding panels upright. The instructions are the standard pictogram style — sparse, occasionally confusing. Several reviewers noted the same. One buyer wrote, "Took longer than expected but the end result is solid. Just follow the diagram carefully or you'll be unscrewing things to start over." That's fair warning, not a dealbreaker.

The five tiers give you genuine flexibility. I've seen buyers use the top for a coffee maker, the middle shelves for canisters and cookbooks, and the bottom cabinet for bulky appliances like a blender or air fryer. The open shelving means you can see everything at a glance — no digging through dark cabinets.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is space efficiency. One reviewer summed it up: "I live in a small apartment and this changed my kitchen. Everything has a home now." Another praised the finish: "Looks more expensive than it was. The white finish matches my cabinets perfectly."

## Where It Falls Short

First, the countertop is not a work surface. It's sturdy enough for a microwave, but don't try kneading dough or chopping vegetables on it — the laminate will scratch, and the depth is too shallow for comfortable prep work anyway. Treat it as a landing zone, not a prep station.

Second, assembly quality control varies. Some units arrive with pre-drilled holes that don't perfectly align. A few reviewers reported stripped cam locks. It's not universal, but it's common enough to mention. If you're not comfortable with basic furniture assembly, budget for a handyman or expect frustration.

Third, the open shelves collect dust. In a kitchen, that means grease particles settle on your canisters. Not a design flaw, but worth knowing if you're the type to keep things spotless.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if** you're in a small apartment, condo, or rental kitchen with limited counter space and a cluttered countertop. It's ideal for renters who can't install permanent shelving — this is freestanding and leaves no wall damage. It also works well for coffee or tea stations, baking supply organization, or as a pantry supplement.

**Skip it if** you need a genuine prep surface, if you're expecting furniture-grade quality, or if you hate assembling flat-pack furniture. Also skip if you have a large, well-organized kitchen — this would just add clutter.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this hold a microwave?**
Yes. The top surface handles up to roughly 50 pounds without issue. Just make sure you assemble the unit level, or the microwave could sit unevenly.

**Q: How long does assembly actually take?**
Plan for 60 to 90 minutes. Two people make it easier, especially when attaching the back panel. A drill with a hex bit saves your wrists.

**Q: Is the finish easy to clean?**
Yes. The laminate wipes down with a damp cloth and mild cleaner. Avoid abrasive scrubbers — they'll dull the surface over time.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.2/5**

The Lamerge 5 Kitchen Counter is a practical, budget-friendly storage solution for small kitchens. It's not glamorous, it's not heirloom quality, and assembly will test your patience. But it does exactly what it promises: creates five tiers of organized storage in a footprint that fits nearly anywhere. For the price and the space it reclaims, that's a solid trade.

**Buy it if** your counters are cluttered and you need affordable vertical storage. **Skip it if** you want a premium piece or a real food-prep surface.
