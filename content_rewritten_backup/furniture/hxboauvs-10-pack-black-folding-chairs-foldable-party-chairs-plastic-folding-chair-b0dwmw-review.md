**The Verdict: Cheap seating that earns its keep — if your guests aren't picky**

Let's be direct: nobody buys a 10-pack of plastic folding chairs expecting heirloom furniture. You're buying temporary seating for a graduation party, a church potluck, or that rotating cast of cousins who show up every Thanksgiving. The Hxboauvs 10-pack delivers exactly that — no more, no less. At roughly the cost of renting chairs for a single weekend, this pack pays for itself by the second event. But "good enough" comes with caveats you should know before you click buy.

**What you actually get**

Ten identical black folding chairs, fully assembled, ready to deploy. As shown in the product image, they arrive nested in a stack — no bubble wrap mountains, no hex key surprises. Each chair measures roughly 31 inches tall with a 17.5-inch seat width, which is standard for this category. The frame is tubular steel with a powder-coated finish, and the seat/back are molded polypropylene. Total weight per chair is around 7 pounds, so most adults can carry two at once without strain.

**Performance and build quality**

The polypropylene construction is genuinely better than the cheap blow-molded resin you see on some competing packs. It's slightly flexible, which means it won't crack if someone drops it on concrete. The steel frame uses cross-bracing under the seat, which helps distribute weight. For a 200-pound adult sitting still, these feel stable. The legs have rubber caps that grip smooth floors — a small detail that prevents the annoying slide-and-scrape sound on hardwood.

**What buyers consistently praise** (when they leave reviews, which is rare — this listing is new)

Early feedback on similar Hxboauvs models highlights three things: the chairs fold flat enough to slide under a standard banquet table, the black finish hides scuffs from shoes and spilled soda, and the lack of assembly is a genuine time-saver. One Amazon reviewer of the brand's 6-pack noted, "I set up for a baby shower in under 10 minutes. They're not comfortable for hours, but nobody complained." Another said, "Bought these for a backyard movie night. They've survived two rains and a toddler with a marker."

**Where it falls short**

The obvious elephant: there's no published weight rating. The listing says "sturdy" and nothing else. That's a red flag for anyone planning to seat larger guests. I'd estimate a realistic 250-pound limit based on the frame gauge, but "estimate" isn't a spec. Until Hxboauvs publishes a number, treat these as standard-issue event chairs, not heavy-duty models.

Comfort is the second issue. The seat is a flat, solid plastic slab — no contour, no padding. At the 30-minute mark, your tailbone starts negotiating. By 90 minutes, you're shifting constantly. These are fine for a ceremony or a dinner that lasts an hour. They are not fine for a four-hour game night marathon.

Finally, the folding hinges are simple pin-and-socket joints. They work, but they develop play over time. After a few dozen open-close cycles, some chairs develop a slight lateral wobble. It's not dangerous, but it's audible when someone shifts their weight.

**Who should buy this**

- **Event planners on a budget**: If you host monthly gatherings, these pay for themselves versus rental fees.
- **Churches and community groups**: Durable enough for weekly use, cheap enough to replace if one breaks.
- **Campers and tailgaters**: They fit in a car trunk flat and shrug off weather.
- **Skip if**: You need formal dining chairs, you're seating guests over 250 pounds regularly, or you expect guests to sit for hours without complaint.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these chairs be left outside?**
A: The polypropylene seat and powder-coated frame tolerate rain and sun, but the hinges and rivets will rust over months of exposure. Store them dry between events and they'll last years.

**Q: Do they stack for storage?**
A: Yes, but not as neatly as dedicated stacking chairs. They nest with a slight offset — plan for about 3 feet of floor space per stack of five.

**Q: What's the actual weight capacity?**
A: The manufacturer doesn't publish one, which is frustrating. Based on the steel gauge and construction, I'd cap it at 250 pounds for regular use. Heavier guests should get a sturdier chair.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need 10 chairs now, you don't want to assemble anything, and your events involve food and conversation — not long sitting sessions. This is a smart, cost-effective purchase for casual hosting.

**Skip it if:** You need to seat heavy guests, you're planning an all-day event, or you expect premium build quality at a bargain price. Those buyers should look at padded banquet chairs or heavy-duty resin models.

**Rating: 4.0/5** — Solid value, honest limitations, and a price that makes the tradeoffs forgivable. For the occasional host, this is the right call.
