**A Solid Pine Bunk That Rewards Patience (and Punishes Impatience)**

Bunk beds are a rite of passage. The problem? Most affordable options are made of particle board that sags, squeaks, and disintegrates by the time the second kid outgrows them. The Merax Bunk Bed with Drawers takes a different route — actual solid pine, real drawers, and a price point that undercuts most solid-wood competitors. But as the 3.9-star average across 195 ratings hints, there's a catch. It's not the wood. It's the build.

**What Shows Up at Your Door**

The box is heavy — expect around 100 pounds of pine, slats, and hardware. You get the full twin-over-twin frame, two pull-out drawers for the bottom bunk, a ladder, full-length guardrails for the top, and the necessary hardware. No mattress is included, so budget for two twin mattresses separately. The wood arrives sanded smooth, which one buyer specifically called out: *"Surprisingly nice finish for the price — no rough edges anywhere on the frame."*

**Real Wood, Real Weight, Real Stability**

Once assembled, this thing is a tank. The pine is thick — not the flimsy 1x2 slats you see on cheaper bunk beds. The frame uses interlocking joints that, when properly tightened, eliminate the side-to-side sway that plagues metal or particle-board alternatives. The ladder attaches flush to the frame, and buyers consistently report it doesn't shift or twist during use. One verified parent noted: *"My 8-year-old climbs up and down fifty times a day. It hasn't budged in six months."*

The drawers are the standout feature. They roll on plastic glides and tuck neatly under the bottom bunk, giving you storage for bedding, toys, or clothes without eating into the room's footprint. On a full-size bed, that's a meaningful win.

**Where the Merax Bunk Falls Short**

Assembly is the elephant in the room. Multiple reviewers report the instructions are minimal — some say they're practically nonexistent — with parts that aren't labeled. Expect a 4-hour build if you're handy, 6+ if you're not. One frustrated buyer wrote: *"The instructions were a joke. I had to watch a YouTube video to figure out the drawer rail placement."* If you don't get the frame perfectly square during assembly, the drawers can bind and stick.

The weight capacity caps at 300 pounds per bunk. That's fine for kids and most pre-teens, but it rules out adults bunking with kids or heavier teens sharing a room. A few reviewers also noted the top bunk's guardrail, while full-length, sits slightly low for very young children — a bed rail add-on might be worth considering for toddlers.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Parents with two kids sharing a room** who want real wood that survives daily climbing, jumping, and general mayhem
- **Families tight on space** who need the drawers to maximize a small bedroom
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who understand that "solid pine at this price" means accepting a weekend of assembly

**Skip It If**

- You hate assembling furniture and won't be patient with vague instructions
- You need adult-level weight capacity or want the option of a trundle bed instead of drawers
- You expect soft-close drawers or premium hardware — this is functional, not fancy

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the Merax bunk bed fit standard twin mattresses?**
A: Yes — both bunks take standard US twin mattresses (38x75 inches). Just keep mattress thickness under 8 inches for the top bunk so the guardrail remains effective.

**Q: Can this be separated into two standalone beds?**
A: No. This is a fixed bunk design. If you want the flexibility to split beds later, look for a modular twin-over-twin with a detachable frame.

**Q: How much weight can the drawers hold?**
A: They're designed for bedding, clothes, and toys — roughly 30-40 pounds per drawer. They're not built for heavy books or tools, and overloading will make them stick.

**The Verdict: 3.7/5 — Buy It If You're Ready to Build**

**Buy it if** you want genuine solid pine, integrated storage, and a stable frame that outlasts cheap alternatives — and you're willing to spend a Saturday assembling it. **Skip it if** you're not prepared for a challenging build, need higher weight capacity, or want premium drawer mechanics.

At this price point, the Merax delivers real wood where competitors offer compressed sawdust. The tradeoff is your time and patience. For families who can handle that, it's a solid investment that'll survive until the kids outgrow it.
