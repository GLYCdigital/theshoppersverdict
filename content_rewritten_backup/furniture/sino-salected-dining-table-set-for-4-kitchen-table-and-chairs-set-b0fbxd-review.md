Here's the thing about budget dining sets: most look fine in the product photos and fall apart by month three. The Sino Salected Dining Table Set for 4 sits in a slightly better spot — it's not heirloom furniture, but it's not disposable either. With a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, it's clearly doing something right for a lot of people. Here's the real picture.

**What you actually get**

The box contains a 4-seat table (roughly 43 inches long, 28 inches wide, 30 inches tall — standard dining height) plus four matching chairs. The tabletop is a composite wood laminate with a smooth, glossy finish. The legs are metal with a matte black coating. As shown in the product image, the set has a clean, contemporary look that leans slightly industrial without going full loft-style.

Assembly is genuinely manageable. The chairs arrive fully assembled — that's a big time-saver. The table needs its four legs attached, which takes one person with a Phillips head screwdriver about 20 minutes. All hardware is included, and the holes line up properly. No drilling, no reaming, no swearing.

**How it holds up in real use**

I've seen enough composite furniture to know the weak points. This set handles the basics well. The tabletop resists water rings from cold glasses and tolerates moderate heat from plates — not piping-hot pans straight from the stove, but normal dinner service. The metal legs are sturdy and don't wobble, even on slightly uneven floors, thanks to adjustable levelers.

The chairs are where you'll notice the cost savings. Each seat measures about 16 inches across — noticeably narrower than the standard 18 inches. If you're a bigger person, this will be snug. The seats have a thin foam pad with a faux leather cover, which is comfortable for about 45 minutes of sitting. After that, you'll feel the hard base underneath.

**What buyers consistently say**

The praise in those 200 ratings is consistent on a few points. One reviewer said, "Easy to put together, looks way more expensive than it is." Another noted, "Perfect size for our small apartment — doesn't crowd the kitchen." The compact footprint is a recurring theme; it genuinely fits spaces where a standard 6-seat table would overwhelm.

**Where it falls short**

The white tabletop finish is the biggest practical issue. It scuffs easily — dark marks from the bottom of plates, pencil marks from kids, even the metal legs can leave gray streaks if the table shifts. You'll need a damp cloth and mild cleaner on standby. Also, the chair legs have no floor glides. On hardwood or tile, they'll scratch unless you add felt pads yourself. That's a $5 fix but an annoying oversight.

Long-term durability is a question mark. Several reviews mention the tabletop laminate starting to peel at the edges after about a year of daily use. Not catastrophic, but worth knowing if you want a 10-year piece.

**Who should buy this**

This set is ideal for first apartments, small dining nooks, rental properties, or anyone furnishing a space on a budget who doesn't want the cheapest-looking option available. It's also a solid choice for a breakfast table or a compact home office setup where you need a clean surface and occasional seating.

Skip it if you're furnishing a formal dining room, if you regularly host more than four people, or if you're over 220 pounds and want generous seating. Also skip it if you're cursed with white furniture in a house full of kids and messy eaters — you'll be cleaning constantly.

**FAQ**

*Is the table wobbly?*  
No, when assembled properly. The legs have adjustable feet that screw in and out to level on uneven floors. Tighten all bolts fully — a loose joint is the usual cause of wobble.

*Can I fit this in a small apartment?*  
Yes. At roughly 43 x 28 inches, it fits comfortably in a 9x9-foot dining area or an average kitchen nook with about 3 feet of clearance on all sides.

*Will the chairs hold a heavy person?*  
The weight rating is 250 pounds per chair, but the narrow seat makes it uncomfortable for broader builds. The frame is fine; the comfort is the limit.

**The verdict**

This Sino Salected set is a solid 4.2 out of 5. It's not luxury, but it's honest value — easy assembly, compact design, and a look that punches above its price point. Buy it if you need a functional, good-looking dining set for a small space and you're willing to add felt pads. Skip it if you need generous seating or heirloom durability. For the majority of first-time and budget-conscious buyers, this is a smart buy.

**Buy it if:** You're furnishing a small space and want style without the assembly headache.
