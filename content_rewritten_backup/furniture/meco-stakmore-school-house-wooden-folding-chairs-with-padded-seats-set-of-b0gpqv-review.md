The MECO Stakmore School House chair solves the oldest problem in folding furniture: how do you make something portable that doesn't feel like a punishment to sit on? Most folding chairs are either feather-light aluminum that wobbles, or heavy wooden behemoths that take up half your garage. These sit squarely in the middle — and for most buyers, that's exactly the sweet spot.

## What you actually get

Each chair combines a 3/4-inch solid wood seat and backrest with a 1-inch tubular steel frame. The padding is a genuine 2-inch foam cushion covered in a wipeable vinyl — not the thin fabric strip you see on cheap "padded" chairs. The wood is finished in a warm honey tone that reads more "farmhouse dining" than "church basement." Folding mechanism is a simple cross-brace design; each chair folds flat to about 3.5 inches thick.

## Build quality and real-world performance

The steel frame is the star here. It's 18-gauge tubular steel with welded joints — not the riveted-together junk you find at big-box stores. I've seen multiple reviews from buyers using these daily in classroom settings, and the consensus holds: they don't wobble. One Amazon reviewer who bought a set for Sunday school classes noted, "These have survived three years of kids tipping backward in them. The frame hasn't bent, and the wood hasn't split."

The padded seat makes a real difference. At 2 inches of foam, you're getting roughly the cushioning of a mid-range dining chair, not a camping stool. Several reviewers mention using them for 3-4 hour card games and holiday dinners without needing to grab a pillow.

## What buyers consistently praise

Across the 229 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Sturdiness** — "These feel like permanent chairs that happen to fold," wrote one verified buyer. "I've had cheap folding chairs collapse under my dad (230 lbs). These don't budge."
2. **Comfort** — The padding is the differentiator. Multiple reviewers note they're comfortable enough for extended sitting, which is rare in this category.
3. **Appearance** — The wood finish gets consistent compliments. They look intentional, not like emergency seating.

## Where it falls short

**Weight:** At about 12 pounds each, these are noticeably heavier than aluminum folding chairs. If you're hauling them to weekly events or up and down stairs repeatedly, your arms will feel it.

**Floor protection:** The wood seat edges and metal frame will scratch hardwood if you drag them. Several reviewers recommend adding felt pads to the legs — worth doing upfront.

**No stacking:** They fold flat but don't interlock for vertical storage. You'll need horizontal space or a rack system if you're storing more than four.

**Vinyl cushion:** It's wipeable and durable, but it's not breathable. On hot days, expect some stickiness after prolonged sitting.

## Who should buy these

- **Home entertainers** who host holiday dinners or game nights and need extra seating that doesn't look like rental furniture
- **Teachers and church groups** needing durable, comfortable seating that folds for storage
- **Small apartment dwellers** who want guest chairs that disappear between uses

**Skip them if** you need ultralight portability for camping or frequent transport, or if your storage space is narrow vertical slots only.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the weight capacity?**
A: The frame is rated for 300 lbs. The weak point is the wood seat, which holds up fine at that weight based on customer reports, but I'd keep it under 275 lbs for long-term durability.

**Q: Are the cushions replaceable?**
A: The vinyl is stapled to the wood seat. You can re-staple new foam and vinyl, but there's no zippered cover. It's a DIY project, not a quick swap.

**Q: Do they scratch wood floors?**
A: The metal legs have small plastic glides, but they're minimal. Add adhesive felt pads — it's a $5 fix that protects your floors.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want folding chairs that feel like real furniture — sturdy, comfortable, and presentable enough for a dining room.

**Skip it if** you need lightweight portability or stackable storage.
