**Is this narrow end table with a charging station actually worth your money — or just another farmhouse-style trend piece that underdelivers? I dug through 220 ratings and the specs to find out.**

Let's be blunt about what the affeivul Fluted Narrow End Table is: a space-saving side table with genuinely useful power built in. At roughly 23.6 inches tall with a 15.7-inch depth, it's designed for the gap between your sofa and wall or that awkward hallway corner where a full side table won't fit. The fluted front panel isn't just decorative — it adds structural rigidity to the drawer face, which matters on a piece this narrow.

**What's actually in the box**

You get the table top, four legs, one drawer with pre-attached slides, the charging station module, and a hardware kit. The charging station is the headline feature: two USB-A ports and one standard AC outlet tucked into a recessed shelf on the back edge. The power cord is 6 feet long, which is adequate for most living room layouts but might be short if your outlet is behind the sofa. Assembly involves attaching the legs and drawer — roughly 30 minutes with a Phillips screwdriver. Nothing here requires special tools.

**Real-world performance**

The fluted detail is the first thing people notice. It's not a cheap stick-on trim — the grooves are routed into the MDF panel and finished consistently. Buyers consistently mention the appearance. One verified purchaser noted, "The fluted design looks way more expensive than the price point. I get compliments every time someone visits." Another mentioned the narrow footprint works perfectly beside a recliner where a standard end table would block the handle.

The charging station functions as advertised — the AC outlet handles a lamp or phone charger without issue, and the USB ports deliver standard 5V/2.1A output. That's fine for phones and tablets, but don't expect fast-charging capabilities. The recessed shelf keeps your device from sliding off, which is a thoughtful touch.

**Where it genuinely falls short**

The charging station location is the biggest design flaw. Because it's mounted on the back edge, cords have to run down the back of the table and across the floor. If your table sits against a wall, you'll see cables snaking up from behind. Several reviewers mentioned this. One wrote: "Love the table, hate the cord situation. I had to zip-tie the cables to the leg to keep them out of sight."

The light oak finish is another consideration. It's a warm, farmhouse-appropriate tone, but it shows water rings and dust quickly. You'll need coasters — this isn't a worry-free surface. The drawer, while smooth-operating, is small: roughly 12 inches wide and 4 inches deep. It'll hold remotes and reading glasses, but not much else.

**Who should buy this**

This table fits three specific buyer profiles. First, anyone with a narrow living room who needs surface space without sacrificing walkway clearance. Second, apartment dwellers who want power access without running extension cords across the room. Third, farmhouse-style decorators looking for an affordable accent piece — the fluted detail delivers that custom look for a fraction of custom furniture prices.

It's not for you if you need substantial storage, prefer dark wood finishes, or expect fast-charging USB-C ports. Also, if your space has outlets behind furniture, the cord management issue will annoy you daily.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the charging station work while the table is unplugged?**
No. The USB ports and AC outlet require the table to be plugged into a wall outlet. There's no battery backup.

**Q: Can you use the drawer and the charging station simultaneously?**
Yes. The charging module is mounted on the back edge, separate from the drawer underneath. No interference.

**Q: How much weight can the top hold?**
The MDF top with four-leg construction handles a 40-pound lamp or decor item comfortably. The drawer maxes out around 10 pounds.

**The verdict**

The affeivul fluted narrow end table earns its 4.4-star rating. The charging station is genuinely useful, the fluted design punches above its price class, and the footprint solves a real space problem. The cord visibility and finish maintenance are annoyances, not dealbreakers.
