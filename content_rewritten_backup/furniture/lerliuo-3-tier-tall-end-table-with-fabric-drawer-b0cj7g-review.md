**Is the Lerliuo 3 Tier Tall End Table the space-saving fix your living room needs?** After digging through 229 ratings and the spec sheet, here’s the real deal.

You’re looking at a 23.6-inch wide, 11.8-inch deep, 25.6-inch tall tower that’s built for one job: holding stuff without eating your floor space. The three-tier layout gives you a fabric drawer up top, then two open shelves below. It’s a classic narrow console approach, but the execution matters.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the table top, steel frame legs, one fabric drawer (the non-woven material is surprisingly sturdy), two open shelf boards, and the hardware bag. No tools included—you’ll need your own Phillips screwdriver. Assembly is straightforward: attach the legs to the top, slide in the shelf boards, snap the drawer onto its rails. Most buyers report 15-25 minutes start to finish. One reviewer noted, *“Took me 20 minutes while watching TV. Everything lined up perfectly.”*

**Build quality and real-world use**

The steel frame is the backbone here—it’s powder-coated matte black and doesn’t wobble. The top is MDF with a wood-grain laminate that looks better than the price suggests. Weight capacity is realistic: the top handles about 22 pounds (a lamp, books, a small plant), each shelf about 11 pounds. The fabric drawer glides on plastic tracks and holds roughly 5 pounds of remotes, chargers, or mail.

The killer feature is the footprint. At under 12 inches deep, it slides into the gap between your sofa and wall, or beside a bed where a nightstand would block the path. That’s the buyer persona this targets: apartment dwellers, dorm residents, anyone with narrow hallways or awkward corners.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The slim profile gets the most love. One verified purchase wrote, *“Finally a table that fits my tiny living room without looking like a toy.”* Another praised the drawer: *“I hide my kid’s crayons and markers in there—out of sight, out of mind.”* The adjustable foot pads also earned mentions for handling slightly uneven floors without rocking.

**Where it falls short**

The fabric drawer is the weakest link. It’s not soft-close, and if you overload it past a few pounds, the tracks can bind—you’ll need to yank it straight to get it moving again. The MDF top is also a maintenance chore: set a sweaty glass down without a coaster and you’ll see a white ring within an hour. And while assembly is easy, the included Allen wrench is cheap—bring your own screwdriver for better leverage.

**Who should buy this**

- **Small-space renters**: If your living room can’t fit a bulky end table, this is your answer.  
- **Bedroom side table alternative**: Tall enough for a lamp and phone, slim enough to not crowd a small bedroom.  
- **Entryway catchall**: Use the drawer for keys and the shelves for shoes or bags.

**Skip it if** you want a wood-veneer piece that doubles as a statement furniture item, or if you plan to store heavy hardcovers in that fabric drawer—it’s not built for that.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can the drawer be removed for cleaning?**  
Yes, it lifts off the rails with a slight upward tug. Wipe with a damp cloth; don’t machine wash.

**Q: Does it wobble on carpet?**  
The foot pads adjust, but on thick shag carpet you’ll still get some sway. On hardwood or low-pile, it’s rock solid.

**Q: What’s the maximum height for items on the shelves?**  
Each shelf has about 8 inches of clearance. A tall plant or wine bottle fits, but won’t slide out easily—you’ll lift it straight up.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need vertical storage in a tight footprint and don’t mind basic drawer mechanics.  
**Skip it if** you demand solid-wood construction or soft-close hardware at this price point.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It’s not a heirloom piece, but for a slim, functional table that hides clutter and fits anywhere, it delivers exactly what it promises. Just buy coasters.
