If you're shopping for a sub-$300 queen mattress, you're probably braced for a compromise: either a slab of dense foam that sleeps hot, or a bouncy innerspring that lacks pressure relief. The Acochey 10-inch hybrid tries to split that difference, and after digging through 215 buyer reviews, here's what actually matters.

**What you're actually getting**

The queen ships compressed in a box roughly 40x20x20 inches. Cut the plastic, unroll it, and you're looking at a 10-inch profile: 2 inches of gel-infused memory foam on top, a comfort layer beneath, and 7 inches of individually wrapped pocket coils as the support core. The cover is a standard knit polyester — nothing premium, but it's removable and washable, which is more than some mattresses twice the price offer.

As shown in the product image, the design is clean and low-profile, no pillow-top bulk. It's built to sit directly on a slatted base or box spring — a solid platform is fine, but skip the old-school box spring with wide gaps or the coils will sag into the openings.

**How it actually sleeps**

The medium-firm feel lands around a 6 out of 10. Memory foam conforms to your hips and shoulders, but the coil layer underneath keeps your spine aligned rather than letting you sink to the frame. Back sleepers — the most common type among reviewers — consistently report waking up without lower back stiffness. Stomach sleepers fare well too, since the firmness prevents the hips from dipping into a swayback position.

Side sleepers are the wildcard. If you're under 150 pounds, the 2-inch foam layer provides decent cushioning, but heavier side sleepers have reported shoulder pressure that requires a mattress topper to fix. That's worth knowing before you buy.

Temperature-wise, the gel infusion in the foam helps marginally. It sleeps cooler than a pure memory foam mattress, but warm sleepers will still notice heat retention through the night. It's not a cooling mattress — it's a "less hot" mattress.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value proposition is the headline. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was skeptical at this price point, but after three months it still feels like the first week."* Multiple buyers compared it favorably to mattresses they'd paid double for at big-box stores. The shipping speed gets consistent mentions — most received it within two days with Amazon Prime, and expansion to full height took under 24 hours.

**Where it falls short**

Edge support is the most common complaint. Sit on the corner and you'll slide toward the floor — the perimeter coils aren't reinforced. Couples who like to sit on the edge to put on socks will feel the give. Motion transfer is the second issue: because of the coil core, you'll feel a partner rolling over more than on an all-foam mattress. Light sleepers sharing a bed should note this.

The off-gassing smell is present but mild — most reviewers said it dissipated within a day, though a few sensitive noses reported it lingering for three days.

**Who should buy this**

- **Back or stomach sleepers on a budget** — this is your sweet spot
- **First-time mattress buyers** furnishing a guest room or apartment
- **Anyone who wants a hybrid feel** (some bounce, some contouring) without paying hybrid prices

**Skip it if** you're a heavy side sleeper needing plush pressure relief, or if you and your partner are both restless movers who need zero motion transfer.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does this need a box spring?**
A: No. It works on slatted bases (slats no more than 3 inches apart), platform beds, or the floor. A box spring is optional and actually not recommended if it has wide gaps.

**Q: Will it sag after a few months?**
A: The 4.3-star average includes buyers at the 6-month mark reporting no significant sagging. The pocket coil core resists compression better than cheap foam alternatives. Just rotate it head-to-foot every 3 months.

**Q: How long does the smell last?**
A: Most buyers report it's gone within 24 hours. Leave the room ventilated during the day and it should be sleepable by night one.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a genuine hybrid — real coils, real memory foam — at a price that undercuts most competitors by 30-40%. It's a solid, honest mattress for back and stomach sleepers who don't need luxury features.

**Skip it if** edge sitting is a daily habit or you're a side sleeper over 180 pounds. Those two issues are the real tradeoffs you're accepting for the price.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A legitimate value that does exactly what it promises, with predictable compromises. For a guest room or budget primary bedroom, this is one of the smarter picks in its category.
