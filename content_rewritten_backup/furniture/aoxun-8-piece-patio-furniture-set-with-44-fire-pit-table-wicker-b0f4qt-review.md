**Is the Aoxun 8-piece patio set the backyard centerpiece you’ve been shopping for, or just another wicker set that’ll fray by fall?** After digging through 204 verified ratings at 4.1 stars, here’s the truth: this set earns its keep for most buyers, but a few quirks could decide if it’s right for you.

## What You Actually Get

This isn’t a two-seater with a side table. The Aoxun ships as a full 8-piece configuration: six wicker chairs (two with matching ottomans), and the star of the show — a 44-inch round fire pit table. The table includes a 58,000 BTU propane burner, a protective lid, and a glass wind guard. The set’s footprint is roughly 10 feet across when fully arranged, so measure your patio before pulling the trigger. You also get six seat cushions and two back cushions, all wrapped in a fade-resistant polyester blend.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The frame is powder-coated steel, which is the right call at this price — it won’t rust through in one season like some aluminum-thin competitors. The PE rattan wicker is hand-woven and feels denser than what you’d find on a $400 budget set. As shown in the product image, the aesthetic leans modern-classic: dark brown wicker, neutral beige cushions, and a sleek black fire table. It looks more expensive than it is, which is a consistent point in buyer feedback.

The fire pit is the differentiator. It’s a real propane unit with push-button ignition — no matches, no fumbling. The 44-inch table surface easily holds drinks and plates when the fire is off. That dual-use capability is why most reviewers rate it four stars or higher. One buyer noted, *“We use it as a dining table during the day and a fire pit at night — it’s the best purchase we’ve made for our deck.”* The heat output is adequate for shoulder-season evenings, but don’t expect to sit around it in January. It’s an ambiance piece first, a heater second.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest friction point. Multiple reviewers report the instructions are “minimal” and the bolt holes on the table base don’t always align perfectly. Plan for a full afternoon with a socket wrench and a second person. This isn’t a 30-minute snap-together job.

The cushions are firmer than most — some buyers love that for lumbar support, others find them “unforgiving” after an hour. They’re also water-resistant, not waterproof. If your patio is uncovered, buy the Aoxun cover (sold separately) or bring cushions inside during heavy rain. Finally, the fire pit’s 58,000 BTU output is modest; on a windy night, you’ll feel the draft. A few reviewers also mentioned the glass wind guard fogs up the flame view — a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker.

## Who Should Buy This

- **The entertainer** who hosts six or more and wants a conversation pit that transitions from dinner to s’mores. This is your set.
- **The value shopper** comparing against $1,800+ brands like Costway or Sunjoy. Aoxun undercuts them on price while matching build quality for most use cases.
- **The DIY-averse** should look elsewhere — or budget a Saturday for assembly.

## FAQ: What Shoppers Actually Ask

**Q: Does the fire pit come with a propane tank?**
A: No. You supply your own standard 20-pound propane tank. The hose and regulator are included, and the tank hides neatly inside the table’s lower shelf.

**Q: Can the chairs be left outside year-round?**
A: The wicker and frame can handle full sun and rain, but the cushions will degrade faster without covers. In snowy climates, store the cushions indoors; the frame can stay out.

**Q: How many people fit realistically?**
A: Six chairs plus two ottomans — but it’s snug with six adults. Four to five is the sweet spot for comfort, especially with the ottomans pulled in as footrests.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a versatile, good-looking conversation set that does double duty as a dining table — and you’re willing to spend an afternoon with a wrench. **Skip it if** you need serious heating power, want plush seating, or despise assembly. At a 4.2/5, this is a solid value for the price, with the fire pit being the clear reason to choose it over a plain dining set.
