If you're shopping for a TV stand that can actually hold a 65-inch television without wobbling like a card table, the St.Mandyu LED TV Stand deserves a hard look. At 4.4 stars from 220 ratings, it's not perfect — but it's priced and built for people who want function over flash. Here's the real story.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main console top, two side panels, two tempered glass shelves, a back panel, and all the hardware. The advertised dimensions are roughly 59 inches wide, 15.7 inches deep, and 19.7 inches tall. That depth is the key number — it's shallow enough to fit against most walls but deep enough to hold a soundbar in front of your TV without it hanging off the edge.

The top shelf is rated for 110 pounds, which covers most 65-inch and even some 70-inch LED panels. Each glass shelf handles 22 pounds. You get two open compartments plus an open center section — no doors, which means no hiding clutter, but also no squeaky hinges to fail later.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The frame is composite wood with a laminated finish meant to mimic wood grain. At this price point, you're not getting solid oak, and the finish shows it if you look closely — the laminate has a slight sheen that reads more "budget" than "premium" in bright lighting. That said, it's sturdy. The legs are wide-set, and once assembled, the unit doesn't shift when you bump it.

Assembly takes most people 45 to 60 minutes. The instructions are mostly diagrams, and the hardware is labeled. One buyer noted, "Took about an hour with a cordless drill — the holes lined up perfectly, which surprised me for the price." Another mentioned, "The tempered glass shelves feel solid, not flimsy like some cheap stands I've owned."

The LED lighting is the standout feature — it's not just a gimmick. The strip runs along the bottom edge and offers multiple color options and brightness levels via a remote. It adds a subtle glow behind your components. Several reviewers specifically mentioned using it as ambient bias lighting while watching movies. It's a nice touch you don't usually get at this price.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring themes in positive reviews are stability and value. One verified purchase said, "Holds my 65-inch TCL with room to spare. Solid, looks good, and the lights are a bonus." Another noted, "For the price, this is way better than the cheap pressboard stands at big-box stores."

## Where It Falls Short

The height is the biggest compromise. At 19.7 inches, this stand sits lower than many competitors that run 22-24 inches tall. If your seating is standard or elevated, your TV's center line will sit below eye level. You'll be looking down slightly during movies — noticeable after an hour.

The back panel is thin. It's meant for cable management, but the cutouts are small, and the panel flexes if you press too hard while routing wires. One reviewer complained, "The back board is flimsy — I had to reinforce it with a bracket to feel comfortable." That's an edge case, but worth knowing if you have a lot of components.

The glass shelves show dust and fingerprints quickly. If you have kids or pets, expect to wipe them down weekly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who need a compact, functional stand that doesn't dominate a small living room.
- **Budget-conscious shoppers** who want LED accent lighting without paying premium AV-furniture prices.
- **Gamers or movie buffs** with a 60-70 inch TV who want a stable base and don't mind the lower height.

Skip it if you need a tall stand for a high bed or recliner setup, or if you want real wood construction that will last 15 years.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this hold a 70-inch TV?**
Yes, if the TV weighs under 110 pounds. Most 70-inch LED panels fall between 50-80 pounds. Check your TV's weight before buying — the stand's top shelf is rated for 110 lbs, but don't push it with a heavy older plasma.

**Q: Is the LED light strip replaceable?**
The remote controls it, but the strip is integrated into the stand's frame. If the LEDs fail, you can't swap them individually — you'd need to replace the whole strip, which requires disassembly.

**Q: Does it come with mounting hardware for the TV?**
No. This is a stand, not a wall mount. You place the TV on top. If you need to secure the TV to the stand to prevent tipping (recommended with kids), you'll need a separate anti-tip strap kit.
