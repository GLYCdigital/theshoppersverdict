A 3.1-star average on Amazon with 155 ratings tells you something immediately: this COLAMY counter stool set is a gamble. At roughly $80–$100 for three stools (pricing fluctuates), you're paying bargain-bin prices for what looks like a solid wood-and-faux-leather set. The real question is whether the savings justify the compromises. I've dug through the buyer feedback, and here's what you actually need to know before clicking add to cart.

**What you get in the box**

Three unassembled stools with wooden legs, a padded faux leather seat, and the necessary hex wrench and bolts. Assembly takes about 20 minutes per stool if you're methodical — the instructions are basic line drawings, but the parts are straightforward enough. The wooden legs have a dark espresso finish that photographs well, and the seat cushion measures roughly 15 inches across. As shown in the product image, these look like a clean, modern addition to a breakfast bar. The reality of that appearance is where things get complicated.

**Performance and build quality**

The stools stand at a genuine 24-inch seat height, which matches standard 36-inch kitchen counters. If you have a bar-height island (42 inches), these will leave you reaching for your food — measure your counter before ordering. The frame uses solid rubberwood legs, which is a legitimate plus at this price point. However, multiple verified purchasers report the crossbar screws stripping after a few months of use. One reviewer noted, "Legs got wobbly after about three months, and retightening only helped for a week." That's the pattern: the wood holds up, but the hardware connecting it doesn't.

The faux leather seat is a mixed bag. It's genuinely easy to clean — a damp cloth handles spills — and the 2-inch foam padding is comfortable for a 30-minute breakfast. But the material is thin. Several long-term reviews describe peeling at the front edge of the seat where thighs press during daily use. If you're buying these for a busy family kitchen, expect visible wear within the first year.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The value equation is the main draw. Buyers who use these in guest spaces, spare rooms, or as occasional seating are largely satisfied. One reviewer summarized it well: "For the price, these look way more expensive than they are. I wouldn't sit on them every day, but for guests they're perfect." The easy-to-clean surface and the aesthetic appeal get consistent mentions. If you need temporary or low-usage seating, these deliver.

**Where it falls short**

Stability is the recurring complaint. The footrest sits too low for most adults — at roughly 6 inches off the floor, taller users report their knees hit the underside of the counter. The wobble issue is the bigger dealbreaker. The stools don't tip, but they sway enough to feel insecure when you lean forward. For a product marketed for daily kitchen use, that's a significant flaw. Assembly quality matters too: if you over-tighten the bolts, you can strip the threads in the wood, and there's no replacement hardware included.

**Who should buy this**

These stools fit two specific scenarios: budget-conscious shoppers furnishing a rarely-used breakfast nook, or anyone needing temporary seating for a rental or guest area. They are not suited for daily family use, heavier individuals (the weight capacity is 250 pounds per stool), or anyone who expects furniture to last beyond a couple of years.

**FAQ**

*Are these stools bar height or counter height?*  
Counter height. The seat measures 24 inches from the floor, designed for 36-inch counters. For 42-inch bar-height surfaces, look elsewhere.

*How much weight can they hold?*  
The manufacturer lists a 250-pound capacity per stool. Buyers near that limit report more pronounced wobble, so factor that in.

*Can you replace the seat covers if they peel?*  
No. The faux leather is glued and stapled to the seat base. Replacement means reupholstering, which costs more than the stool is worth.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need affordable, good-looking stools for occasional use and understand they're disposable furniture with a 12–18 month lifespan. **Skip it if** these will see daily use, you're over 200 pounds, or you expect stability comparable to $150-per-stool options. At 3.3/5, this is a "fine for the price, nothing more" product. The counter-height sizing is correct, the look is solid, but the structural integrity issues are real. For a few dollars more per stool, you can find sturdier options with better hardware — and that's worth it if you plan to actually sit on these.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A budget-friendly visual win with durability tradeoffs you need to accept upfront.
