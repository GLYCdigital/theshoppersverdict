**The chair that lets you sit like a pretzel — without the back pain**

You've seen the Instagram ads: someone curled up cross-legged in a "criss cross" office chair, typing away like they're meditating at a standing desk. The Kassipo Criss Cross Legged Office Chair is that chair — and after 215 Amazon ratings averaging 4.3 stars, it's clear this isn't just a gimmick.

The headline feature is the flip-up arms. Push a button, and both armrests swing up and out of the way, opening a roughly 20-inch gap. That's enough room to tuck your legs up, sit Indian-style, or even straddle the chair backward like a drummer. It's a genuinely useful feature for people who hate traditional chair arms digging into their hips.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the chair base, five caster wheels, the gas lift cylinder, the seat pan with the memory foam cushion already attached, the backrest, both flip-up armrests, and the hardware kit with an Allen wrench. The upholstery is a breathable mesh back with a padded faux leather seat — a combo that keeps your back cool while giving your rear some squish. The frame is a welded steel base rated for 250 pounds. Assembly runs about 25 minutes; the trickiest part is getting the armrest brackets aligned with the pre-drilled holes. Take your time with that step.

**Build quality and real-world performance**

The memory foam seat is the standout. One buyer put it plainly: *"I've had the chair for three months and sit in it 8 hours a day for work. The cushion hasn't flattened out at all. My tailbone doesn't hurt anymore."* That tracks with the 4.3-star average — comfort complaints are rare in the review breakdown.

The caster wheels are smooth and quiet. On hardwood, they glide without rattling. On a low-pile office rug, they still roll fine, though you'll feel a bit more drag. The gas lift adjusts smoothly from about 17 to 21 inches in seat height. The backrest tilts with a simple tension knob, but don't expect a full recline — this is a task chair, not a lounger.

**Where it falls short**

The seat depth is 19.5 inches. If you're over 6 feet tall with long femurs, your thighs won't get full support. You'll feel the front edge of the seat pressing into the back of your knees. It's not a dealbreaker, but it's a real limitation.

The flip-up arms, while clever, are a bit of a compromise when they're down. They're padded but narrow — about 2.5 inches wide — and they don't adjust forward or back. If you're broad-shouldered, you might find them slightly too close together. The mechanism itself feels solid, but the plastic release button is a little stiff for the first week.

**Who should buy this**

This chair is perfect for three specific people: the WFH worker who loves sitting cross-legged (you know who you are), the petite-to-average-height user (5'2" to 5'10") who wants a compact chair that doesn't dwarf a small desk, and anyone who plays guitar or does crafts at a desk and needs arms out of the way. Skip it if you're tall, if you need lumbar support that actually moves, or if you want a chair that reclines past 15 degrees.

**FAQ**

**Is the seat actually comfortable for 8-hour days?**
Yes, for most people. The memory foam is dense, not marshmallow-soft, and the mesh back keeps air flowing. Multiple reviewers report full workdays without numbness or backaches. The exception is taller users who run out of thigh support.

**Can the arms really flip up while you're sitting?**
Once you're seated, you can reach down and flip each arm up with one hand. The latch releases smoothly after the initial break-in period. You can also flip them up before you sit, which is easier.

**Will it fit under a standard 30-inch desk?**
With the arms down, the armrest height is about 26 inches at the lowest seat setting — that clears most standard desks. With arms flipped up, you have full clearance to push the chair completely under.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want a comfortable, affordable chair that lets you sit however you want — cross-legged, legs tucked, or traditional. It's a solid value for the flip-arm feature alone.

**Skip it if:** You're over 6 feet tall, need adjustable lumbar support, or spend your day leaning back in a reclined position.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely useful twist on the standard task chair, held back only by size limitations and a slightly finicky assembly process. At this price point, it's hard to beat for the flexibility it offers.
