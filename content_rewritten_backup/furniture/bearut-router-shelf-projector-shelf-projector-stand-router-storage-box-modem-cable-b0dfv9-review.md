**The BEARUT Router Shelf solves a problem you didn't know you had — until you've tripped over a router one too many times.**

This isn't a glamorous purchase. It's a "get this thing off my floor" purchase. And honestly, that's exactly what it should be. The BEARUT Router Shelf Projector Shelf is a dual-tier metal shelf designed to mount under a desk, on a wall, or beside your entertainment center — holding both your router and projector (or modem and cable box) while hiding the cable spaghetti behind them.

Here's the real breakdown after digging through 225 ratings and what buyers actually say.

---

## What You Actually Get

The box contains two powder-coated steel shelves (one for the router, one for the projector), mounting brackets, screws, and — notably — **no drywall anchors**. That last point matters, and I'll come back to it.

The shelves measure roughly 12.5 inches wide by 8 inches deep, with about 7 inches of vertical clearance between tiers. Each tier is rated for **15 pounds**, which covers the vast majority of routers, modems, and mini projectors on the market. The black matte finish looks clean and doesn't scream "utility rack" — it blends into a media setup reasonably well.

## Performance and Build Quality

The steel is thicker than I expected at this price point. There's no flex when you load it up, and the powder coating resists scratching during installation. The cable management channels run along the back edge of each shelf — small slots that let you feed power and Ethernet cables through rather than letting them dangle.

Buyers consistently mention the space savings. One reviewer wrote:
> *"Finally got my router off the bookshelf. Mounted it under my desk and I barely notice it's there. The cable slots actually work — my setup looks 10x cleaner."*

Another noted the versatility:
> *"Using mine to hold a projector in my bedroom. The dual tiers are perfect — projector on top, streaming stick and cables below. Solid for the price."*

Installation is straightforward if you have a stud finder and a drill. The bracket slides onto the mounting plate and locks with a small screw — no need to hold the shelf in place while you fasten it. But here's the catch: the included screws are meant for **wood studs spaced 16 inches apart max**. If you're mounting to drywall without a stud, you'll need to supply your own anchors.

## Where It Falls Short

**The depth is the biggest limitation.** At 8 inches deep, this shelf won't accommodate wide routers with protruding external antennas. A TP-Link Archer or Netgear Nighthawk with angled antennas will overhang — and if you're mounting it under a desk, that overhang can interfere with your legs. Buyers with bulkier equipment have reported returning it for this reason.

**No cable ties or velcro included.** The slots route cables, but they don't secure them. You'll want to grab a small pack of velcro straps to keep everything from sliding back out.

**The 7-inch vertical clearance is tight.** If you're stacking a thick modem on the bottom tier and a router on top, you might find the clearance restrictive. It's fine for standard equipment, but not for stacked units with heat sinks or tall feet.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this hold a full-size projector?**
Yes, up to 15 pounds per shelf. Most home projectors weigh 5–10 pounds, so you're fine. But check the projector's depth — a long-throw projector might overhang the 8-inch shelf.

**Q: Does it work on drywall without studs?**
Only if you supply your own drywall anchors. The included hardware is stud-only. Many buyers use toggle bolts rated for the weight and report success.

**Q: Will it fit under a standard desk?**
If your desk has at least 10 inches of clearance below the surface, yes. The shelf is about 8 inches deep and 7 inches tall, so it clears most standard desks.
