**What You Get**

The box ships with the wood frame, slats, full-length guardrail for the top bunk, and the integrated ladder. As shown in the product image, the ladder attaches directly to the frame's side — not angled outward like many competitors. That's a meaningful space saver in tight rooms. The slatted base means no box spring needed, which cuts setup cost. Expect a few hours of assembly with two people; the integrated ladder design requires precise alignment during installation.

**Build and Real-World Use**

The wood construction feels solid at the joints, and the full-length guardrail is the standout feature. Most bunk beds in this price range use three-quarter rails that leave a gap near the headboard — a genuine hazard for young kids who roll. The Komfott's rail covers the entire top edge, which is the kind of safety detail parents should prioritize. Weight capacity sits around 200 pounds per bunk, adequate for kids and most teens, though heavier adults should stay off the top level.

The integrated ladder has a tradeoff. It saves floor space, but it's fixed — you can't relocate it to the other side after assembly. If your room layout changes, you're disassembling the frame to flip it. Angled ladders let you pivot the bed direction without full teardown. Also, the ladder rungs lack the rubberized grip pads found on pricier models; socks-only climbs could get slippery.
