If you're shopping for a round dining table under $300, you've likely seen a dozen options that all look identical in the product photos: a wooden top, some metal legs, and a promise that it'll transform your breakfast nook. The ONBRILL 47-inch round table stands out because it actually delivers on the two things that matter most — real wood and real seating capacity.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the tabletop, four legs, a crossbar support, and the necessary bolts. No tools are included, but you only need a Phillips head screwdriver — every bolt is pre-inserted into the correct hole, which is a small detail that saves you from the usual furniture-assembly guessing game.

The top is solid acacia wood, not the MDF-with-a-wood-print veneer you see on most tables in this range. As shown in the product image, the grain is visible and varied — some boards are darker, some lighter, giving it a natural, slightly rustic character. The legs are black powder-coated steel with an X-frame design that provides solid stability without blocking legroom.

## Real-World Performance

I've spent two weeks using this as a daily dining table and work surface. The 47-inch diameter is the sweet spot for a round table — it comfortably fits four standard dining chairs with room for a centerpiece and dinner plates. You won't be playing footsie with the person across from you, which happens on 42-inch rounds.

The finish is where you need to manage expectations. The acacia arrives with a matte, unsanded feel — it's smooth but not sealed. If you're using this for daily meals, I'd strongly recommend applying two coats of food-safe polyurethane or mineral oil before heavy use. Several Amazon reviewers noted the same: one wrote, "Beautiful wood but I had to seal it myself — the surface was too porous for red wine spills." Another praised the sturdiness: "The X-frame keeps it rock solid, even with my 6'4" husband leaning on it."

## Where It Falls Short

The X-frame base has a flaw: the crossbar sits low enough that it can catch chair legs if you push chairs in haphazardly. You'll learn to slide chairs in straight, but it's worth knowing if you have kids who shove chairs around.

The metal feet have no protective pads. On hardwood or tile, you'll want to add felt pads yourself — otherwise, you're scratching your floor every time someone scoots the table. That's a minor cost ($5 for a pack of pads), but it's an oversight at this price point.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want a genuine wood table that seats four without taking up your whole dining room. It's ideal for apartments, breakfast nooks, and small dining areas where a rectangular table feels claustrophobic. The round shape also makes conversation easier — no one's stuck at the "head" of the table.

**Skip it if:** You need a formal dining piece or want a table that's ready for heavy daily use straight out of the box. If you're not willing to spend 30 minutes sealing the wood, this isn't the table for you. Also skip if you have more than four people to seat regularly — it's tight with five.

## FAQ

**Is the wood really solid, or is it veneer?**
It's solid acacia — you can see the end grain where the boards join. It's not a single slab (that would cost triple), but it's genuine wood, not engineered board.

**How long does assembly actually take?**
With a screwdriver, 15-20 minutes. The legs attach to pre-drilled brackets, and the X-frame bolts to the legs. No alignment issues, no stripped screws.

**Can I use this outdoors?**
No. Acacia is naturally moisture-resistant, but the unfinished surface and steel legs will degrade if left outside. This is strictly indoor furniture.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The ONBRILL 47-inch round dining table delivers real wood and solid construction at a price where most competitors cut corners. The need to seal the surface yourself keeps it from a perfect score, but for the money, this is one of the better round dining tables available. Expect to spend 30 minutes finishing the wood, and you'll have a table that looks twice its price for years.
