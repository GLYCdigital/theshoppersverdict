Let's be honest: most bed frames under $200 are either flimsy particleboard traps or rattling metal skeletons that wake you up every time you roll over. The Likelalaso 24-inch high bed frame tries to split the difference — and for the most part, it works. Here's what you actually need to know before buying.

**What's in the box**

You get the full steel frame structure, center support legs, rounded corner brackets, floor protectors, and all necessary hardware. The frame arrives in two main sections that connect with locking plates. Notably absent: a headboard. The product images show one, but that's the separate wooden headboard upgrade — the base frame is just the metal platform and rails. Factor that into your total cost before ordering.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The selling point here is the 24-inch clearance. That's genuinely useful. Standard rolling under-bed bins (the 11-gallon clear Sterilite ones) slide underneath with room to spare. If you've been cramming storage into a 12-inch low-profile frame, this alone justifies the upgrade.

The steel rail structure uses a triangular reinforcement design that minimizes flex. At 24 inches, you're above the wobble zone where most cheap high-rise frames get dicey. The rounded corner brackets do what they claim — no more catching your hip on a sharp metal edge when you walk past in the dark. That's a small thing until you've bruised yourself on a square-cornered frame.

Assembly is refreshingly straightforward. Parts are labeled with letters, and the instructions are mostly pictorial. The locking mechanism for the center support uses a cam-style latch — tool-free, which means no stripped bolts from an overtightened screwdriver. Most buyers report 30–45 minutes solo.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The storage height is the recurring theme in positive reviews. One verified buyer put it plainly: "Finally a frame that doesn't make me choose between storage bins and a decent bed height. My 14-inch bins fit perfectly underneath." Another noted the stability: "I was worried about wobble at this height — there isn't any. Even with two people it feels solid."

The rounded corners get frequent mention from families. One reviewer mentioned their toddler running into the frame — the rounded edge meant a bump, not a gash.

**Where it falls short**

The center support bar sits higher than the outer rails. If you're using a standard 9-inch box spring, it won't sit flush — you'll get a slight hump in the middle. The fix is shimming the outer rails, which is annoying but workable. If you're using a platform mattress (no box spring), this isn't an issue.

On carpet, the frame can develop a creak at the joints if the floor protectors aren't perfectly seated. This is a "read the instructions carefully" problem, not a design flaw, but it's a common complaint in the 4-star reviews.

The lack of an included headboard is the biggest value gap. At this price point, many competitors include at least a basic headboard. Here, you're paying extra for what's shown in the marketing photos.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters and apartment dwellers** who need under-bed storage without a bulky platform bed
- **Parents** with young kids — the rounded corners and no sharp edges matter
- **Anyone upgrading from a low-profile frame** who wants bed height without buying a full loft system
- **Skip it if** you need a headboard included, or if you're using a box spring and don't want to fiddle with shims

**FAQ**

**Does this work with a box spring?** Yes, but the center support sits higher than the rails. You'll need to shim the outer rails to level it, or use a platform mattress instead.

**What's the actual weight capacity?** The manufacturer rates it for 800 pounds distributed. Real-world testing suggests that's conservative — the steel structure is solid.

**Does the frame require tools?** No. The locking mechanism is tool-free, which is rare at this price point.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need the storage height, want a stable frame without the wobble, and don't mind adding a headboard separately. **Skip it if** you want an all-in-one solution with a headboard included, or if you're married to your box spring and don't want to shim.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — a genuine value for the storage-focused buyer, with a couple of fixable quirks that keep it from being perfect.
