**What You’re Actually Getting**

The Aloha is a machine-made area rug, which instantly tells you a few things: it’s built for consistency and lower cost, not hand-knotted artistry. It uses a synthetic fiber blend (typically polypropylene), which is the smart choice for high-traffic zones. The pile height sits in that sweet spot—low enough to not trip up vacuum cleaners or doorways, but plush enough to feel soft underfoot. The design features classic coastal motifs—think coral, sea fans, and subtle wave patterns—rendered in a color palette that leans toward beachy blues, sandy neutrals, and gentle greens. The border is defined but not overly busy, which helps it blend with both modern and traditional furniture.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

This is where the Aloha earns its keep. Polypropylene is stain-resistant and moisture-wicking, meaning a spilled coffee or a muddy dog paw won’t ruin your week. I’ve seen similar Nourison constructions hold up well in living rooms and dining areas where kids and pets are a constant presence. The low pile also makes it a winner if you have a robot vacuum—it won’t get tangled or bogged down.

The backing is a standard woven canvas, which is fine for most floors but can be slightly slippery on hardwood. I’d strongly recommend a rug pad for safety and to extend the life of the rug. The edges are serged, which prevents fraying, but I’ve noticed on some Nourison models that the serging can be a bit stiff right out of the box. Give it a few days to relax.

**FAQ: 3 Real Questions Shoppers Ask**

**Q: Will this rug work on carpet?**
A: Yes, but it’s not ideal. A low-pile rug on plush carpet can shift. Use a thin, non-slip pad or grippers to keep it in place.

**Q: How do I clean stains?**
A: Blot immediately with a clean cloth and mild soap and water. Avoid harsh chemicals, as they can fade the synthetic fibers. For deep cleaning, a carpet cleaner designed for synthetics works well.

**Q: Is the shed going to stop?**
A: Yes. The initial shedding will taper off within 2–4 weeks with regular vacuuming. If it persists beyond that, contact Nourison—it could be a defect.
