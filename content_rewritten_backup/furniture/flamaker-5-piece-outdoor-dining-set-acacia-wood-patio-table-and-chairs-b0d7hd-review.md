If you're shopping for a wood patio set under serious budget pressure, the Flamaker 5-piece acacia dining set is the one that keeps showing up in your search results. Here's what you need to know before you click buy.

**What actually shows up**

The box contains one rectangular table (approximately 47" L x 28" W x 29" H) and four side chairs. The table includes a 1.6-inch umbrella hole with a plug cover — a small detail that matters more than you'd think if you eat outdoors in direct sun. There's also a lower shelf between the legs, which I initially dismissed as decorative until I realized it holds a full stack of dinner plates or a large serving bowl without wobbling. Each chair measures roughly 22" W x 22" D with a seat height around 17.5 inches.

The acacia wood is the real story here. It's a dense hardwood with visible grain patterns and natural color variation — no veneer, no composite hiding underneath. At roughly 60 pounds total for the set, it feels substantial without being immovable.

**How it holds up in real use**

I've had this set on a covered porch for three months of daily use, including several rainstorms. The wood has not warped, cracked, or developed splinters. The joints are mortise-and-tenon style with metal brackets reinforcing the corners — you can see the hardware, but it's painted matte black and blends in well.

The finish is where you need to pay attention. Flamaker ships this with a light coat of oil, not a sealed polyurethane. That means water beads up initially, but after a few weeks, you'll notice the wood starting to absorb moisture. One Amazon reviewer put it bluntly: *"Great table, but I should have sealed it the first weekend. After a month of morning dew, the surface started graying."* That's not a defect — it's just how unfinished acacia behaves. Plan to apply an outdoor wood sealer within the first 30 days.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The comfort factor comes up repeatedly. The chair backs are angled at roughly 15 degrees, which is enough recline to be relaxing without tipping the chair. The seats are contoured — a slight scoop shape that keeps you from sliding forward. One verified buyer wrote: *"I expected to need cushions, but honestly, I've sat for two-hour dinners without discomfort."* That's high praise for a wood chair.

The storage shelf under the table gets consistent mentions too. It's not just for looks — it's structurally braced and can handle weight. Several reviewers use it as a plant stand or a place to stash serving dishes.

**Where it falls short**

Assembly is the biggest hurdle. The instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text, and the bolts require an Allen wrench that's included but flimsy. Multiple reviews mention stripped bolt heads — the fix is to hand-tighten until snug, then do the final torque with the wrench. Budget a full hour and a half if you're doing this solo.

The lack of armrests is a legitimate design choice — it lets the chairs tuck fully under the table, saving space — but if you have back issues or just prefer arm support, this isn't the set for you. Also, the seat depth is about 17 inches. Most adults fit fine, but taller folks (6'+) have noted their thighs extend past the seat edge.

**Who should buy this**

- **Renters or first-time patio owners** who want real wood without a $1,000+ investment
- **People with small balconies or narrow decks** — the rectangular footprint fits where round tables won't
- **Anyone who grills often** — the lower shelf is genuinely useful for holding sauces, tongs, and platters

Skip it if you want a zero-maintenance set (go aluminum or resin), if you need armrests, or if you're not willing to spend 20 minutes with a sealer within the first month.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the umbrella hole fit a standard patio umbrella?**  
A: Yes, it accepts poles from 1.5 to 2 inches in diameter. The plug cover keeps rain out when the umbrella is removed.

**Q: How much weight can the chairs hold?**  
A: The frame is rated for roughly 250 pounds per chair. The joinery is solid, but the wood will flex slightly under heavy load — that's normal for acacia.

**Q: Can this stay outside in winter?**  
A: It can survive, but it won't thrive. Covered storage or a furniture cover is strongly recommended. Unprotected, expect significant graying and potential cracking in freeze-thaw cycles.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want genuine acacia wood construction, a practical rectangular footprint, and a set that looks twice its price point — and you're willing to spend an hour on assembly and a half hour on sealing.

**Skip it if** you need armrests, want zero maintenance, or expect this to last a decade without care. With proper sealing and winter storage, it's a 5-7 year set. Without care, you'll get 2-3 seasons.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — The Flamaker set delivers honest value for the price. It's not heirloom furniture, but it's real wood that looks good, sits comfortably, and earns its place on your patio.
