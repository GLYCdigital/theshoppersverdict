## Where It Falls Short

The ladder is the weakest link. It's fixed at a steep angle (roughly 75 degrees), which is fine for older kids but genuinely difficult for a 4- or 5-year-old to navigate safely. If your youngest is under 6, budget for a separate angled toddler ladder or plan to supervise climbs for the first year.

The guardrail coverage is the second concern. The upper bunk has rails on both long sides and the head end, but the foot end is open. For a 6-year-old who rolls in their sleep, that's a real (if remote) risk. I'd recommend a bed rail on the foot end for any child under 8 — it's a $25 add-on that buys peace of mind.

Finally, there's zero storage built in. The space under the lower bunk is just open floor — you'll want under-bed bins or a separate storage solution, which adds to the total cost.
