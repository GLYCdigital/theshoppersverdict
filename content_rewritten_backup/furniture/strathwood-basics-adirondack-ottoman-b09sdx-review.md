# Strathwood Basics Adirondack Ottoman: The Missing Piece for Your Patio Chair

You bought the Adirondack chair. You love the deep recline, the wide armrests, the way it forces you to actually relax. But after twenty minutes, your feet start to dangle, and the relaxation fades into restless leg syndrome. The Strathwood Basics Adirondack Ottoman exists to solve exactly that problem—and for the most part, it does.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the ottoman in four pieces: two side panels, the slatted top, and the front stretcher. Also included are stainless steel screws and hex wrench—no specialized tools needed. Assembly takes most people under ten minutes, which is refreshing compared to the hour-long projects some patio furniture demands.

The dimensions work for standard Adirondack chairs: about 20 inches wide, 17 inches deep, and 15 inches tall. That puts it right at the seat height of most chairs from brands like Polywood, Lifetime, and the classic wooden options from Home Depot or Ace Hardware. If your chair has a seat 14 to 16 inches off the ground, this will line up correctly.

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

Strathwood uses solid pine—not cedar, not teak, not composite. That's fine at this price point, but it means you're getting a wood that's softer and more prone to dents than premium hardwoods. The upside is that pine is lightweight (about 8 pounds), easy to move around the patio, and takes stain beautifully.

The slatted top design matches most Adirondack chairs aesthetically and allows water to drain through rather than pooling. Stainless steel hardware is a thoughtful touch—cheaper ottomans use zinc-coated screws that rust after one rainy season, leaving ugly streaks down the sides.

In practice, the ottoman holds up well under normal use. It supports adult feet and legs without creaking or flexing, and the fixed angle sits at a comfortable 15-degree tilt that matches most chair recline angles.

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest issue is the unfinished wood. The ottoman ships raw, with no protective coating. If you leave it bare, pine will gray and crack within a single season, especially in sunny or rainy climates. You should plan to apply an exterior wood sealer or stain before first use. That's an extra $15 to $25 and an afternoon of work—important to know before you buy.

The lightweight build is also a double-edged sword. When paired with a chair, it stays put. Standing alone on a windy deck, it will tip over. Not a dealbreaker, but don't expect it to stay in place without furniture anchoring it.

Finally, the fixed angle doesn't adjust. Taller users (6'2" and up) may find their legs at an awkward angle, since the 15-inch height is optimized for average-height adults.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You already own an Adirondack chair and want a matching footrest without paying $80+ for a full set. You're comfortable spending 30 minutes applying a protective finish. You want something light enough to move between patio and pool deck.

**Skip it if:** You want a zero-maintenance product—look at polywood or resin options instead. You need a footstool that works standalone indoors. You're over 6'2" and need adjustable height.

## FAQ

**Q: Does this fit my existing Adirondack chair?**
A: Measure your chair's seat height first. If it's between 14 and 16 inches off the ground, this should align properly. The 20-inch width also matches standard chair widths.

**Q: Do I need to seal the wood before using it outside?**
A: Yes. The pine is untreated, and outdoor exposure will cause graying and cracking within 6-12 months without protection. Apply a spar urethane or exterior wood stain before first use.

**Q: Can I leave it outside all winter?**
A: Technically yes, but you shouldn't. Pine absorbs moisture and will warp through freeze-thaw cycles. Store it indoors or under a cover during winter months.
