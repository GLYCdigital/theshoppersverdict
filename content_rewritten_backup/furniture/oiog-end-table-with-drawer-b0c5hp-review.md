**The Short Version**

Most end tables under $60 are flimsy afterthoughts — wobbly legs, drawers that catch, finishes that scratch if you look at them wrong. The OIOG end table with drawer isn't that. It's a compact, genuinely useful piece that earns its 4.5-star average across 225 ratings. The drawer is the differentiator here, and it's executed better than most tables in this price class.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the table top, four legs, a drawer assembly, and the necessary cam locks and dowels. No tools included — you'll need a Phillips screwdriver. Assembly runs 20–30 minutes if you've done flat-pack furniture before; closer to 45 if you haven't. The instructions are illustrated, not wordy, which is honestly better.

The piece measures roughly 15.7 inches square with a 19.7-inch height — a proper end table, not a nightstand, not a plant pedestal. The drawer sits beneath the main surface and runs on metal slides, which is the first thing that separates this from cheaper alternatives using plastic glides.

**Build Quality and Real-World Use**

The surface is a wood-grain finish over engineered wood. It's not real timber, and at this price nobody should expect it to be. What matters is the finish — it's sealed well enough to resist the condensation rings from a cold drink, and it wipes clean without ghosting. That's more than I can say for several tables I've tested in this bracket.

The drawer is the star. It glides smoothly, closes flush, and the magnetic catch keeps it shut even when you bump the table. One buyer noted: *"The drawer actually works — it doesn't stick or wobble like the last two tables I tried."* That aligns with the broader feedback pattern: reviewers consistently mention the drawer function as the reason they chose this over drawerless alternatives.

Stability is solid for the footprint. The legs bolt into pre-drilled inserts (not just wood screws), which makes a real difference in wobble resistance on carpet. On hardwood, it's rock-solid.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

Across the ratings, three themes emerge:

1. **The drawer** — repeatedly called out as smooth, functional, and larger than expected for the table's size.
2. **Aesthetic neutrality** — the finish reads as more expensive than the price suggests. Multiple reviewers mention guests assuming it cost double.
3. **Packaging** — corners are protected, parts arrive undamaged, and the box isn't comically oversized. Minor, but it tells you the manufacturer cares about the unboxing experience.

**Where It Falls Short**

The drawer is shallow. If you wanted to store a tablet standing up, forget it — remotes, chargers, and small books lie flat or not at all. That's the tradeoff for the compact footprint.

Assembly tolerances are fine but unforgiving. If you overtighten a cam lock, the particleboard can strip. Go finger-tight plus a half turn, and you'll be fine. If you're the type to muscle hardware, this table will punish you.

The finish, while moisture-resistant, will show scratches if you drag heavy objects across it. Use a coaster, use a mat, and it'll last. Treat it like a $30 table and it'll look like one.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers** with limited floor space who need storage without visual bulk
- **Living rooms with a chair-and-table setup** where a drawer beats a shelf for hiding clutter
- **Anyone tired of drawerless end tables** that collect dust and remote controls in equal measure

**Skip it if:**

- You need a full-size nightstand — this is too small for a bed with a lamp, book, and glass
- You want solid wood construction — this is engineered wood, period
- You hate assembling furniture — it's manageable, but it's still assembly
