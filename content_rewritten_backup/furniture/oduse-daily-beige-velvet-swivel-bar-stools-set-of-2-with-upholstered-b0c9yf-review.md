**Real-World Performance and Build**

The swivel mechanism is the headline feature here, and it’s smooth. It rotates a full 360 degrees without catching or wobbling, which is a pleasant surprise at this price point. The footrest is a metal ring that’s welded to the base—it’s sturdy enough for a 200-pound adult to push off from, but I wouldn’t lean all your weight on it repeatedly. The seat cushion is medium-firm. It’s not a cloud, but it’s not a park bench either. For a 45-minute breakfast or a working session at the island, you’ll be comfortable. For a four-hour dinner party marathon, you’ll feel the padding compress.

The base has rubber floor protectors that do their job on hardwood, but they’re thin. If you have tile with grout lines, the stool can catch slightly when swiveling under pressure. A small rug or felt pads will fix that immediately.

**Where It Falls Short—Be Honest**

Here’s the reality check. The seat height is fixed at 24.5 inches. There is no gas lift, no adjustment. If you bought these for a 36-inch counter, they’ll sit too low. They’re designed for a 35–37-inch counter height, and that’s it. Also, the velvet is a magnet for pet hair and lint. You’ll need a lint roller or handheld vacuum weekly if you have a shedding dog.

The biggest structural concern is the base. It’s a single-piece metal cross, not a full circular ring. That means the footprint is slightly narrower than you might expect. On plush carpet, the stool can tip if you lean too far to one side. On hard floors, it’s rock solid. And the stitching on the seat cushion is clean, but the piping around the edge is thin—over a year of daily use, it may fray. This is not a commercial-grade stool.

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**1. Do these stools scratch wood floors?**
The included rubber pads help, but they’re thin. I’d recommend adding adhesive felt pads to the bottom for peace of mind, especially on engineered hardwood.

**2. Are they easy to clean?**
The velvet is stain-resistant to a point. For spills, blot immediately with a damp cloth. For dust and crumbs, a vacuum brush attachment works best. Avoid harsh chemical cleaners—they’ll discolor the fabric.

**3. Can you replace the seat cushion if it wears out?**
No. The cushion is sewn onto the frame and not removable. If the fabric tears, the whole stool needs replacing. Keep that in mind for longevity.
