# LIDYUK End Tables Review: Is This $40 Side Table Actually Worth Your Money?

**Skip this review if you're shopping for heirloom furniture.** The LIDYUK end table isn't trying to be that. It's a lightweight, modern side table priced to compete with the likes of Amazon Basics and Mainstays — and after digging through 229 ratings and what real buyers are saying, it's clear this one punches above its weight class.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains everything you need: the tabletop, steel legs, a lower shelf, and a hex key. No hidden assembly surprises. The table measures roughly 16 inches deep by 16 inches wide, with a surface height around 20.5 inches — right in the sweet spot for most sofas and armchairs.

The top is a wood-grain laminate bonded to a solid MDF core. The frame is powder-coated steel. It weighs about 11 pounds, which means it's easy to move but won't slide around when you're setting down a mug.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this table earns its keep. The two-tier design is genuinely useful. The top holds a lamp and your coffee; the lower shelf catches magazines, a TV remote, or a book. Multiple buyers noted the spacing between tiers is tall enough for a cereal bowl or a tall glass — a small detail that makes daily use less fiddly.

The steel frame is welded, not bolted at the corners, which adds rigidity. One verified purchaser wrote, *"I've had this for 6 months and it hasn't wobbled once. My cat jumps on it daily."* That tracks with the engineering: the legs splay outward slightly at the base, which improves stability without adding bulk.

The laminate top resists water rings — I saw no complaints about staining, which is rare for budget furniture. Scratches are a different story. The finish is printed wood grain, so deep gouges will reveal the brown MDF underneath. Keep sharp objects away.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The overwhelming theme across positive reviews is **ease of assembly**. Nearly every 5-star review mentions it. One buyer put it plainly: *"Took me 8 minutes from box to fully assembled. No frustration at all."* The pre-drilled holes align correctly, and the hardware is sized properly — the two things budget furniture manufacturers usually get wrong.

The minimalist design also earns repeated mentions. It's visually light, doesn't dominate a small living room, and the black steel frame pairs well with both mid-century and industrial decor.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the flaws. **The lower shelf has no lip or railing.** Items placed there can slide off if you bump the table. A stack of magazines is fine; loose pens or a TV remote might end up on the floor.

**The printed wood grain is convincing from 3 feet but obviously fake up close.** If you're putting this next to a real wood sofa table, the difference will be noticeable.

**There's no cable management.** If you want to charge a phone on this table, the cord will dangle down the side. Minor, but worth knowing.

One more thing: the plastic feet are thin. On carpet they're fine; on hardwood they can scratch if you drag the table. Add felt pads if you're a furniture dragger.
