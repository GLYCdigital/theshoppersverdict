**The Verdict at a Glance:** The Alunaune Metal Bar Stools Set of 4 delivers exactly what the listing promises — sturdy, swiveling, high-back seating that doesn't look like it came from a warehouse sale. At a 4.0-star average across 200 ratings, buyers are mostly satisfied, but there's a split between "these are fantastic" and "the seat is brutal." Here's the real story.

---

**What You Actually Get**

Four identical stools, each weighing roughly 14 pounds. The frame is tubular steel with a powder-coated finish (black or white depending on your order). The seat is a formed metal pan, roughly 14.5 inches across — not enormous, but adequate for standard kitchen counters. The backrest rises 22 inches above the seat, which is genuinely higher than most budget stools in this price range. You also get a welded footrest ring, which is a meaningful upgrade over the bolted versions that loosen after a few weeks. Assembly hardware, an Allen wrench, and instructions are included. Plan for 45–60 minutes for all four if you're reasonably handy.

---

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The swivel mechanism is the standout feature. It's a full 360-degree rotation on ball bearings, and it's smooth — no grinding, no catch points. One buyer noted, "I was skeptical at 360 degrees, but these spin like office chairs." That's accurate. The high back is the second differentiator. Most stools in this category stop at lumbar-height; these actually curve slightly at the top, which makes leaning back during a long dinner genuinely comfortable.

The welded footrest deserves specific praise. Multiple reviews mention that even with "two kids who treat them like playground equipment," the ring never wiggled loose. That's the kind of durability you want in a piece of furniture that gets daily abuse.

However, the metal seat is the elephant in the room. At 180+ pounds, the pan flexes slightly and you feel every edge. One reviewer put it plainly: "Looks great, but I need a donut cushion after 20 minutes." That's not hyperbole — it's the most consistent criticism across the feedback.

**Where It Falls Short**

**Assembly is a two-person job.** The seat plate has four bolts that must align perfectly with the frame bracket; if you're off by even a couple of millimeters, you'll strip the threads. Several reviews mention this exact frustration. Go slow, use the included Allen wrench, and don't power-drive them.

**The finish is not indestructible.** Dragging the stools across a rough floor will scratch the powder coat. Lift, don't slide.

**Counter height only.** These are 24-inch stools designed for 36-inch counters. If you have a 42-inch bar, these will sit too low — measure before you buy.

---

**Who Should Buy These**

- **Renters or first-time apartment dwellers** who want a modern look without a permanent installation commitment.
- **Families with kids** who need something that survives being jumped on.
- **Anyone who hosts regularly** — the swivel and high back make conversation easier than fixed stools.

**Skip them if** you're over 200 pounds and plan to sit for hours, or if you have a bar-height (42-inch) counter.

---

**FAQ: Three Questions Shoppers Actually Ask**

**1. Do these scratch hardwood floors?**  
The feet have small plastic glides, but they're thin. On hardwood, add felt pads immediately. On tile or concrete, you're fine as-is.

**2. Can I use these outdoors?**  
No. The powder coat is not UV-stable, and the swivel mechanism will rust if exposed to rain. Indoor use only.

**3. What's the weight capacity?**  
Alunaune doesn't list a formal spec, but based on feedback and frame construction, 250 pounds is the realistic ceiling. Beyond that, the seat pan flex becomes uncomfortable and the swivel starts to strain.

---

**The Final Call**

**Buy it if** you want a solid, good-looking set of swivel stools with real back support at a reasonable price point. **Skip it if** comfort is your absolute priority — you'll need to budget for cushions, or look at upholstered options.
