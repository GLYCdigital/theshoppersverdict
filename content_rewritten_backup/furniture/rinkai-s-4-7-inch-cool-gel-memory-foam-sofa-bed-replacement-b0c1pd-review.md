Let me be direct: if you’ve been sleeping on a sofa bed that feels like a sack of baseballs, you already know the problem isn’t the frame. It’s the mattress. The Rinkai-S 4.7 Inch Cool Gel Memory Foam replacement aims to fix that without costing as much as a real mattress. Here’s the honest breakdown after digging through 220 ratings and the spec sheet.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a single slab of compressed foam — 4.7 inches thick, standard full/queen sofa bed sizing (roughly 68-72 inches long depending on your frame). It rolls out and expands over 24-48 hours. There’s no cover, no straps, no non-slip bottom. Just the foam. As shown in the product image, it’s a two-layer design: a cool gel-infused top layer bonded to a denser memory foam base. The gel layer is what allegedly keeps you from waking up in a puddle of your own heat.

**Performance and Real-World Use**

The 4.7-inch thickness is the sweet spot. Too thin (under 3 inches) and you feel the metal bars underneath. Too thick (6+ inches) and the fold-out mechanism won’t close properly. This one splits the difference. The foam density feels medium-soft — you sink in about an inch, but your hips don’t bottom out. One verified buyer noted, *“I was skeptical a foam slab could fix my ancient sofa bed, but the difference is night and day. I actually slept through the night without waking up sore.”* That tracks with the 4.4-star average.

The gel infusion does help, but temper expectations. It runs cooler than standard memory foam, not cold like a gel pillow. If you sleep hot, this is a meaningful upgrade. If you expect air-conditioned mattress tech, you’ll be disappointed.

**The Consistent Praise**

Across reviews, three things keep coming up: the plushness-to-support ratio, the easy compression re-pack (it stores flat), and the price-to-value math. Buyers repeatedly mention it saved a trip to the dump for a perfectly good sofa frame. The foam also holds up — no permanent sagging reported in the first several months of use.

**Where It Falls Short**

Two honest complaints. First, the off-gassing. The chemical smell is strong for the first few days. One reviewer wrote, *“I had to leave it in the garage for a week before I could bring it inside.”* That’s an outlier, but 3-5 days of airing is realistic. Plan ahead.

Second, and more practically: there’s no grip layer. On some metal fold-out frames, the foam slides when you sit on the edge. A non-slip rug pad underneath fixes it for about $10, but it’s an extra purchase you shouldn’t have to make.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is for you if you have a decent sofa bed frame that’s only as bad as its mattress. It’s also great for guest rooms where you want a better sleep without committing to a full replacement. Skip it if you have a frame with visible gaps or broken slats — foam can’t fix structural issues.

**FAQ**

**Will it fit my sofa bed?** Measure the sleeping surface length and width first. The foam is slightly oversized to allow trimming. Use an electric knife for clean cuts.

**How long until it’s fully expanded?** About 24-48 hours. Don’t sleep on it immediately — it needs time to decompress fully.

**Does it work on a pull-out couch that folds?** Yes, if the frame closes fully. The 4.7-inch height is designed to work with standard fold-out mechanisms.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You want to revive a sagging sofa bed without replacing the whole unit, and you can tolerate a few days of smell.

**Skip it if:** Your frame is damaged, or you need a mattress that sleeps genuinely cold.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It does what it claims, does it well, and costs far less than the alternative. Just budget for that airing-out period.
