## What You Actually Get

The package includes four complete leveling feet assemblies. Each unit consists of a 3/8-inch diameter threaded steel rod, a welded steel base plate, and a black rubber pad bonded to the bottom. The thread length gives you roughly 2 inches of total adjustment range per foot — that's substantial. Most competing levelers top out around 1.5 inches of travel.

The base plates measure about 1.5 inches in diameter. That's wide enough to distribute weight without looking clunky, but compact enough to fit under most furniture rails. The rubber pads are about 1/8-inch thick and do double duty: they grip smooth floors to prevent sliding and protect hardwood from metal-on-wood contact.

## Real-World Performance

Installation is straightforward if you have basic tools. You screw the threaded rod into an existing T-nut or insert in your furniture leg, or you drill a 3/8-inch pilot hole and drive the rod in directly. For wood furniture, a 5/16-inch pilot hole followed by threading the bolt in with a wrench works cleanly.

The 500-pound per set rating is honest. I've seen these under a solid oak dining table loaded with a leaf extension and six people's dinner — no bending, no stripping, no drama. The welded joint between the rod and base plate is the weak point on cheap levelers, and this one holds up.

Buyers consistently mention the stability improvement. One reviewer noted, "My washer and dryer no longer dance across the laundry room floor during spin cycle." Another said, "Used these on a heavy workbench in a garage with a sloped floor — took about 10 minutes to get it perfectly level." That's the use case this product nails.

## Where It Falls Short

The 3/8-inch thread is a double-edged sword. It's beefy and strong, but it won't fit furniture that uses smaller standard inserts — many IKEA pieces and budget store items use 1/4-inch or metric threads. You'll need to drill out the existing hole or replace the insert entirely. That's a modification, not a drop-in install.

Second, there's no locking nut. On pieces that get moved or vibrated (appliances, exercise equipment), the foot can slowly rotate and lose its setting over months. A $2 pack of 3/8-inch jam nuts from any hardware store solves this permanently — worth doing preemptively.

Finally, the rubber pads are bonded, not replaceable. When they wear out in a few years, you're buying new levelers rather than swapping pads.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:**
- You have heavy wood furniture on uneven floors (dining tables, desks, hutches, workbenches)
- You need serious adjustment range, not just a 1/4-inch tweak

**Skip them if:**

- You're working with hollow-core or particleboard legs that can't handle the torque of threading a 3/8-inch rod

## FAQ

**Q: Will these fit my IKEA furniture?**
A: Probably not directly. Most IKEA legs use 1/4-inch or special cam-lock fittings. You'd need to drill out the existing hole to 3/8-inch, which weakens thinner particleboard legs. For IKEA, look for levelers with 1/4-inch threads instead.

**Q: Can I use these outdoors on patio furniture?**
A: The steel will rust eventually if exposed to rain. Indoors or covered patios only, unless you paint them with rust-inhibiting enamel.

A: The 500-pound rating is for the full set of four. Per-foot, that's roughly 125 pounds, but the actual failure point is well above that — the rating is conservative.
