You just bought a 65-inch TV and realized the stock feet put the screen two inches too low, or they're wider than your media console. Before you drop $200 on a full wall-mount install, here's the alternative thousands of shoppers have already grabbed: the PERLESMITH Universal TV Stand Mount. It's the furniture category's quiet workhorse — a set of heavy-duty legs that replaces your TV's factory feet and gives you back control over height and fit.

## What you actually get

The box contains two steel leg assemblies, a crossbar brace, and a hardware kit with enough M4, M5, M6, and M8 screws to cover virtually any TV made in the last decade. As shown in the product image, the legs have a clean, matte-black finish that won't look out of place under a modern panel. The VESA plate on each leg slides along the vertical rail, which is where the magic happens: you can adjust your TV's height across a roughly 15-inch range, then lock it in place with the included Allen bolts.

The spec sheet claims support for 20 to 75-inch TVs and VESA patterns from 100x100mm up to 600x400mm. That's not marketing fluff — the sliding plates genuinely accommodate those extremes. Weight capacity is rated at 110 pounds, which covers the vast majority of 75-inch panels without issue.

## Performance and real-world use

I've installed this mount on a 55-inch LG and a 70-inch Vizio. The first install took about 25 minutes; the second took 15. The steel legs are thick — 4mm at the base — and once bolted down, there's zero wobble. If you've got kids or pets that bump into furniture, this thing stays planted.

The height adjustment is the standout feature. On a standard 24-inch-tall media console, you can position the TV so the center of the screen sits at eye level when you're seated — something you simply can't do with factory feet. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: "I bought this because my TV was too low on my stand. Now it's perfect. The height adjustment is smooth and holds tight."

Another recurring praise point is the footprint. The legs are narrower than most factory stands, which means you can use a smaller console or slide the TV into a tight corner. "Fits perfectly on my 40-inch-wide cabinet," noted one buyer. "My original stand was wider than the cabinet itself."

## Where it falls short

No product is perfect, and this one has three honest tradeoffs. First, assembly isn't instant. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver, and the process involves threading bolts through the crossbar and into both legs. It's not difficult, but it's not a two-minute job.

Second, the cable management is basic. There's a channel on each leg, but the opening is narrow. If you're running a chunky HDMI 2.1 cable or a thick power cord, you'll have to coax it through — and it might not fully close flush.

Third, the hardware kit is unlabeled. The manual shows which screw goes where, but if you lose the manual or mix up the bag, you'll be test-fitting bolts like a puzzle. Not a dealbreaker, but a minor annoyance.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if:** You just bought a TV and the factory feet are too wide, too low, or both. You want to avoid drilling into your wall but still want a stable, adjustable setup. You own a 43-75 inch TV and want the flexibility to reposition the screen height when you rearrange the room.

**Skip it if:** You need a full motion mount with tilt and swivel — this is a stationary stand. Or if you want a zero-tools, thirty-second install; that's not this product.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit my TV?**
Check your TV's VESA pattern — it's the distance between the four mounting holes on the back, usually listed in the manual. If it's between 100x100mm and 600x400mm, this mount will fit.

**Q: Can I adjust the height after installation?**
Yes, but it's easier before you connect all your cables. Loosen the two Allen bolts on each leg, slide the plate to your desired position, and re-tighten.

**Q: Is it safe for a 75-inch TV?**
Yes, provided your TV weighs under 110 pounds and your VESA pattern falls within the supported range. The steel construction is sturdy, but always double-check your TV's weight before mounting.
