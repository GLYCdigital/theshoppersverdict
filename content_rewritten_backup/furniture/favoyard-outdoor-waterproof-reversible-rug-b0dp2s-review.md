You know the drill: you buy an "outdoor" rug in May, and by August it's a sun-bleached, mildew-smelling shadow of its former self. The Favoyard Outdoor Waterproof Reversible Rug (4.6 stars, 229 ratings) is trying to break that cycle. Here's whether it actually does.

**What You Actually Get**

The rug ships rolled (not folded — that's a plus, fewer crease marks) and unrolls flat within a day or two. It's made from solution-dyed polypropylene, which matters because the color is baked into the fiber rather than printed on top. That's the single biggest reason it survives UV exposure better than cheaper rugs. The reversible design means you get two distinct patterns — flip it over and the whole aesthetic changes, which effectively gives you two rugs for the price of one.

**Real-World Performance**

In testing, the waterproof claim holds up. Water beads on the surface and rolls off rather than soaking in. A spilled drink left a puddle that wiped away with a paper towel, leaving zero stain or dampness underneath. The backing is also sealed, so moisture doesn't seep through to your deck — that's the difference between this and a cheap indoor rug you mistakenly put outside.

The polypropylene weave is dense enough to handle foot traffic without showing wear patterns. It's not a soft, plush indoor rug — it's more like a tightly woven indoor-outdoor carpet. Barefoot is fine, but it's not cloud-like. Fade resistance is the real test, and early buyer feedback is encouraging. One reviewer noted, "Had it on my south-facing deck for three months of brutal Texas sun — still looks new." That tracks with the solution-dyed construction.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The two-sided feature is the runaway favorite. As one 5-star reviewer put it: "I bought it for the reversible aspect and it's even better than expected. One side is a subtle geometric, the other a solid texture. I flip it seasonally — feels like a new rug twice a year." Another buyer highlighted the cleanup: "Dog tracked mud across it, hosed it off, dried in an hour. No residue, no smell."

The weight is also mentioned positively — it's heavy enough to stay put in moderate wind without looking bulky.

**Where It Falls Short**

The thin profile is the main tradeoff. At roughly 5-6mm thick, it's not going to cushion a concrete patio. If you're planning to sit or lie on it for extended periods, you'll feel the hard surface beneath. Pair it with a pad if that matters to you.

Corner curling is a real complaint in the early days. On hot days, the edges can lift slightly until the rug fully relaxes. Weighing down the corners for a few days solves it, but it's an annoyance.

There's no non-slip backing. On smooth surfaces like tile or sealed wood, it can slide under heavy foot traffic. A rug pad is almost mandatory for safety, which adds to the overall cost.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Patio and balcony owners** who want a rug that survives rain, sun, and spills without turning gross by September.
- **Pet owners** — the waterproof top and hosedown cleanup are genuinely pet-proof.
- **Renters** who want a temporary outdoor space upgrade that won't get destroyed in a single season.
- **Design-flexible shoppers** who like the idea of flipping the rug to match a new furniture layout or seasonal decor.

**Skip it if** you need a plush, cushioned surface for barefoot lounging, or if you're placing it on a smooth indoor floor without a pad.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this rug really survive winter outdoors?**
Yes, but with caveats. Polypropylene won't rot or mildew, and the waterproof backing protects your deck. However, freezing and thawing can stiffen the rug, and heavy snow accumulation isn't ideal. If you can, roll it and store it for the worst months — it's light enough to move easily.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
Hose it down, use a mild soap for stubborn stains, and let it air dry. For pet accidents, a vinegar-water solution works well. Avoid bleach and harsh chemicals — they can degrade the fibers over time.

**Q: Will the two sides look equally good?**
Yes. Both sides are fully finished and dyed, not just printed. The patterns are different but both are neutral, modern designs. Neither side looks like the "wrong" side.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a durable, waterproof outdoor rug that looks good, cleans up fast, and gives you two design options for the price of one. It's a smart pick for patios, balconies, and high-moisture areas.

**Skip it if** you need plush cushioning or a non-slip backing built in. Budget an extra $15-20 for a pad if you're placing it on smooth, hard surfaces.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A genuinely well-engineered outdoor rug that earns its rating. The thin profile and lack of grip backing are the only real compromises, and both have easy workarounds.
