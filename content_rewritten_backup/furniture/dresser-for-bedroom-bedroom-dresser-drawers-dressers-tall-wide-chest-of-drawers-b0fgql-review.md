**A Tall Dresser That Actually Earns Its Floor Space**

Most tall dressers under $200 are wobbly nightmares held together with cam locks and hope. This one surprised me. With a 4.2-star average across 210 ratings, it's not perfect — but it solves the vertical storage problem without feeling like a cardboard wardrobe.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the six-drawer chest, a full hardware kit, an anti-tip wall strap, and a hex key. There are no tools included beyond that, so budget for a Phillips screwdriver. The panels arrive with edge banding already applied, which is a nice touch at this price point — no sharp MDF edges to cut your hands on during assembly.

The dimensions work for standard bedrooms: roughly 23.6 inches wide, 15.7 inches deep, and 47.2 inches tall. Each drawer interior measures about 19.5 x 13.5 x 5.5 inches — enough for folded jeans or sweaters, but a bit shallow for bulky knitwear stacked high.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The frame uses engineered wood with a melamine finish that mimics a light oak grain. It's not solid wood, and you shouldn't expect it to be at this price. What matters is how it holds up, and here it does better than most competitors in the same bracket.

The drawer slides are the standout feature. They're metal ball-bearing tracks with full extension, meaning you can pull each drawer all the way out to see the back. That's a feature I've seen on dressers costing twice as much. The drawers close with a soft push — no slamming, no misalignment after weeks of use.

The anti-tip anchor is worth mentioning specifically. This is a tall, narrow piece of furniture, and the included wall strap takes two minutes to install. Given that tip-over accidents are a real hazard with tall dressers, this isn't just a checkbox feature — it's a safety essential, especially in homes with kids.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The most common positive thread in reviews is the storage capacity relative to the footprint. One verified buyer wrote: "I live in a small apartment and this holds everything my old 6-drawer dresser did but takes up half the floor space." Another noted, "The drawers don't stick like my last one did — they glide even when I overstuff them."

Multiple reviewers also mentioned the clean, modern look. The melamine finish photographs well and doesn't scream "cheap furniture" from across the room, which is more than I can say for many dressers in this price range.

**Where It Falls Short**

Assembly is the biggest friction point. Expect 60 to 90 minutes of work, and the drawer fronts are the tricky part — they arrive as separate pieces that you must align perfectly before tightening. Get one slightly off and the drawer will catch on the frame. Several reviewers noted this exact issue. Take your time with the alignment and you'll be fine, but know going in that this isn't a 20-minute setup.

The top surface is a genuine weak spot. The melamine finish scratches with surprising ease — one reviewer reported a permanent mark after placing a ceramic lamp base without a pad. A table runner or felt pads under anything heavy are non-negotiable.

The drawers are also shallow. At 5.5 inches deep, they work great for t-shirts and folded pants, but if you have chunky sweaters or thick bath towels, you'll struggle to close them without compressing everything.

**Who Should Buy This**

This dresser is ideal for small apartments, guest rooms, or dorm-style setups where floor space is tight and vertical storage is the priority. It's also a solid pick for a first apartment on a budget — you're getting functional, reasonably sturdy storage without paying premium furniture-store prices.

Skip it if you need deep drawers for bulky items, if you're not comfortable with multi-step assembly, or if you're looking for a piece you'll keep for 15 years. This is a starter dresser, not a heirloom.

**FAQ**

*Does the dresser come with a wall anchor?*
Yes. An anti-tip strap and screws are included, and installation requires only a drill or screwdriver. Given the tall, narrow profile, I strongly recommend using it.

*Is the wood real or particleboard?*
The frame is engineered wood (particleboard) with a melamine finish. It's not solid wood, but the construction is solid enough for everyday use, and the drawer slides are metal, which is where it counts most.

*Can I put a TV on top?*
The top is rated for roughly 30 pounds. A small TV (32 inches or under) is fine, but use a protective pad to prevent scratches, and keep heavier items on the floor.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You need maximum storage in a minimal footprint, you're okay with a 90-minute assembly session, and you're shopping on a budget without expecting heirloom quality.

**Skip it if:** You want deep drawers for bulky items, you're not handy with a screwdriver, or you need a piece that can take heavy daily abuse without showing wear.
