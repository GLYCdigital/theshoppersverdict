It's not heirloom furniture, but for the money, it's a solid, comfortable conversation pit that actually fits six people. Here's the honest breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

This is a full modular system, not just a couch with a couple of chairs. The 7 pieces break down as:

- **3 corner seats** (the L-shaped building blocks)
- **3 armless middle seats**
- **1 rectangular coffee table**
- **7 seat cushions + 7 back cushions**
- **1 tempered glass tabletop**

The whole configuration measures roughly 108 inches wide when fully assembled—that's nine feet of seating. As shown in the product image, you can arrange it as a U-shape, an L-shape, or split it into separate seating groups. That modularity is the biggest selling point here.

---

## Build Quality and Real-World Use

The frame is powder-coated steel, not aluminum, so it's heavier—expect around 120 pounds total. That weight is actually a plus on windy days; this thing isn't blowing over. The rattan is PE wicker, which is standard at this price point. It's UV-stabilized, so it won't fade into a chalky mess after one summer, but don't expect it to look brand-new after three years of full sun either.

The cushions are the standout feature. At 4 inches thick with a 2-inch foam base and a soft poly-fill topper, they're noticeably more comfortable than the 3-inch flat slabs you get on most sets under $800. One buyer put it simply: *"I was worried they'd feel like sitting on cardboard, but honestly they're as comfy as my indoor couch."* That matches my assessment—these are genuinely sit-for-hours cushions.

The tempered glass tabletop is 5mm thick, which is standard. It sits in a recessed frame, so drinks won't slide off every time someone shifts. Just know that you'll be wiping it down constantly—glass shows every leaf and crumb.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the pain points.

**Assembly is a project.** Plan for 2–3 hours with two adults. The frame pieces bolt together, but the holes don't always align perfectly, and you'll need to loosen and re-tighten bolts in sequence. The table is the trickiest part—the glass top requires careful handling, and the frame around it is easy to scratch. Budget a full afternoon.

**Cushions need patience.** They ship vacuum-compressed. When you unbox them, they look flat and sad. Give them 24–48 hours to expand fully. Some reviewers thought they were defective at first—they weren't, but the disappointment is real if you're planning to use them the same day.

**No cover included.** For a set this size, a cover is essential for winter or heavy rain. You'll spend another $40–60 on a properly sized one. Factor that into your budget.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a large patio or deck, you host regularly, and you want a flexible setup that can adapt to your space. It's also great for renters—when you move, you can reconfigure it to fit a new layout.

**Skip it if:** You want a "set it and forget it" piece, you're not comfortable with a couple hours of assembly, or you live in a climate with harsh winters and don't want to buy a separate cover and storage plan.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this stay outside year-round?**
The rattan and steel frame handle sun and rain fine, but the cushions will degrade faster if left exposed. For winter, either store the cushions indoors or cover the whole set. Without a cover, water can pool in the crevices.

**Q: How much weight can each seat hold?**
The steel frame is rated for roughly 300 pounds per seat section. The corner pieces are the most structurally solid; the armless middle seats are fine but feel slightly less rigid.

**Q: Is the glass tabletop safe for outdoor use?**
It's tempered, so it resists shattering from impact. It's not shatterproof—a heavy dropped cast-iron skillet will still break it—but normal use and light impacts are fine.
