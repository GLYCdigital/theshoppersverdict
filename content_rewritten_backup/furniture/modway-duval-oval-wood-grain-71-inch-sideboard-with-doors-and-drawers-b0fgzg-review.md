**The 71-inch sideboard that keeps showing up in "mid-century modern" searches is also the one that has 225 Amazon reviewers averaging 4.5 stars. Here's the honest breakdown of whether the Modway Duval earns a spot in your dining room or office.**

## What You're Actually Getting

The Duval arrives as a flat-pack box that's heavy—around 100 pounds—so have a second person help you carry it inside. Inside you'll find the oval-shaped cabinet body, two hinged doors, three drawers, and the necessary hardware. What you won't find: tools. You'll need a Phillips screwdriver and a rubber mallet for the cam locks. Most buyers report assembly taking 60–90 minutes. One reviewer put it simply: *"Instructions were clear enough that I didn't want to throw the Allen wrench across the room."*

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The star here is the oval, curved silhouette—it's not a boxy rectangle like most sideboards. The wood-grain laminate finish (available in walnut or oak) does a convincing job imitating real wood from a distance. The cabinet doors use soft-close hinges, which is a genuinely premium touch at this price. The three drawers in the center also have soft-close slides.

The dimensions matter: at 71 inches wide, 18 inches deep, and 31 inches tall, it clears most standard dining tables. The interior of each cabinet is spacious enough for dinnerware stacks, barware, or board games. The drawer depth is the compromise—about 4 inches—so plan accordingly.

**Where it stumbles:** The laminate top will show scratches. One reviewer warned, *"I placed a ceramic vase without a felt pad and now there's a permanent ring. Use protection."* The back panel is thin hardboard that flexes if you push on it. It's fine once assembled, but anchor it to the wall if you have toddlers doing laps around the dining room.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The most repeated compliments in the reviews center on the look and the value. *"This looks like a $1,200 piece from a boutique store,"* one buyer noted. Others highlight the storage capacity—the two side cabinets are genuinely deep, fitting stacked plates and mixing bowls without cramming. The soft-close drawers also get consistent shout-outs as a feature people didn't expect at this price.

## Where It Falls Short

Beyond the scratch-prone surface and shallow drawers, a few reviewers noted the oval shape makes the cabinet doors slightly less accessible at the curved edges—you have to reach around the curve to grab items stored at the far corners. It's a minor annoyance, not a dealbreaker. Shipping damage is the other recurring complaint; a handful of reviews mention dented corners or cracked panels on arrival. Amazon's return policy covers this, but it's worth inspecting the box before you start assembly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Mid-century modern enthusiasts** on a budget who want the look without the $1,500+ price tag for solid wood
- **Dining room organizers** who need serious storage for dinnerware, linens, and bar essentials
- **Office or entryway shoppers** wanting a statement piece that's not another generic rectangle

**Skip it if** you need a true wood surface that can handle heavy daily use, or if you're looking for deep drawers—this is a cabinet-first, drawer-second design.

## FAQ

**Q: Can two people assemble this easily?**
A: Yes, and it's actually recommended. The cabinet body is heavy and awkward to flip during assembly. Solo is possible but frustrating. Budget 60–90 minutes.

**Q: Does the wood grain look cheap in person?**
A: No—the laminate is matte and textured, not glossy or obviously printed. It passes the "is that real wood?" test from across the room. Up close, you'll notice it's not wood, but it's far from cheap-looking.

**Q: Will it fit under a wall-mounted TV or in a small apartment?**
A: The 71-inch width is the limiting factor. It needs about 6 feet of clear wall space. The depth (18 inches) is manageable, but measure your space first.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a statement sideboard with real storage, soft-close hardware, and a mid-century look that punches above its price class—and you're willing to use coasters and felt pads to protect the surface.

**Skip it if** you need deep drawers, want genuine wood, or have a tight space where 71 inches won't comfortably fit.

The Modway Duval earns its 4.5-star rating. It's not heirloom furniture, but it's a well-executed, attractive piece that delivers on its promise for the price. Just budget for protective pads and have a wall anchor handy.
