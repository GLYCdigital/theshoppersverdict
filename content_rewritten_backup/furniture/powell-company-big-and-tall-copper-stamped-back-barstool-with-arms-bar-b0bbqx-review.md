Let me be direct: most "big and tall" barstools are standard stools with a longer label slapped on the box. The **Powell Company Big and Tall Copper Stamped Back Barstool with Arms** is not that. This one actually earns the name.

At 400 pounds rated capacity with a reinforced steel frame and a 19.5-inch seat width, this is built for actual big and tall users — not average-sized people who want extra legroom. The copper-stamped back panel is real metal with a hammered texture, giving it a rustic industrial look that photographs well and looks better in person. As shown in the product image, the distressed copper finish catches light differently from every angle, which is a nice touch you don't get with painted wood or cheap faux metal.

## What you're actually getting

The stool ships in three main pieces: the seat assembly, the backrest, and the footrest bar. Hardware is included, and the build takes about 30 minutes with a standard Phillips screwdriver. The box is heavy — roughly 35 pounds — so plan for that.

**Key specs at a glance:**
- Seat height: 30 inches (bar height, not counter height)
- Seat width: 19.5 inches
- Weight capacity: 400 pounds
- Frame: Reinforced steel with powder-coated finish
- Back panel: Stamped copper with hammered texture

## Real-world performance

The 30-inch seat height is measured from floor to seat top, which puts it squarely in bar-height territory. If your counter is 36 inches tall, this works well. If you have a 42-inch bar overhang, you'll want to check your clearance — the arms add about 7 inches on each side, so measure your space before ordering.

Buyers consistently mention two things in their reviews. First, the stability. One customer wrote: "I'm 6'4" and 280 pounds — this stool doesn't wobble, doesn't creak, and doesn't make me feel like I'm sitting on a folding chair." That tracks with the steel frame design and the wide, splayed legs that distribute weight evenly.

Second, the footrest. This is where most big-and-tall stools fail — the footrest sits too low or too high, forcing you to dangle or tuck your legs awkwardly. The Powell stool places the footrest at a height that works for taller users, and it's wide enough to actually rest your feet on, not just hook your toes.

## Where it falls short

The copper finish is a double-edged sword. It looks great out of the box, but copper is a living metal — it will tarnish and develop a patina over time, especially in humid environments. If you want that shiny penny look forever, this isn't the stool for you. If you're okay with a rustic, aged appearance, it'll develop character.

Assembly is the other pain point. The back panel has to align with pre-drilled holes on the seat frame, and getting the angle right solo is frustrating. Plan for two people — one to hold the panels in place while the other drives the screws. It's a 20-minute job with a helper, closer to 45 minutes alone.

The seat cushion is firm. Not rock-hard, but it's not a lounger. For a barstool you'll sit on for 30-60 minutes at a time, it's fine. For a full evening of sitting, you might want a seat pad.

## Who should buy this

- **Taller men (6'0" and up)** who are tired of their knees hitting the underside of the counter
- **Heavier users (250+ pounds)** who want a stool that doesn't flex or wobble
- **Anyone building a rustic or industrial-style bar area** — the copper back is a statement piece
- **Buyers who want a 400-pound rated stool under $250** — this is one of the few that delivers

Skip it if you have a counter-height setup (36-inch counters) — you'll need the shorter version. And if your bar area is tight, the arms make this a wider footprint than armless stools.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the copper finish require maintenance?**
A: No — and that's the point. It's sealed with a clear coat from the factory. It will still patina slightly over time, but you don't need to polish or treat it. If you want to slow the patina, keep it out of direct sunlight and away from humidity.

**Q: Will the seat hold up to daily use?**
A: The cushion is dense foam wrapped in a bonded leather-look material. It's not genuine leather, but it's abrasion-resistant and wipes clean. Multiple reviewers mention using it daily for over a year with no visible wear.

**Q: What's the actual seat height?**
A: 30 inches from floor to seat top. This is a true bar-height stool. If your bar counter is 40-42 inches, this works. If it's a standard 36-inch kitchen counter, you'll need the 24-inch version instead.
