If you're outfitting a home office and need something that stores printer paper, files, and assorted desk clutter without looking like a dorm-room afterthought, the Bush Business Furniture Studio C Low Storage Cabinet deserves a serious look. It's not flashy, but at 4.2 stars across 210 ratings, it's quietly doing its job for a lot of people.

**What you actually get**

The cabinet arrives flat-packed in one box. Inside you'll find the two-door cabinet body, one adjustable shelf, cam locks, dowels, a hex wrench, and the hardware bag. The dimensions are roughly 30 inches wide, 24 inches deep, and 24 inches tall — low enough to double as a printer stand or a side table beside a desk, but substantial enough to swallow letter-size hanging files if you flip the shelf.

Assembly is straightforward but not instant. Plan on 30 to 45 minutes if you've built flat-pack furniture before, closer to an hour if you haven't. The cam-lock system is forgiving, and the pre-drilled holes line up correctly. One thing worth noting: the included instructions are clear, but the thin back panel requires patience. It's stapled at the factory and easy to bend if you're rough with it during installation.

**Real-world performance**

The Studio C line is marketed as commercial-grade light-duty, and that's a fair description. The particleboard core is dense and the laminate surface holds up well against coffee rings, errant pen marks, and the occasional bump from a rolling chair. The adjustable shelf rests on metal pins and can be moved to three heights, which is genuinely useful if your storage needs change.

Buyers consistently mention how surprisingly sturdy it feels once assembled. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I was expecting wobbly pressboard junk, but this thing is solid. It doesn't budge when I lean on it."* Another noted, *"The white finish looks way more expensive than the price tag suggests."* That tracks with my assessment — the laminate has a subtle texture that hides smudges better than glossy alternatives.

The doors align flush out of the box, and the magnetic catches keep them shut. The handles are brushed nickel, which adds a slightly more premium feel than the all-plastic hardware you often see at this price point.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. The back panel is the weakest link — it's thin fiberboard, and if you overtighten the screws or drop the cabinet while moving it, that panel can crack. Handle it gently during assembly and you'll be fine, but it's a known weak point.

The hinges also need periodic attention. After a few months of daily open-and-close, the doors can start to sag a fraction of an inch. It's a five-minute fix with the hex wrench, but it's annoying if you expect set-and-forget performance. And there's no soft-close mechanism. If you let a door swing, it closes with a firm thud that's noticeable in a quiet room.

A few buyers also mentioned that the cabinet arrives with a slight chemical smell from the laminate. It dissipates within a week or two if you leave the doors open, but it's not pleasant on day one.

**Who should buy this**

This cabinet is ideal for home office users who need dependable, mid-tier storage without paying solid-wood prices. It's also a strong fit for small apartments where every piece of furniture pulls double duty — the low profile makes it a natural printer stand or a TV console for a bedroom.

Skip it if you're looking for heirloom-quality furniture or something with soft-close hardware and real wood construction. This is engineered wood, and it looks and feels like it up close. It's honest about what it is.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Can it hold letter-size hanging files?**
Yes, if you position the shelf at the lowest setting. The interior depth is about 23 inches, and hanging file frames fit comfortably. Legal-size files won't fit without the doors bowing.

**Does it come in other colors?**
Yes. The Studio C line offers several finish options, including graphite gray, espresso, and a walnut laminate. The white version is the most popular based on ratings.

**Is it easy to move once assembled?**
It's manageable but not light. At roughly 60 pounds, two people should handle it. The cabinet has no wheels, so sliding it across carpet will scuff the laminate — lift it instead.

**The verdict**

The Bush Business Furniture Studio C Low Storage Cabinet earns its 4.2-star rating honestly. It's a solid, functional piece that assembles cleanly, looks respectable, and handles everyday office storage without drama. The flaws — thin back panel, hinge drift, no soft-close — are real but minor for the price point.

**Buy it if** you need a sturdy, low-profile storage solution for a home office and you're comfortable with a small assembly project.

**Skip it if** you want furniture-grade construction with premium hardware, or if you can't tolerate the initial off-gassing smell.

For most shoppers, this is a smart purchase that does exactly what it promises — no more, no less.
