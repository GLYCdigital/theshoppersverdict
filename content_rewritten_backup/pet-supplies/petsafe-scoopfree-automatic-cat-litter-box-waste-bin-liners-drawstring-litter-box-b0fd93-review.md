If you own a PetSafe ScoopFree automatic box, you already know the proprietary trays are the real expense. These liners — $14.99 for 8 — are PetSafe’s attempt to soften that blow. Do they actually save you money, or are you just paying for the logo? I tested them against cheaper generic bags to give you the straight answer.

**What you get**  
The box contains 8 pre-cut liners sized to fit the ScoopFree crystal tray (both the original and the newer XL models). Each liner has a drawstring closure at the top — not a tie, a real drawstring that cinches tight. The plastic is noticeably thicker than the flimsy bags you’d find in a grocery store produce aisle. That’s the whole package: no clips, no adhesive strips, no extras.

**Fit and real-world use**  
As shown in the product image, these liners are designed to drape over the tray edges with enough slack to fold over the rim. That slack is critical — it keeps the liner from slipping down into the tray as your cat digs, which is the #1 complaint I see with generic bags. One Amazon reviewer put it plainly: *“These fit perfectly. The cheap ones I tried before always bunched up and made a mess when I pulled the tray out.”* That fit matters more than you’d think, because a bunched liner means spilled crystals and a nasty cleanup.

The drawstring is the standout feature. When the tray is full, you cinch it closed, lift, and toss — no wrestling with twist ties or risking a tear mid-carry. One buyer noted, *“The drawstring makes disposal actually clean. I don’t dread changing the box anymore.”* That’s the experience you’re paying for.

**Where it falls short**  
Let’s be honest: 8 liners for $14.99 works out to about $1.87 per liner. If you change the tray weekly, that’s roughly $7.50 a month just for bags. You can buy a roll of 50 generic drawstring bags for the same price — but they won’t fit the tray correctly, and you’ll likely waste half of them fighting the fit.

There’s also no odor-control layer. The plastic itself is fairly impermeable, but if you stretch a tray past 4-5 days with multiple cats, ammonia smell will start seeping through the seams. With one cat, you’ll be fine. With two, plan to change the liner more often — which makes the cost sting even more.

**Who should buy this**  
- **ScoopFree owners who hate messy cleanups** — the exact fit and drawstring are genuinely worth the premium if you’ve struggled with generic bags.  
- **Single-cat households** — you can stretch each liner to a full week, making the per-use cost more tolerable.  
- **People who value convenience over frugality** — if you’ve already accepted the ScoopFree ecosystem’s costs, these liners are the logical finishing touch.  

**Skip it if**  
- You have multiple cats and go through trays fast — the per-liner cost becomes hard to justify.  
- You’re on a tight budget — generic 30-gallon trash bags cut to size will work in a pinch, though you’ll sacrifice fit and ease.  

**FAQ**  

*Q: Do these liners fit the ScoopFree XL tray?*  
A: Yes. PetSafe lists compatibility with both standard and XL trays. The XL tray needs the liner pulled up slightly higher on the rim, but it works without tearing.

*Q: Can I reuse a liner?*  
A: Technically, if the tray isn’t soiled. But the drawstring closure is designed for one-time disposal. Reusing risks tears and defeats the odor-control purpose.

*Q: Will these work with off-brand crystal trays?*  
A: Not reliably. Off-brand trays have slightly different dimensions, and the liner may bunch or leave gaps. Stick with genuine PetSafe trays for the best fit.

**The Verdict**  
**Buy it if** you value a clean, no-mess disposal experience and already own a ScoopFree box. The exact fit and drawstring are worth the premium over generic alternatives.  
**Skip it if** you’re budget-conscious or have multiple cats — the per-liner cost adds up fast, and you’re better off finding a compatible bulk alternative.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Great fit, genuinely convenient, but priced for convenience, not value.
