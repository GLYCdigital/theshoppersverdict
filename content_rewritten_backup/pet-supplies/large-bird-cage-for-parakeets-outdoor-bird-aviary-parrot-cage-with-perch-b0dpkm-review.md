Let’s cut through the listing photos. This cage promises "outdoor aviary" durability at a mid-range price point, and buyers have pushed it through a full year of weather. Here’s what 195 ratings at 3.9 stars actually tell you — and what they don’t.

## What You Actually Get

The box contains the main cage body, a slide-out metal tray, two plastic food/water cups, three wooden perches, and the hardware bag. As shown in the product image, the cage measures roughly 32 inches long by 21 inches wide by 43 inches high — tall enough for a cockatiel to stretch wings, and long enough for two parakeets to actually fly short distances.

The wire is 1.5mm thick, which is noticeably sturdier than the flimsy .8mm wire on cheap starter cages. The powder-coated finish feels smooth initially, and the two full-width front doors swing open wide enough to reach every corner during cleaning.

## Performance and Real-World Build

The design gets the basics right. The removable tray slides out without removing the cage bottom, which buyers consistently mention as a win — one reviewer noted, "I can swap the liner in under two minutes without startling my bird." The perches are 0.75 inches in diameter, appropriate for parakeets and cockatiels, though too thin for larger parrots.

But here’s where the 3.9-star average starts to make sense. The bar spacing is 1.2 centimeters. That’s fine for cockatiels, conures, or lovebirds. It is **not** safe for budgies or finches — small birds can squeeze through or get their heads stuck between bars. Multiple reviews mention returning the cage after a parakeet escaped on day two.

Outdoor durability is the other concern. Several buyers report the paint chipping at the weld points and corners within 6–8 weeks of outdoor use, exposing bare metal that begins to rust. If you live in a humid climate, plan to add a coat of outdoor-grade clear sealant yourself.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Size-to-price ratio**: "Best value I found for a flight cage this size under $150."
- **Door access**: "The double front doors make changing food and water actually painless."

## Where It Falls Short

- **Assembly is a two-person job**, and the instructions are minimal line drawings. Expect 45–60 minutes of work.
- **The included cups are flimsy** plastic that cracks if dropped during washing. Replace them with stainless steel.
- **No seed guard** — millet hulls and feathers will scatter around the base. Buy a floor mat.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Cockatiel or conure owners** who want an indoor flight cage or sheltered outdoor space.
- **Parakeet owners with larger species** (English budgies, not standard budgies) — but only if you verify bar spacing first.
- **DIY-minded bird owners** comfortable adding rust-proofing and upgrading cups.

## Skip It If

- You own standard budgies, finches, or any bird under 30 grams. The bar spacing is a genuine safety hazard.
- You live in high-humidity or coastal areas without covered outdoor space.
- You expect a truly weatherproof product straight from the box.

## FAQ

**Q: Can this cage stay outdoors year-round?**  
Not without modification. It survives light rain and partial shade, but the paint will chip and rust within months. Use it under a covered patio or add rust-proofing.

**Q: What bird species fit safely?**  
Cockatiels, lovebirds, conures, and larger parakeets. Avoid standard budgies and finches — the 1.2cm bar spacing is too wide.

**Q: Is the tray truly removable for cleaning?**  
Yes, it slides out fully without tools. It’s the best feature of the design.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 3.7/5**

**Buy it if** you have a medium-sized bird, want a spacious flight cage, and don’t mind spending an hour with a screwdriver and adding a coat of sealant.

**Skip it if** you have small parakeets or need a true all-weather outdoor aviary. This is a solid indoor flight cage with outdoor aspirations — not a permanent outdoor habitat. For the price, it’s a fair deal if you understand its limits before you buy.
