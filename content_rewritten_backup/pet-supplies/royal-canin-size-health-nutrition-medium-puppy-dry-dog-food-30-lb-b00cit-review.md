If you're raising a medium-breed puppy — think Beagle, Bulldog, Corgi, or Australian Shepherd — you've probably noticed most puppy foods treat all young dogs the same. That's the gap Royal Canin's Size Health Nutrition line targets, and for the 235 Amazon reviewers who've pushed it to a 4.7-star average, it's a formula that earns its premium shelf position.

## What You Actually Get

The 30-pound bag delivers a kibble that's visibly different from standard puppy chow. As shown in the product image, the pieces are a rounded, slightly flattened shape — not too large for a 12-week-old's mouth, but substantial enough that a 10-month-old Lab-mix won't inhale it without chewing. The formula is built around chicken by-product meal as the primary protein source, followed by brewers rice, corn, and chicken fat.

Before you balk at the by-product meal, understand what it means here: it's concentrated protein from organ meats and cartilage, not feathers or beaks. For puppies with sensitive stomachs, this digestibility matters more than the ingredient label aesthetics.

## Real-World Performance

The headline feature is the growth curve management. Medium-breed puppies hit their adult weight between 25 and 55 pounds, and that rapid development period is exactly when skeletal issues like hip dysplasia take root. Royal Canin's calcium-to-phosphorus ratio is deliberately moderate — not the maximum levels you'll find in all-breed puppy foods — which slows the growth rate to something a medium frame can handle safely.

Owners report the digestive benefits almost immediately. One verified buyer notes: *"My Golden Retriever puppy had soft stool on three other brands. Within four days on this, it firmed up and stayed that way."* That's the prebiotic fiber blend (FOS and beet pulp) doing real work. Another common thread in reviews: puppies actually eat it. A reviewer with a notoriously picky Shiba Inu writes, *"She finishes her bowl in under two minutes. I've never seen her this excited about food."*

The kibble's texture also holds up. It's not greasy, doesn't crumble into dust at the bottom of the bag, and the pieces have enough structural integrity that even aggressive chewers don't turn it into paste.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this is not a budget food. At roughly $2 per pound, you're paying for the veterinary nutrition research, not exotic ingredients. If you're looking for grain-free, high-protein, or named-meat-first formulations, this isn't it. Corn and brewers rice are present, which disqualifies it for owners committed to grain-inclusive-only diets that avoid corn.

The transition period also catches people off guard. Multiple reviewers mention that switching cold turkey from another brand caused a few days of digestive upset. Royal Canin's own feeding guidelines recommend a 7-day gradual transition — follow that, and you'll avoid the mess.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a medium-breed puppy under 12 months old, you want a food backed by actual veterinary research rather than marketing buzzwords, and you're willing to pay more for predictable, reliable results.

**Skip it if:** Your dog is a small breed (under 20 lbs adult weight) — the kibble size and calcium levels aren't right — or if you're firmly committed to grain-free or named-meat-first ingredient lists. Also skip if budget is your primary constraint; Costco's Kirkland puppy food will work fine for most dogs.

## FAQ

**Q: How long should I feed this?**
A: Until your puppy reaches about 80% of expected adult weight — typically 10–12 months for medium breeds. Then transition to Royal Canin's Medium Adult formula to avoid a sudden nutrient shift.

**Q: Can I mix this with wet food?**
A: Yes, but account for the calorie difference. The dry food alone provides complete nutrition; adding wet food means reducing the dry portion by roughly 10–15% to prevent weight gain.

**Q: My puppy is a picky eater. Will they accept this?**
A: Based on reviews, most puppies take to it quickly due to the palatability enhancers and chicken fat coating. But individual dogs vary — start with a small bag if you're concerned about waste.
