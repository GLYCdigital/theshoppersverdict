If your cat ignores half the toys you buy, the Potaroma Saury Fish earns its keep for one simple reason: it combines two of the most reliable cat attractants — crinkle sound and catnip — into one lightweight, tossable shape. With 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, this 3-pack is clearly doing something right. But is it worth your money? Here's the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes three saury fish toys, each roughly the size of an actual small fish (about 6 inches long). They're made from soft plush fabric with an embroidered face and stitched fins — the kind of construction that looks deceptively simple but survives real abuse. Inside, there's a crinkle layer throughout the body and a pouch of catnip sewn into the belly. The three colors vary depending on the listing batch, but you'll typically get one in a natural silver-green, one in a brighter tone, and a third accent color. No bells, no batteries, no motion sensors — just noise and scent.

## Real-World Performance

The crinkle factor is the star here. Cats that are bored with jingle balls or laser pointers often perk up the instant they hear that papery crunch. The sound isn't overwhelming — it's subtle enough that it won't drive you crazy when your cat bats it around at 3 a.m., but loud enough to trigger your cat's hunting instincts. One buyer noted, "My picky tabby ignored every toy I bought her for a year. The first time she heard the crinkle, she picked it up and carried it to her bed."

The catnip is the secondary hook. It's actual dried catnip leaf, not the stem-heavy filler you find in discount toys. It's potent on arrival and stays reasonably strong for a couple of weeks with regular play. After that, the scent fades, and the toy becomes dependent on the crinkle sound alone — which for many cats is still enough to keep them engaged. If your feline is one of the 30% that don't react to catnip, the crinkle still gives this toy a purpose.

Build quality is genuinely better than the price point suggests. The seams are double-stitched around the edges, and the embroidered details aren't just decorative — they add texture that cats like to knead and bite. I've seen these survive being carried by the tail, thrown across rooms, and kicked like a soccer ball. That said, these are not indestructible. They're plush toys, and a determined chewer will eventually find a seam.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the reviews, three themes dominate. First, the instant engagement — cats that usually ignore toys go after these immediately. Second, the value of getting three for roughly what you'd pay for one premium toy at a pet store. Third, the longevity; several reviewers mention their cats still play with these weeks after purchase, which is rare for soft toys. One 5-star review summed it up: "My cat is 14 and has never played with a single toy. She now carries these fish around like a kitten. I've bought three more packs."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The catnip does fade, and there's no way to refresh it — you can't unzip the belly or add more. Once the potency drops, the toy relies entirely on the crinkle sound. For most cats, that's fine. For catnip-obsessed cats that only care about the scent, you'll need to rotate toys or buy a refill pack.

Also, the size is a tradeoff. At 6 inches, it's perfect for average cats and kittens, but if you have a Maine Coon or a heavy chewer, the fish is small enough to be gripped fully in the mouth, which accelerates wear. And while the stitching is solid, the fabric isn't chew-proof. If your cat is a dedicated shredder, expect to find stuffing on the floor within a month.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Kittens and young cats** — the crinkle sound and lightweight size are perfect for developing hunting skills.
- **Bored indoor cats** — especially ones that ignore traditional toys; the sound novelty is a strong reset.
- **Multi-cat households** — three fish means no territorial battles over a single toy.
- **Skip it if** your cat is a power chewer who destroys every plush toy in under an hour, or if you specifically need a toy with motion/electronics.

## FAQ

**Is the catnip safe if my cat ingests it?**
Yes. Dried catnip is non-toxic and commonly ingested by cats during play. The pouch is sewn closed, so it's unlikely to spill unless the toy is torn open. If that happens, just vacuum up the loose nip and keep the toy away until you can repair the seam.

**Will the crinkle sound annoy me at night?**
It's quieter than jingle bells. The crinkle is a soft, papery rustle that carries maybe 10 feet. It's noticeable but not disruptive, especially compared to toys with bells or squeakers.

**How do I clean these?**
Spot clean with a damp cloth and mild soap. They're not machine washable — the crinkle layer and catnip pouch will degrade in a washer. Air dry completely before giving back to your cat.
