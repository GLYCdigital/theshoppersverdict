# Stella & Chewy's Freeze Dried Raw Puppy Food – Dinner Patties: Worth the Hype?

**The Short Version:** If you're feeding a puppy, you already know the stakes — those first months set the foundation for a lifetime of health. Stella & Chewy's Dinner Patties for Puppies is a raw food option that skips the freezer aisle. At 4.6 stars from 229 ratings, most puppy parents are thrilled. But it's not perfect, and it's not cheap. Here's the full breakdown.

## What's Actually in the Bag

You're getting freeze-dried raw patties — 11 ounces per bag, which breaks down to about 16 patties depending on how you break them. The recipe is 90% meat, organs, and bone, with organic fruits and vegetables making up the rest. First five ingredients: beef, beef liver, beef kidney, beef heart, and beef tripe. No grains, no fillers, no artificial preservatives.

The patties are about 2 inches across and crumble easily — which matters because you'll be breaking them into smaller pieces for a puppy's mouth anyway. Each patty is about 150 calories, so portioning is straightforward once you weigh your pup.

## Real-World Performance: How It Feeds

Here's where this product shines. The freeze-drying process preserves nutrients better than kibble's high-heat extrusion, and you can see the difference in ingredient quality — the patties look like actual meat, not brown cereal bits.

Rehydration is simple: add warm water, wait two minutes, mash. It turns into a texture similar to canned food. Most puppies take to it immediately — the smell is distinctly meaty, not processed.

The feeding transition is usually smooth. Unlike some raw foods that cause digestive upset when introduced, the freeze-dried format seems gentler. Several reviewers noted their puppies had firmer stools and shinier coats within two weeks.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star rating isn't accidental. Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**"My picky eater actually finishes her bowl."** — This comes up repeatedly. Puppies that turned their noses up at kibble eat these patties enthusiastically.

**"The ingredients are the reason I switched."** — Many buyers explicitly mention reading the label and feeling confident about what they're feeding. The transparency of the sourcing (grass-fed beef, cage-free poultry) matters to this crowd.

**"No more freezer space taken up."** — This is the biggest practical win. Traditional raw food requires a dedicated freezer drawer. These patties sit in your pantry until opened.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides, because they're real.

**The price.** At roughly $2.50–$3.00 per day for a medium-breed puppy, this is significantly more expensive than premium kibble. You're paying for the raw nutrition and convenience. Budget accordingly.

**Storage after opening.** The bag reseals, but the patties need to be used within a few weeks once opened. If you have a small dog, you might not get through a bag before they start to lose freshness.

**It's not a complete solution.** Some reviewers supplement with additional calcium or omega-3s, especially for large-breed puppies. Check with your vet if you're feeding this exclusively to a growing giant breed.

**The crumble factor.** The patties break apart in the bag during shipping. It doesn't affect nutrition, but if you expect uniform patties, you'll be disappointed.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You want raw nutrition without the freezer hassle, your puppy is a picky eater, or you're transitioning from a raw diet and need convenience. It's especially good for small-to-medium breeds where the cost is more manageable.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, you have a giant breed puppy that will need massive quantities, or you prefer the convenience of kibble that requires zero prep.

## FAQ: Three Questions Buyers Actually Ask

**Q: How do I transition my puppy from kibble to these patties?**
A: Start with 25% patties mixed with 75% current food. Gradually shift the ratio over 7–10 days. If your puppy has a sensitive stomach, go slower — some reviewers took two weeks.

**Q: Can I feed these dry without rehydrating?**
A: Yes, but puppies need the extra moisture. Rehydrating also makes the patties easier to chew and digest. If feeding dry, ensure fresh water is always available.

**Q: Is this safe for all puppy breeds?**
A: Generally yes, but large-breed puppies have different calcium-to-phosphorus needs. The beef recipe is balanced for growth, but consult your vet for giant breeds like Great Danes or Mastiffs.
