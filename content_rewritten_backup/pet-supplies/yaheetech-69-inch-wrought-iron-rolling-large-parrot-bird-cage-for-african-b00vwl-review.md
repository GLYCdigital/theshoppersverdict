**Is the Yaheetech 69-inch Wrought Iron Rolling Parrot Cage the spacious, durable home your feathered friend deserves? Or is it just another cage that looks good in photos but falls apart in practice? Let's cut through the marketing and see what 225 buyers discovered.**

First, the headline numbers: a 4.5-star average from 225 ratings. That's solid, not stellar. The cage stands 69 inches tall, measures roughly 30.7 inches long by 22.8 inches wide, and weighs around 45 pounds. It's positioned for medium-to-large parrots — African greys, Amazons, cockatoos, and larger conures.

**What you actually get**

The box contains the main cage body, a detachable play top, a wide storage shelf, two plastic seed guards, two food bowls, two perch dowels, and a swing. The wrought iron frame is powder-coated in a glossy black finish — visually clean, and as shown in the product images, it looks far more expensive than it is. You also get four caster wheels, two of which lock, which is a small detail that matters more than you'd think.

**The real-world experience**

Assembly takes most people 45 to 90 minutes. The instructions are mostly diagrams, and a few screw holes need gentle persuasion. One reviewer noted, "The wrench that comes with it is junk — use your own hex key and it goes together in half the time." Fair point. The bar spacing sits at 0.6 inches, which is fine for grey parrots and Amazons but an absolute no-go for budgies or cockatiels — they can squeeze through or get their heads stuck. This is a medium-large bird cage, full stop.

The wheels are the standout feature. On hardwood or tile, the cage glides smoothly, and the locking casters keep it planted when you don't want movement. Cleaning is dramatically easier when you can roll the cage to a sink or outside rather than wrestling a 45-pound metal structure across the room. The removable drop tray is similarly thoughtful — slide it out, wipe it down, slide it back.

The play top adds genuine vertical value. Your parrot can climb out onto the perch rings and ladders without needing a separate play stand, which is a $50–$100 savings on its own. The storage shelf underneath is shallow but useful for storing food, toys, and cleaning supplies.

**What buyers consistently praise**

Owners repeatedly highlight the build quality relative to price. "This cage is a tank," one buyer wrote. "My African grey has chewed on the bars for six months and there's not a single dent." The powder-coated finish holds up better than galvanized cages, which often rust or flake. Several reviewers also mentioned the size — "Finally a cage tall enough for my Amazon to stretch his wings without hitting the sides."

**Where it falls short**

The plastic seed guards are the weakest link. They snap into place but larger birds can dislodge them during energetic wing-flapping or climbing. A few reviewers replaced them with metal guards after a few months. The included perches are sand-coated and rough on bird feet — most owners swapped them for natural wood perches immediately. And the food bowls are standard stainless steel, but they sit in shallow holders that some birds learn to tip.

**Who should buy this cage**

This is for owners of medium-to-large parrots — African greys, Amazons, cockatoos, Senegal parrots — who want a tall, movable cage with a built-in play area without spending $400+. It's also a smart pick for anyone with limited floor space who needs vertical height, or for owners who dread cleaning day and will appreciate the rolling wheels.

**Skip it if** you have a small parrot (budgie, cockatiel, lovebird) — the bar spacing is a safety risk. Also skip if you're not comfortable with a somewhat involved assembly.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the play top detach?**

**Q: How hard is it to move with a bird inside?**
A: Easy. The four wheels roll smoothly, and the locking casters keep it stationary when needed. Just make sure your bird is comfortable with motion before rolling across rooms.
