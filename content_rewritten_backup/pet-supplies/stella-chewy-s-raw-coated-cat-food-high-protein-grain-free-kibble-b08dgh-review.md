**The Short Version: Is This Just Overpriced Kibble?**

Stella & Chewy's Raw Coated Cat Food sits in a weird middle ground. It's not a full raw diet, but it's also not your average grocery-store kibble. Each piece of crunchy, high-protein kibble gets a dusting of freeze-dried raw chicken — enough to make the bag smell like actual meat and enough to win over cats who turn their nose up at ordinary dry food.

At 4.5 stars across 225 Amazon ratings, it's clearly working for a lot of households. But is it worth the premium? Let's break down what you're actually paying for.

**What's in the Bag**

You get a sealed bag of small, dark-brown kibble pieces visibly coated with a lighter freeze-dried raw dusting. As shown in the product image, the coating isn't subtle — it clings to every piece. The first ingredient is deboned chicken, followed by chicken liver and chicken giblets. Protein clocks in at 40%, which is substantially higher than the 28–32% you'll find in most mass-market grain-free options. Crude fat sits at 17%, keeping calories reasonable for indoor cats.

The "raw coated" process means the kibble is baked first, then sprayed with a raw meat coating and gently freeze-dried to preserve nutrients. This gives you the convenience of shelf-stable kibble with a flavor punch that mimics raw feeding.

**Real-World Performance**

The most consistent praise from verified buyers centers on picky eaters. One reviewer wrote, *"My cat is 14 and has refused every dry food I've tried for the last three years. She licked the bowl clean on the first try."* Another noted, *"I have three cats with completely different preferences and all three eat this without hesitation."*

The texture gets mention too — the coating creates a slightly tacky exterior that softens quickly when mixed with wet food, while the interior stays crunchy. That dual texture seems to be the secret sauce for finicky felines.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. First, the price. You're paying a notable premium over brands like Purina Pro Plan or even Blue Buffalo. If you have multiple cats, this adds up fast.

Second, the transition issue. Several reviewers reported loose stools for the first week. One said, *"My cat had diarrhea for four days before I realized I needed to mix it 50/50 with her old food for two weeks."* This is normal for high-protein diets, but it's worth planning for.

Third, and this is important: **this is not a raw diet.** If you're looking for the full benefits of raw feeding — like the moisture content and natural enzymes — this won't deliver that. It's a compromise product: better than standard kibble, but not a replacement for actual raw or high-quality wet food.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **The picky-cat owner** whose current food is being ignored — this is your most likely fix
- **The multi-cat household** where one cat needs grain-free and the others are finicky
- **The raw-curious owner** who isn't ready for the hassle of handling raw meat daily

**Skip It If:** You're on a tight budget, your cat has a confirmed poultry allergy (this formula is chicken-heavy), or you're expecting a complete raw diet replacement.

**FAQ**

**Q: How much should I feed my cat?**
A: For a 10-pound cat, plan on about 1/2 cup daily, split into two meals. Adjust based on activity level and whether you're supplementing with wet food.

**Q: Is this safe for kittens?**
A: Yes, it's formulated for all life stages. The small kibble size works well for kittens, though you may want to soften it initially.

**Q: Does the raw coating survive shipping?**
A: Yes — it's freeze-dried, not fresh. It arrives as a stable powder coating that stays intact in the bag. No refrigeration needed until after opening, and even then it's shelf-stable.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your cat is a picky eater, you want grain-free nutrition with real meat as the first ingredient, and you're willing to pay for quality.

**Skip it if** budget is your primary concern or you need a true raw diet.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It's not perfect, but for what it claims to be — a high-protein, grain-free kibble that actually gets cats to eat — it delivers consistently. The price is the only real barrier, and for many owners, the end of the food-strike standoff is worth every penny.
