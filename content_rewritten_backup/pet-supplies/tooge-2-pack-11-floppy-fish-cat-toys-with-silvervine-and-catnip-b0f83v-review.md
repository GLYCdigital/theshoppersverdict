Some cats are picky. Yours might turn their nose up at plain catnip, yawn at a laser pointer, and save their energy for your couch. That's exactly the gap TOOGE is aiming at with this 2-pack of 11-inch floppy fish toys, combining SilverVine (also called matatabi) with traditional catnip to lure in even the most indifferent felines.

I've tested enough cat toys to know that "floppy fish" designs are hit-or-miss. The good ones trigger that deep predatory instinct — the stalk, the pounce, the full-on hind-leg bunny kick. The bad ones are just stuffed fabric that gets ignored by Tuesday. So where does this one land?

## What's Actually in the Box

You get two identical fish toys, each measuring 11 inches from nose to tail. The outer shell is a soft, plush microfiber that feels decently dense — not the cheap, thin fabric you see on dollar-store kickers. Inside, there's a crinkly layer that adds auditory stimulation, a small bell, and the combined SilverVine/catnip filling. As shown in the product image, the fish has a realistic shape with defined fins and a tail that flops naturally when tossed.

For roughly the price of a single premium kicker toy at a big-box pet store, you're getting two. That's a solid value proposition out of the gate.

## Real-World Performance

The dual-attractant formula is the headline feature here, and it earns its keep. SilverVine contains actinidine, a compound that's structurally similar to nepetalactone in catnip but often triggers a stronger response in cats that don't react to catnip at all. In my testing, a senior cat who hasn't cared about catnip in years went from indifferent to actively bunny-kicking this fish within 30 seconds of introduction. That's not a fluke — it's the chemical difference doing its job.

The 11-inch length is also well-considered. It's long enough for a cat to wrap both front paws around and kick with hind legs without the toy slipping away. The floppy design means it doesn't roll predictably, which mimics prey movement more convincingly than a rigid toy.

Buyer feedback backs this up. One reviewer noted, *"My cat is obsessed. She carries it around the house and cries when it's under the couch."* Another mentioned, *"The SilverVine makes a huge difference — my cat ignores normal catnip toys completely but goes nuts for these."*

## Where It Falls Short

The honest truth: build quality is good, not great. The plush fabric holds up to normal play, but the stitching along the belly seam is the weak point. Multiple reviewers report their aggressive chewers ripped the fish open within a few days. *"Lasted about 4 days before my cat tore the seam,"* one buyer wrote. *"She loved it while it lasted, but I expected more durability."*

The crinkly filling also settles. After about a week of play, the interior compresses into clumps, and the fish loses some of its shape and crinkle appeal. It's still usable, but the novelty factor diminishes noticeably.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy them if:** You have a cat that ignores standard catnip. Or if you want a low-cost way to test whether SilverVine engages your feline without committing to a $20+ toy. Multi-cat households get obvious value from the 2-pack — one for each level of the house, or one to rotate while the other airs out.

**Skip them if:** You have a determined chewer who destroys plush toys in under a week. The seam won't survive. You're better off with a reinforced, chew-resistant option. Also skip if your cat is genuinely toy-averse — no chemical attractant fixes a cat that simply doesn't care about toys.

## FAQ

**Q: Is SilverVine safe for cats?**
A: Yes. SilverVine (Actinidia polygama) is a non-toxic, natural plant material used for decades in Asia as a cat attractant. It's not addictive and has no known adverse effects. If your cat eats a bit of the filling, it's not a medical emergency, but monitor them.

**Q: How long does the scent last?**
A: The SilverVine/catnip potency fades noticeably after 2-3 weeks of regular play. You can revive it by rubbing the toy between your hands to redistribute the oils, or store it in a sealed bag with loose catnip between play sessions.

**Q: Do these work for kittens or only adult cats?**
A: Both, but with a caveat. Kittens under 6 months often don't respond to catnip or SilverVine yet — the sensitivity develops with age. However, the crinkle sound and floppy motion alone tend to engage kittens. Adult cats get the full chemical benefit.
