### Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. The most common complaint is that some picky species simply won't eat them. If you have a leopard gecko or a snake, skip this—they want live or frozen prey, not dried shrimp. Similarly, some tortoise owners reported their animals showing zero interest.

The jar size is also worth noting. At 3.5 ounces, it's not a bulk purchase. If you have multiple reptiles or a larger turtle, you'll be reordering frequently. There's no resealable pouch inside the jar, so if you don't use it regularly, moisture can degrade the texture over time.

Finally, the shrimp are naturally high in iodine. If you're feeding a turtle with thyroid issues, consult your vet first. This is a treat, not a staple, and shouldn't replace a balanced pellet diet.

### FAQ

**Q: How often should I feed these shrimp to my turtle?**
A: Treats should make up no more than 10-15% of your turtle's diet. For a juvenile, 3-5 shrimp every other day is plenty. Adults can handle a slightly larger pinch. Overfeeding can lead to obesity and shell pyramiding.

**Q: Are these shrimp safe for hermit crabs?**
A: Yes, many owners report success. The chitin provides beneficial fiber, and the calcium supports shell growth. Offer one or two shrimp per crab, crushed into smaller pieces if needed.

**Q: Do these need to be rehydrated before feeding?**
A: No, they're safe to feed dry. Aquatic turtles will soak them naturally in the water. For terrestrial species, you can offer them dry or soak them for a few minutes to soften the texture—many owners find dry works fine.
