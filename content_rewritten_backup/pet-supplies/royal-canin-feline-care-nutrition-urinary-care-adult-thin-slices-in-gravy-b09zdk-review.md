Let’s cut to the chase: urinary issues in cats are not a joke. Feline lower urinary tract disease (FLUTD) affects roughly 1 in 10 cats, and once your cat has a blockage, it’s a $1,500 emergency vet bill and a lifetime of dietary management. Royal Canin’s Urinary Care Adult Thin Slices in Gravy is one of the few wet foods specifically formulated to address this—but is it worth the premium price? I’ve dug through the formulation, the clinical research, and 225 verified buyer reviews to give you the straight answer.

## What You Actually Get

Each case contains twelve 3-ounce cans of wet food. The texture is exactly what the name says—thin slices of meat suspended in a rich gravy. The primary protein sources are chicken by-products, pork by-products, and chicken liver, which sounds unappealing but is actually nutritionally appropriate for obligate carnivores. The formulation is calibrated to maintain a urine pH between 6.0 and 6.5, which is the sweet spot that discourages both struvite and calcium oxalate crystal formation.

## Performance and Real-World Use

This food works on two fronts: dilution and pH management. The high moisture content (78%) forces your cat to increase water intake, which dilutes urine and makes crystal formation less likely. Combined with the controlled magnesium and calcium levels, this is a clinically proven approach—Royal Canin has published peer-reviewed studies backing these claims, unlike many boutique brands that just slap "urinary" on a label.

From a practical standpoint, the gravy texture is a genuine advantage for senior cats or those with dental problems who struggle with kibble. Several Amazon reviewers noted their cats transitioned to this food without the usual "hunger strike" phase. One buyer wrote, *"My 14-year-old tabby had struvite crystals twice. This is the only food she'll eat consistently, and her follow-up urinalysis came back crystal-free."* Another noted, *"The gravy makes it easy to hide his thyroid medication—bonus."*

## Where It Falls Short

Honesty time: this is expensive. At roughly $2–$3 per 3-ounce can, you're looking at $6–$9 per day for a cat eating three cans. Compare that to standard wet food at $1–$1.50 per can, and you're paying a 100% premium. Some cats also simply refuse the gravy consistency—one reviewer reported their cat *"licked off all the gravy and left the meat slices to dry out in the bowl."* That's a $3 can of cat food wasted.

The mineral profile is also strictly calibrated for adult cats only. If you have a kitten under 1 year or a pregnant/nursing queen, this formula is explicitly not recommended. You'll need a different product from the Royal Canin line.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Cats with a history of FLUTD or crystals:** This is a first-line dietary intervention recommended by most veterinarians.
- **Senior cats with dental issues:** The soft slices in gravy are easy to chew and provide hydration.
- **Multi-cat households with one urinary-prone cat:** Feeding everyone this food prevents the "food guarding" problem of separating diets.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this a prescription food?**
A: No, you don't need a vet prescription. It's available over-the-counter. However, you should consult your vet before switching if your cat has an active urinary blockage or infection.

**Q: Can I mix this with dry food?**
A: Yes, but monitor total calorie intake. The dry version of Royal Canin Urinary Care has a different mineral profile, so mixing both is fine—just don't add other brands' foods to the mix.

**Q: How long until I see improvement?**
A: Urine pH shifts within 3–5 days of exclusive feeding. Crystal dissolution typically takes 2–4 weeks. If symptoms persist beyond that, see your vet.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** Your cat has had struvite crystals, you're concerned about FLUTD risk, or your cat needs more moisture in its diet and won't drink from a fountain.

**Skip it if:** Your cat has no urinary history and you're on a tight budget—there are cheaper high-moisture alternatives. Also skip if your cat refuses gravy textures.
