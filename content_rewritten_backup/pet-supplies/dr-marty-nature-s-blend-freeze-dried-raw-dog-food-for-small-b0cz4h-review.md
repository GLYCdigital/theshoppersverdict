If you've been circling the raw food debate for your small dog, Dr. Marty's Nature's Blend keeps coming up in every conversation. With a 4.6-star average across 229 ratings, it's one of the better-reviewed freeze-dried raw options on Amazon. But the real question isn't whether it's good — it's whether it's good *for your dog* and *for your budget*. Let's dig in.

**What You Actually Get**

The Nature's Blend formula is a mix of three animal proteins — beef, turkey, and salmon — paired with organic sweet potatoes, carrots, apples, blueberries, and flaxseed. The freeze-drying process removes moisture while preserving the nutritional profile of the raw ingredients, so you're getting something closer to fresh food than the high-heat-extruded kibble most dogs eat daily.

The bag arrives as lightweight, airy nuggets that crumble easily. For small breeds, the pieces are appropriately sized — most owners of Chihuahuas, Yorkies, and Shih Tzus report no need to break them up further. Rehydration is straightforward: add warm water, wait 60–90 seconds, and you've got a soft, palatable meal.

**Real-World Performance**

The most compelling feedback from buyers centers on three things: picky eaters finally eating, coat and skin improvements, and increased energy. One verified purchaser noted, *"My 14-year-old Maltese has never cleaned her bowl in her life. She now finishes every meal and licks the bowl clean."* Another owner of a French Bulldog with chronic itching reported, *"The scratching stopped within two weeks. I wish I'd switched years ago."*

The nutrient density is legit. The first five ingredients are all animal-based proteins, and the inclusion of salmon provides natural omega-3s for skin and joint health. There's no corn, wheat, soy, or artificial coloring — a meaningful difference if your dog has sensitivities.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the downsides. Price is the elephant in the room. At roughly $3–5 per day for a small dog, this costs 3–4 times more than a mid-range kibble. If budget is tight, this isn't the sustainable long-term solution.

Transitioning is also trickier than with kibble. Several reviewers mentioned digestive upset during the first week. One owner wrote, *"My dog had soft stools for about five days. I should have mixed it more gradually."* Start with 25% new food and 75% old, then adjust over 7–10 days.

The rehydration requirement is a practical inconvenience. Once mixed with water, the food must be refrigerated and used within 24–48 hours. That's a non-starter for owners who free-feed or leave food out all day.

**Who Should Buy This**

This food makes sense for three specific types of owners: those with picky eaters who refuse everything else, small dogs with skin or allergy issues that haven't resolved on kibble, and owners who want raw nutrition but are uncomfortable handling fresh raw meat. It's also a strong option for senior small dogs with dental problems who struggle with hard kibble.

Skip it if you need an affordable everyday food, if your dog has a sensitive stomach that reacts to protein variety, or if you can't commit to the rehydration routine.

**FAQ**

**How much should I feed my small dog?**
The feeding guide suggests roughly 1–1.5 patties per 10 lbs of body weight daily, split into two meals. A 10 lb dog typically eats about 1.5–2 ounces per day. Start at the low end and adjust based on your dog's weight and activity level.

**Can I mix this with kibble?**
Yes, but it's not ideal. Mixing dilutes the raw benefits and can make digestion less predictable. If you mix, keep the kibble portion small and consistent.

**Is this a complete and balanced diet?**
Yes. The formula meets AAFCO nutrient profiles for adult maintenance. It's designed as a complete food, not a topper.
