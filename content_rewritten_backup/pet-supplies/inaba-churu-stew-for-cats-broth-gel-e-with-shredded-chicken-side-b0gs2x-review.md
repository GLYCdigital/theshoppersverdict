If your cat turns up their nose at water bowls and you're fighting a losing battle against dehydration, the INABA Churu Stew for Cats with Broth Gelée and Shredded Chicken might be the peace treaty you need. This isn't your standard wet food — it's a hybrid between the wildly popular Churu lickable treats and a proper meal, designed to sneak hydration into even the stubbornest feline.

**What you're actually getting**

Each package contains individual serving trays of broth gelée packed with visible shredded chicken. The texture sits somewhere between a loose jelly and a thin stew — it pours out easily and coats the bowl rather than holding a firm shape. The chicken comes in actual shreds, not pulverized paste, which is a welcome change from cheaper wet foods that rely on mystery meat slurry. As shown in the product image, the broth has a light, appetizing color that signals real poultry rather than artificial gravy.

**How it performs in the real world**

The moisture content here is the headline feature. At over 90% water, this delivers serious hydration per serving. For cats with a history of urinary crystals, constipation, or chronic dehydration, incorporating a couple of these trays daily makes a measurable difference. The gelée format also slows down fast eaters — they have to lap rather than inhale, which reduces the likelihood of regurgitation.

The shredded chicken texture is where this product wins or loses depending on your cat. The shreds are tender and easy to chew, making it suitable for senior cats with dental issues. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"My 16-year-old with no teeth left licks the broth clean and manages the tiny shreds without any trouble."* Another praised its versatility: *"I mix this over kibble and suddenly my picky eater finishes every single bite."*

**What buyers consistently praise**

The hydration angle dominates positive reviews. Owners report shinier coats, better litter box habits, and fewer vet visits for urinary complaints. Picky-eater testimonials are also widespread — the chicken aroma is strong and enticing, which helps win over cats who've rejected other wet foods. The clean ingredient list also earns points: no carrageenan, no grains, no artificial colors, and chicken listed as the first ingredient.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be honest about the limitations. At roughly $2 per serving tray, this is not economical as a full meal replacement. Most buyers use it as a topper or hydration booster, which means the cost adds up quickly if your cat demands it daily. The gelée texture is also polarizing — some cats who prefer chunky pâtés or traditional shreds in gravy may walk away. One reviewer captured the frustration: *"My younger cat loves it, my older one licks the broth and leaves the gelée to dry out in the bowl."*

**Who should buy this**

- **The hydration-challenged household**: If your cat refuses water fountains, this is a reliable way to add fluid intake.
- **Senior cat parents**: The soft texture and easy chewing make it ideal for aging cats with dental pain.
- **Picky eater owners**: The strong chicken scent and real shreds often break through stubborn feeding habits.
- **Multi-cat homes**: The individual trays make portion control simple, though you'll want to separate cats with different preferences.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this be a complete meal?**
A: Technically yes, but it's not designed as a nutritionally complete daily diet. It's better used as a topper, treat, or hydration supplement alongside a balanced kibble or wet food.

**Q: How many servings should I give per day?**
A: Most owners use 1-2 trays daily as a topper. For hydration support, you can give up to one tray per 5 pounds of body weight, but consult your vet for cats with medical conditions.

**Q: Does it need refrigeration after opening?**
A: Yes. Unopened trays are shelf-stable, but once opened, refrigerate and use within 24 hours.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your cat needs more water in their diet, turns up their nose at standard wet food, or you're looking for a clean-ingredient treat that feels like a meal. **Skip it if** your cat is already well-hydrated, prefers chunky textures, or you're on a tight budget — in that case, this is a luxury, not a necessity.
