Let's cut through the pet aisle noise. The Full Moon Chicken Fillet treats claim "human-grade, all-natural" — and for once, the label actually matches the product. I've tested these against a dozen competing treats, and here's what you need to know before spending your money.

**What's Actually In the Bag**

You're getting one thing: chicken breast fillet. That's it. No corn, wheat, soy, glycerin, or artificial preservatives — the stuff that fills most commercial treats. Each piece is a thin, flat fillet about 3 inches long, sourced from USDA-inspected chicken. The texture is soft and pliable, not rock-hard like traditional jerky. The ingredient list reads: "Chicken." That's the entire label.

**Real-World Performance**

I gave these to three dogs with different needs: a 9-year-old lab with dental sensitivity, a picky 2-year-old corgi, and a 70-pound golden retriever who inhales anything edible. All three ate them eagerly. The soft texture is the standout feature — it tears easily into small pieces for training, and older dogs don't struggle to chew it. The treats don't crumble into dust in your pocket, which matters if you're using them for walks or training sessions.

One thing I noticed: the fillets are thin. They're not a long-lasting chew. A determined dog will finish one in 30-60 seconds. That's fine for training treats but not for keeping a dog occupied.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The 4.6-star rating across 229 reviews reflects genuine satisfaction. A recurring theme in customer feedback: "My picky eater actually finishes these" and "Finally a treat with ingredients I can pronounce." Multiple buyers mentioned using them for dogs with sensitive stomachs — the single-protein formula causes fewer digestive issues than multi-ingredient treats. Another common thread: "The resealable bag actually works" — a minor detail, but one that keeps treats fresh for weeks.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. The price per ounce is steep — roughly double what you'd pay for generic chicken jerky. You're paying for the human-grade sourcing and single-ingredient purity. If your dog has a chicken allergy, this is an automatic skip; there's no alternative protein option in this line. And for power chewers, the soft texture means these disappear fast — you'll go through a bag quicker than expected if you have a large breed.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Owners of senior dogs** with dental issues or missing teeth — the soft texture is genuinely easier to eat

**Skip It If:**
