Let’s be honest: most dog treats on Amazon are either rock-hard biscuits that make you worry about your dog’s teeth or greasy jerky strips with ingredient lists that read like a chemistry experiment. Blue Buffalo Stix sit in a different category entirely — soft-moist, chicken-first, and genuinely useful for training. But are they worth the premium? Here’s the breakdown after digging through the specs and 229 verified buyer ratings.

## What You’re Actually Getting

The 13-ounce bag contains roughly 40–50 stix, depending on how the pieces break during shipping. Each stick is about 3 inches long and a quarter-inch wide — think a flattened, chewy twizzler rather than a meaty jerky strip. The texture is what sets these apart: pliable enough to bend without snapping, but firm enough that they don’t crumble into a mess in your pocket.

The ingredient list is genuinely clean for the category. **Real deboned chicken leads the formula**, followed by chicken broth, oats, and barley. No chicken by-product meal, no corn, wheat, or soy, and no artificial preservatives like BHA or ethoxyquin. For dogs with grain sensitivities, these are a solid option — the grains present are whole and identifiable, not mystery fillers.

## Real-World Performance

The soft-moist format is the star here. You can break a single stix into 10–12 small training pieces without any tools, and they stay pliable even after being in a treat pouch for hours. One buyer noted, “My 70-pound lab demolishes these in one bite, but my 8-pound Yorkie takes her time chewing — the texture works for both.” That versatility is rare.

The chicken smell is noticeable — distinctly savory, not artificial. If you’ve ever opened a bag of treats that smelled like a fast-food chicken nugget factory, you know what I mean. Most dogs go nuts for it, but if you’re sensitive to pet food aromas, this one is potent.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The 4.6-star average isn’t an accident. Across the reviews, three themes dominate:

**Picky eater approval.** Multiple owners of notoriously finicky dogs — Shih Tzus, Maltese, and one particularly stubborn Corgi — report these are the only treats their dogs will consistently take. One reviewer wrote, “My dog turns her nose up at everything, but she does tricks for these.”

**Senior dog friendliness.** The soft texture is a genuine win for older dogs with dental issues. Several reviewers specifically mention using these for dogs with missing teeth or sensitive gums, where hard biscuits are no longer an option.

**Training effectiveness.** The small-piece breakability makes portion control easy, and the high-value smell keeps dogs focused. Trainers and agility owners specifically call these out as reliable high-reward treats.

## Where It Falls Short

No product is perfect, and the complaints here are worth noting. **The stix are shorter than many buyers expect.** If you’re picturing long, jerky-like strips, you’ll be surprised — these are more like stubby chew sticks, closer to 3 inches than the 5–6 inches some product photos suggest.

Storage is also a factor. Several reviewers report that if the bag isn’t resealed completely, the treats dry out within a few days and lose their soft texture. And a handful of buyers received bags where the stix had partially melted or stuck together during shipping — a known issue with soft-moist treats in warm weather.

## Who Should Buy These

**Get these if:** You have a picky eater, a senior dog with dental sensitivities, or you need a high-value training treat that breaks into small pieces without crumbling. They’re also a solid choice if you’re avoiding treats with artificial preservatives or by-product meals.

**Skip these if:** Your dog prefers crunchy biscuits, you need long-lasting chew treats (these are gone in seconds, not minutes), or you’re looking for a budget option — the price per ounce runs higher than standard training treats.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
A: Yes, but supervise. The soft texture is easy for puppies to chew, and the small pieces are appropriate for training. Just account for the calories in your puppy’s daily allowance.

**Q: How many calories per stix?**
A: Each stix is about 12 calories — roughly half the calorie count of a typical milk-bone biscuit. That makes them a reasonable choice for frequent training sessions.

**Q: Can these replace a meal?**
A: No. They’re treats, not a complete diet. They don’t meet AAFCO nutritional requirements for a full meal, so stick to using them as supplemental rewards.
