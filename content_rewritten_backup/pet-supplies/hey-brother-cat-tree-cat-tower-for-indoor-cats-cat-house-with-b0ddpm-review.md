**The Hey-brother Cat Tree isn't trying to reinvent the cat tower — it's trying to nail the basics. And for the most part, it does.**

If you've shopped cat furniture recently, you know the market is flooded with flimsy towers that wobble after three weeks or fall apart when a 15-pound cat launches off the top platform. The Hey-brother Cat Tree sits in the mid-range tier, and its 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews suggests it's doing something right. But let's dig into whether it's the right purchase for *your* cat — and *your* living room.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains a four-level tower with an enclosed cubby house at the base, two open platforms, a top perch, and two sisal-wrapped scratching posts. There's also a hanging toy (the type cats lose interest in within a month, but it's a nice touch) and the aforementioned felt pads for floor protection. The dimensions are roughly 30 inches tall and 20 inches wide at the base — compact enough for an apartment corner but substantial enough to feel like real territory for your cat.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The particleboard core is wrapped in a soft faux fleece that's comfortable but not plush. The sisal posts are the standout feature — they're tightly wound and dense, surviving daily scratching sessions without fraying into strings. One reviewer noted, "My cat attacked this thing daily for two months and the posts still look new." That aligns with what I'd expect from this price point.

The stability is decent once assembled. The base is wide enough that a full-speed jump onto the top perch doesn't tip it — though I'd still anchor it to a wall if you have a particularly rambunctious cat. The felt pads do eliminate the wobble on hardwood floors, which is a thoughtful inclusion.

Assembly is the main friction point. You're looking at 30-40 minutes with a screwdriver, and several reviewers mentioned that some brackets lack pre-drilled pilot holes, making alignment tricky. It's not difficult, but it's not the "5-minute setup" some brands claim. Plan for an evening, not a quick break.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The enclosed cubby is the clear crowd-pleaser. Cats love having a hideaway, and multiple reviewers confirmed their cats claimed it within hours. One wrote, "My shy rescue cat finally has a safe space — she sleeps in the house every night." Another praised the size: "Perfect for a 12-pound cat — the platforms are actually big enough to stretch out on."

**Where It Falls Short**

The top perch has no raised lip. Confident cats are fine, but cautious kittens or older cats may feel exposed and avoid it — which defeats the purpose of a vertical tower. The faux fleece also attracts cat hair like a magnet. You'll be lint-rolling this thing weekly. And while the sisal is durable, the posts are vertical only — no angled or horizontal scratching surfaces, which some cats strongly prefer.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Apartment dwellers** with limited floor space who want vertical territory without a massive footprint
- **Owners of one or two cats** in the 8-14 pound range — the platforms are generously sized
- **First-time cat furniture buyers** who want a solid, no-frills tower without spending premium prices

Skip it if you have a Maine Coon or other giant breed — the top perch will feel cramped — or if you need a tower that survives a multi-cat household with daily high-speed chases. This is built for calmer homes.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this hold up with a heavy cat?**
A: The base is stable and the structure holds cats up to roughly 15 pounds comfortably. The top perch is the weak point — it's not reinforced for repeated hard landings from heavier cats.

**Q: Is the cover removable for washing?**
A: No. The fleece is attached, so spot-cleaning with a damp cloth is your option. This is a common complaint in reviews.

**Q: Does it come with all tools needed for assembly?**
A: The included Allen wrench covers most bolts, but you'll need your own Phillips head screwdriver for several brackets.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a dependable, mid-sized cat tree that handles daily scratching and provides a cozy hideaway — at a reasonable price. **Skip it if** you have a giant breed, need washable covers, or want a tower that survives a chaotic multi-cat household.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, honest cat tree that does what it promises without frills. Your cat will likely love the cubby, and you'll tolerate the assembly and hair maintenance. For the price, that's a fair trade.
