The IRIS USA Large Covered Litter Box is the sensible middle child of the litter box world — not a $30 plastic tray, not a $200 robotic spaceship. If you're tired of litter kicked across your bathroom floor but your cat refuses to use top-entry boxes, this one deserves a hard look.

## What You Actually Get

Out of the box: the base pan, the domed cover, and a clear front door flap that swings inward. No tools, no assembly beyond snapping the cover onto the base. The plastic is a sturdy polypropylene that doesn't flex or bow under a 12-pound cat's weight — a real upgrade from the flimsy bins that crack at the corners within six months.

As shown in the product image, the design is plain and functional: white or gray plastic, a recessed lid lip, and a full-width front opening. It's not pretty, but it's not an eyesore in a laundry room either.

## Real-World Performance

The front door flap is the standout feature. Unlike hooded boxes with a small circular opening that cats have to crouch through, this one gives a full frontal entry. For senior cats with arthritis or kittens learning the ropes, that's a meaningful difference. One Amazon reviewer put it simply: "My 14-year-old cat stopped missing the box entirely. She walks right in like it's a door, not a trap."

The high back wall matters more than most shoppers realize. Standard covered boxes let urine pool along the seam where lid meets base — that's where the smell lives no matter how often you scoop. The IRIS design overlaps the lid onto the base, closing that gap. Cleaning is straightforward: lift the lid off, dump, spray down, rinse. The smooth interior doesn't harbor odors like textured plastic does.

Across the 210 ratings, three themes dominate:

1. **Scatter control.** The flap catches litter on the way out. One buyer wrote, "I went from sweeping daily to weekly. The flap does what it says."
2. **Durability.** Multiple reviewers note it survives rambunctious cats that cracked their previous boxes. The polypropylene is genuinely thick.

## Where It Falls Short

Honest downsides, because they exist:

**The flap stiffness.** Several reviewers report cats that initially refused to push through. The flap is springy, not weighted, but some cats hate the sensation on their back. Propping it open with a clothespin for a week usually solves it — but that's a workaround, not a feature.

**Size constraints.** This is "large" for a standard box, but not for big breeds. At 22 inches long, a 16-pound Maine Coon will feel cramped. Measure your cat nose-to-tail before ordering. If they can't turn around comfortably, they'll find somewhere else to go — likely your rug.

**No odor filter.** At this price point, some competitors include a charcoal filter in the lid. This box relies entirely on your litter management. If you're not scooping daily, this won't mask anything.

## FAQ

**Will my cat use this if she's never used a covered box before?**
Usually yes, but give it time. Leave the flap propped open for the first few days. Once she's comfortable inside, lower it gradually. Most cats adapt within two weeks.

**How often do I need to clean it?**
Scoop daily — the enclosed space traps ammonia faster than open trays. Deep clean the entire box every 2-3 weeks with mild soap and water. Avoid bleach; it can irritate sensitive paws.

**Does it fit standard litter scoops?**
Yes, the opening is wide enough for any standard scoop, but the 16-inch height means you'll be reaching down a bit. A long-handled scoop helps.
