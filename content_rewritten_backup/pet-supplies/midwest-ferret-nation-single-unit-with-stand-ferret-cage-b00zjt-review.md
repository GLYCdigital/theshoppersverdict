## What you actually get

The box contains the cage itself (36"L x 24.5"W x 38.5"H), a 6.5" tall stand with locking casters, two leak-proof plastic pans, two ramps, two shelf platforms, and the hardware. The total height with the stand is 62.5 inches. The wire spacing is a tight 1 inch — too narrow for even the smallest ferret head to squeeze through, which is the whole point.

The double doors on the front are the standout feature. Each door is 24.5 inches wide and swings fully open. That's not a small detail — it's the difference between wrestling a sleepy ferret out of a tubular opening and just reaching in and scooping them up. The pans slide out like drawers, which means you can pull one out, wipe it down, and slide it back in under two minutes without disturbing whatever's sleeping on the upper shelf.

## How it holds up in real use

The frame is coated wire with a powder finish that resists the occasional ferret chew attempts and survives regular disinfecting wipes. Owners consistently report the cage lasting 5+ years with no rust or sagging. One reviewer put it plainly: *"I've had mine for 3 years and two ferrets. It's been through multiple moves and still looks brand new."*

The locking casters on the stand are a genuine bonus — you can roll the entire cage to a new spot for deep cleaning, then lock it back down. That's not a feature you'll use daily, but when you need it, it saves your back.

## What owners consistently praise

The overwhelming theme in the positive reviews is the combination of size and accessibility. Ferret owners specifically call out the double doors and pan system. One wrote: *"Cleaning used to take 30 minutes with my old cage. Now it's 5 minutes, tops. The pans just slide out."* Another noted: *"My ferrets actually use the shelves and ramps — they're not just decorative. The cage gives them enough vertical space to feel like they have territory."*

The 1-inch wire spacing also gets mentioned by rat and sugar glider owners who use the cage for multiple species. That versatility expands the buyer pool well beyond ferret keepers.

## Where it falls short — honestly

Assembly is the most common complaint, and it's legitimate. The included hex wrench works, but it's small and you'll be tightening dozens of bolts. Plan for 45–60 minutes. Two people make it dramatically easier — one to hold panels square while the other drives bolts. Don't attempt it alone at 11 PM.

The included plastic shelves and ramps are functional but basic. Many ferrets ignore them, preferring hammocks or just the floor. Budget an extra $30–50 for aftermarket fleece liners and hammocks if you want your ferrets to actually use the vertical space.

The height is also worth measuring. At 62.5 inches with the stand, it won't fit under most standard shelving units or in tighter basement spaces. The stand is removable, which drops the height to 38.5 inches, but then you lose the casters and storage underneath.

## Who should buy this

**Buy it if** you're a first-time ferret owner who wants a cage that's easy to clean and hard to mess up, or a multi-pet household with ferrets and small critters that can share the same cage model. **Buy it if** you value being able to reach your pet without contorting your body.

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget and don't mind a smaller, harder-to-clean single-door cage — you can save $50–70 with a lower-tier option. **Skip it if** you have very limited floor space; the 36" x 24.5" footprint is modest, but the 62.5" height demands ceiling clearance.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I fit two ferrets in this cage?**
Yes. Two adult ferrets will have plenty of room, especially if you add a hammock or two. Three is pushing it — consider the double unit for that.

**Q: Is this cage escape-proof?**
With the 1-inch wire spacing and secure door latches, yes. Ferrets can't squeeze through, and the latches require two-step release that even clever ferrets haven't figured out.

**Q: How often do I need to clean it?**
Daily spot-cleaning of the pans takes under a minute with the slide-out design. A full deep clean with cage disinfectant every 1–2 weeks is sufficient for most ferret households.
