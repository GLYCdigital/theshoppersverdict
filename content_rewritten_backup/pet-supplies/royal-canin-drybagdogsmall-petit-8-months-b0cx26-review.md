## What You're Actually Getting

Royal Canin's Small Dog 8+ Months is a dry kibble formulated specifically for small-breed puppies transitioning out of the growth phase. The bag in the product images shows the classic Royal Canin design — clean, clinical, and informative. Inside, you're getting kibble that's noticeably smaller than standard adult formulas, which matters more than you'd think for a 10-pound dog with a tiny jaw.

The formula targets three specific needs: digestive sensitivity (common in small breeds), dental health through kibble texture, and skin/coat condition via omega fatty acids. The protein comes primarily from poultry by-product meal and chicken — ingredients that often raise eyebrows but are actually highly digestible and nutrient-dense when sourced properly.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this food earns its keep. Small-breed puppies have fast metabolisms and small stomachs, which means they need calorie-dense food that doesn't bloat them. Royal Canin addresses this with a high-energy formula that requires smaller portions — most owners feed between 1/4 and 1/2 cup per day depending on weight.

The kibble shape deserves specific mention. It's designed to slow down fast eaters while still being easy to crunch. One owner noted, "My 9-month-old Maltipoo used to inhale food and vomit. With this, she actually chews, and no more throw-up." That's a real, practical benefit that shows the design isn't just marketing.

Digestive health is the other standout. Multiple reviewers report firmer stools within a week of switching. The prebiotic fiber blend (including beet pulp and FOS) does what it's supposed to do.

## What Owners Consistently Praise

Across the 240 ratings (4.8 stars), the recurring themes are clear:

- **Picky eaters eat it.** One reviewer wrote, "My Shih Tzu refuses everything else. This is the only food she doesn't turn her nose up at."
- **Coat and skin improvements.** "After 3 weeks, his dandruff is gone and his coat is noticeably softer," reported another.
- **Portion control is easy.** The feeding guide on the bag is straightforward, and the kibble size makes it easy to measure accurately.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides.

**The price.** This is a premium product at a premium price. Per-pound, it costs noticeably more than brands like Purina Pro Plan or Hill's Science Diet. If you're on a tight budget, that's a real consideration.

**The bag design.** There's no resealable zipper. Once you open it, you need a clip or transfer to an airtight container. For a food that costs this much, that's an annoying oversight. Stale kibble loses palatability, and dogs notice.

**It's not for adult dogs.** This is specifically for dogs 8–12 months (or up to 18 months for larger small breeds). If you're buying for an adult, you want the adult formula — don't try to stretch this one.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a small-breed puppy (under 22 pounds adult weight) between 8 and 12 months, especially if your dog is a picky eater or has a sensitive stomach.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, your dog is already an adult, or you prefer grain-free formulas — Royal Canin uses rice and corn, which some owners avoid.

## FAQ

**Q: How long should I feed the 8+ months formula?**
A: Until your dog reaches adulthood. For most small breeds, that's around 12 months. Some toy breeds mature earlier, around 10 months. Check with your vet if you're unsure.

**Q: Can I mix this with wet food?**
A: Yes, but adjust the dry portion accordingly. The bag's feeding guide assumes dry-only feeding. Mixing can lead to overfeeding if you don't compensate.

**Q: My puppy is 6 months — is this too early?**
A: Yes. Royal Canin makes a separate puppy formula for dogs under 8 months. The nutritional profile differs significantly, especially in calcium and phosphorus ratios.
