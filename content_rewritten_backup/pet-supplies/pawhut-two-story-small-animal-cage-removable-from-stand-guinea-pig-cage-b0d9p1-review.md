**The PawHut Two-Story Small Animal Cage isn't just another wire box.** At 4.6 stars across 229 ratings, it's earned real traction with guinea pig and rabbit owners. But the removable stand is the feature that actually sets it apart — and it matters more than you'd think.

Here's what you're actually getting, what owners rave about, and where this cage genuinely falls short.

## What's in the Box

You get the two-story wire cage, a deep plastic base tray, two platforms, a ramped ladder between levels, and a detachable metal stand with locking casters. The cage itself measures roughly 30 inches long by 18.5 inches wide and stands about 38 inches tall on the included legs. Remove the stand and it drops to a tabletop height that works on counters or low furniture.

That dual-mode design is the smartest thing PawHut did here. One buyer summed it up: *"I set it up on the floor with the stand, then moved it to my desk when my puppy got curious. Takes two minutes."*

## Real-World Performance

The wire spacing is tight enough for adult guinea pigs and dwarf rabbits — they won't squeeze through. The two levels connect via a ramp with raised crossbars, which gives smaller animals decent traction. Owners of older or arthritic pets should note: the ramp sits at a fairly steep angle, so senior animals may struggle. A few reviewers added their own cardboard or fleece to soften the slope.

The four front doors swing open wide, and the top splits in half for access. Cleaning is straightforward — pull the plastic tray, wipe it down, done. The tray depth is adequate for bedding, though not generous. You'll want a liner underneath if you're using loose paper bedding.

Chew-resistance is the biggest caveat. The wire frame is solid, but the plastic tray corners are the weak point. Guinea pigs that habitually gnaw on cage edges can eventually wear through. One long-term owner warned: *"After eight months, my chewer started denting the tray corners. I lined them with a metal file guard and it's been fine since."*

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Reviewers repeatedly mention three things:

1. **The stand makes floor-to-table transitions effortless.** This is the differentiator.
2. **Cleaning is genuinely easier** than single-door cages. Wide doors, removable tray, no awkward reaching.
3. **The price-to-space ratio.** For the footprint, you get two usable levels — good for households with limited floor space.

## Where It Falls Short

The assembly process tests patience. It's not hard, but it's fiddly — expect 30 to 45 minutes with the included Allen wrench. The instructions are diagram-heavy with minimal text; if you're not handy, watch a video first.

The casters on the stand are functional but small. They roll fine on hard floors, less smoothly on carpet. If you plan to move the cage daily, upgrade the wheels.

And the plastic tray, as mentioned, won't survive a determined chewer indefinitely. Budget for a replacement tray or metal liner if you have a persistent nibbler.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a guinea pig, dwarf rabbit, or ferret and want a cage that works both on the floor and as a tabletop setup. You have limited floor space and want vertical territory. You hate wrestling with tiny doors during cleaning.

**Skip it if:** You own a large breed rabbit (they need more horizontal space — this is too narrow). You have a chronic chewer and don't want to add protective liners. You want a zero-assembly cage.

## FAQ

**Is this cage big enough for two guinea pigs?**
Yes, just barely. Two adults can coexist here, but they'll be more comfortable in the 30-inch width than the 18.5-inch depth. If you have the floor space, a single-story wider cage is better long-term. For one small animal, this is plenty generous.

**Can the stand be removed permanently?**
Yes. Four bolts hold the stand to the cage base. Remove them and the cage sits directly on any flat surface. The locking casters keep the stand stable when it's in use.

**Is the ramp safe for baby animals?**
The crossbars provide decent grip, but the angle is steep. Young or uncoordinated animals may slip. Add a strip of fleece or a textured shelf liner to the ramp for extra traction — most owners end up doing this.

## The Verdict: 4.6/5

**Buy it if** you want a versatile two-level cage that works on the floor or on a tabletop, clean easily, and don't mind a 40-minute assembly session. **Skip it if** you have a serious chewer or a larger breed rabbit that needs horizontal run space.

For the price, this is one of the better small animal cages at this size — the removable stand alone justifies it for most owners. Just budget for a chew guard if you have a determined nibbler.
