**Is this the treat that actually fixes your dog's itchy skin? Probably not overnight — but here's why 225 buyers rate it 4.5 stars anyway.**

Let me save you the research time: BIXBI's Skin & Coat Support treats are a legitimately well-formulated option in a category flooded with "chicken jerky with a fish oil splash" pretending to be specialty nutrition. At 4.5 stars across 225 ratings, the consensus is real — but it's not perfect, and I'll tell you exactly who should skip it.

**What you're actually getting**

The bag delivers a soft, chewy treat about the size of a nickel. The first ingredient is chicken, not a filler — that's a good sign. The skin and coat angle comes from flaxseed (omega-3 fatty acids), vitamin E, and zinc. These are the same nutrients your vet would recommend for dry skin, so the formulation makes sense on paper and translates to real benefits for many dogs.

Each treat runs about 3.5 calories. That's modest for training purposes, but they're not calorie-free — budget accordingly if you have a smaller dog.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The most common theme in positive reviews is the treat's effectiveness for dogs with specific skin issues. One verified purchaser noted, *"My Corgi had flaky patches near her tail for months. Two weeks on these and the flaking stopped. I'm not sure if it's the omega-3s or just that she's getting consistent supplementation, but I'm not switching."* Another owner of a senior Golden Retriever mentioned the soft texture being *"the only treat she can chew without struggling."*

The US-made claim also resonates. Multiple reviewers specifically mentioned trusting the brand more because of the domestic manufacturing — BIXBI publishes their sourcing standards, which is more than you'll get from most treat brands in this price bracket.

**Where it falls short**

The single-protein formulation is chicken. That's a dealbreaker for the growing number of dogs with poultry sensitivities. If your dog is allergic to chicken, this treat is off the table entirely — no fish or venison alternative exists in this specific line.

The texture is also a double-edged sword. While soft treats are great for training and seniors, they dry out noticeably after the bag is opened. I've seen several reviews noting that leftover treats hardened within a few weeks, losing their appeal. The resealable zipper works, but you'll want to use these within a month of opening.

**Who should buy this**

- **Owners of dogs with dry, flaky skin, or dull coats** — this is the core audience, and the reviews suggest it genuinely helps
- **Training-focused owners** — the soft, small size makes them ideal for clicker training or reward-based work
- **Senior dog owners** — the chewy texture is gentle on aging teeth

**Who should skip it**

- **Poultry-allergic dogs** — this treats is chicken-based, period
- **Owners looking for a low-calorie everyday chew** — at 3.5 kcal each, these are best used as a supplement, not a daily snack
- **If you're expecting a miracle cure** — this is a support supplement, not a veterinary treatment. If your dog has severe skin issues, see a vet first.

**FAQ: What buyers actually ask**

**Q: How fast will I see results for my dog's coat?**
Most reviewers reported noticeable changes in 2–4 weeks of daily use. Some saw improvement in a week; others didn't see meaningful change. Consistency matters — these work as a supplement, not a quick fix.

**Q: Can I give these to a puppy?**
Yes, provided your puppy is at least 8 weeks old and eating solid food. The soft texture is actually easier for teething puppies than hard biscuits. Just account for the calories in their daily food intake.

**Q: How many treats per day is safe?**
The bag recommends 2–4 treats for dogs under 30 lbs, and up to 6–8 for larger breeds. That aligns with the 3.5-calorie count. For a 50-lb dog, 6 treats is roughly 21 calories — about 1% of their daily caloric needs. Safe, but don't exceed the recommended amount without adjusting their meals.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** your dog has dry skin, a dull coat, or you want a genuinely well-sourced training treat with a functional purpose. The US-made commitment and thoughtful ingredient list justify the price premium over generic supermarket treats.

**Skip it if** your dog has a poultry allergy, or you need a crunchy treat for dental health — this is soft, and it won't help scrape plaque.

For the dog owner who's tired of treats that are all hype and no substance, BIXBI delivers. It's not revolutionary, but it's honest, effective, and backed by a solid 4.5-star rating for a reason.
