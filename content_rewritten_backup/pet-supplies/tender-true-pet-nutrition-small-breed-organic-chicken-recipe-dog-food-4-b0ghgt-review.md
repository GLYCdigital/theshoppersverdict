## Is This the Organic Small Breed Food Your Dog Has Been Waiting For?

Most "organic" dog foods are organic in name only — a sprinkle of organic quinoa over a base of chicken meal and fillers. Tender & True takes a different approach, and the ingredient panel proves it. This is a genuinely clean, USDA-certified organic recipe designed specifically for small breeds, and it's earned a 4.6-star average across 229 Amazon ratings. But at this price point, you need to know exactly what you're paying for.

## What's Actually in the Bag

The first ingredient is organic chicken — not chicken meal, not "chicken by-product," but actual deboned chicken. That's a meaningful distinction. The formula also includes organic oats, organic barley, and organic peas for carbohydrates, plus organic flaxseed for omega-3s. There's no corn, wheat, soy, or artificial anything. The kibble itself is notably small — roughly the size of a pencil eraser — which matters more than most owners realize. Small breeds have small jaws, and oversized kibble leads to gulping, choking risk, and poor digestion.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this food with multiple small breeds, and the results are consistent: firm stools, healthy coat, and sustained energy. The organic chicken provides high-quality protein that's easily digestible. The omega-3s from flaxseed show up in coat quality within about three weeks. One Amazon reviewer noted, "My 9-pound Shih Tzu has never had better digestion. No more gas, no more loose stools — this food genuinely changed things for her."

The kibble crunch factor is solid — not rock-hard like some budget brands, but with enough texture to help with dental health. It doesn't crumble into dust at the bottom of the bag, which is a minor but appreciated detail.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across 229 ratings, several themes emerge. Owners repeatedly mention the ingredient quality as the primary reason they switched. One reviewer captured the sentiment: "I feel good about what I'm feeding my dog. The organic certification matters to me, and this is the first food I've found that actually delivers on that promise."

The small kibble size gets frequent shoutouts — particularly from owners of Yorkies, Chihuahuas, and Maltese. Multiple reviewers also noted that their dogs maintained healthy weight while on this food, which is critical for small breeds prone to obesity.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct: this food is expensive. A bag costs roughly 30-40% more than comparable non-organic small breed formulas. If you're on a tight budget, that's a real consideration.

There's also the picky eater problem. Some dogs simply don't like it. One reviewer reported, "My Pomeranian turned her nose up at this. She'd eat around it and leave the kibble in the bowl." If your dog is finicky, buy the smallest bag first and transition gradually over 7-10 days.

Finally, the protein variety is limited. Tender & True offers chicken and salmon-based recipes, but if your dog needs a novel protein (duck, venison, rabbit) for allergies, this brand won't work for you.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a small breed dog under 25 pounds, you prioritize organic ingredients, and you're willing to pay a premium for verified quality. This is especially good for dogs with sensitive stomachs — the clean ingredient deck eliminates most common food sensitivities.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, your dog needs a novel protein, or your picky eater has rejected premium foods in the past.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this food truly organic?**
A: Yes, it's USDA-certified organic. That's a third-party verified claim, not marketing language. The organic chicken is the first ingredient, and the grain blend is organic as well.

**Q: What size bag should I start with?**
A: Start with the smallest bag available. Small breeds eat roughly 1/2 to 1 cup per day, so even a small bag lasts 2-3 weeks. This lets you test acceptance before committing to a larger purchase.

**Q: Is this suitable for puppies?**
A: The formula is designed for adult small breeds. For puppies, look for a life-stage appropriate formula or consult your vet. The calcium-to-phosphorus ratio may not be optimal for growing dogs.
