## What you actually get

The box contains five glass panels (four sides plus a base), a top mesh screen with a hinged access door, and two sliding front doors. No tools included — you supply a screwdriver. As shown in the product image, the silicone-sealed corners are clean and the glass is 3mm thick, which feels appropriately sturdy for a tank this size. The front doors slide on plastic tracks, and the mesh top is aluminum frame with a fiberglass screen.

Assemble it yourself. Budget 30–45 minutes, plus a full 24 hours for the silicone to cure before adding substrate and animals. Several buyers noted this in their reviews — one wrote, "Took my time, followed the instructions, and it's been holding strong for 6 months." Rushing the cure time is the #1 cause of leaks and panel shifting.

## Real-world performance

The full-glass design is the standout feature. Unlike screen-fronted terrariums, this holds humidity remarkably well — a 70–80% humidity level for crested geckos or dart frogs stays stable for hours without misting. The front doors are the right call for accessibility. You can reach in to change a water dish or remove waste without disturbing the thermal gradient, which matters more than most first-time keepers realize.

The top screen handles heat lamps correctly. The mesh is fine enough to keep crickets in but open enough that a 40-watt basking bulb raises the basking spot appropriately. The hinged top door is a nice touch for feeding without opening the main doors.

**The catch:** The sliding door tracks have no bottom stop. Substrate inevitably gets kicked into the channel, and the doors become gritty and harder to slide. You'll be cleaning the tracks weekly — more often if you use loose substrate like coconut fiber. This is the most common complaint across the 210 ratings. One verified buyer put it bluntly: "Great tank, but the doors are the weak point. I vacuum the track every week."

## Who should buy this

- **New reptile keepers** starting with a juvenile leopard gecko, crested gecko, or small corn snake. The size is appropriate for young animals (you'll upgrade in 12–18 months).
- **Invertebrate keepers** — tarantulas, isopods, or mantises. Humidity control is excellent.
- **Hobbyists on a budget** who want a functional glass terrarium without paying for brand-name markup.

**Skip it if** you're housing an adult reptile that needs more floor space, or if you want a fully escape-proof enclosure for arboreal species that climb the screen top. The mesh lid is secure but not lockable — a determined snake can push it up.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this tank escape-proof for small snakes?**
A: The front doors close flush and the top screen sits tight, but there's no locking mechanism. A juvenile corn snake under 20 inches won't push through, but a determined adult could lift the mesh top. Use a clip if you have an escape artist.

**Q: Can I use an under-tank heater with this?**
A: Yes, but place it on the bottom glass before adding substrate. The glass conducts heat efficiently, and the 24-hour silicone cure ensures the base is fully sealed. Just don't let the heater overlap the silicone seams — thermal expansion could weaken them.

**Q: How many gallons is this "small" size?**
A: The dimensions are roughly 12" × 12" × 18" (30 × 30 × 45 cm), which works out to about 10 gallons. Adequate for juveniles and small species, not for adults.
