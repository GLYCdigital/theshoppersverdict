# Two Dogs, One Piece of Furniture That Doesn't Scream "Crate"

Most double dog crates look like holding cells dropped into your living room. The Hzuaneri is different — it's built like an actual cabinet, with a wood grain exterior that could pass for a sideboard or media console if you don't look too closely. That's the entire point, and it mostly delivers.

## What You Actually Get

The crate ships flat-packed in a heavy box. Inside you'll find the wood-framed panels, two steel wire doors with powder-coated finish, hardware, and an assembly guide. There's no included tray liner or dog bed — that's separate. The dimensions vary by size option you order, but the structure is consistent: two side-by-side compartments, each with its own front door and a shared divider wall.

The frame is real wood — not particleboard pretending to be wood — wrapped in a walnut or espresso-style finish depending on the variant. The doors are thick steel bars spaced closely enough that even determined small-breed chewers won't get a muzzle through. As shown in the product image, the top surface is flat and usable, though I'd caution you about putting anything heavy on it (more on that below).

## Real-World Performance

The dual-door design is the standout feature. You can open one side to let a dog out while the other stays secured — genuinely useful if you're crating two dogs with different schedules or one is recovering from surgery. The latches are slide-bolt style, easy to operate one-handed, and secure enough that a determined dog hasn't figured them out based on buyer reports.

The wood frame does more than look good. It dampens sound compared to wire crates, and the solid panels on three sides give dogs that den-like security that reduces anxiety. Multiple buyers noted their dogs settled faster in this than in their previous wire crates. One reviewer wrote, "My anxious rescue finally stops pacing when the door closes. The solid sides make a huge difference."

The heavy-duty claim holds up for chewing. The steel bars are thicker than typical wire crates, and the powder coating prevents rust and resists tooth marks. Owners of determined chewers reported no bar damage after months of use — though the wood frame around the door openings isn't chew-proof if your dog targets the edges.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The cam-lock connectors require precise alignment, and the instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text. Expect 45–60 minutes with two people. One buyer said, "Took me two hours solo and I had to redo two panels because I misread the diagram." Bring patience.

The top panel issue is real. The flat surface looks like it should hold a lamp or plants, but several buyers reported the MDF top bowing under adult body weight. It's fine for decor — do not sit on it.

Sizing is the second issue. The dimensions run conservative. The largest option fits two small-to-medium dogs (say, a Shih Tzu and a Corgi) comfortably but will be cramped for two Golden Retrievers. Measure your dogs' shoulder height and length before ordering — this is not a crate you want to size up after assembly.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Apartment dwellers** who want a crate that doesn't announce itself as a crate
- **Two-dog households** with small-to-medium breeds who need separate access
- **Owners of anxious dogs** who benefit from the enclosed, den-like feel
- **Design-conscious pet parents** who'd otherwise hide a wire crate under a blanket

Skip it if you have two large breeds, need something you can assemble in 15 minutes, or want a crate that doubles as a bench seat.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I take the divider out to make one large crate?**
No. The divider is fixed — this is strictly two separate compartments. If you need one large crate, look elsewhere.

**Q: Will this fit under a standard 30-inch counter or table?**
The height varies by size option, but the smaller variants are around 24–26 inches tall. Check the exact dimensions for the size you're ordering.

**Q: How do I clean it?**
The interior floor is smooth and wipes down easily. The wood frame needs only a damp cloth — avoid soaking it. The steel doors can be wiped with pet-safe cleaner.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a crate that functions as furniture, have two small-to-medium dogs, and don't mind a patient assembly session.

**Skip it if** you need a quick-assembly solution, have large breeds, or want a crate you can sit on.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — It's not perfect, but for the dual-dog furniture category, this is near the top. The build quality justifies the price; the assembly frustration and sizing conservatism keep it from a perfect score.
