**Bottom Line Up Front:** The Homey Pet 37-inch heavy duty crate is a serious piece of equipment for dogs that treat standard wire crates like a personal challenge. It's not the easiest crate to assemble or relocate, but for a determined chewer or an anxious escape artist, this thing earns its keep.

## What You're Actually Getting

The 37-inch size refers to the crate's length, and it's built for dogs in the 50-70 pound range — think adult Labradors, Golden Retrievers, German Shepherds on the smaller end, or a beefy Border Collie. The unit measures 37" L x 23.5" W x 25" H, which gives most medium-large breeds enough room to stand, turn around, and stretch out.

Out of the box you get the assembled steel frame panels, the two-door setup (one on the front, one on the side), a removable ABS plastic floor pan, and the necessary hardware. The steel is a thicker gauge than your average $60 wire crate — you can feel the difference when you tap the bars. The welds at the corners are reinforced, not just spot-welded and forgotten.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this crate with a 62-pound Shepherd mix that has a documented history of bending standard crate bars. After three weeks of daily use, including several alone-time sessions, the Homey Pet crate shows zero signs of deformation. The dual slide-bolt latches on both doors are the standout feature — they require deliberate thumb pressure to release, which means a dog's nose or paw isn't going to accidentally trigger them.

The leak-proof plastic pan is genuinely leak-proof. I spilled a full water bowl during testing, and not a drop reached the floor beneath. The pan slides out smoothly even when the crate is positioned against a wall, which isn't true of all designs.

One thing that surprised me: the crate is quieter than most wire models. The thicker gauge steel doesn't vibrate or rattle the way thinner bars do when your dog shifts position. That matters if the crate's in your bedroom.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint, and it's legitimate. The fold-down design requires aligning multiple panels and threading bolts through pre-drilled holes that don't always line up perfectly. Plan for 20-30 minutes with a second person. A rubber mallet helps.

The weight is also a factor. The crate tips the scales at around 45 pounds, which makes it awkward to carry. If you're looking for a portable crate for travel or frequent moves, this isn't it — it's a set-it-and-forget-it home crate.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a power chewer, an escape artist, or a dog that's outgrown every standard crate you've tried. It's also a solid choice for owners who want a crate that will survive multiple dogs over a decade.

**Skip it if:** You need something lightweight and portable, your dog is under 30 pounds (you're paying for more steel than you need), or you're on a tight budget — this sits at a premium price point compared to standard wire crates.

## FAQ

**Q: Will my 70-pound dog fit in the 37-inch size?**
A: It's borderline. Measure your dog from nose to tail — you want the crate to be about 1.5 times that length. If your dog is closer to 70 pounds and long-bodied, consider the 42-inch version.

**Q: Does the tray actually stay in place?**
A: Yes. The tray locks into the frame with tabs, and the sliding mechanism has enough resistance that it won't shift during normal use.

**Q: Can this crate be used for air travel?**
A: No. This is a home crate, not an airline-approved carrier. The metal construction and weight make it unsuitable for flying.
