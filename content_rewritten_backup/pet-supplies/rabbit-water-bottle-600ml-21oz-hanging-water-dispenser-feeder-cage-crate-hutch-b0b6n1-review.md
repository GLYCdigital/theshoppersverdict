**Is this the last water bottle you'll buy for your rabbit? Probably not — but it's close, if your setup matches its quirks.**

At 600ml (21oz), this hanging dispenser is aimed at rabbit owners tired of refilling tiny 250ml bottles twice a day. It holds enough for a medium rabbit (4–6 lbs) for roughly 24 hours, depending on how much your animal drinks. The clear plastic body is a practical touch — you can see the water line without tilting the cage or sticking a finger in the spout.

**What you actually get:** The bottle, a metal ball-bearing spout, a plastic mounting bracket with two screws, and a wire hanger. Assembly takes under two minutes — slide the bottle into the bracket, hang it on the cage bars, and you're done. The spout uses a standard ball-bearing mechanism, which is the same design found on most mid-range rodent bottles. It works, but it's not leak-proof by magic — it's leak-resistant.

**Real-world performance:** The ball-bearing spout does its job when the bottle is properly angled. Position it at a slight downward tilt (10–15 degrees) and the water flows when your rabbit licks, then stops cleanly. One verified buyer noted: *"My lop-eared rabbit figured it out in five minutes. No drips on the cage floor for the first week."* That's the good news. The bad news? The mounting bracket is the weak link. It's designed for wire cage bars up to about half an inch thick. If your hutch has solid walls or thicker gauge bars, you'll need zip ties or a different mounting system. Another reviewer put it plainly: *"The bottle itself is fine — the bracket is garbage for anything but standard wire cages."*

**Where it falls short, honestly:**  
- **Plastic durability:** The bottle is made from a lightweight PET plastic. It's fine for indoor use, but if you leave it outside in freezing temps, the water expands and can crack the body. Several winter-season buyers reported exactly that.  
- **Spout angle is fixed.** You can't rotate the spout to accommodate a shallow litter tray or a low-profile cage. If your rabbit's cage has a ledge, you may need to raise the bottle with a spacer.  
- **Cleaning is fiddly.** The wide mouth (about 1.5 inches) lets you use a bottle brush, but the spout assembly requires disassembly to scrub the ball bearing properly. Neglect that, and algae builds up fast.

**Who should buy this:**  
- **Rabbit owners with standard wire cages** — this is a solid, affordable upgrade from smaller bottles.  
- **Guinea pig owners** — the 600ml size works well for pairs, and the ball spout suits their drinking style.  
- **Indoor use primarily.** If your hutch is outdoors in a cold climate, buy a glass bottle instead.

**Who should skip it:**  
- Owners of solid-wall hutches or wooden cages — you'll fight the bracket more than you'll enjoy the bottle.  
- Anyone with a chewer. The plastic cap and spout ring are not chew-proof. A determined rabbit can gnaw through the cap in a weekend.

**FAQ:**  
**Q: Does this bottle leak?**  
A: Not if you mount it at the correct angle (spout slightly lower than the base). If it leaks, check the rubber gasket on the cap — it can shift during shipping. Re-seat it and tighten firmly.

**Q: Can I use this for a baby rabbit?**  
A: Yes, but the spout is standard size. Young rabbits (under 8 weeks) may struggle with the ball mechanism. Offer a shallow water dish until they're bigger.

**Q: How do I clean the spout?**  
A: Unscrew the cap, pull out the ball bearing, and rinse both parts in warm soapy water. Do this weekly — it takes two minutes and prevents clogging.

**The Verdict:**  
**Buy it if** you have a standard wire cage, want a no-frills 600ml bottle, and don't mind a little DIY if the bracket doesn't fit perfectly.  
**Skip it if** you need a frost-proof bottle, have a solid-wall enclosure, or want a chew-resistant design.  

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A functional, mid-tier bottle that does the basics well but cuts corners on mounting hardware and cold-weather durability. For the price, it's a fair deal — just know its limits before you buy.
