**What you're actually buying**

Each 13-oz can contains a pâté-style wet food with beef listed as the first ingredient, followed by meat broth, liver, and rice. The protein clock comes in at 36% on a dry matter basis, which is notably higher than the 28–30% you'll find in Purina's standard Pro Plan puppy formulas. That extra protein matters for breeds like Labs, Goldens, and German Shorthairs that pack on muscle fast during their first 18 months.

You're also getting DHA from fish oil — the omega-3 fatty acid linked to cognitive development. This isn't just marketing; it's the same ingredient science used in Purina's veterinary diets. The texture is smooth and spreadable, not chunky, which makes it easy to mix with kibble or use as a topper for transitioning puppies off mother's milk.

**Real-world performance**

I tested this with a 14-week-old Labrador who was burning through kibble like it was nothing. Within three days of switching to this as a half-and-half mix with dry food, her energy stabilized — fewer post-meal crashes, more consistent play sessions. The moisture content (78%) also helped, since she's one of those dogs that would rather lick a wall than drink from a bowl.

One thing I noticed: the cans are dense. A single 13-oz can feeds roughly a 20-pound pup for two days when mixed with kibble. That's decent value per can, but the per-ounce cost is about 30% higher than Purina One puppy wet food. You're paying for the protein density and the Sport line's performance focus.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The Amazon reviews at 4.6 stars across 229 ratings tell a consistent story. One verified buyer wrote: *"My GSP puppy was underweight at 8 weeks — this put weight on him properly without giving him diarrhea like other high-protein foods did."* Another owner of a working-line Border Collie noted: *"He's a picky eater but scarfs this down. I've tried three other wet foods and this is the only one he finishes."*

The most common praise centers on digestion. Multiple reviewers mention that their puppies had soft stools on other brands but firmed up within a week on this formula. That's likely due to the rice content — it's a gentle carbohydrate source that's easier on young digestive systems than corn or wheat.

**Where it falls short**

Here's the honest tradeoff list:

- **The beef smell is strong.** It's not unpleasant, but it's noticeably more pungent than chicken-based puppy foods. If you're sensitive to pet food odors, this might bother you at feeding time.
- **Dented cans happen.** The aluminum is thin, and a few 1-star reviews mention damaged cans on arrival. Check each can before feeding — if the seal's compromised, return it.
- **Not ideal for toy breeds.** The calorie density (about 1,150 kcal/kg) is geared toward medium and large breeds. A 5-pound Chihuahua puppy would need tiny portions, and the cans would go bad before you finish them.

**Who should buy this**

- **Sporting breed owners** — Labs, Goldens, Pointers, Setters, and Spaniels that need high protein for muscle development
- **Puppy owners dealing with picky eaters** — the strong beef flavor and smooth texture win over most reluctant pups
- **Owners transitioning from raw or home-cooked diets** — the ingredient list is clean enough for a gradual switch

**Skip it if** you're on a tight budget, have a toy breed, or your puppy has a known beef sensitivity.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I feed this as a complete meal, or only as a topper?**
It's formulated as a complete and balanced food for puppies up to one year old. You can feed it alone, but most owners mix it with kibble to balance cost and texture variety.

**Q: How does this compare to the dry Sport formula?**
The wet version has higher moisture and a softer texture, but the dry formula has slightly more protein per cup. Many owners use both — dry for meals, wet for training treats or toppers.

**Q: At what age should I switch off this food?**
Purina recommends feeding until 12 months for large breeds and 9–12 months for smaller breeds. After that, transition to the adult Sport formula over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a high-protein wet food that supports growth, digestion, and hydration in a high-energy puppy — and you don't mind paying a premium for those benefits.

**Skip it if** your puppy is sensitive to beef, you're on a strict budget, or you have a toy breed with tiny portion needs.
