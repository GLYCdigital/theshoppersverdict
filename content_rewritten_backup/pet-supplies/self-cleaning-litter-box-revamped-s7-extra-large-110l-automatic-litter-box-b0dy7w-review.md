Let’s be blunt: a 3.2-star average on Amazon for a $400+ automatic litter box is a red flag you shouldn’t ignore. But it’s not the whole story. The Revamped-S7 (110L) is a massive unit that promises weekly cleaning instead of daily scooping. After digging through 160 ratings and cross-referencing the specs, here’s what you’re actually buying.

**What’s in the Box**
You get the main unit (which is genuinely enormous), a waste drawer liner, a power adapter, and a quick-start guide. There’s no included litter scoop or mat, which feels cheap at this price point. The S7 measures roughly 24” x 22” x 27” — this is not a subtle piece of furniture. It’s taller than most side tables and requires a dedicated corner.

**Build Quality & Performance**
The 110L capacity refers to the *overall* bin volume, not the waste drawer. The actual waste receptacle holds about 8-10 days of waste for a single cat (less for multi-cat households). The sifting mechanism uses a rotating drum design with a clumping rake. When it works, it works well — the clumps drop cleanly into the sealed drawer, and the carbon filter does a decent job containing odor for the first few days.

The motor is surprisingly quiet, which buyers consistently noted. One verified owner wrote: *“I was bracing for a blender sound at 3 AM, but it’s quieter than my old automatic feeder.”* That’s the high point.

**Where It Falls Short**
Here’s where the 3.2 rating makes sense. The most repeated complaints in the reviews revolve around three specific issues:

1. **Sensor calibration problems.** Multiple users report the unit either cycles *too often* (waking light sleepers) or *not at all* when the litter level drops below a certain threshold. The sensor that detects waste mass is finicky with lightweight, unscented litters.
2. **The drawer liner is proprietary.** You cannot use standard grocery bags. You must buy the Revamped-brand refills, which adds a recurring cost that isn’t disclosed on the main listing page until you’re in checkout.
3. **Cat refusal rate.** This is the biggest silent killer. The drum is enclosed, and some cats — especially seniors or those with whisker sensitivity — refuse to enter. One review noted: *“I gave it three weeks. My cat peed on the floor next to it every day until I returned it.”*

**Who Should Buy This**
This is for a **single-cat household** with a **young, curious cat** (under 5 years old) that isn’t picky about enclosed spaces. It’s also suited for tech-savvy owners who don’t mind troubleshooting the app (which does sync with Alexa, but the setup requires a 2.4GHz network — 5GHz routers will fail to pair). If you have multiple cats or a senior cat, skip this entirely.

**FAQ**

*Q: Can I use any clumping litter?*
A: No. Use fine-grain clumping litter only. Crystal litters confuse the sensor and cause the drum to over-rotate, scattering dust everywhere.

*Q: How often does the waste drawer need emptying?*
A: For one cat, expect 7-9 days. For two cats, plan on every 4-5 days. The "110L" marketing refers to the total volume, not the waste capacity.

*Q: What happens during a power outage?*
A: The unit locks in place. Your cat cannot use it until power returns. There is no battery backup. This is a dealbreaker for anyone in areas with frequent outages.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have one young cat, a stable power grid, and you’re willing to buy proprietary liners indefinitely. The quiet motor and genuine weekly-cleaning convenience are real benefits when the sensors cooperate.

**Skip it if:** You have multiple cats, a senior cat, or you want a set-and-forget appliance. The sensor finickiness and cat-refusal risk are too high.

**Rating: 3.3/5** — A promising concept with reliability issues that make it a gamble, not a guarantee. If you buy it, keep your return window open and your old litter box stored for the first month.
