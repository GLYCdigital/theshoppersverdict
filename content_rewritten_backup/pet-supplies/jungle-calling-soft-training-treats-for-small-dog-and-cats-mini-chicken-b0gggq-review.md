If you've ever fumbled with a crumbly biscuit while trying to reinforce a "sit" in the 3-second window trainers talk about, you understand why treat size and texture matter. The Jungle Calling Soft Training Treats are engineered for exactly that moment — and for the small-pet households that usually get overlooked.

These are mini chicken bites, roughly the size of a pea, and that's the entire point. As shown in the product image, they come in a compact 4.5-ounce stand-up pouch that's easy to grab mid-walk or keep clipped to a treat bag. Each pouch holds about 350 pieces, and at 3.5 calories per treat, you can run through a solid 15-minute training session without blowing your dog's daily calorie budget.

## What You Actually Get

The pouch contains soft, moist, chicken-based morsels. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: chicken, chicken liver, tapioca starch, glycerin, and natural preservatives. No corn, no wheat, no soy, no artificial dyes. For small-breed owners who've read the fine print on competing treats, that's a meaningful differentiator.

The texture deserves specific mention. These treats are soft enough to pinch apart but firm enough to hold their shape in a treat pouch. That's a harder balance to hit than most manufacturers manage — either treats turn to powder in your pocket or they're rubbery and hard for senior pets to chew. Jungle Calling lands in the sweet spot.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

Across 225 ratings (4.5 stars), the most consistent praise centers on versatility. One reviewer whose household includes both a 6-pound Yorkie and a 9-year-old cat noted, "I bought these hoping my picky cat would accept them, and now both pets come running when they hear the pouch." Another long-time trainer mentioned using them for "high-value recall practice at the dog park — the smell is strong enough to get attention but not offensive to humans."

The resealable pouch draws mixed feedback. The zipper closure works initially, but several buyers report that the treats lose moisture after a few weeks. One 5-star review included a practical workaround: "I transfer half the bag into a small glass jar. The rest stays in the pouch. No freshness issues that way."

## Where It Falls Short

The moisture issue is the most cited drawback. If you're a once-in-a-while trainer who won't finish the pouch within a month, you'll want a separate airtight container. The treats can develop a slightly harder exterior as they dry, which defeats the purpose for dental-sensitive pets.

Also, while the chicken liver inclusion boosts palatability, it's worth noting that these are training treats, not meal replacements. The protein content (roughly 18%) is adequate, but don't substitute these for a balanced diet — they're meant for reinforcement, not nutrition.

## Who Should Buy These

Get these if you have a small dog (under 20 pounds), a cat, or both. The bite size works for all of them. They're particularly well-suited for:

- Puppy owners in the critical socialization window (8-16 weeks) who need frequent, low-calorie reinforcement
- Senior pets with missing teeth or sensitive gums — the soft texture is genuinely gentle
- Multi-pet households where one bag needs to serve both a dog and a cat

Skip them if you have a large-breed dog. The pieces are so small that big dogs will either inhale them without noticing or you'll spend the whole session dispensing individual treats rather than training.

## FAQ

**Can these be broken into smaller pieces for very tiny puppies?**
Yes — the texture tears cleanly, so you can halve or quarter each treat. That brings the calorie count down to roughly 1-2 kcal per piece, which is ideal for 2-3 pound puppies.

**Are these suitable for cats with poultry allergies?**
No. Chicken and chicken liver are the primary ingredients, so these are absolutely not for pets with poultry sensitivities. For those pets, look for fish-based training treats instead.

**How long does an opened pouch stay fresh?**
Expect about two to three weeks at peak softness if you store it in the original pouch and press the air out before sealing. Transferring to a glass jar extends that to roughly six weeks.
