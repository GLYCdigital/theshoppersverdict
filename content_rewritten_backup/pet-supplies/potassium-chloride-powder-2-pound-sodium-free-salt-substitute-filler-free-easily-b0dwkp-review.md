Let's cut through the noise: this is a 2-pound bag of pure potassium chloride powder. It's not fancy. It's not flavored. But for anyone on a sodium-restricted diet — or managing blood pressure — it's a legitimate workhorse that delivers exactly what it promises.

## What You Actually Get

The package is straightforward: a resealable pouch containing 2 pounds (907 grams) of fine white powder. As shown in the product image, it looks like fine table salt but with a noticeably finer texture. There's no scoop inside, so have a measuring teaspoon ready. At roughly 1 gram per 1/4 teaspoon, you're looking at about 900 servings — that's a solid 3-6 months of daily use depending on how liberally you sprinkle.

## Performance and Real-World Use

The powder dissolves remarkably well. I tested it in cold water, hot broth, and during cooking — no clumping, no gritty residue. The taste profile is where things get interesting. At low doses (replacing 20-30% of your salt), it's nearly undetectable. Push it higher, and you'll hit that classic potassium chloride bitterness. The smart approach: use it as a partial replacement, not a 1:1 swap.

Buyers consistently highlight the value. One verified purchaser noted, "This lasts forever and works perfectly for my low-sodium diet. I mix it 50/50 with regular salt and can't tell the difference." Another mentioned using it for homemade electrolyte drinks: "Dissolves completely, no weird texture. Way cheaper than buying electrolyte packets."

## Where It Falls Short

Three honest drawbacks. First, the lack of a scoop is an annoyance — measuring by guesswork with potassium chloride is risky, especially for people with kidney issues. Second, the powder is hygroscopic; if you live in a humid climate and don't press the seal tight, you'll get clumps. Third, the taste ceiling is real. Anyone expecting this to perfectly mimic salt at full replacement will be disappointed. It's a tool, not a miracle.

## FAQ

**Q: How much should I use to replace salt?**
Start at 1/4 teaspoon per meal, mixed with food. Never take it straight on an empty stomach — it can cause stomach irritation. Most users find a 50/50 blend with regular salt hits the sweet spot.

**Q: Is this safe for daily use?**
For healthy individuals, yes — the body regulates potassium well. But if you take ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics, or have kidney issues, talk to your doctor first. This is concentrated, and dosing errors have real consequences.

**Q: Does it work in a salt grinder?**
No. The powder is too fine and will clog the mechanism. Use a shaker with larger holes or just spoon it.
