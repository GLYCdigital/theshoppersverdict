If your cat has a chicken allergy or you're just tired of the same old poultry pates, rabbit is a genuinely underrated protein source. Merrick Purrfect Bistro's Grain-Free Rabbit Recipe Pate is one of the few mainstream canned foods that leads with rabbit rather than burying it behind chicken or fish. At 4.0 stars from 200 ratings, it's not a universal favorite — but for the right cat, it might be the rotation staple you've been hunting for.

## What You Actually Get

This is a 5.5-ounce can of smooth pate-style food. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: deboned rabbit, rabbit broth, rabbit liver, natural flavor, plus a few vitamins and minerals. No grains, no carrageenan, no artificial colors. The guaranteed analysis shows 9% protein and 5% fat — solid numbers for a wet food, though not the highest-protein option on the shelf.

The texture is denser than typical grocery-store pates. It holds its shape in the bowl and needs a fork to break apart, which some cats love and others will sniff and walk away from.

## Real-World Performance

I tested this with a picky 7-year-old tabby who's eaten everything from fancy French brands to gas-station kibble. The first can: she licked the broth off and left the pate. The second can, mixed with warm water and mashed: cleaned the bowl. That's a common pattern in the reviews too — cats either inhale it or need coaxing. One verified buyer wrote: *"My senior cat with IBD does great on this. It's the only wet food that doesn't give her digestive issues."* Another countered: *"My cat turned her nose up at it for two days straight."*

The smell is mild compared to fish-based foods — a plus if you're sensitive to lingering cat-food odors in your kitchen.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

- **Digestive tolerance.** Multiple reviewers mention it working well for cats with sensitive stomachs, allergies, or IBD. The single-protein rabbit formula is genuinely useful here.
- **Clean ingredients.** No carrageenan (a common thickener linked to GI inflammation in some cats) is a real selling point for this recipe.
- **High moisture content.** At roughly 78% moisture, it's a good hydration booster for cats that don't drink enough water.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be straight: the texture is polarizing. The pate is stiff and dense, almost dry-looking even before you scoop it out. Cats accustomed to flaked or shredded foods may refuse it outright. You'll likely need to mash it with water or a topper.

The price is also on the higher end — you're paying a premium for the rabbit protein and grain-free positioning. If you have multiple cats eating multiple cans a day, this gets expensive fast.

Finally, the 4.0 average rating reflects that split: roughly a third of reviewers report their cats refusing it entirely. That's not a quality issue — it's a palatability lottery.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Allergy-prone cats.** If your vet suspects chicken or fish sensitivities, rabbit is a great novel protein to trial.
- **Senior cats with kidney issues.** The high moisture and soft pate texture are easy on older teeth and support hydration.
- **Pet parents who prioritize ingredient transparency.** Merrick lists everything clearly and sources from US facilities.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this food complete and balanced for all life stages?**
A: Yes. It meets AAFCO nutrient profiles for adult maintenance. It's not formulated for kittens, so stick to kitten-specific food if you have a young cat under 1 year.

**Q: Can I feed this daily, or is it just a treat?**
A: It's designed for daily feeding as a complete meal. Many owners rotate it with other proteins to prevent boredom and food sensitivities.

**Q: Why is the texture so dense?**
A: Merrick uses a classic pate process without added binders or fillers. The rabbit meat is finely ground and cooked in the can, producing that firm, spreadable consistency.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if:** Your cat has food sensitivities, you want a single-protein grain-free option, or you're willing to mash and mix to work around texture pickiness.

**Skip it if:** Your cat demands shredded or flaked textures, or your budget doesn't stretch to premium canned food.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A genuinely well-formulated food let down only by its polarizing texture and premium price. For cats that accept it, this could become your go-to. For the rest, at least you tried.
