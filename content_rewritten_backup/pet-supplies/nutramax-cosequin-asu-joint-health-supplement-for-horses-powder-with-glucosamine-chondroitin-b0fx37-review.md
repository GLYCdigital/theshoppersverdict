**Bottom line up front:** If your horse is showing early stiffness or you're managing an older athlete, this is the joint supplement I'd put my own money on — but it's not the cheapest option in the aisle, and your horse has to actually eat it.

## What you're actually getting

The Nutramax Cosequin ASU comes as a powder in a white bucket with the familiar blue label. As shown in the product image, it's a fine, light-tan powder that scoops easily and dissolves into feed without clumping. The active ingredients are where this differs from the bargain brands: you're getting glucosamine hydrochloride, chondroitin sulfate, and — the differentiator — ASU (avocado/soybean unsaponifiables). That ASU is the clinically studied component that's been shown to support cartilage health, not just mask inflammation. Nutramax also includes manganese, which supports collagen formation.

The dosing is straightforward: a scoop per day for maintenance, double for the first two weeks if you're loading. A 2.2-pound bucket runs roughly a 5-6 week supply for an average 1,100-pound horse.

## How it performs in the real world

The 4.7-star rating across 235 reviews isn't inflated. What I see consistently in buyer feedback is that owners notice improvement in **stiffness within 3-4 weeks**, not overnight. One commenter put it plainly: *"My 22-year-old QH was struggling to get up from rolling. Three weeks in, she's moving noticeably better and trotting out sound."* Another owner of a retired dressage horse with ringbone reported the powder was the only supplement that made a visible difference after trying three cheaper alternatives.

The powder format is genuinely convenient — no syringes, no oil to measure, no sticky residue. It mixes into a small amount of wet feed cleanly. Palatability is the one catch: several reviewers noted their horses initially refused feed with the powder. The smell isn't pleasant — it's distinctly supplement-like — and picky eaters may need the powder hidden in applesauce or molasses.

## Where it falls short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs. **The price per dose is steep** compared to glucosamine-only powders. You're paying for the ASU, which is the ingredient that actually has the research behind it, but if your horse has mild issues, you might not need that level of support. Also, the bucket isn't resealable — it has a lid that snaps on but doesn't lock, so moisture can get in if you store it in a humid barn.

Some owners also reported the powder didn't help horses with **severe, advanced arthritis** — and that's not a failure of the product. Once cartilage is significantly degraded, no supplement is going to rebuild it. This is a maintenance and early-intervention tool, not a miracle cure.

## Who should buy this

- **Owners of senior horses** (18+) starting to show stiffness or reluctance to move
- **Competitive riders** with horses in regular work who want joint protection before problems start
- **Owners who've tried cheap supplements** and seen no results — this is the upgrade that typically works

Skip it if your horse has severe, diagnosed arthritis requiring veterinary intervention, or if you have an extremely picky eater who refuses medicated feeds.

## FAQ

**Q: How long until I see results?**
A: Most owners report visible improvement in 3-6 weeks. The loading dose (double for 14 days) helps speed this up. If there's no change after 8 weeks, your horse's joint issues may be beyond what any supplement can address.

**Q: Can I give this with other medications like Bute?**
A: Yes, but talk to your vet. Cosequin ASU isn't known to interact with NSAIDs, but if your horse is on daily pain medication, you should be working with a veterinarian anyway to manage the underlying condition.

**Q: Is the powder messy to handle?**
A: It's fine — scoop, mix, done. No oil stains on your hands or tack. Just make sure the bucket stays dry, because the powder will clump if moisture gets in.
