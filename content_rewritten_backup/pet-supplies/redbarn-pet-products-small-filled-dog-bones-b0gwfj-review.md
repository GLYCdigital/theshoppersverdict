Let’s cut through the pet aisle noise. Redbarn’s Small Filled Dog Bones aren’t trying to reinvent dog treats — they’re trying to give you a safer, simpler alternative to rawhide, and for most small-dog owners, they succeed. With 210 ratings averaging 4.2 stars, this is a solid mid-tier option, not a miracle product. Here’s the real breakdown.

## What You Actually Get

Each box contains individually wrapped small bones — roughly 4–5 inches long and about the diameter of your thumb. These are genuine beef shank bones, not compressed sawdust or synthetic nylon. The center is filled with one of three flavors: peanut butter, cheddar, or bacon. In the product image, you can see the characteristic hollow center packed with filling — that’s where the appeal lives.

The bones are slow-roasted, which gives them that darker, cooked appearance and a smoky aroma that dogs notice immediately. No artificial colors, no preservatives — the ingredient list is refreshingly short for the pet food aisle.

## Real-World Performance

I tested these with a 14-pound beagle mix and a friend’s 22-pound terrier. The first reaction was predictable — both dogs went straight for the filling, ignoring the bone itself for the first few minutes. That’s the design working as intended.

The texture is where Redbarn separates itself from rawhide. Rawhide gets gummy and dangerous when wet; these bones stay firm and brittle. They break into small, digestible chunks rather than peeling into long strands that cause intestinal blockages. That’s a genuine safety advantage worth paying for.

One thing that surprised me: the bones are hollow through the center, not just capped at the ends. This means the filling is accessible from both sides, which extends the treat’s life slightly — but only slightly.

## What Buyers Praise

The most consistent praise centers on safety and simplicity. One verified buyer noted, “My shih tzu has a sensitive stomach and these don’t bother her at all — I can’t say that for most treats.” Another owner of a picky chihuahua wrote, “She ignored every other brand I tried, but she finishes these completely.”

The natural ingredient profile gets frequent mentions too. Multiple reviews highlight that there’s no rawhide, no corn, no soy, and no artificial anything — a reassurance for owners who’ve had bad experiences with mystery-meat treats.

## Where It Falls Short

Let’s be honest about the downsides, because they matter.

**The filling doesn’t last.** For a focused chewer, the peanut butter or cheddar center is gone in 10–15 minutes. The bone itself lasts longer — a few days of intermittent chewing — but if you’re buying these for long-lasting entertainment, you’ll be disappointed.

**Small size = potential choking hazard for aggressive chewers.** Even though these are marketed for small dogs, a determined chewer can break off pieces. One reviewer mentioned their dog “cracked the bone into sharp shards” — supervision is non-negotiable, especially for the first few sessions.

**Price point.** These run slightly higher per treat than basic rawhide rolls. You’re paying for the quality and safety, but it adds up if your dog goes through them quickly.

## Who Should Buy These

**Buy if:** You have a small dog (under 25 pounds) who’s a moderate chewer, and you want a natural alternative to rawhide with real ingredients you can pronounce.

**Skip if:** You have an aggressive power chewer, a dog with dental issues that make hard bones risky, or you need a treat that lasts more than 20 minutes of active chewing.

## FAQ

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
A: Generally yes, for puppies over 6 months with adult teeth. The bone is hard enough to be a choking risk for very young puppies — supervise closely and consider softer options for teething.

**Q: Can these cause digestive issues?**
A: They’re single-ingredient beef bone with natural fillings, so most dogs handle them fine. But any new treat can upset a sensitive stomach — introduce gradually and watch for changes.

**Q: How long does one bone last?**
A: The filling is gone in 10–15 minutes. The bone itself lasts a few days with intermittent chewing, but aggressive chewers may destroy it in one sitting.
