## What You're Actually Getting

Freshpet is fresh, refrigerated dog food sold in a resealable bag. The formula here is a rolled or chunky food made with visible chicken, carrots, and rice — not the brown powder you find in most kibble bags. The product image shows a bag that looks more like something you'd pull from your own refrigerator than a pet food aisle, which is exactly the point.

The packaging is designed to be resealed and stored in the fridge after opening. This is a fundamental shift from dry food — you can't leave it out all day. The freshness window after opening is typically around a week, so you'll need to portion it properly.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the tradeoffs, because there are real ones.

**The fridge problem**: This isn't a free-feeding food. You can't leave a bowl out all day. If you're someone who fills a bowl in the morning and lets your dog graze, Freshpet will spoil. You'll need to feed on a schedule, which is a genuine lifestyle change for some households.

**The cost factor**: Freshpet is more expensive than mainstream kibble. You're paying a premium for fresh ingredients and refrigeration throughout the supply chain. For large-breed owners feeding 4+ cups a day, this adds up significantly.

**The smell**: It smells like actual food — which means it smells like chicken and vegetables sitting in your fridge. Some reviewers noted the odor is noticeable when opening the bag. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

**Shelf life**: The shorter window after opening means you need to plan portions carefully. Buy too much and you'll be throwing away spoiled food.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if**: You have a picky eater, a dog with a sensitive stomach, or you want to feed your pet something closer to whole food. It's also a strong choice for senior dogs with dental issues who struggle with hard kibble.

**Skip it if**: You free-feed, have a large-breed dog on a tight budget, or travel frequently and can't guarantee refrigeration.
