**The Short Version**

Made by Nacho — the brand from celebrity chef Bobby Flay — brings restaurant-style thinking to cat food. The Flaked Filets Recipe in Bone Broth is a grain-free wet food that leans heavily on hydration and protein. At 3.7 stars from 185 ratings, it's good but not great, and the gap between what's advertised and what lands in the bowl is where the complaints live.

**What You're Actually Getting**

Each can is a grain-free, high-moisture formula built around flaked fish filets suspended in bone broth. The protein source varies by variety (tuna, salmon, whitefish), but the format stays consistent: shreddable flakes in a savory broth rather than a pâté or chunked loaf. This is a complement or complete meal depending on how you use it — the brand markets it as complete, but many buyers treat it as a topper because of the broth-heavy ratio.

The ingredient list is clean: no corn, wheat, soy, or artificial preservatives. First ingredient is the named fish, followed by bone broth. That's the kind of transparency that justifies the premium price point — if the texture delivers.

**Real-World Performance**

Here's where the split personality shows up. When the cans are packed correctly, this is some of the most palatable wet food you'll find. The flakes are tender, the broth is fragrant (maybe too fragrant), and cats who turn their noses up at pâtés will often clean the bowl. One buyer noted, *"My senior cat has stopped eating everything except this. I've tried six other brands — this is the only one she finishes."* That kind of loyalty is real, and it's driven by the texture.

But the consistency issue is the elephant in the room. Multiple reviewers reported receiving cans that were mostly liquid with small, broken flakes rather than the generous filets shown in the product images. One frustrated buyer wrote, *"First order was perfect. Second order was all broth and mush. Same product, totally different experience."* That's a manufacturing QC problem, not a recipe problem — and it explains why the rating sits at 3.7 instead of 4.5+.

**Where It Falls Short**

The broth-to-fish ratio is the biggest gamble. When you're paying a premium for "flaked filets," you expect visible flakes. Too many cans arrive as soup with shreds. It's still edible and nutritious, but it feels like a downgrade from the price point.

The aroma is also a genuine issue. This smells like real fish — strongly. If your feeding area is anywhere near your living space, you'll smell it well after the bowl is empty. For single-cat households that's manageable; for multi-cat homes or small apartments, it's a daily consideration.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Hydration-focused owners**: If your cat has had urinary issues or doesn't drink enough water, the bone broth base is a legitimate solution. High moisture content is genuinely beneficial.
- **Picky eaters who prefer flaked textures**: Cats that reject pâtés often respond to this. The flaked format is closer to real prey texture.
- **Grain-free households**: If you're strict about avoiding grains and fillers, this formula checks those boxes cleanly.

**Who Should Skip It**

- **Budget-conscious buyers**: This is premium-priced, and the inconsistency means you might pay top dollar for a can that's mostly broth.
- **Sensitive noses**: If you're bothered by strong fish smells, this isn't the one for your household.
- **Those who want predictable texture**: If you need consistency in what you're feeding, the QC variance will frustrate you.

**FAQ**

**Is this a complete meal or a topper?**
The brand markets it as complete, but many buyers use it as a topper because of the broth-heavy ratio. If your cat eats it alone, it's nutritionally complete. If you're using it over kibble, it adds moisture and protein — both are valid uses.

**How many cans come in a case?**
That depends on the listing you purchase. Check the specific pack size before ordering — the per-can price drops significantly on multi-packs, which offsets the premium cost.

**Does the bone broth actually help with hydration?**
Yes. Wet food in general provides more moisture than kibble, and the broth-forward format here pushes that even further. Cats with a history of urinary crystals or dehydration benefit most.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your cat is a picky eater who loves flaked textures, or if hydration is a medical priority. The bone broth format is genuinely useful, and when the cans are packed right, the quality is excellent.

**Skip it if** you need consistency in every can, or if strong fish odors are a dealbreaker in your home. The QC variance makes this a gamble at a premium price.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A great recipe with an execution problem. Worth trying for picky eaters, but buy a single can before committing to a case.
