If your 60-pound Labrador has turned your hardwood floors into a skate rink and your baseboards into kindling, the FL Falatier Dog Scratch Board Large is worth a hard look. This isn't another flimsy cardboard scratcher. It's a 20 x 14 inch rigid board with a self-trimming sandpaper surface designed to do double duty: give your dog an approved outlet for scratching while quietly filing down those daggers on their paws.

**What You Actually Get**

The package includes the main scratching board, multiple sandpaper sheets, and a cleaning brush. The board itself is surprisingly sturdy — it's not corrugated cardboard that'll disintegrate in a week. The working surface is coarse enough to catch and file nails naturally, but not so aggressive that it feels like sandpaper on your dog's paws. The anti-slip silicone base is the unsung hero here; as shown in the product image, it grips the floor properly, so the board stays put even when your dog really digs in.

**Real-World Performance**

The verdict on this board comes down to training and consistency. Buyers who report success spent the first week rewarding their dog for touching and scratching the board. One reviewer noted, "My German Shepherd learned in three days to use this instead of the door frame. Her nails are noticeably shorter after two weeks." Another owner of a 50-pound pit bull mix said, "It's genuinely saved my floors — I keep it right by the back door where she scratches to come in."

The self-trimming function is legitimate. The abrasive surface files nails down gradually — you won't get a perfect pedicure, but you'll dramatically reduce how often you need to clip or grind. For dogs that hate having their paws handled, this is a game-changer because they do the work themselves.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the drawbacks. The sandpaper sheets wear down — with daily use from a large dog, expect to replace them every 4 to 6 weeks. Replacement packs run about $10, which adds up over a year.

The bigger issue: this board will not fix a dog that already has a furniture-scratching habit without training. It's a redirect tool, not a cure. If your dog scratches walls or baseboards, you'll need to supervise, catch them in the act, and guide them to the board consistently. It's also not suitable as a nail-trimming replacement for dogs with overgrown nails that need immediate attention — that requires a vet or groomer.

**Who Should Buy This**

This board is ideal for:
- Owners of large or medium dogs with high scratching drive (Beagles, Terriers, Shepherds, Labs)
- Anyone with hardwood, laminate, or tile floors showing claw marks
- Dogs that hate nail trims and need a low-stress alternative
- Multi-dog households where one board can serve several dogs

Skip it if you have a toy breed (they won't use the large surface efficiently), or if you're unwilling to put in the redirect training time.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this work for a puppy?**
A: Yes, and it's actually easier to train a puppy than an adult dog. Introduce it early with treats and praise. The large surface is fine for puppies — they'll grow into it.

**Q: How do I clean the board?**
A: Use the included brush to sweep off loose hair and debris after each use. For deeper cleaning, wipe the surface with a slightly damp cloth and let it air dry completely before the next use.

**Q: Does it really trim nails, or just scratching?**
A: Both. The abrasive surface files nails down naturally as your dog scratches. You'll notice shorter nails within 2-3 weeks of consistent use, though results vary by dog and how often they use it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you have a medium-to-large dog that scratches floors or furniture, and you're committed to a week of redirect training. **Skip it if** you expect instant results without any effort, or you need a solution for severely overgrown nails.
