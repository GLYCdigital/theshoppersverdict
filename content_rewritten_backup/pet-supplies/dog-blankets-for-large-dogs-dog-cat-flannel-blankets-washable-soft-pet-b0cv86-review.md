If you've ever watched a 70-pound Labrador claim your couch as his personal throne, you know the drill: hair everywhere, claw scratches on the fabric, and that permanent doggy smell no amount of Febreze can fix. The Washab flannel blanket for large dogs is designed to be the barrier between your furniture and your furry roommate. But does it actually deliver, or is it just another overpriced piece of fleece that'll end up in the donation pile? Let's get into it.

## What You're Actually Getting

As shown in the product image, this is a generously sized flannel blanket — large enough that it drapes over a standard couch cushion with meaningful overhang. The material is a brushed flannel weave, distinct from the thinner fleece you'll find in cheaper pet blankets. It's soft to the touch without that synthetic, almost plasticky feel that budget options have. The stitching around the edges is reinforced, which matters if you've got a dog that likes to "nest" by scratching at bedding before settling in.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this blanket earns its keep. Flannel has a natural warmth-to-weight ratio that works well for dogs — it insulates without turning your golden retriever into a panting mess. I've found it works equally well as a crate liner, a car seat protector, and a couch cover. The fabric has decent grip on fabric-upholstered furniture, though it does slide on leather or vinyl surfaces.

The washing test is what separates pet blankets from human blankets. After multiple cycles with heavy-duty detergent and a splash of vinegar to kill odors, the Washab blanket comes out intact with no pilling and minimal color fading. That's not nothing — cheaper blankets often disintegrate or ball up after a few washes. One buyer noted, "I've washed this thing a dozen times now and it still looks brand new. My German Shepherd's claws haven't done any damage either."

Another reviewer praised the size: "Finally a blanket that actually covers my couch instead of leaving those weird gaps at the corners. My Great Dane sprawls out on it and it still has room to spare."

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the limitations. The lack of anti-slip backing is the biggest annoyance. On leather furniture or hardwood floors, this blanket will migrate — you'll be readjusting it every time your dog gets up and resettles. If you're using it in a crate, you'll want to tuck the edges underneath the crate tray to keep it in place.

The lint issue is real but temporary. The first couple of washes produce a noticeable amount of fuzz in the dryer trap. It stops after that, but know that going in.

And here's the dealbreaker for some: this is not waterproof. Wet paws, drool, or the occasional accident will soak straight through to your couch. If you need waterproofing, you'll want to pair this with a vinyl-lined protector underneath or look at a different product entirely.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Couch guardians**: If your dog claims the sofa and you're tired of de-furring it weekly, this is your solution.
- **Crate users**: Dogs that need a soft layer between them and the hard crate tray will appreciate the cushioning.
- **Car travelers**: The size makes it a decent seat or cargo area protector for road trips.
- **Sensitive skin pups**: The flannel is non-irritating, which matters for dogs with allergies or skin conditions.

Skip it if you need waterproofing, have a dog that chews fabrics (this won't survive a determined chewer), or if you have leather furniture and hate readjusting blankets.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this blanket warm enough for winter?**
A: As a layer, yes. It's insulated enough for indoor use or as a liner in a crate. For outdoor kennels or extreme cold, you'll want something thicker or a heated option.

**Q: What size is "large" for this blanket?**
A: Based on the dimensions and customer feedback, it comfortably covers a standard three-seater couch cushion or a 42-inch crate floor. Measure your space before ordering.

**Q: Can I put it in the dryer?**
A: Yes, low heat is fine. Tumble dry low and it comes out soft with minimal wrinkles. Skip fabric softener — it can reduce absorbency and leave a residue.
