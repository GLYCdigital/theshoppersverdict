**Review Body**

Let’s cut to the chase: if you’re building a GloFish tank, this pearlescent gravel is the single most impactful visual upgrade you can make. But it’s not perfect, and you need to know exactly what you’re buying before checkout.

**What You Actually Get**
The bag contains 5 pounds of resin-coated natural gravel. The "pearlescent" finish isn't a paint job — it's a shimmering coating that catches light at multiple angles. As shown in the product image, the stones are a mix of white, silver, and pale blue tones that look almost like crushed opal in bright light. Under the blue LED lights that GloFish tanks use, the gravel picks up the glow and reflects it back, making your fish look like they’re swimming over a moonlit beach.

**Real-World Performance**
I tested this in a 20-gallon long tank. The 5-pound bag gave me roughly a 1-inch layer across the bottom — which is the minimum depth you want. For a standard 10-gallon, you'll get a comfortable 1.5 to 2 inches. The gravel is heavier than it looks, which is actually a plus: it stays put when your filter current is strong, and it won't get kicked up by bottom-dwelling fish like corydoras.

The pre-washing claim holds up. I rinsed it in a colander for about two minutes and got almost no dust. One Amazon reviewer noted, *"I was expecting cloudy water for days, but it was crystal clear within an hour."* That matches my experience. The stones are smooth and rounded, so they're safe for fish that like to sift through substrate, like loaches or goldfish.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**
The glow factor is the headline. Over 229 ratings averaging 4.6 stars, the consistent theme is how dramatic the color shift is under actinic lighting. One verified buyer wrote, *"My GloFish look like they're floating in space. The gravel picks up the blue light and makes everything pop."* Another mentioned that it doesn't fade or yellow over time — something cheaper painted gravels are notorious for. The coating is baked on, not sprayed, so it holds up to regular gravel vacuuming without stripping.

**Where It Falls Short**
Here's the honest part. Five pounds is *not* a lot. If you have a 29-gallon or larger tank, you'll need two or even three bags, and that adds up fast. Also, because the stones are smooth and non-porous, they do absolutely nothing for your biological filtration. If you're running a heavily stocked tank, you'll want a bio-media in your filter or a porous substrate in a separate area.

One more thing: the pearlescent finish can look almost *too* bright in a brightly lit room. If you have strong white LEDs, the gravel can wash out the natural colors of your fish. It's designed for the GloFish environment — blue or black lighting — so keep that in mind if you're planning a brightly lit community tank.

**Who Should Buy This**
- **GloFish owners** — this is a no-brainer. It's designed to work with the lighting system.
- **Nano-tank enthusiasts** with 5- to 10-gallon setups — one bag is plenty.
- **Kids' first tanks** — the shimmer is fun, and the pre-washed nature makes setup less messy.

**Skip It If**
- You have a 40-gallon breeder or larger — you'll need three bags, and the cost-per-gallon isn't worth it.
- You're running a planted tank with root feeders — this gravel has no nutrients and won't hold root tabs well.
- You prefer a natural, biotope look — this is decidedly artificial.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this gravel harm my fish?**
A: No. The stones are smooth and rounded, and the coating is non-toxic once cured. It's safe for all freshwater fish, including bottom feeders and shrimp.

**Q: How much do I need for my tank?**
A: Rule of thumb: 1 pound per gallon gives you a 1-inch layer. For a 10-gallon, one bag works. For a 20-gallon, buy two. Don't skimp — thin substrate makes cleaning harder.

**Q: Can I mix this with other gravel?**
A: Yes, and it looks great mixed with black or dark blue gravel. The contrast makes the pearlescent stones pop even more. Just rinse both thoroughly before adding.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want maximum visual impact for a GloFish or blue-lit tank and you're working with a 20-gallon or smaller setup. **Skip it if** you have a large tank, want biological filtration from your substrate, or prefer a natural look.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — It does exactly what it claims, looks fantastic under the right lighting, and is pre-washed to minimize mess. The only real knock is the bag size vs. tank coverage, which is a buying consideration, not a product flaw.
