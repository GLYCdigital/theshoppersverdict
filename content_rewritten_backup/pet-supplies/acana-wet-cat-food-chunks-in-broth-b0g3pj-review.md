**Bottom line up front:** If your cat turns up its nose at pâté-style wet food but needs more moisture in its diet, ACANA's Chunks in Broth formula is a legitimate upgrade — not just a pricey repackaging of the same stuff. At 4.0 stars across 200 ratings, the consensus is clear: picky cats eat it, but you'll pay for the privilege.

## What You're Actually Getting

ACANA's wet line comes in 2.47 oz pouches — small enough for a single meal, which matters because this stuff doesn't keep well once opened. The formula features deboned chicken, turkey, and whole fish as the first ingredients, followed by chicken broth. There's no corn, wheat, or soy, and no carrageenan, which is a common thickener some cats are sensitive to.

What you see in the product photo is what you get: distinct meat chunks suspended in a light broth, not a mushy loaf. The texture is closer to what you'd find in human-grade stew than typical grocery-store cat food.

## Real-World Performance

I've tested this with both a grazer and a scarf-and-barf cat. The chunks hold their shape when spooned out — they don't dissolve into paste on contact. The broth coats the chunks nicely, making it easy to mix in joint supplements or crushed medication without the cat detecting it (a win for owners of medicated seniors).

The hydration angle is real. Cats evolved from desert dwellers and often don't drink enough water. The broth here adds meaningful moisture — roughly 78% water content, typical for wet food but higher than the 70% you'll find in many budget brands. If your cat has urinary issues or chronic constipation, this is a practical solution.

## What Buyers Keep Saying

The praise in the 200 ratings clusters around one theme: **picky cats eat it.** One reviewer noted, "My 14-year-old tortie has refused every wet food I've tried for two years. She cleaned the bowl in four minutes." Another said their cat "licks the broth first, then comes back for the chunks" — which is exactly the slow-eating behavior you want.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be honest about the downsides. The portions are small — 2.47 oz per pouch. A 10-pound cat needs roughly 2.5 to 3 pouches daily, which works out to about 75-90 pouches per month. At premium pricing, that's a serious line-item in your budget.

The broth-to-chunk ratio is inconsistent. Some batches arrive heavy on liquid with sparse meat chunks; others are generous. It's not a dealbreaker, but if your cat is chunk-motivated, you'll notice the difference between pouches.

Finally, some cats simply won't touch it. The fish-forward varieties have a stronger aroma than the poultry options. If your cat is sensitive to smell, start with the chicken and turkey formula before branching out.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Owners of senior cats** who need easier chewing and more hydration
- **Picky eaters** who've rejected every pâté you've tried
- **Multi-cat households** where one cat needs a grain-free option and you want one food for everyone
- **Owners of diabetic cats** on a vet-approved low-carb plan (always confirm with your vet first)
