Let's be honest: most cardboard scratchers are glorified shoeboxes. The SmartyKat Super Scratcher is different — and not just because of the name. At 28.7 inches long and 11.8 inches wide, this thing gives your cat room to actually extend. That's the difference between a cat tolerating a scratcher and a cat abandoning your sofa.

**What's actually in the box**

You get one flat, double-sided corrugated cardboard pad — roughly the size of a large cutting board — plus a small pouch of loose catnip tucked into a center channel. No assembly, no tools, no nonsense. Unbox it, sprinkle the catnip across the surface, and place it on the floor. That's the entire setup.

The cardboard is thick — noticeably denser than the cheap brown stuff you get in generic scratchers. The surface has that distinct corrugated texture cats love, and because it's double-sided, you flip it over when the top wears out. That effectively doubles the lifespan.

**Real-world performance**

I've watched cats hit this thing. They don't just scratch it — they dig in. The flat design means no wobble, no tipping, and no chasing a flimsy scratcher across the room. The 28-inch length matters more than you'd think. A cat stretching fully to scratch engages their whole back, and that's what keeps them coming back instead of finding your armchair.

The catnip is a smart touch. It's not pre-impregnated into the cardboard like some competitors, which means it's potent when you apply it. The downside? It fades fast. A few weeks in, you'll want to add your own catnip to keep the scratcher interesting.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The 4.4-star rating across 220 reviews isn't inflated. The recurring theme is cats who ignored every other scratcher suddenly using this one. One reviewer noted: *"My cat shredded my $2,000 couch for two years. This thing stopped her cold. She's obsessed."* Another said: *"I bought three of these and placed them around the house. My furniture is finally safe."*

Buyers also love the size. Multiple reviews mention that the long, flat surface doubles as a lounging pad — cats nap on it, roll on it, and generally claim it as their territory. That's not just a scratcher; that's a piece of furniture your cat actually wants to use.

**Where it falls short**

Let's be real about the mess. Corrugated cardboard sheds. You will find tiny brown confetti pieces scattered around your floor, and if you have carpet, you'll be picking them out with tweezers. It's unavoidable with this type of product.

The catnip pouch is underwhelming. It's small, and the potency drops off noticeably after the first couple of weeks. You'll need to buy loose catnip separately to keep this effective long-term.

And if your cat prefers vertical scratching — like stretching up against a doorframe or a scratching post — this won't cut it. This is strictly a floor-level option.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** with cats who scratch furniture — this is a cheap, effective deterrent
- **Multi-cat households** — the size accommodates multiple cats scratching simultaneously
- **Kittens** — the low profile is perfect for young cats learning to scratch
- **Skip it if** your cat is a dedicated vertical scratcher or you can't tolerate cardboard debris

**FAQ**

**How long does it last?** With average use, expect 4-6 weeks per side. Flip it over and you get roughly 2-3 months total. Heavy scratchers will burn through it faster.

**Is the catnip safe?** Yes. It's standard loose catnip, same quality you'd buy in any pet store. Just note it loses potency over time.

**Does it really stop furniture scratching?** Yes, if you place it strategically — near where your cat scratches now. Don't expect a cat to cross the house to use it. Put it where the behavior happens.
