Royal Canin's Extra Small wet puppy food is one of those products that makes you question whether you're overpaying for a tiny can of mush — until you watch a 2-pound Yorkie puppy try to eat regular kibble. The 3-ounce cans hold a smooth, fine-textured pâté that's specifically engineered for toy breed puppies who top out under 10 pounds as adults. It's not just smaller kibble in a can; it's a completely different nutritional profile.

## What You Actually Get

Each case contains twelve 3-ounce cans. The pâté is uniform, soft, and almost mousse-like in consistency — there are no chunks, no jelly, no bits that could pose a choking hazard for a teacup-sized jaw. As shown in the product image, the can design is clean and clearly labeled with the Extra Small branding and a puppy on the front. The formula is complete and balanced for growth, meaning it's suitable as a sole diet, not just a topper.

## How It Performs in Real Life

The texture is the standout feature. Owners of Chihuahuas, Maltese, and Pomeranians consistently report that even the pickiest eaters go after this stuff. One verified buyer noted, *"My 9-week-old Papillon had zero interest in dry food. This pâté changed everything — she licks the bowl clean."* The high moisture content (around 78%) also helps puppies who don't drink enough water stay hydrated, which is a genuine concern for toy breeds prone to urinary issues.

The nutrition is where Royal Canin earns its premium price. The calcium-to-phosphorus ratio is precisely calibrated for small breeds' rapid growth phase — this matters because toy breed puppies are at higher risk for skeletal abnormalities if they grow too fast. The kibble size and shape are irrelevant here; what matters is the digestibility. The formula includes highly digestible proteins and prebiotic fiber, and owners report noticeably firmer stools compared to grocery-store brands.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 235 ratings at 4.7 stars, the recurring themes are: picky eaters finally eating, gentle digestion, and portion control. Another buyer wrote, *"The 3-oz can is the perfect size for my 4-pound puppy — one can lasts two meals with no waste."* Owners with multiple toy breed puppies also appreciate that the cans are easy to split without creating a mess.

## Where It Falls Short

The cost is the elephant in the room. At roughly $2-$3 per can depending on the seller, this is significantly pricier than standard puppy wet food. If you have a larger "extra small" puppy (like a 9-pound Miniature Pinscher), you'll go through cans quickly, and the cost adds up fast. The 3-ounce size also means you'll be opening a lot of cans weekly — and if you don't refrigerate the leftovers promptly, they'll spoil.

The other limitation: this formula is specifically for puppies under 10 months. Once your pup crosses that threshold, you'll need to transition to the adult Extra Small formula, which means another purchase cycle. Some owners also note the pâté can be mushy to serve straight from the fridge — a quick nuke for 5 seconds or mashing with warm water solves it.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a toy breed puppy (Chihuahua, Yorkie, Maltese, Pomeranian, Papillon) under 10 months old, especially if they're a picky eater or you're concerned about proper growth nutrition. It's also ideal for puppies with sensitive stomachs who react poorly to cheaper wet foods.

**Skip it if:** You have a puppy that will grow past 10 pounds, you're on a tight budget, or your puppy already eats dry food without issues — you can save money with a high-quality kibble and use this only as an occasional treat.
