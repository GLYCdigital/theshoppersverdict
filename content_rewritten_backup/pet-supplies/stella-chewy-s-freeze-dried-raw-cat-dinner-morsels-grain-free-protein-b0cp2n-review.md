**The Short Version:** If your cat turns up its nose at everything you've tried, Stella & Chewy's freeze-dried raw morsels are worth the premium. At 4.2 stars from 210 ratings, this isn't a perfect product — but for cats who need high-protein, minimally processed nutrition, it's one of the most reliable options on Amazon.

## What You Actually Get

The bag contains freeze-dried raw morsels made from 90% meat, organs, and bone. The "Gra" formulation uses grass-fed beef as the single animal protein source. Each morsel is roughly the size of a pea — small enough for most cats to eat whole, though you can crumble them for kittens or seniors.

One 12-ounce bag provides approximately 1,360 kcal, which breaks down to around 460 kcal per cup. For an average 10-pound cat, that's roughly a week of food if fed as a complete meal — closer to two weeks if you're using it as a topper. The bag reseals with a zip closure, and the product carries a two-year shelf life unopened.

## Real-World Performance

The standout feature here is ingredient transparency. The first ingredient is beef, followed by beef liver, beef kidney, and beef bone. There's no corn, wheat, soy, or carrageenan — just meat, organs, and added vitamins and minerals. For cats with food sensitivities or owners who distrust rendered by-products in mainstream kibble, this is a meaningful upgrade.

Rehydration matters. Fed dry, these morsels act like a crunchy treat. Fed with water (roughly 1/4 cup per 2 morsels), they soften into a texture closer to fresh raw food. Most cats seem to prefer the rehydrated version — one reviewer noted, "My picky tortie snubbed every canned food I tried, but she cleans her bowl with these. I add warm water and she goes crazy."

The transition period is real. Cats adapted to high-carbohydrate kibble may experience loose stools for the first week. Several reviewers reported this — it's a normal adjustment, but worth knowing if you're switching cold turkey.

## What Buyers Praise

The most consistent positive feedback centers on picky eaters. Reviewers repeatedly describe cats who "refuse everything else" suddenly eating enthusiastically. The strong natural aroma is a factor — freeze-dried raw smells like actual meat, not rendered meal.

Coat condition and energy levels also get frequent mentions. Multiple reviewers report shinier coats and more active cats within two to three weeks of switching. One long-term buyer wrote, "My 12-year-old cat has more energy than she's had in years. Worth every penny."

## Where It Falls Short

The pricing is the biggest barrier. At roughly $1.50–$2.00 per ounce, this costs two to three times more than premium kibble. For multi-cat households, feeding this exclusively gets expensive fast.

Bag quality is inconsistent. Some reviewers received bags with significant morsel breakage — more dust than intact pieces. It doesn't affect nutrition, but it's annoying when you're paying premium prices.

Finally, the brittle texture means you'll want to handle the bag gently. Dropping it or compressing it in storage accelerates crumbling.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a picky eater, a cat with grain sensitivities, or you want to move toward a raw diet without the hassle and risk of handling fresh raw meat. It's also ideal for travel — no refrigeration needed.

**Skip it if:** You're on a tight budget, feeding multiple cats, or your cat has a medical condition requiring a veterinary-prescribed diet. Also skip if your cat is prone to pancreatitis or has had reactions to high-protein foods.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I feed this as a complete meal, or only as a topper?**
A: Both. It's formulated to meet AAFCO nutritional standards for all life stages, so it works as a standalone meal. Many owners start with a 25/75 blend (25% morsels, 75% current food) and increase over a week.

**Q: How much water should I add?**
A: For a complete meal, add about 1/4 cup of water per two morsels and let it sit for 2–3 minutes. For topper use, crumble dry directly over food.

**Q: My cat has never eaten raw — will she take to this?**
A: Most cats adapt within a week, especially if you start with small amounts mixed into familiar food. The freeze-dried texture is less foreign than wet raw, which helps. Some cats eat it dry instantly.
