# Ram Board Stair Armor Review: Does This 1.58-Ft Roll Actually Protect Your Stairs?

If you've ever watched a contractor track mud across new carpet or scuff fresh hardwood treads with a ladder, you know the sinking feeling. Stairs are the highest-traffic zone in any renovation — and the hardest to protect. Ram Board's Stair Armor claims to solve that with a peel-and-stick film designed specifically for treads and risers. After digging through 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, here's what real buyers say.

## What You Actually Get

The product is a single roll of 12-mil, textured polyethylene film measuring 1.58 feet wide. That's roughly 19 inches — enough to cover a standard residential tread (typically 10–11 inches deep) with overlap for the riser. You're not getting adhesive strips, tape, or applicator tools. It's just the film, a scored cutting edge if you're lucky, and your own utility knife.

The textured surface is the key feature. It's not slick plastic like cheap drop cloths — the embossed pattern gives you traction underfoot, which matters when you're hauling paint buckets up and down. Several buyers noted it felt "grippy even with wet boots," which is a real safety win on stairs.

## Performance and Build Quality

The film itself is tough. Multiple reviewers reported dragging furniture up stairs without puncturing the surface. One buyer wrote: "I put this down before refinishing my hardwood steps. The contractor dropped a crowbar — it bounced off the film, no dent in the wood underneath." That's the kind of protection you want.

The lack of adhesive is a double-edged sword. The film stays put thanks to friction and the textured backing, but on carpeted stairs, it can shift slightly over days of heavy use. Buyers who secured edges with painter's tape had zero issues. Those who skipped it occasionally saw corners curl. On bare wood or tile, the film grips better — but you'll still want tape at the top and bottom edges to prevent creep.

One limitation worth noting: the 1.58-ft width. Standard residential stairs are fine, but if you have wide, modern treads (anything past 12 inches), you'll need to overlap two strips. It works, but the seam can catch a heel if you're not careful. Commercial stairs or floating treads? Look elsewhere or budget for extra rolls.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

The recurring theme in positive reviews is **reusability**. Because the film is thick and doesn't tear easily, several buyers peeled it up after a project, stored it flat, and used it again for a second renovation. One reviewer explicitly mentioned "used it on three separate jobs — still didn't have a hole in it."

Another consistent win: **no residue**. Unlike adhesive-backed stair treads that leave gummy buildup on finish, this film leaves nothing behind. A reviewer with brand-new oak treads confirmed: "Peeled it off, no sticky mess, no marks. The finish looked untouched."

## Where It Falls Short

The biggest complaint is the lack of built-in adhesive. For a product marketed as "temporary protection," expecting buyers to supply their own tape feels like an oversight — especially on carpeted stairs where the film doesn't grip naturally. It's fixable, but it's an extra step.

Second, the 1.58-ft width is a limitation. If you're protecting stairs with deep treads or bullnose edges that wrap around, the film won't cover the full profile. You'll need to cut additional pieces, which adds time and creates seams.

Finally, the film is slippery on the *underside* if you're applying it to a smooth surface like polished concrete. The textured top grips your feet, but the film itself can slide on ultra-smooth floors until weighted down. Tape solves this, but it's worth knowing upfront.

## Who Should Buy This

- **DIY renovators** protecting carpet or wood stairs during painting, drywall, or flooring work
- **Contractors** who need a fast, reusable solution for standard residential treads
- **Home stagers** or landlords prepping a property between tenants — the film covers scuffs and is cheap insurance

**Skip it if** you have extra-wide or non-standard stairs, or if you need a totally adhesive-backed product that requires zero setup.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this damage my carpet or wood finish when removed?**
No. The film is non-adhesive, so it won't pull fibers or leave sticky residue. On carpet, the textured backing might slightly flatten the pile temporarily, but it springs back within a day or two.

**Q: Can I walk on it immediately after applying?**
Yes — there's no drying time or curing period. Just lay it down, tape the edges if needed, and you're done.

**Q: How many stairs does one roll cover?**
That depends on your tread depth. For a standard 10-inch tread with a 7-inch riser, you can cover roughly 10–12 stairs per roll. Measure your staircase before ordering to avoid running short mid-project.

## The Verdict

**Rating: 4.6/5**

**Buy it if** you want heavy-duty, reusable stair protection that won't leave residue and can handle real abuse. The 12-mil film is genuinely tough, the textured surface adds safety, and the no-mess removal is a huge plus for anyone protecting fresh finishes.

**Skip it if** you need adhesive-backed convenience or have oversized stairs — the 1.58-ft width and no-stick design will frustrate you. For everyone else with standard residential steps, this is the reliable, budget-friendly choice that gets the job done without drama.
