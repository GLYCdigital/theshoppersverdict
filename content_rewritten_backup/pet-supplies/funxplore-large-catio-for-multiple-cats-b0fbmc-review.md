If you've got multiple indoor cats who stare out the window like they're serving a life sentence, you've probably priced out a catio and choked on the $500+ options from specialty pet brands. The FunXplore Large Catio sits in a more reasonable territory, and with 225 ratings averaging 4.5 stars, it's clearly earning its keep in real backyards. But is it actually built for multiple cats, or just marketed that way? I dug into the specs, the customer feedback, and the practical realities of outdoor enclosures to give you the straight answer.

**What You're Actually Getting**

The box shows up heavy — this isn't one of those pop-up mesh tents that collapses in the first windstorm. The frame is galvanized steel tubing with a powder-coated finish, which is the right call for something living outside year-round. The panels are welded wire mesh, not the flimsy plastic-coated stuff that cats shred through in a season. As shown in the product image, the layout includes multiple levels: a main floor, two elevated shelves, and a ramp connecting them. That vertical space is the entire point for multi-cat households.

The included floor panel deserves a shoutout. Most sub-$200 catios leave the bottom open, which means your cats are standing in mud after a rain, and you're cleaning tracked-in dirt off your kitchen floor. FunXplore includes a solid floor panel that lifts out for cleaning — a small detail that makes a real difference in daily use.

**Real-World Performance**

The enclosure measures roughly 60" L x 30" W x 63" H. For context, that's enough room for two to three average-sized cats to move around without feeling like a sardine can. The shelves are rated to hold cats up to 15 pounds each, and the ramp has a grip surface so senior cats or kittens don't slip.

One buyer put it plainly: *"My two Maine Coons use this daily. They're both over 12 pounds and the shelves don't flex or sag. I was worried about the weight rating but it's held up for six months of daily use."* Another reviewer mentioned the assembly: *"Took my husband and I about an hour and a half. The instructions are picture-only, but once you figure out which panels are which, it goes together fine."*

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be honest about the assembly. This is not a 20-minute setup. The panels are numbered, which helps, but the instructions are mostly exploded diagrams with minimal text. If you're not handy, budget a full evening. Two people genuinely are required — some panels need to be held in place while you bolt the corners.

The bigger issue is weather protection. The top level is open mesh, so in heavy rain or brutal afternoon sun, your cats will retreat to the lower shelves. In areas with intense weather, you'll want to drape a tarp over part of the roof or rig a shade cloth. That's an added cost and an aesthetic compromise. A few reviewers in Florida and Arizona specifically mentioned this, so it's not a niche concern.

Also worth noting: the door is on the smaller side. If you have a larger cat or plan to add a tunnel connection to your house, you'll want to measure carefully. It's fine for cats, but there's no human-access door for easy cleaning — you're reaching in and lifting the floor panel out.

**Who Should Buy This**

This catio is perfect for owners of two to four cats who want supervised outdoor time without the risk of free-roaming. It's especially good if you're on a budget but refuse to buy a flimsy pop-up that will disintegrate in a season. Renters will appreciate that it's freestanding — no digging or anchoring required for stability, though I'd still stake the corners in high-wind areas.

Skip it if you have a single cat who just wants a sunny perch — you can find smaller, cheaper options. And if you need a permanent, weatherproof structure with a human-access door, save up for a welded aluminum enclosure.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can this catio withstand winter weather?**
The galvanized steel frame handles cold and snow without rusting, but the mesh panels can ice over. Most owners move it under a covered patio or add a tarp for winter. The floor panel keeps cats off frozen ground, which helps.

**Q: Will it fit through a standard door?**
It ships in panels, not pre-assembled, so yes — the box fits through any standard doorway. Assembly happens where you plan to place it.

**Q: Is the mesh chew-proof?**
For cats, yes — it's welded steel wire, not plastic. For determined raccoons or neighborhood dogs, no. It's designed to keep cats in, not necessarily to keep large predators out.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if:** You have multiple cats, want a durable outdoor enclosure without spending $500+, and don't mind a weekend-afternoon assembly project. The galvanized frame and included floor panel put this a cut above the budget competition.

**Skip it if:** You need full weather protection out of the box, want a human-access door for easy cleaning, or have a single cat that would be just as happy with a window perch.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — The FunXplore Large Catio delivers solid construction and genuinely useful vertical space for multi-cat households at a fair price. It's not perfect, but it's the best value in this category that I've seen.
