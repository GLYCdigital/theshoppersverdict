If you've ever spent a Saturday morning wrestling a siphon hose, a bucket, and a mouthful of aquarium water, the Aqueon SmartClean is aimed directly at you. This isn't a gimmick — it's a genuinely different way to handle the worst chore in fishkeeping.

**What you actually get**  
The box contains the tank (available in 5, 10, and 20-gallon sizes), a lid with integrated LED lighting, the SmartClean pump assembly, a control valve, and a starter filter cartridge. The pump sits in a dedicated chamber on the back wall — it's not hidden, but the gray plastic blends reasonably well. The control valve is the key piece: twist it to switch between "filter" and "clean" modes. In clean mode, the pump redirects water flow through a gravel vacuum head that you glide over the substrate. Debris gets trapped in a mesh bag inside the chamber. That's it — no separate vacuum, no bucket, no priming.

**Performance and build quality**  
The acrylic tank is crystal-clear and scratch-resistant, but it's the pump that matters. At 53 gallons per hour on the 10-gallon model, it's adequate — not powerful. Fine sand and light mulm get picked up cleanly; pea gravel and compacted waste will need a few passes. The control valve has a low-flow setting that's genuinely gentle — one reviewer with a betta noted, *"My fish hardly notices the current on the lowest setting, but it still keeps the water clear."*

The filter cartridge uses a three-stage media (mechanical, chemical, biological) that's more robust than typical starter cartridges. But here's the catch: it's proprietary. You're locked into Aqueon's replacement cartridges, which run $12–15 for a two-pack. After a year, that's $60–90 in consumables — worth factoring into your budget.

**What buyers consistently praise**  
The convenience is the headline. A 20-gallon owner wrote, *"I used to dread water changes. Now I vacuum the gravel in five minutes, swap 20% of the water, and I'm done. The bucket is gone."* The tool-free disassembly gets frequent mentions — the pump head, hoses, and valve all pop apart without tools, which makes deep cleaning the filter chamber genuinely easy. The LED light is also a pleasant surprise: it's bright enough for low-light plants like java fern and anubias, though not for demanding species.

**Where it falls short**  
The pump's modest output is the most common complaint. On a 20-gallon tank with a heavy bioload, you'll find yourself running the vacuum over the same spot multiple times. The mesh bag needs rinsing after every cleaning session — it clogs fast with mulm. And the 5-gallon model feels cramped for the pump assembly; the chamber eats about 15% of the tank's usable volume. A few buyers also noted that the pump gets noisy after a few months — a quick disassembly and rinse usually fixes it, but it's not a set-and-forget system.

**Who should buy this**  
This is for the hobbyist who values time over tinkering. Beginners will appreciate that there's no siphon to learn, no priming, no accidental mouthfuls of fish water. Busy adults with small-to-medium tanks (5–20 gallons) will find the five-minute cleanup routine genuinely transformative. Skip it if you're running a heavily planted tank with fine substrate you don't want disturbed, or if you're on a tight budget — the cartridge costs add up. It's also not ideal for 30+ gallon tanks; you'd need multiple cleaning passes and the pump just isn't strong enough.

**FAQ**  
*How often do I replace the filter cartridge?*  
Every 3–4 weeks, depending on bioload. The cartridge has a carbon layer that exhausts quickly, but you can stretch it to 6 weeks if you rinse it weekly.

*Can I use it with a gravel substrate?*  
Yes, but use the "clean" mode carefully. The vacuum head is wide enough for gravel, but you'll need to lift it slightly to avoid picking up larger stones.

*Does it work for saltwater?*  
The pump and acrylic tank are fine for saltwater, but the cartridge media isn't designed for it. You'd need to swap in marine-specific media, which defeats the convenience.

**The Verdict**  
Buy it if you want to cut aquarium maintenance time in half and hate manual siphons. Skip it if you're on a strict budget or have a tank larger than 20 gallons. It's a well-executed convenience product with a real tradeoff: proprietary consumables and modest pump power. At 4.2 stars, it earns its rating — for the right owner, it's genuinely game-changing.
