A 3.6-star average on 180 ratings tells you one thing immediately: this kennel is a mixed bag. Some owners swear by it. Others wanted to throw the Allen wrench across the yard. Here's the honest breakdown so you know which camp you'll land in.

**What You Actually Get**

The Lyromix arrives as a flat-folded wire panel assembly. Inside the box: the kennel panels, the roof panel, the openable feeding door mechanism, hardware, and an Allen wrench that's functional but not comfortable. No floor, no ground tarp, no anchors. For a kennel marketed as "outdoor," the missing floor is a real oversight — you're either putting this on concrete or buying a separate ground cover.

Setup is where the frustration starts. As shown in the product image, the panels look straightforward. In reality, the bolt holes don't always line up cleanly, and the instructions are basic line drawings with minimal text. Plan for 30-45 minutes of assembly if you're handy, longer if you're not. Several owners noted that having a second person helps significantly, especially when attaching the roof.

**Build Quality and Real-World Performance**

The wire mesh is the big question mark. It's not flimsy, but it's not heavy-gauge either. For a calm adult dog who wants a shady spot to lounge, it's perfectly adequate. For a determined chewer, digger, or escape artist, the panels will eventually lose that battle. One reviewer wrote, "My 70-pound lab mix leaned on the side and the panel bowed outward slightly. It held, but I wouldn't trust it with a dog that's determined to get out."

The roof is the standout feature. It's a solid panel that actually sheds rain and provides meaningful shade. In a typical backyard setting, your dog stays dry during light to moderate rain. Heavy downpours with wind will still blow moisture in, but that's true of most wire kennels in this price range.

The openable feeding door is clever and practical. You can slide food and water bowls through without unclipping the main door — a genuine convenience for dogs who bolt for freedom the second that latch clicks. It's a small detail, but it's the kind of feature that makes daily use noticeably easier.

**What Owners Consistently Praise**

The feeding door gets repeated mentions. One owner noted, "This is the only kennel I've owned where I can feed my rescue without her trying to escape every time. She stays calm, I stay calm." The fold-flat design also earns consistent praise from campers and travelers who need a portable containment solution.

The size is another frequent positive. The dimensions work well for medium to large breeds, giving them room to stand, turn, and lie down comfortably. Most owners felt the advertised size matched the actual product.

**Where It Falls Short**

Beyond the assembly frustration and wire gauge concerns, the lack of included ground coverage is the biggest practical issue. Dogs will dig at the dirt line under the panels, which both undermines the kennel's stability and creates a muddy mess after rain. You'll want a tarp, paver base, or gravel pad underneath.

The latch mechanism on the main door is functional but not beefy. It's easy for humans to operate, which also means an intelligent dog can learn to work it over time. If you have an escape-prone breed, replace the latch with a carabiner or padlock.

**Who Should Buy This**

This kennel makes sense for three specific buyers: campers and travelers who need a portable, foldable containment solution; owners of calm, medium-to-large dogs who need a shaded outdoor spot during the day; and people on a budget who understand they're getting a mid-tier product, not a permanent fortress.

Skip it if you have a powerful chewer, a confirmed escape artist, or a dog who will be left unsupervised in it for long stretches. You'll end up replacing this with something sturdier within a year.

**FAQ**

**Q: Does the roof provide real rain protection?**
A: Yes, for light to moderate rain. It's a solid panel that sheds water effectively. Heavy wind-driven rain will still get in around the edges.

**Q: Does it come with a floor?**
A: No. You'll need to purchase a ground tarp, gravel, or a paver base separately. This adds to the total cost, so factor that in.

**Q: Is this suitable for a puppy?**
A: Yes, for supervised outdoor time. The wire spacing is tight enough that puppies won't slip through. Just be aware they'll grow into the chewing stage where these panels will be tested.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you need a portable, foldable kennel for a calm dog, or you want a budget-friendly shaded enclosure for daytime use. **Skip it if** you're looking for a permanent, escape-proof structure or have a destructive dog.

**Rating: 3.7/5** — A solid mid-tier option with a genuinely useful feeding door and roof, undermined by assembly headaches and a wire gauge that won't survive determined dogs. Worth the money if your expectations match what it actually is.
