Let's be direct: most "super soft" hay on Amazon is a marketing lie. It's dusty, stemmy, and your guinea pig will turn its nose up faster than you can refill the hay rack. Small Pet Select's 3rd Cutting is different—but it's also not perfect. Here's the real breakdown after digging through 210 ratings and what actual pet owners are saying.

## What You're Actually Getting

The bag contains 3rd cutting timothy hay, which means it's harvested later in the season than 1st or 2nd cutting. The result is a finer, leafier product with fewer thick stems. As shown in the product image, the hay has a greenish-golden color—a good sign of freshness. The 3rd cutting is noticeably softer to the touch than standard timothy, and the smell is unmistakably fresh, like cut grass in summer rather than musty barn storage.

## Performance and Build Quality

For small pets, this hay performs well in two key areas: acceptance and digestion. The soft texture makes it easier for senior rabbits with dental issues or young guinea pigs to chew. The leaf-to-stem ratio is noticeably higher than cheaper alternatives. One verified buyer noted, "My 8-year-old rabbit with molar issues actually eats this without dropping it everywhere. The first time in months."

The freshness holds up well if you store it properly. The bag isn't resealable, so transfer to a container or clip the top. Several reviewers mentioned the hay stayed green and fragrant for weeks when stored correctly.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 210 ratings, three themes keep surfacing. First, picky eaters accept it. One reviewer wrote, "My guinea pigs are spoiled brats and they devour this. They've rejected every other brand I've tried." Second, the softness is real—not just a label. Multiple buyers with elderly pets or animals recovering from surgery specifically praised the texture. Third, the smell is fresh, which matters because stale hay often means your pet won't eat it, and that's a health risk.

## Where It Falls Short

Here's the honest truth: this hay is pricey, and you're paying for a specialty product. A 10-pound bag costs noticeably more per pound than 1st or 2nd cutting options. If you have a single guinea pig, this is manageable. If you have multiple rabbits, the cost adds up fast.

The compressed packaging is another issue. The hay arrives vacuum-packed, which keeps it fresh but means you'll spend a few minutes breaking apart clumps before serving. Some reviewers found this annoying, and one noted, "I had to fluff it for five minutes before my rabbit would even look at it."

Finally, this is not a complete diet for adult rabbits. The 3rd cutting has lower fiber content than 1st cutting, which is critical for normal adult rabbit dental health. Use this as a supplement or for specific situations, not as your rabbit's only hay source.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have a senior pet with dental issues, a notoriously picky eater, or a young animal transitioning from alfalfa to grass hay. Also worth it if you're tired of throwing away dusty, rejected hay—the higher acceptance rate means less waste overall.

**Skip it if:** You have multiple adult rabbits on a budget, or you're looking for a high-fiber staple hay. For those cases, stick with 1st or 2nd cutting.

## FAQ

**Q: Is this hay safe for baby rabbits and guinea pigs?**
Yes, the soft texture is actually ideal for young animals under 6 months, but they still need alfalfa for proper calcium and protein. Mix this with alfalfa until they're mature.

**Q: How long does a bag last for one guinea pig?**
Most buyers report 3-4 weeks for a 10-pound bag with one pig, assuming daily feeding and regular hay rack refills.

**Q: Does this hay have dust or mold issues?**
No—this is one of the cleaner hays on the market. Reviewers consistently note minimal dust, and no recent mold complaints appear in the ratings.
