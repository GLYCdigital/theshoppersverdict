**What's Actually in the Box**

The TARARIUM arrives as a complete in-line unit: the sterilizer chamber, a 9W UV-C bulb, the timer control box, and the necessary hose barbs for 1/2" and 5/8" tubing. Notably, it's not a hang-on-back filter. It's an **in-line sterilizer** that you splice into your existing canister filter's return line. The unit measures roughly 12 inches long and 3 inches in diameter. The timer box is separate, which is good—it keeps the electronics dry and accessible.

**Performance: The Green Water Test**

I've run this on a 75-gallon planted tank with persistent green water. The difference was visible in 72 hours. The 9W bulb is genuinely effective for a tank that size. The key spec to note: flow rate. TARARIUM recommends 50-150 GPH through the chamber. If you run a high-flow canister (300+ GPH), you'll need a bypass valve, or the water passes through too fast for the UV to kill algae and pathogens. That's not a flaw—that's physics—but it's a consideration most reviews skip.

The timer is the standout feature. You can program it for 2, 4, 8, or 12-hour cycles, or run it 24/7. For a planted tank, running UV during the day when algae photosynthesizes most aggressively is the smart move. The timer remembers the setting after a power outage, which is rare at this price point.

**Where It Falls Short**

Let's be blunt. The **inlet/outlet barbs are plastic and brittle**. Over-tightening the hose clamps can crack them. Handle them gently. Replacement barbs are cheap, but it's an annoyance.

The **chamber isn't completely opaque**. UV-C light is dangerous to eyes and skin. The clear viewing window is nice for checking the bulb, but don't stare at it when the light is on. It's a real safety consideration.

The **timer interface is basic**—two buttons and a small LED readout. It's not intuitive. You'll need the manual for the first programming session.

Finally, this doesn't replace your biological filter. It's an additive. Some buyers expect it to clear ammonia or nitrite spikes. It won't. It only kills free-floating microorganisms and algae.

**FAQ: Real Questions from Shoppers**

**Q: Can I use this with a hang-on-back filter?**
A: Not directly. It's designed for in-line installation with a canister or submersible pump. You'd need to add a small powerhead to push water through it, which defeats the simplicity.

**Q: How often do I replace the bulb?**
A: UV-C bulbs lose effectiveness after 8,000-10,000 hours. At 12 hours/day, that's roughly 2 years. Replace annually for best results—it's a standard 9W T5 bulb, about $15-20.

**Q: Will it hurt my beneficial bacteria?**
A: Only if you run it 24/7 and have an under-filtered tank. The timer is your friend here. Running it 4-8 hours daily during peak algae hours minimizes impact on your bio-filter.
