Let's be honest about dog treats: most are either nutritionally questionable or so processed your dog might as well be eating cardboard. Buddy BUDDER's squeeze packs sit in a rare middle ground — a functional training tool that doesn't make you feel like a negligent pet parent. Here's the breakdown after digging through 220 ratings and the product specs.

**What you're actually buying**

The six-pack gives you three flavors — original peanut butter, bacon, and honey. Each pouch is a squeeze tube, roughly the size of a travel toothpaste, designed for on-the-go dispensing. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: peanuts (or the flavoring agent), and nothing else problematic. Critically, there's no xylitol — the artificial sweetener that's toxic to dogs and shows up in far too many "natural" peanut butters marketed to humans.

**Real-world performance**

The squeeze design is genuinely clever for training scenarios. You can dispense a pea-sized amount without touching slimy peanut butter, which matters when you're mid-walk or in a training class. The texture is thicker than human peanut butter — more like a paste — which means it stays on the pouch tip without dripping everywhere.

That thickness cuts both ways though. Several buyers noted the squeeze packs need warming up in your hand for a minute before they flow properly, especially in cooler months. One reviewer put it plainly: "Great for training but my fingers got tired squeezing it out. It's really dense." That's not a dealbreaker, but it's worth knowing before you buy.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The flavor variety is the standout feature across reviews. Dogs that turned their noses up at standard peanut butter apparently go nuts for the bacon variant. One reviewer wrote: "My picky golden retriever ignores every treat except these. The bacon one is his kryptonite." Another mentioned using them for pill administration: "Squeeze a little on the pill and my dog thinks it's a treat. Works every single time."

The portability also earns consistent praise. The packs are lightweight, don't need refrigeration, and survive being tossed in a bag without leaking. Multiple reviewers mentioned using them for travel and vet visits.

**Where it falls short**

The lack of resealability is the biggest practical flaw. Each pouch is single-use — once you squeeze, you're committed. For a 70-pound lab that's fine, but for a Chihuahua, one pouch is several days' worth of treats, and you can't reseal it. You'll either waste product or give your dog way more than intended.

The price per ounce also runs higher than jarred peanut butter. You're paying for convenience and portion control, which is fair — but if you're only using this at home, a jar and a spoon does the same job for less.

**Who should buy this**

This is tailor-made for three types of owners: people actively training their dogs (the squeeze delivery is genuinely superior to treats for rapid reinforcement), dogs that are picky eaters or pill-averse (the bacon flavor is a reliable bribe), and anyone who travels with their dog and wants a mess-free treat option.

Skip it if you have a small dog, primarily feed treats at home, or want a budget option — you'll get more value from a standard jar.

**Frequently asked questions**

*Is this safe for dogs with peanut allergies?* No — the base is peanuts. If your dog has shown sensitivity to peanuts, avoid this and look for a seed-based alternative.

*How many calories per pack?* The exact count varies by flavor, but expect roughly 90-110 calories per pouch. Factor that into your dog's daily intake.

*Can I use this in a Kong toy?* The texture is too thick to flow into the toy's interior. It works better as a smear on the outside or as a direct squeeze.

**The verdict**

Buy it if: You're training your dog, dealing with a picky eater, or need a mess-free travel treat. The natural ingredients and xylitol-free formula give it a clear safety edge over most competitors.

Skip it if: You have a small dog (portion control issues), want the cheapest per-ounce option, or only need a home treat.

At 4.2/5, this earns its rating — a genuinely useful product with one notable design flaw (no reseal) that keeps it from being excellent. For training purposes specifically, it's the best squeeze treat option I've seen at this price point.
