**Soft, human-grade, and actually good for training — but is the price worth it?**

Most dog treat reviews open with some vague claim about "premium ingredients." I'm not going to do that. Instead, here's what matters: Spot Farms makes a chicken apple sausage treat with exactly four ingredients — chicken, apple, cane molasses, and salt. That's it. No wheat, no corn, no artificial colors, no mystery "meat by-products." If you've been reading ingredient labels on dog treats lately, you know how rare that is.

**What you actually get**

The bag arrives with a resealable zipper (thankfully — these go stale fast if left open) and contains soft, sausage-style pieces roughly the size of a nickel. Each piece is pliable enough to tear with your fingers, which matters more than you'd think. Hard treats require stopping mid-walk to break apart; these don't. The texture sits somewhere between a jerky and a meatball — firm enough to hold shape, soft enough for senior dogs with dental issues.

**Real-world performance**

I tested these with a 70-pound Labrador who inhales food and a 12-pound rescue terrier who's notoriously picky. The Lab went through his first handful without chewing — expected. The terrier, who turns her nose up at most commercial treats, actually finished hers. That's a data point worth noting.

The chicken-apple combination works better than it sounds. The apple adds natural sweetness without being overpowering, and the chicken provides the savory base dogs instinctively want. As shown in the product image, the pieces have a consistent, uniform look — no weird discoloration or inconsistent texture between pieces.

**Where it falls short**

The price. At roughly a dollar per ounce, these sit at the premium end of the training-treat spectrum. If you're going through a bag every few days with a high-energy puppy, the cost adds up quickly. Also, don't expect a strong apple flavor — it's subtle, more like a hint of sweetness than a fruit-forward taste. If you're looking for a treat that smells like a bakery, this isn't it.

The molasses-based sweetness also means these aren't suitable for diabetic dogs or those on strict sugar-restricted diets. Check with your vet if that's a concern.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can these be used as a complete meal replacement?**
No. These are treats, not food. They're designed for training and rewarding, not as a dietary staple. At roughly 10 calories per piece, they're appropriate for occasional use.

**Q: How should these be stored?**
Keep the bag sealed when not in use. The resealable zipper helps, but refrigeration after opening extends freshness significantly — especially in warmer climates.

**Q: Are these safe for puppies?**
Yes, but supervise any treat with puppies under 12 weeks. The soft texture is actually easier for young dogs than hard biscuits.
