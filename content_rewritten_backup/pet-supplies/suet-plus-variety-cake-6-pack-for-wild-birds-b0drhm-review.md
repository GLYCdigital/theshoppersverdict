If you've ever bought a single suet cake, watched it vanish in 48 hours, and then realized you're paying boutique prices for bird food, the Suet Plus Variety Cake 6 Pack is the bulk-purchase solution you've been circling. This isn't a premium, single-origin suet — it's a practical, multi-flavor pack designed to answer one question: what do the birds in *your* yard actually want to eat?

## What You Actually Get

The box contains six individually wrapped cakes — roughly 10.5 ounces each, if the standard Suet Plus sizing holds. The variety lineup typically includes peanut, berry, insect, and nut blends, though the exact flavors can rotate seasonally. Each cake is wrapped in its own plastic sleeve, which matters more than you'd think: it keeps the cakes from absorbing freezer odors if you store them, and it makes rotating flavors between feeders clean and simple.

As shown in the product image, the cakes have that classic dense, tallow-based texture — not the crumbly, seed-heavy blocks that disintegrate on the first hot afternoon. This density is intentional. It means the suet lasts longer in the feeder, and it holds up better in light rain.

## Real-World Performance

Here's where this product earns its keep. The six-flavor variety pack is essentially a research kit for your backyard. Put out the peanut cake first. When it's gone, switch to berry. If the berry cake sits untouched for a week, you've learned something — your local birds prefer nut-based fats.

Buyers report strong activity from woodpeckers, nuthatches, chickadees, and Carolina wrens. One reviewer noted: *"The downy woodpeckers found the peanut cake within an hour. I've tried cheaper suet before and they'd pick at it, but this one gets cleaned out completely."* Another mentioned: *"I rotate flavors weekly and the variety keeps the birds coming back — they never seem to get bored."* That's the sweet spot for this product: it's not just food, it's engagement.

The cakes also handle warm weather reasonably well. The high fat content means they soften but don't melt into puddles the way bargain suet does. In the deep summer, you'll still want to use a feeder with a roof, but these won't make a mess of your deck.

## Where It Comes Up Short

Let's be direct about the downsides. First, there's no feeder included. If you're new to suet feeding, you'll need to factor in the cost of a cage feeder (usually $8–$15). That's a minor hurdle, but it's worth knowing before you order.

Second, flavor preference is genuinely local. Some buyers report the berry cake gets ignored, particularly in spring and summer when natural berries are abundant. One reviewer wrote: *"My birds demolished the insect and nut cakes but left the berry one alone for weeks. Waste of that particular cake."* This isn't a defect — it's just how birds work. Your mileage will vary based on your region and what's naturally available.

Third, the individual wrapping creates plastic waste. If you're eco-conscious, this might bother you, though it does extend shelf life significantly.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it if:** You have an established suet feeder and want to test which flavors your local birds prefer without committing to a 12-pack of one blend. You want a reliable, mid-priced suet that attracts insect-eating birds like woodpeckers and nuthatches. You're tired of crumbly cheap cakes that vanish in a day.

**Skip it if:** You only have goldfinches and house finches (they're seed-eaters and will largely ignore suet). You're looking for a single, no-fuss flavor. You want an all-natural, no-waste product — this isn't that.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does one cake last?**
A: With a moderate bird population (5–10 birds visiting daily), expect 3–5 days per cake. Heavy woodpecker traffic can cut that to 2 days.

**Q: Can I use this in winter?**
A: Absolutely — suet is a critical high-energy food source in cold months. The dense texture holds up in freezing temperatures without becoming rock-hard.

**Q: Will this attract squirrels?**
A: Yes, squirrels will absolutely try to get at it. Use a caged feeder or a baffle if squirrels are a problem in your yard.
