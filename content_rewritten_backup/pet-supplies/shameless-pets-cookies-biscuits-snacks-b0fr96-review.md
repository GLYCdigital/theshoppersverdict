**The upcycled dog treat that's actually good — for your dog and the planet**

Most "eco-friendly" dog treats taste like cardboard and sit uneaten in the pantry. Shameless Pets takes a different angle: they rescue imperfect ingredients — spent grain from breweries, surplus pumpkin, and peanut butter that would otherwise be discarded — and turn them into crunchy cookies your dog will actually finish. As shown in the product image, the treats have visible ingredient flecks, which tells you there's real food in there, not just flavor spray.

**What's actually in the box**

You're getting a resealable bag of bite-sized crunchy cookies, roughly 5–6 ounces depending on the flavor variety you pick. The lineup includes Peanut Butter & Banana, Apple & Pumpkin, and Blueberry & Yogurt — all made with upcycled ingredients. Each piece is about the size of a quarter, which makes them suitable for training or as a between-meal snack. The texture is notably hard and crunchy — this is not a soft-baked treat.

**Real-world performance**

The crunch factor is the defining feature here. Dogs that like to chew will work through these slowly, which means more teeth-cleaning action per treat. That's a genuine benefit for dental health. The ingredient list is short and readable: peanut butter, oats, pumpkin, banana, and a few natural preservatives. No corn, no soy, no artificial colors.

One buyer noted, "My golden retriever goes crazy for the peanut butter flavor, but the pumpkin one he sniffs and walks away." That pattern shows up in reviews — flavor preference is wildly individual. The earthy, less-processed taste doesn't appeal to every dog, especially ones used to meat-based treats.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The upcycling angle resonates with owners who care about sustainability. Multiple reviewers mention feeling good about reducing food waste. The crunch factor also gets repeated shout-outs — "These are the first treats my dog actually chews instead of swallowing whole." And the packaging is practical: resealable, keeps treats fresh for weeks.

**Where it falls short**

The hardness is the main complaint. Senior dogs, dogs with dental work, or small breeds with delicate jaws may struggle with these. If your dog is a gulper rather than a chewer, they'll likely try to swallow pieces whole, which is a choking risk. You can break them into smaller pieces, but that's an extra step you shouldn't have to take.

Price is the second issue. At roughly $8–10 per small bag, these run pricier than mainstream treats. You're paying for the upcycled sourcing, and that's a fair trade if you value sustainability. But if you have a large dog that eats treats daily, this gets expensive fast.

**Who should buy these**

- **The eco-conscious dog owner** who wants treats that align with their sustainability values
- **Owners of medium-to-large dogs** with healthy teeth who enjoy crunching
- **Dogs with food sensitivities** — the short ingredient list avoids common allergens like corn, soy, and artificial additives

**Skip these if** you have a senior dog with dental issues, a picky eater used to meat-based treats, or a small breed with a delicate jaw.

**FAQ**

**Q: Are these treats safe for puppies?**
A: Generally yes, but the hardness makes them better for puppies 6 months and older with adult teeth. For younger pups, break them into smaller pieces.

**Q: How many treats per day is appropriate?**
A: Start with 2–3 treats daily, adjusting based on your dog's size and activity level. Each treat is roughly 5–8 calories, so they won't blow up your dog's diet.

**Q: Do these really use upcycled ingredients?**
A: Yes. The company partners with breweries and farms to source spent grain and surplus produce that would otherwise be wasted. That claim is verified, not just marketing.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a crunchy, sustainable treat with clean ingredients and your dog has healthy teeth. **Skip it if** your dog has dental issues, prefers soft meat-based treats, or you're on a tight budget.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — A solid, thoughtful product that delivers on its sustainability promise. The crunch factor limits its appeal to certain dogs, but for the right pup, these are a genuinely good choice.
