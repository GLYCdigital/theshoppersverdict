## Where it falls short

Honesty time. The silvervine sticks splinter. Heavily-chewed sticks will break into small, sharp shards that you need to sweep up and monitor. If your cat is an aggressive chewer, inspect the sticks after each session and toss anything that's splitting.

The rope also lacks a handle. For interactive play where you're dangling it and moving it around, you'll be gripping the rope directly — which works but gets awkward for longer sessions. Your hand will start to smell like cat saliva, and the rope's rough texture isn't comfortable after 10 minutes.

Finally, there's no storage solution. The sticks and rope will live loose in your toy basket or on the floor. Minor, but worth noting.

## FAQ

**Are silvervine sticks safe for kittens?**
Yes, but supervise them. Kittens have softer teeth and may try to swallow larger chunks. Stick to supervised sessions until they're older.

**How long do the sticks last?**
Depends on your cat's chewing intensity. Light chewers get 2-3 weeks per stick. Heavy chewers might destroy one in a few days. Replace any stick that starts splintering.

**Does the rope work with catnip spray?**
It can, but the rope's primary function is chewing, not rolling. If you want to add catnip, sprinkle it on the knots — it'll extend the toy's novelty.
