If your dog has destroyed three $15 Woof Pupsicles by chewing the silicone tip off, you already know the real problem: the refill pods are overpriced and underfilled. The BABORUI 6 Cavities Silicone Dog Treat Molds exist to fix that exact pain point. These molds let you batch-freeze homemade pupsicle refills at roughly 15 cents per serving instead of $2.50 per pod. But do they survive real-world freezer abuse? I tested them for three weeks with peanut butter, pumpkin, and Greek yogurt blends.

## What You Actually Get

The package includes one rectangular silicone mold with six identical cavities — each cavity holds about 2.5 ounces of liquid filling. The silicone is thick (roughly 3mm at the walls), not flimsy like cheap cupcake liners. The cavities are shaped with a slight taper at the bottom, which is the critical design feature: the frozen puck slides out cleanly when you push from the underside. There’s no tray, no lid, no scraper — just the mold. That’s fine, because the simplicity is the point.

## Real-World Performance

Here’s where this mold earns its keep. After mixing 1 cup of plain Greek yogurt, 2 tablespoons of peanut butter, and 1 mashed banana, I poured the mixture into all six cavities. Freeze time at 0°F was 4 hours for a solid freeze — not the 2 hours some reviews claim. The pucks released perfectly after a 20-second dip in warm tap water. They slid out intact, no crumbling, no stuck edges. The tapered bottom is what makes this work; flat-bottomed molds leave you chiseling frozen goo with a butter knife.

The pucks fit the classic Woof Pupsicle toy with a satisfying click — no wiggling, no falling out mid-lick. That’s a huge upgrade over the official refill pods, which sometimes arrive crushed or melted in shipping.

## Where It Falls Short

The lack of a lid is the biggest annoyance. If you fill the cavities to the very top, the liquid expands during freezing and spills over the edges. You must leave at least 1/4 inch of headspace — that means each puck is slightly smaller than the advertised capacity. Not a dealbreaker, but worth knowing.

Second, the silicone absorbs odors. After I made a batch with sardine oil for a joint-health recipe, the mold smelled fishy for four washes. A vinegar soak fixed it, but it’s an extra step.

Third, the cavity size is fixed for the classic Woof Pupsicle. If you have the larger XL toy, these pucks will fall straight through. Check your toy size before ordering.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Batch-preppers**: If you like making a Sunday batch of frozen treats, this mold gives you six servings at once.
- **Budget-focused dog owners**: The math works — at $0.15 per serving, you break even after one week versus buying pods.
- **Owners of picky eaters**: Customizing flavors (peanut butter, blueberry, sweet potato) beats the limited store-bought options.

Skip it if you only make one treat at a time, if your dog has an XL Pupsicle, or if you can’t tolerate a little extra cleanup from the missing lid.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I microwave this mold to release stuck treats?**  
A: Yes, but only on low power for 10 seconds max. High heat can degrade the silicone over time. The warm-water method works better and doesn’t risk warping.

**Q: Is the silicone BPA-free and food-safe?**  
A: The manufacturer states food-grade, BPA-free silicone. I’ve seen no discoloration or degradation after 12+ cycles. It’s safe for human food too, though I wouldn’t use it for both — the odor absorption is real.

**Q: Will this fit other frozen treat toys, like the Bento Ball?**  
A: No. The dimensions are specifically matched to the classic Woof Pupsicle. For other toys, you’re better off with a universal ice cube tray.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you own a classic Woof Pupsicle and want to cut refill costs by 90% without sacrificing treat quality. **Skip it if** you need a lid, have an XL toy, or only make treats occasionally — in that case, a $6 regular ice cube tray works fine.

**Rating: 4.6/5** — A practical, well-made accessory that solves a real problem. The missing lid and fixed cavity size keep it from a perfect score, but for the price, this is the best value in frozen dog treat prep right now.
