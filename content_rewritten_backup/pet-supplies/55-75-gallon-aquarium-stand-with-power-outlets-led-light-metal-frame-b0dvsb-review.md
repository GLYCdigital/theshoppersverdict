Let me be direct: most aquarium stands under $200 are either particleboard disasters waiting to warp or flimsy metal frames that wobble. This 55-75 gallon stand from the metal-frame category actually threads the needle — but not without caveats.

**What You Actually Get**

The box contains the welded steel frame (disassembled into two side panels and crossbars), a 4-outlet power strip with surge protection already mounted, an LED light bar pre-attached to the lower shelf, and all necessary bolts, Allen keys, and rubber floor pads. Assembly runs 30-45 minutes with two people — manageable, though the included instructions are mostly diagrams with minimal text.

The footprint measures roughly 48" x 18" — standard for 55, 65, and 75-gallon tanks. The top surface has a thin rubber mat to cushion the tank's bottom frame. The advertised 1,000 lb weight rating is the real story here: a filled 75-gallon tank weighs around 850 lbs, so you've got headroom. That's not marketing fluff; the steel crossbars are genuinely thicker than what you'd find on budget racks.

**Real-World Performance**

The integrated power strip is the standout feature. Four outlets mounted on the right rear leg mean your filter, heater, and light plug in cleanly without an octopus of extension cords behind the tank. One buyer noted, "The surge protector placement is genius — I finally got rid of the tangled mess under my tank." The LED light bar on the bottom shelf is a subtle touch: it's not a plant-growing grow light, but it's plenty bright for seeing your sump or canister filter during maintenance.

The open-frame design is a double-edged sword. On one hand, it gives you unobstructed access to equipment. On the other, everything's visible. As one 4-star reviewer put it: "Great stand, but if you want to hide your gear, buy a cabinet-style stand instead. My canister filter and hoses are out in the open."

**Where It Falls Short**

The cons are straightforward. First, the mounting hardware — several buyers reported the included M6 bolts stripping if over-torqued. Use the Allen key gently, or swap in higher-grade bolts from a hardware store for $5. Second, there's no storage. This is a bare metal frame; no shelves, no doors, no space for food, test kits, or nets. You'll need a separate cabinet nearby.

Third, the LED light bar is wired into the power strip, so you can't switch it independently without unplugging. Minor annoyance, but worth knowing.

**Who Should Buy This**

- **Sump users**: The open frame and bottom LED make this ideal for sump tanks. You'll actually see your equipment and access it easily.
- **Budget-conscious aquascapers**: You get a load-rated stand with power management for roughly the same price as a basic wooden stand with zero features.
- **Renters**: Metal frame disassembles flat for moves. A solid wood stand does not.

**Skip it if** you want a furniture-grade piece that hides equipment, or if you're placing it in a living room where aesthetics matter more than functionality.

**FAQ**

**Q: Will this hold a 75-gallon tank safely?**
A: Yes. The 1,000 lb rating gives you proper margin over the ~850 lb filled weight. Just make sure the floor is level and use the included rubber pads.

**Q: Can I mount this on carpet?**
A: It works, but the rubber pads sink into soft carpet. Consider a plywood base board for even weight distribution.

**Q: Is the power strip replaceable if it dies?**
A: It's mounted with standard screws and plugs into a regular outlet. Replacing it takes about 10 minutes with basic tools.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you prioritize function, need a load-rated stand for a large tank, and want integrated power management. **Skip it if** you need storage or a furniture-grade appearance.
