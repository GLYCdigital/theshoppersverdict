Let's be honest about what you're actually considering here: a vegetarian dinner for a carnivorous pet. It sounds like a contradiction, but Evanger's has been making this formula for decades, and it fills a real niche. The question isn't whether dogs *should* eat vegetarian — it's whether this particular can delivers complete nutrition without making mealtime a battle. After digging through 220 ratings sitting at 4.4 stars, here's the real story.

**What's in the box (and what's in the can)**

You get 12 cans of 12.5-ounce wet food. That's a solid month's worth for a small dog or two weeks for a medium breed. As shown in the product image, the food is a homogeneous, pâté-style loaf — no discernible chunks, just a smooth, uniform texture. The ingredient list is refreshingly short: carrots, peas, potatoes, brown rice, and a vitamin/mineral premix. It's certified complete and balanced by AAFCO standards, which is the critical part — this isn't a supplement or a "treat" masquerading as a meal. It's a legit dinner.

**Performance and real-world use**

The texture is where this food both wins and loses. On one hand, it's easy to mix with dry kibble — it acts like a gravy binder that coats every piece. On the other, it has a pronounced earthy, vegetal smell that's unmistakable when you crack a can open. Some buyers have noted their dogs gobble it up; one reviewer wrote, "My senior dog with kidney issues can't tolerate meat protein anymore, and this is the only thing she'll eat without coaxing." Another mentioned, "I use it as a topper for my picky Shih Tzu, and she licks the bowl clean."

But it's not universal. Several owners of notoriously picky breeds reported their dogs sniffed it once and walked away. That mushy consistency also means it's not ideal for slow-feeders or treat-dispensing toys — it'll smear rather than hold its shape.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The standout theme in positive reviews is relief — relief for dogs with meat allergies, pancreatitis, kidney disease, or digestive sensitivities. Multiple reviewers specifically called out using this during veterinary-recommended elimination diets. The portion size also gets consistent praise; a 12.5-ounce can is bigger than the standard 5.5-ounce competitor cans, so you're getting better value per feeding.

**Where it falls short**

The smell is the most common complaint, and it's not subtle. It's not rancid — just intensely "vegetable soup" in a way that lingers after serving. Second, the ingredient list includes brown rice, which is fine for most dogs but a dealbreaker for grain-free households. And while the price point varies, this is not a budget option compared to standard meat-based wet foods. You're paying a premium for the specialized formula.

**Who should buy this**

- **Dogs with meat protein sensitivities**: This is a genuinely effective option when your vet recommends eliminating animal proteins.
- **Owners who want a plant-based option for ethical reasons**: It's AAFCO complete, so you're not DIY-ing nutrition.
- **Dogs on medication that requires low-protein meals**: The moderate protein content works well here.

**Skip it if** you have a dog that demands meat-flavored food, you need a grain-free recipe, or you're looking for the cheapest wet food per ounce.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can puppies eat this?**
A: It's formulated for adult maintenance, not growth. Puppies need higher protein and specific calcium-to-phosphorus ratios. Stick to puppy-specific formulas unless your vet directs otherwise.

**Q: How much should I feed?**
A: For a 30-40 lb dog, roughly one full can per day, split into two meals. Adjust based on your dog's activity level and body condition. The can includes feeding guidelines, but your vet's recommendation trumps those.

**Q: Is it actually vegetarian or vegan?**
A: Vegetarian. It contains no meat, but it does include eggs (check the full ingredient list on the label). If you need strictly vegan, this isn't it.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** your dog has meat sensitivities, you've gotten vet approval for a vegetarian diet, or you want a reliable plant-based wet food that won't shortchange nutrition. **Skip it if** your dog is a picky eater with a history of rejecting unfamiliar textures, or you need a grain-free formula.

This earns a solid 4.2 out of 5. It does exactly what it claims — complete vegetarian nutrition in a convenient 12-pack — with the caveats around aroma and texture being genuine but manageable. For the dogs that need it, this can be a literal lifesaver. For everyone else, it's a well-made niche product that earns its place in the rotation.
