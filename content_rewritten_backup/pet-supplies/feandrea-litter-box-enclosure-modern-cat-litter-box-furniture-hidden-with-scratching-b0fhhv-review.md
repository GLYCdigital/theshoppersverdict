If you're tired of looking at a plastic pan full of clumped litter every time you walk into your living room, the Feandrea Litter Box Enclosure is one of the more practical fixes on the market. This isn't a cheap fabric tent that collapses after three weeks — it's a solid piece of furniture rated 4.4 out of 5 stars from 220+ buyers, and it actually looks like it belongs in a home rather than a pet supply aisle.

## What You Actually Get

The box arrives flat-packed with all hardware included. You're getting a cabinet-style enclosure measuring roughly 23.6"L x 18.5"W x 21.7"H — compact enough for apartment living but substantial enough to feel like real furniture. The unit features a side-entry swing door for your cat, a large open top for easy scooping access, and a built-in sisal scratching post attached to one side panel. The engineered wood has a water-resistant laminate coating, which matters more than you'd think when you're cleaning up litter dust and occasional accidents.

## Build Quality and Real-World Performance

The particleboard construction is thicker than what you typically find at this price point — we're talking 0.5-inch panels, not the flimsy 0.3-inch stuff that bows under weight. The water-resistant coating genuinely holds up; one buyer noted, "I've spilled water on the top twice and wiped it off without any swelling or warping. The finish is surprisingly durable." The top surface is sturdy enough to hold a plant, a small lamp, or even a 15-pound cat who decides it's a better perch than the actual cat tree.

The scratching post is the standout feature here. Wrapped in natural sisal rope, it's attached firmly to the side panel and gives your cat a legitimate alternative to your couch. Multiple reviewers confirmed their cats actually use it — one wrote, "My cat ignored the scratching post I bought separately for months, but she uses this one daily. I think it's because it's right next to where she does her business."

The interior space is generous: approximately 20.5 inches long, 17.7 inches wide, and 17.7 inches tall. That fits a standard XL litter pan with room to spare, which is critical for larger breeds like Maine Coons or for cats who prefer not to feel cramped while doing their business. The side-entry design also helps contain litter scatter — cats naturally kick backward, and the solid walls catch most of it.

## Where It Falls Short

Assembly is the biggest complaint across reviews. You're looking at 30 to 45 minutes of work, and several buyers mentioned that screw holes occasionally didn't line up perfectly. One frustrated reviewer wrote, "The instructions are mostly pictures, and one of the cam locks was stripped out of the box. I made it work, but it took longer than it should have." If you're not handy with tools, set aside an evening.

The swing door is functional but not secure. It swings freely, which means a determined or heavy cat can push it open from the outside — and when they do, litter tracks out with them. There's no latch or magnetic closure, which feels like a miss at this price point. A simple magnet would have solved it.

The top surface, while attractive, is real wood veneer that scratches relatively easily. If you plan to put decor on top, use a protective mat or coasters. One buyer learned this the hard way: "I put a ceramic planter on top and it left a permanent mark. The wood is nice but not super durable."

## Who Should Buy This

This is ideal for apartment dwellers who want to hide the litter box without sacrificing floor space, or for anyone who's tired of explaining why there's a plastic box in their dining room. It's also a solid choice for multi-cat households with a single enclosure — the interior is large enough for two smaller cats to share comfortably. If you have a very large breed (think 15+ pounds), measure your litter tray before ordering to ensure the 17.7-inch height clearance works.

## FAQ

**Q: Will this fit a standard 20-inch litter pan?**
Yes, but check the height. The interior is 17.7 inches tall, so a standard 10-12 inch high pan fits fine. Taller pans with high backs might not clear the door opening.

**Q: Does this control litter odor?**
It contains odor somewhat because it's enclosed, but it's not airtight. You'll still need a good odor-control litter or a carbon filter. The door gap and top opening let air circulate, which helps drying but doesn't trap smell.

**Q: Can this be used outdoors or in a garage?**
Not recommended. The particleboard, even with its water-resistant coating, isn't built for humidity or temperature extremes. Keep it indoors.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you want a genuinely attractive, functional piece of furniture that hides the litter box and gives your cat a scratching surface. The 4.4-star rating holds up — this is one of the better-built options in this category, and the dual-purpose design earns its keep.

**Skip it if** you're not comfortable with a 45-minute assembly, or if you need a fully enclosed unit that locks. This is a privacy screen, not a fortress.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — Deducting points for the assembly headaches and the door that doesn't latch. Otherwise, this is a smart purchase that makes your home look better and your cat happier.
