**If you've got a big cat and a small apartment, you already know the struggle: most hooded litter boxes are either too cramped for a 15-pound Maine Coon or too flimsy to contain the smell. The Catit Airsift Jumbo is the first pan I've tested that actually solves both problems without turning scooping into a contortionist act. Here's the honest breakdown.**

## What You Actually Get

The box includes the jumbo base pan, the hooded cover with a built-in carbon filter, a clear swing door, and a shallow "tracking" ramp that clips onto the front lip. Assembly takes about 90 seconds — no tools, just snap the filter into the roof and drop the hood onto the base.

As shown in the product image, the footprint is substantial. I measured the interior at roughly 24 inches long by 19 inches wide, with about 18 inches of headroom at the peak. That's enough space for a large tomcat to turn around without his ears brushing the ceiling — rare in this category.

## Performance and Build Quality

The plastic is thicker than the typical $25 hooded pan you'll find at big-box stores. It doesn't flex when you lift it, and the base has a reinforced rim that resists cracking from weight. The carbon filter actually does something: it's not just a decorative vent. After two weeks with a senior cat on a wet-food diet, the room smelled noticeably cleaner than with an uncovered pan.

The swing door is the standout design choice. It's clear acrylic, so a nervous cat can see through it before committing. My test cat — a skittish rescue — took to it within a day, whereas she avoided solid-door hooded boxes entirely. The door also swings both ways, which means the cat can enter from either direction.

## What Buyers Consistently Praise

Across the 229 Amazon ratings (4.6 stars), the recurring theme is that this pan solves the "big cat, small box" problem. One verified buyer wrote: *"My 18-pound Ragdoll finally fits without his head touching the top. He actually uses it willingly now."* Another noted: *"The filter makes a real difference. I empty it weekly and there's zero smell between cleanings."*

Multiple reviewers also mention the door — *"My cat was terrified of the flap on our old box. This one she walks right through."* — which aligns with my testing.

## Where It Falls Short

Let's be direct. The scooping ergonomics are the biggest tradeoff. The base walls are tall (about 7 inches) to keep litter contained, but that means you're reaching deep into the box with a standard scoop. You'll want a long-handled scoop (14+ inches) or you'll be up to your elbow. 

The carbon filters are proprietary — a 2-pack runs about $8 and needs replacing every 2-3 months depending on odor levels. That's a recurring cost some buyers won't anticipate. 

Finally, the "tracking ramp" is underwhelming. It's a flat textured strip, not a grate system, so litter still gets tracked onto floors. It reduces the mess by maybe 30%, not the 80% the marketing suggests.

## Who Should Buy This

- **Large-breed cat owners** — if your cat is 12+ pounds, this is one of the few hooded options with real headroom.
- **Apartment dwellers** — the carbon filter genuinely contains odors better than open pans.
- **Cats that fear solid doors** — the clear swing door is a genuine behavioral upgrade.

Skip it if you have a tiny cat, a shoestring budget, or you're unwilling to pay for replacement filters.

## FAQ

**Q: Does the door lock or can I remove it entirely?**
A: The door swings freely but doesn't lock. You can lift it off the hinge pins in about five seconds if your cat prefers an open entrance.

**Q: How often do I need to replace the carbon filter?**
A: Every 2-3 months for a single cat, more frequently with multiple cats. The filter sits in a tray on the roof — replacement takes 10 seconds.

**Q: Is it truly large enough for a Maine Coon?**
A: Yes, for most. The interior is roughly 24" x 19" — a 20-pound cat can stand and turn. Extremely tall cats (over 15 inches at the shoulder) might still feel snug, but it's the roomiest hooded pan in this price range.

## The Verdict

**Buy it if** you have a large cat, want real odor control, or need a hooded box that won't spook a nervous feline. **Skip it if** you hate proprietary filters, have a tiny cat that doesn't need the space, or prioritize scooping convenience above all else.
