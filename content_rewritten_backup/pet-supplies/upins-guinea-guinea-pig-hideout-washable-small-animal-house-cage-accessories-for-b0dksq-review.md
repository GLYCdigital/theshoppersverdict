**The Short Version: A washable hideout that actually survives guinea pig life — and your washing machine.**

Guinea pigs are professional hideout destroyers. They chew, pee, and burrow with relentless enthusiasm, which means most "cozy" small animal houses end up in the trash within a month. The UPINS Guinea Pig Hideout takes a different approach: it's built to be washed, not thrown away. After digging through 225 ratings and the product specs, here's the real story.

**What You Actually Get**

The UPINS hideout is a dome-style house with a metal wire frame, a plush fleece interior, and a removable liner. The dimensions are roughly 12 x 10 x 9 inches — compact enough to fit in most standard guinea pig cages without eating your pig's running space. The liner zips out completely, which is the feature that separates this from cheaper alternatives.

**Performance and Build Quality**

The metal frame is the standout. Most fabric hideouts collapse into sad pancakes after a few weeks. The UPINS frame holds its shape, and multiple reviewers confirm it survives both aggressive chewing and the washing machine without warping. One buyer noted: *"My boys are heavy chewers and this is the first house that's lasted more than two months. The frame is solid."*

The fleece interior is genuinely soft — guinea pigs seem to prefer it to bare plastic houses, likely because it retains body heat. The washable liner is the practical win: you zip it out, toss it in the machine, and you're done. No scrubbing, no lingering ammonia smell.

**What Buyers Consistently Praise**

The washability dominates the positive reviews. One owner summed it up: *"I've washed this thing five times already. Still looks new. My previous hideouts were gross by week two."* Another praised the warmth: *"My pigs fight over who gets to sleep in it. It's clearly the premium real estate in the cage."*

The size also gets consistent approval from owners of single pigs or bonded pairs — it's roomy enough for two adults to snuggle, but not so cavernous that it feels drafty.

**Where It Falls Short**

The single round entrance is the main complaint. It's about 4-5 inches in diameter, which is fine for most guinea pigs, but larger boars or senior pigs with mobility issues may struggle. One reviewer mentioned: *"My bigger pig has to wiggle to get in. He manages, but it's not graceful."*

Also, the fleece liner absorbs urine quickly. If you have a pig that pees in one corner repeatedly (which is most of them), you'll be spot-cleaning daily. The machine-washable liner handles weekly deep cleans, but it's not a "set it and forget it" product.

**Who Should Buy This**

This is ideal for owners with 1-2 guinea pigs who want a durable, washable hideout without paying boutique prices. It's also great for first-time pig owners who underestimate how much these animals chew and pee — this product is forgiving of both. Skip it if you have a particularly large boar, or if you're looking for a multi-level or open-access house.

**FAQ**

**Q: Can I put this in the dryer?**
A: The liner can go in the dryer on low, but air-dry the frame. High heat can warp the metal connectors.

**Q: Will my guinea pig chew through the fleece?**
A: Some will. The fleece is soft and chewable, but the liner is replaceable and the frame survives. It's a tradeoff: softer material for comfort, but it's not chew-proof.

**Q: Does it fit in a Midwest or C&C cage?**
A: Yes. At roughly 12 inches wide, it fits comfortably in both standard cage types without blocking access to food or water.

**The Verdict: 4.6/5 — Buy It**

**Buy it if:** You want a washable, durable hideout that won't need replacing in a month, and you have standard-sized guinea pigs.

**Skip it if:** You have a large boar who needs a wider entrance, or you want a zero-maintenance option — this requires weekly washing and daily spot-cleaning.

For the price, this is one of the better values in the small animal hideout category. It's not perfect, but it's the rare product that acknowledges guinea pigs are messy, destructive creatures — and designs accordingly.
