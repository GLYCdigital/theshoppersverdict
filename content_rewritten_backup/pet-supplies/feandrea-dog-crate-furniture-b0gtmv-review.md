The Feandrea Dog Crate Furniture sits in a crowded category: dog crates that pretend to be furniture. Most fail because they're obviously cages with a tabletop slapped on. This one gets closer than most, but it's not without compromises. Here's what actually matters if you're considering it.

**What's in the box**

You get the crate frame, a fleece crate cover, a plush mat, and two stainless steel bowls that slide into the front. The mat is thin — roughly half an inch — so if you have a senior dog with joint issues, plan to add a memory foam insert. The cover is the surprise standout: it's not the cheap nylon sheet you see on budget crates. It's a structured, quilted fleece that fits snugly and zips off for machine washing. That alone is worth $30 in the aftermarket.

**Build quality and real-world use**

The frame is engineered wood with a rustic brown finish that reads more "coffee table" than "kennel" from across the room. The top is genuinely load-bearing — several reviewers mention setting plants, lamps, and even a 30-pound TV on it without sagging. The front door slides up and locks with a thumb latch, which is easier for arthritic hands than the twist-lock mechanisms on cheaper crates.

Assembly is tool-free. The cam locks and dowels align intuitively, and most buyers report 15–20 minutes from box to built. One recurring complaint: the door hinge holes occasionally don't line up perfectly. It's fixable with a bit of wiggling, but if you're not handy, budget an extra 10 minutes and some patience.

**What buyers consistently praise**

The dual-purpose design is the headline. One reviewer put it plainly: "I was skeptical it'd actually look like furniture, but my guests didn't even realize it was a crate until the dog walked in." Another noted the size accuracy: "The 30-inch model fits my 45-pound beagle mix perfectly — she has room to stand and turn around, but not so much space she treats it like a playpen."

Ventilation is solid too. The wire mesh runs along both sides and the back, so airflow is noticeably better than the fully-enclosed wood crates in this price range. Dogs that run hot won't be panting constantly.

**Where it falls short**

Be honest about the material. The wood composite surface scratches if your dog paws at the door when they want out. Several long-term owners report visible claw marks within a few months. If you have a determined digger, this isn't the crate for you — get a fully metal unit.

The bowls are also shallow. They're fine for water, but if your dog eats fast, the kibble will scatter. Consider replacing them with weighted bowls if that's your dog's style. And the wheels on the bottom are small and plastic — fine for sliding across hardwood, but they'll catch on carpet transitions.

**Who should buy this**

- **Apartment dwellers** who need a crate that doesn't scream "crate" when guests come over
- **Small-to-medium dog owners** (the 30-inch fits dogs up to about 50 pounds; the 36-inch stretches to 70)
- **Anyone tired of buying separate crate covers** — the included one is genuinely good

Skip it if you have a heavy chewer, a dog over 70 pounds, or you need something that will survive years of roughhousing. This is a lifestyle product, not a heavy-duty containment solution.

**FAQ**

**Q: What size Feandrea crate should I get for my dog?**
Measure your dog from nose to tail base and add 4–6 inches. That's the crate length you need. The 30-inch fits most beagles, corgis, and small terriers. The 36-inch handles labs and golden retrievers up to about 70 pounds.

**Q: Is the top actually strong enough to sit on?**
Yes. The tabletop supports up to 100 pounds per the manufacturer, and reviewer reports confirm it holds lamps, books, and small electronics without flexing.

**Q: Can I machine wash the cover?**
Yes, the fleece cover zips off and is machine washable on cold. Air dry it — the stitching holds up better than a hot dryer cycle.

**The Verdict**

**Buy it if** you want a crate that blends into your living space and you have a calm-to-moderately-active dog under 70 pounds. The included cover and bowls make it a genuine value despite the mid-range price.

**Skip it if** you need maximum durability, have a determined escape artist, or your dog is over 70 pounds. This is furniture first, crate second — and that's exactly the point.

**Rating: 4.2/5** — knocks off points for the scratch-prone finish and finicky door hinges, but the design execution and included accessories earn it a solid recommendation for the right owner.
